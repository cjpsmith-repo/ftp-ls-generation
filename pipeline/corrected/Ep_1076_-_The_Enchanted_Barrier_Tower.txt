Ep 1076 - The Enchanted Barrier Tower

Word count: 1150

[AMBIENT: QUIET STUDY ROOM]

Layla had finally gotten Zane’s attention, and a smug smile spread across her face.

“So,” she purred, “you said you didn’t want to talk? That you just wanted to focus on this stone carving?”

Zane shot her a cold glare. He clearly didn’t like being played with.

Taking the hint, Layla cut to the chase. “The most important part of Passing the Torch is getting through the Law Storm. It’s made of every single Law Power in the universe, and it can be a little… feisty.”

“Every single type of Law is in the storm?” Zane asked, his focus finally broken.

Layla nodded. “Yep. But don’t worry, they’re just tiny fragments left behind by a bunch of Supreme Lords who fought a long time ago. The whole thing is trapped inside the Enchanted Barrier Tower.”

She leaned in conspiratorially. “The tower was also a parting gift from a Supreme Lord. It looks like a tower, but it’s actually a massive enchanted barrier, built back when the world was a very different place.”

[MUSIC: EPIC DRAMATIC ORCHESTRA]

The story went that a huge battle between the Supreme Lords basically shattered the entire Orange Sea Realm. The Laws they unleashed went wild, turning the place into a chaotic, deadly vortex. Any warrior who dared enter could be torn apart in seconds by a rogue current of Law.

“Eventually,” Layla continued, “the human Supreme Lord took a huge gamble. He gathered all those rogue currents and sealed them inside the Enchanted Barrier Tower. After that, the Orange Sea Realm slowly stabilized.”

[AMBIENT: DISTANT WIND HOWLING]

Over a vast stretch of time, those chaotic powers inside the tower bled energy, eventually swirling together to form a storm. So many years had passed that now, even the storm itself was a shadow of its former self. For the last few hundred years, it had been just weak enough for the Thirteen Palaces to use as the ultimate test for their warriors.

After Layla’s explanation, it all clicked for Zane. This was why the warriors had to study the law-stones, each carved with six different Laws. The Law Storm was a chaotic mix of countless Original Laws, and the only way to survive it was to have mastered enough Law Power yourself.

“So, you’re trying to cram at the last minute, huh?” Layla said, her smugness returning. “It’s pointless, you know. Even twenty days of training here wouldn’t be enough, let alone forty hours. If you want to do well in the Law Storm, you have to rely on the work you’ve already put in.”

Zane glanced at her, unfazed. He could tell her confidence wasn’t just an act.

“Even so,” he replied quietly, “I have to try.”

“See? Useless,” Layla chirped, beaming. “Like I said, I don’t need to study this thing. I’m positive I can take first place and break the record without it. We should just hang out and talk!”

Zane rolled his eyes and turned back to his work. “Unlike you, Layla, I’m not that cocky.”

Layla’s smile didn’t fade. She watched him drop his head back over the tablet, utterly focused, and felt a flicker of something — not quite irritation. More like anticipation. She was already picturing the look on his face when she left him behind anyway, serious studying and all. What expression would he make then?

She picked up her own stone tablet and, after a moment, quietly started studying it.

[MUSIC: FOCUSED CONCENTRATION]

[AMBIENT: QUIET STUDY HALL]

Zane turned the tablet over in his hands, thinking.

He’d seen two Laws fused perfectly before — Fenton Summers’ Sword Painting merged Metal Law and Wind Law into something seamless and devastating. But this tablet had six sides, each face carrying a different Law. And where any two of them met, the powers bled into each other — a partial fusion, incomplete, but real.

Six Laws at once. He couldn’t picture what that would look like at full strength. But he didn’t need to. What he could extract from the tablet was something more practical: the method for fusing any two Laws together. That was enough to work with.

He settled in and let everything else fall away.

The hours dissolved. When the old man’s voice finally cut through the square, Zane had been deep in the tablet for just over twenty hours — most of the window gone before he’d even sat down.

[AMBIENT: LARGE HALL]

[MUSIC: CEREMONIAL DRUMS]

Elsewhere, an old man in simple cotton robes was announcing the rewards for the trial.

“Traveling a thousand miles will earn you one Tribulation Exterminating Pill,” he declared.

[SFX: MURMURING CROWD]

Zane’s eyes flickered with interest. Layla, apparently reading his expression, leaned over. “It doesn’t actually destroy a Celestial Tribulation,” she said quietly. “It delays one. Buys you time if you’re injured or caught off-guard when it hits.”

Zane nodded slowly. He thought of how easily things could go wrong mid-tribulation — an ambush, a surprise attack, a body not yet recovered from a previous fight. A pill that bought you even a few extra moments could be the difference between surviving and not.

“That could matter,” he said.

“It could,” Layla agreed.

The crowd of warriors murmured. After all, the prizes were a huge part of why they were here. They were hoping for something big.

“That seems kind of lame for Passing the Torch.”

[AMBIENT: EXCITED CROWD MURMUR]

“Are there any other prizes? C’mon, tell us!”

“Those who can travel three thousand miles will be rewarded with a Grade-One Divine-Class weapon!”

[SFX: DISAPPOINTED CROWD GROAN]

A wave of disappointment went through the other warriors from the Thirteen Palaces.

“Hey, wait a minute. They’re getting cheap on us!”

“Yeah, the prizes are definitely worse this time around. I wonder what’s up.”

The old man ignored the grumbling and continued.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]

“As for the reward for traveling ten thousand miles… I’ll announce that when one of you actually makes it that far.”

He was clearly trying to build suspense, but the crowd wasn’t having it. Getting through the Law Storm was incredibly difficult; only a handful of warriors would ever make it a thousand miles. Reaching the three-thousand-mile mark was a feat only a dozen or so could even dream of.

[AMBIENT: SKEPTICAL CROWD CHATTER]

But ten thousand miles? That was an impossible fantasy. The warriors of the Thirteen Palaces were sure no one could ever travel that far. Announcing a prize for it was pointless because no one was ever going to claim it.

Still, for all their complaints, no one was about to give up. They knew the benefits of Passing the Torch went far beyond the official prizes. A good performance meant you’d be treated like royalty back in the palaces, with access to far more resources than you had now.

From here on out, it was every warrior for themselves.
