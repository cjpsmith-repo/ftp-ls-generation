Ep 422 - Don't Be Cocky

Word count: 993

[AMBIENT: QUIET WORKSHOP]

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

Zane wasn't sure what to say to Cedric Vance. He already knew how to create divine textures—an art so sophisticated that the rune-making techniques practiced throughout the Heartlands seemed like children's scribbles in comparison. The gap between the two disciplines was almost laughable.

Still, the Cobalt Dragon had given him clear instructions about how to behave. Cedric was Cloud Hall's foremost rune expert—a big shot he couldn't afford to piss off—so he offered a respectful bow, keeping his expression carefully neutral.

He played the part, adopting the tone of a humble student. "I'm just an amateur. I know my skills don't measure even half of yours, but I'd love to learn a thing or two if you're willing to teach me."

Cedric didn't bother to respond. Instead, he studied the young man before him with narrowed eyes, his gaze sweeping from head to toe, obviously sizing the kid up. Cedric took far too much pride in his own abilities to waste his precious time on beginners. Besides, Tia had sent him here for one reason: to tear Zane down a peg. Why would he actually help the boy?

"Show me what you've made," Cedric demanded, his tone sharp and dismissive.

Zane offered a quiet nod, though his heart beat faster with anticipation. Despite the uncomfortable situation, he genuinely wanted an expert's opinion of the divine textures he'd drawn the previous night. Even if they represented only the most elementary work—crude attempts at an impossibly complex art—he needed to understand how they appeared to someone versed in traditional techniques. 

The feedback, however harsh, would at least let him know where he stood.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL PIANO]

He cleared his throat, fighting to keep his voice steady. "I… I bought some materials yesterday and drew these two divine th—I mean, runes. Please, take a look." The near-slip sent a jolt of alarm through him, but he maintained his composure, watching their faces carefully.

Neither Cedric nor the shopkeeper caught his stumble. Their minds had already seized on something far more shocking—the claim itself. 

[SFX: DRAMATIC STING]

Their jaws dropped. He’d created a rune? In a single day?

[AMBIENT: QUIET WORKSHOP]

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

The old man was even more flabbergasted than Cedric. He knew for a fact that Zane was a total rookie who knew nothing about making runes. He’d figured the expensive materials Zane bought were already scrap. He’d even felt a pang of pity for the top-tier supplies, convinced they were being wasted on someone who would inevitably ruin them. It was a rite of passage for every rune master to fail and learn, which was why training one cost a fortune—far more than training a master warrior. But starting with fourth-tier materials? That was just insane. No beginner could possibly succeed at this stage.

The old man had anticipated Zane's return—a humbled youth crawling back, desperate for more materials after his inevitable failure. Instead, the boy had achieved something that shattered every reasonable expectation. Did he really think rune-crafting was nothing more than idle sketching with expensive ink?

[MUSIC: GROWING TENSION]

The shopkeeper cleared his throat, drawing out the moment. "Mister Lowell," he began, each word saturated with mockery, "you've just commenced your studies, yet you claim to have produced not one, but two functional runes in a single day?" He paused, letting the absurdity hang in the air. "How remarkable. Please, do enlighten us. Show us this masterpiece you've created!"

Cedric fought to suppress his laughter, a bitter understanding crystallizing in his mind. Ah, so that's it. The youth's brazen confidence stemmed not from hidden expertise or genuine talent—he was simply delusional. The arrogance wasn't calculated; it was the product of complete ignorance. The kid was cocky as hell, and out of his mind.

His eyes narrowed as he studied the crude markings before him. If Zane had somehow crafted an authentic meditation rune, it would require a design entirely unknown to Cedric—a man who had devoted forty years to studying every documented pattern across three continents. The possibility was laughable. No, this was the work of an amateur who couldn't distinguish between genuine craftsmanship and childish scrawling.

[MUSIC: ANGRY CONFRONTATION]

He shook his head in frustration. "How dare you?" he snapped. "Do you really think this mess of a scribble is a rune? It doesn’t matter what materials you use; you can't activate something if you don't even understand the basics! What you drew is not a rune. No matter how much energy you pour into that thing, it will never work."

[SFX: SPIT]

Cedric spat on the ground for emphasis.

[MUSIC: RESIGNED MELANCHOLY]

Zane let out a heavy sigh. It was useless. He was never going to get through to them with words. They were too close-minded to believe him.

So, he decided to show them. He stepped toward the rune and, with a casual wave of his hand, sent a stream of energy flowing into the divine texture.

[SFX: MAGICAL ACTIVATION]

[AMBIENT: ETHEREAL HUM]

The pattern lit up instantly. The meditation rune began to glow a soft, light green as the Potent Azurewood Root activated. A moment later, a tangible wave of energy pulsed outward, washing over the three men.

[MUSIC: MYSTICAL WONDER]

As the ethereal blue-green light bathed them, they all felt an incredible surge of vigor, as if their spirits had been cleansed and re-energized.

The old man was speechless, feeling the rune’s immediate power. He’d handled plenty of runes in his time as a merchant, but he knew, instantly, that this one was on a completely different level.

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC REVELATION]

But Cedric's face twisted into a mask of pure horror.

The old man was right—it was powerful. But as one of the best rune experts in the Heartlands, Cedric saw something more. He saw the details. He stared at the rune’s strokes, which had looked so sloppy and amateurish just moments ago. Now, illuminated from within, their true nature was revealed. The realization hit him like a physical blow, and he began to stutter.

"I-It's… this color…" he stammered, his eyes wide with disbelief. "The brushwork. It's… perfect!"
