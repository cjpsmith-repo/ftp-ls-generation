Ep 485 - Twin Swords

[AMBIENT: TOURNAMENT ARENA CROWD MURMUR]

Across the arena, people were shocked.

The warriors in the audience and the disciples from the fourth-grade sects on the platforms all stared, utterly bewildered. Some leaned forward in their seats, craning their necks to get a better look at the unexpected competitor.

[SFX: SHOCKED CROWD REACTION]

"A disciple of Cloud Hall?" The murmur rippled through the crowd like a stone dropped in still water. "He got through the pre-qualifiers? But he's only at the Fourth Grade of the Soul-Illuminated Realm! Is this some kind of joke?"

A disciple from Bloodwood Cliff scoffed, his lips curling into a sneer of contempt. "Fourth Grade of the Soul-Illuminated Realm? He'll be dead the second he steps on that stage." The comment drew nods of agreement from those nearby.

An Inner-Core Realm warrior rubbed his nose, a gesture of mild disappointment crossing his features. "Too bad he's not my opponent. That'd be an easy win—wouldn't even break a sweat."

"Yeah, someone from the Soul Realm won't last a single round up there," another Bloodwood Cliff disciple chimed in. He crossed his arms, his expression mixing amusement with genuine letdown, as if watching a lamb wander into a den of wolves. "What a waste of everyone's time."

The sentiment spread through the platforms. Disciples exchanged knowing glances, some shaking their heads in disbelief that Cloud Hall would send such an obviously outmatched competitor. A few even began placing quiet wagers on how quickly the match would end.

[MUSIC: DISCIPLINED TENSION]

On the Ethereal Spirit Sect's platform, six Inner-Core Realm disciples sat cross-legged. Known for their rigid discipline, they watched the arena with ramrod-straight backs and stone-cold expressions.

The youngest among them was only fifteen, his face still holding the soft edges of boyhood. While he might not be the weakest warrior in the tournament, he was definitely the youngest.

He blinked and turned to one of his companions. "Hey, Philip, didn't you say I was the weakest one here? That guy's power level is even lower than mine!"

Philip, the most senior disciple of the Ethereal Spirit Order, kept his gaze fixed on Zane. "A Fourth-Grade Soul-Illuminated warrior," he murmured. "That is strange." He turned to the younger disciple. "But Brent, never underestimate an opponent based on their power level alone. You're at the peak of the Soul Realm, and no one would dare look down on you."

"I get it," Brent nodded, but his tone was unconvinced. "Still, there's no way he's as strong as me. He couldn't have touched me even two months ago, when I was at his level."

Brent’s gaze shot back to Zane, his expression brimming with unshakeable confidence.

Had anyone else overheard, they would have been stunned. Leaping from the Fourth Level to the peak of the Soul Realm in just two months? Brent's talent was simply off the charts.

Philip simply smiled and shook his head, puzzled by Brent’s fixation on a complete nobody.

In The Heartlands, geniuses were sorted into four ranks: human, earth, heaven, and saint. The top disciples of a fourth-grade Order were typically considered human or earth-level talents. A recognized heaven-level talent, on the other hand, would have any sect scrambling to recruit them.

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD ROAR]

A voice roared from the stands."Take down that Fourth-Level weakling! Who cares if he's from Cloud Hall? I want to see some blood!"

[MUSIC: GROWING SUSPENSE]

Amid the rising chatter, Alex's mouth curled into a smirk. This was the home-field advantage he'd been counting on. The crowd's energy fed his confidence, their bloodlust a familiar companion. He knew the pressure bearing down on his opponent would be crushing—the weight of hundreds of hostile eyes, all eager to see Cloud Hall's arrogant upstart humbled.

Not that Alex needed such advantages for this particular fight. His opponent was pathetic, really—a Fourth-Level Adept who'd somehow stumbled into relevance through sheer luck.

[SFX: STORAGE RING]

Alex retrieved two swords from his Storage Ring—one blade shimmering green like spring leaves, the other gleaming gold like captured sunlight. Both were middle-grade Mythic weapons, their quality evident in the way they hummed with latent power.

Zane's eyebrows lifted. "The Twin-Sword School?"

Dual-wielding swordsmen were exceptionally rare. In fact, the only other practitioner Zane had encountered was Dalton from Skytop Peak—and that brief observation had taught him much about this demanding style.

The tactical advantage was obvious: a skilled warrior with a blade in each hand could attack and defend at the same time, creating devastating combinations impossible for single-sword fighters. But the true mastery of swordsmanship lay in perfecting one's sword intent—that crystallization of will and technique into something transcendent. Achieving such unity with two separate weapons was exponentially more difficult than with one. The divided focus, the conflicting rhythms, the need to harmonize two distinct energies—these challenges made it a path most swordsmen wisely avoided, no matter how alluring the initial benefits appeared.

[SFX: SWORD SWOOSH]

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

"Oh? So you've heard of the Twin-Sword School?" A thin smile crossed Alex's face as he flourished both blades, unleashing a bright flash of golden radiance that illuminated the arena.

Zane studied the display with quiet interest, noting the synchronized movement of the twin weapons. He met Alex's condescending tone with a faint, knowing smile of his own. Being underestimated had become familiar territory—a tactical advantage he'd learned to exploit. His opponents always paid for their arrogance in the end, and Alex would be no exception.

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC PERCUSSION]

"Your power level is pathetic." Alex leveled both swords at Zane, his voice dripping with disdain. The words were meant to sting, to provoke a reaction. "But don't expect any mercy. I make it a point never to underestimate my opponents, regardless of how weak they appear."

The contradiction wasn't lost on Zane, though he kept his expression neutral.

"As they say, a lion uses its full strength even when it's hunting a rabbit." Alex shifted his stance, the golden blades catching the light as he positioned them for attack. His confidence bordered on theatrical, as if he were performing for an invisible audience. "Since you made it to this stage, I have to assume you're prepared to face the consequences of challenging me."

The threat hung in the air between them, heavy with implication. Zane remained still, his breathing steady, his mind already calculating the patterns he'd observed in those twin-blade flourishes.

Then he laughed and gave Alex a big smile.

This was gonna be fun.
