Maddox stood across from Zane, his fists clenched, every instinct screaming at him to end the boy's life right there.
Maddox had brought his students here for the Martial Tournament, and killing some kid from the Soul Realm in the middle of it would be a bad look.
He'd be a laughingstock. People would say the great, top-ranked warrior from The Heartlands was just a sore loser who couldn't stand getting shown up.
Besides, the three big guys from the World Merchants' Guild would never stand for it. On top of that, Cloud Hall’s formidable old benefactor could be watching from anywhere.
The Guild also had that ancient tycoon on their side, a man who’d been alive for over a thousand years. Old or not, his power was immeasurable. Maddox wasn’t afraid to take any of them on one-on-one. But if they all came at him together? Not even he was strong enough for that.
[MUSIC: DARK OMINOUS DRUMS]
After weighing his options, Maddox reined in the urge to tear Zane apart and redirected his fury toward a different approach. He would simply crush the boy beneath the overwhelming weight of his soul.
If Tia had stayed put, shielding Zane, her protective presence would have forced his hand. Maddox would have needed to confront Cloud Hall's leader first—a complication he'd rather avoid. But he never expected Zane to step forward and pull her out of the way himself.
"You want to play the hero and get yourself killed? Be my guest," Maddox thought. A thrill coursed through his veins even as his face remained an impassive mask. His lips curved into the faintest smile.
[SFX: ENERGY SURGE]
For someone wielding his immense soul power, crushing a mere Soul Realm fighter required no more effort than stepping on an insect. The boy didn't even comprehend the gulf between them.
Maddox gathered his spiritual energy, preparing to unleash it in a single devastating wave. But something unexpected happened.

Tia's vision blurred as a memory came to her. She was seven years old again, huddled in a horse-drawn carriage with her family. The rhythmic clatter of hooves echoed against frozen ground. The north wind sliced through gaps in the worn canvas, cutting at her face like countless tiny blades. Her stomach cramped with hunger. Her fingers had gone numb hours ago.
Then warmth. Someone wrapped her in a thick quilt, tucking the edges around her small frame. The cold retreated. The hunger faded to a distant ache. For the first time in days, she felt safe.
[MUSIC: WARM EMOTIONAL]
The moment Zane pulled her into his arms, she was back under that quilt. This young man, hardly an imposing figure, was using his own body to shield her from danger. A small warmth bloomed in her chest, a feeling that was all her own.

How could this kid’s soul withstand that? It was impossible! Maddox scrambled for an explanation. Zane had to have some kind of powerful artifact protecting his soul, or something that made him immune to such an attack.

To most of the audience, the clash between Zane and Maddox was a minor scuffle that lasted less than a minute. But for those two, it had been the most perilous moment of the entire tournament. The true danger was lost on most of the crowd, though a few of the more skilled cultivators in the stands could sense the raw tension.
Even then, they couldn't have grasped the specifics. Besides, the World Merchants' Guild faced a far more pressing concern: the damaged runes that formed the enchanted barrier. The Guild had invested a fortune installing them years ago, and repairs required summoning the same master runic experts who had originally constructed the protective array.

The crowd failed to grasp the true danger. The Guild wasn't suspending the competition out of caution—they were preventing a massacre. When Inner-Core Realm masters clashed, their power transcended ordinary combat. A single stray blast, unleashed without the barrier's protection, could obliterate an entire section of spectators in an instant.
The eager crowd screaming for blood had no idea that they themselves stood on the precipice of annihilation, their excitement blinding them to the lethal forces ready to be unleashed just yards away.
The Tournament wasn't just dangerous to compete in; it was dangerous to watch, as well.
How long would they be able to stall?
