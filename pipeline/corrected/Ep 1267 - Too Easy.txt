Ep 1267 - Too Easy

Word count: 961

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]
[AMBIENT: ECHOING STONE HALL]
If this had been an ordinary rock, the ten warriors wouldn’t have blinked. Even at thirty feet tall, it wouldn’t have been able to damage the sword spirit. 
But this was no ordinary rock. It was a giant stone sitting in the middle of the hall, and the fact that they couldn’t seem to destroy it was definitely going to be a problem.
The human warrior controlling the sword spirit gave his iron sword a flick, signaling the spirit to pull back.
[SFX: MASSIVE STONE IMPACT]
[SFX: ROLLING BOULDER]
[SFX: CHAMBER TREMBLING]
A loud explosion echoed through the hall as the giant stone rolled to the side, crashing down with enough force to make the entire chamber tremble. It kept rolling, following the sword spirit’s path as she retreated, its speed quickly surpassing her ability to dodge. Seeing this, the warrior just smiled.
[SFX: METALLIC TRANSFORMATION]
[SFX: BLADES SCATTERING]
His sword quivered again, and the sword spirit suddenly transformed, splitting into a swarm of seven or eight blades. They scattered in all directions before converging again, this time into the shape of a man.
“This second hall isn’t so tough,” the warrior said, confident he could clear it easily.
[SFX: STONE RUMBLING]
But as the huge block of stone rumbled past, one of its sides came into view, revealing a ghastly, terrifying face. 
It had dark, empty eyes, sharp teeth, and a wicked smile that was deeply unsettling.
[MUSIC: INTENSE CHASE]
The stone seemed to have some level of intelligence because it immediately identified the warrior controlling the sword spirit and changed its course.
[SFX: BOULDER ROLLING AT HIGH SPEED]
The living boulder, a thirty-foot cube, tore across the hall at an incredible speed. In the limited space, it had no trouble closing the distance, but the warrior stayed calm. Despite its menacing appearance, the stone seemed confined to a limited area, rolling only within a grid of squares marked on the floor.
Each roll carried the boulder exactly thirty feet — and it could only move forward, left, or right. To cut across on a diagonal, it had to roll once to the side and once forward, never both at once. That half-step delay was the opening the warrior needed.
“Is there some kind of mechanism?” Zane wondered aloud, dodging between the rolling stones. “Or a condition we have to meet to get through?”
The warrior shifted his weight, angling himself onto a diagonal line against the boulder’s square. Sure enough, it couldn’t close the gap in a single move — it had to correct its path first, and by then he’d already moved again. “Everyone, stay on a diagonal against your boulder,” he called out. “Do that, and this hall won’t give us any trouble.” The group caught on quickly, falling into step with the method.
“No sense wasting time in here, then,” one of the cultivators said, and broke into a run toward the far end of the hall. Two of the boulders peeled off immediately, rolling after him.
Janet frowned, watching the stones move.
“What’s wrong?” Zane asked, noticing her expression.
“Something’s off,” she said slowly. “The second hall shouldn’t be this easy. Last time I came through here, only two of the nine who started with me made it to the final stage. Two of the others died in this exact hall.”
“You think we haven’t actually figured out the rules yet?” Zane asked.
“I have a feeling,” she said. “But let’s keep moving for now.”
Her sleeves snapping behind her, she strode forward, and the rest of the group fell in, weaving diagonally against the rolling stones. The hall was several times wider than the first, but between the ten of them moving in sync, it didn’t take long to reach the far end.
“We made it!”
“Heh. Reward’s not much, but that was easy enough. Might even earn back some of those five million Illusion Points I dropped earlier.”
“Wait—”
[SFX: LIGHT BARRIER RISING]
Rows of white light shot up from the floor at the hall’s exit, sealing it off like a fence. No one could get through.
Someone swore, staring at the light. “What the hell is this? Why won’t it let us out?”
Behind them, the boulders didn’t wait for anyone to think it over — they surged forward again, faster than before.
“Fall back!” Janet shouted, her whole body flickering into a pale shadow as she dove clear. A boulder swept through the space she’d just occupied, missing her by inches.
“There’s got to be a mechanism, or some condition we’re missing,” Zane muttered, weaving between the stones again.
“Ah! Help!” someone screamed from nearby.
Zane glanced over and saw another warrior trapped in a corner, pinned by three of the giant stones.
Without a second thought, Zane shot forward and slammed his palm into the man’s back.
[SFX: MASSIVE STONE IMPACT]
[SFX: BODY THROWN THROUGH AIR]
[SFX: STONE SCRAPING]
The force of the blow sent the warrior flying out of danger. In the same motion, Zane slipped through a narrow gap between the stones, barely escaping himself.
“Zane?!” Janet screamed, her voice tight with panic. Why was he showing off now, of all times?
What was he thinking, putting himself in danger like that? She was still hoping for a chance to challenge him, and it would be a real shame if he got himself killed this early. If he got crushed, he wouldn’t just be throwing away his five million Illusion Points—he’d also lose half his remaining points and plummet down the rankings.
It wasn’t just her; the others were also shocked by what he’d done.
[MUSIC: SOMBER REFLECTIVE]
They were technically a team, but only because they’d been forced together. They were far from a tight-knit group, and some of them were outright enemies. 
For Zane to risk his own neck to save someone else was bizarre. His selflessness was stunning, but it also seemed incredibly stupid. No one could survive being crushed by those things. If one of them rolled over you, you were dead. Period.
