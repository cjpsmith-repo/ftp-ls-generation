Ep 336 - Payback is Hell
Word count: 1064

[AMBIENT: TENSE MEETING ROOM]
[MUSIC: POLITICAL INTRIGUE]
The Lord of House Carter scoffed, his face contorting with disdain as he slammed his fist onto the polished table. "He wants us to give the property back to House Lowell? The boy can keep dreaming!"
[MUSIC: OMINOUS BACKGROUND]
In Castermere County, resources were scarce, and this harsh reality shaped every political decision. If one House held too much wealth, the others were left to fight for scraps like starving dogs. This brutal economic landscape forced the Houses into fierce competition, transforming neighbors into enemies and turning family alliances into fragile arrangements of convenience.
Ford had seized a huge chunk of property from House Lowell after helping Zane’s two uncles murder Milo Lowell. The blood had barely dried on Milo’s chambers when Ford’s men arrived to claim their prize. Almost overnight, House Stone had become the second most powerful family in Castermere County, right behind House Hawthorne.
House Carter, House Hill, and House Blackwood had all taken their cut from Milo’s death, too. Their coffers had swelled with Lowell gold, and they sure as hell weren’t about to give it all back. Each House had servants and guards to feed, luxuries to maintain. Returning the property meant giving up the comfortable lives they’d grown used to.
Ford had briefly considered giving in, but he knew Zane wouldn’t let him off the hook either way. The boy’s eyes had burned with a vengeance that transcended mere property disputes. Their only choice was to unite and crush him before his power grew any further.
His lips curled into a sneer. "Exactly. House Lowell was too weak to hold its own property, so it was ours for the taking! There’s no way Zane can take it back by himself. We need a plan to stop him."
The Lord of House Hill laughed in agreement. "He just got lucky with The Blood Prisoner, that's all. The man was already half-dead and could barely put up a fight. He must've been at ten percent of his strength, if that. It's no surprise Zane beat him. Besides, Zane is just an Adept.
I don’t think he’s any real match for us."
The Lord of House Hill sounded sure of himself. His confidence gave the others pause. They'd all been rattled by Zane's reputation, but Lord Hill had a point. Their fear began to fade as they reconsidered Zane's actual strength.
A murmur of agreement went through the room. There was no way a fighter at just Level Three of the Adept Realm could have defeated The Blood Prisoner at full strength. Zane only won because his opponent was weakened.
"Hmph. If he's not as powerful as we thought, then there's nothing to be afraid of. I'll kill that bastard myself!" Chuck declared, his eyes flashing with malice. As the most powerful Lord in the room, his confidence was infectious.
Ford chimed in, nodding with excitement. "With Chuck spearheading our efforts, that bastard’s demise is inevitable!"
Lord Hill clenched his fists until his knuckles whitened, his eyes narrowing with a mixture of fear and determination. "We need to wipe out House Lowell once and for all. Zane and Elara have frightening abilities despite their youth. If we hesitate now, their power will only grow stronger, and we could lose everything."
[SFX: OMINOUS CHUCKLE]
Before Lord Hill could finish his thought, a soft, menacing chuckle echoed from the corridor outside the room, freezing the blood in their veins.
[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSE]
That singular, bone-chilling sound was the only warning they got.
[SFX: VIOLENT SLASH]
[SFX: PAINED SCREAMS]
In the space of a single heartbeat, it was over. Chuck and Ford, two of the most respected fighters in Castermere County, were on their knees, collapsed to their knees, clutching the bloody stumps where their right hands had just been. If Zane had shown a fraction less mercy, their corpses would now lie cooling on the polished floor.
[SFX: SWORD SLASH]
The other three House Lords felt a cold sweat break out across their skin. They hadn't moved a muscle, and thank God for that. If they'd joined the attack, they might be missing hands, too.
Zane's blade had been a blur—so fast none of them had even seen his technique, let alone dreamed up a way to block it.
But it wouldn’t have mattered. Even if Zane had moved his sword in slow motion, they would have stood no chance. His technique was built on the principle of ultimate simplicity, an art so refined that not even Evan, the top disciple of the Skyreach Sect, could have seen through it. What hope did a few provincial lords have?
[MUSIC: TENSE CONFRONTATION]
When Zane spoke, his voice carried a dangerous serenity that chilled the air. "Chuck, I killed your son because he tried to murder one of my own people—right in our Martial Arts Hall. Darcy was your flesh and blood. I understand how important he was to you. Well, my people are important to me, too. His death was a direct consequence of his own actions."
Chuck’s face flushed with humiliation. The burning shame crawled up his neck as he stood there, severed hand throbbing, feeling utterly diminished—a once-proud man reduced to a spectacle. He felt like a fool, a complete joke, and couldn’t find the courage to say a single word back.
A profound realization dawned on Chuck as blood dripped from his wrist.
Power—true power—is what gives a person the right to speak his mind freely. For years, his status as a Level Five Adept had commanded respect throughout Castermere County, making lesser men tremble and bow before him. Now, that same power appeared laughably insignificant before Zane’s overwhelming presence. I clearly meant nothing to the boy.
Zane calmly held up three fingers. "You will repay House Lowell triple the value of everything you’ve taken. And listen close—if you don’t agree immediately, here and now, your debt increases to quadruple."
[AMBIENT: CROWD MURMURS]
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC IMPACT]
Triple? The assembled lords exchanged panicked glances. That was far more than what they had originally seized from House Lowell. This demand would force them to surrender their own hard-won properties, assets worth twice what they had gained through their opportunistic plundering.
This wasn’t negotiation—it was unabashed extortion, delivered with the casual confidence of someone who knew his terms would not be refused.
