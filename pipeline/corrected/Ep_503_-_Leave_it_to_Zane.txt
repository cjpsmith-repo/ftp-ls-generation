Ep 503 - Leave it to Zane
Word count: 1033
[AMBIENT: ANCIENT LIBRARY]
[MUSIC: MYSTICAL STRINGS]
Back in the day, if you wanted to study runes, the Ethereal Spirit Sect was the only place to go.
After all, it was the home of Arnold, the Son of Rune himself. For centuries, runic experts of all stripes flocked to the order to hone their craft.
Unfortunately, the order rested on its laurels, relying too heavily on Arnold’s fame instead of advancing the study of runes. Over time, their techniques began to decline. Aside from the powerful protective arrays Arnold had left behind, their rune work was nothing to write home about.
With the rise of the World Merchants' Guild, the constant evolution of the craft, and the founding of the Runic Tower, every expert in the Heartlands now chose to study at the Tower instead.
The Runic Tower was the foremost authority on runes in the entire Heartlands, and these three men were its highest-ranked experts. Their knowledge was considered second to none.
So, when Nate said they needed three days, no one in the Heartlands would have dared to argue.
[MUSIC: RISING TENSION]
That confidence fueled his boast: "If you can find anyone in the Heartlands who can repair them in less than three days, I'll call him my master."
The three experts now openly mocked Zane's ignorance, exchanging knowing glances that spoke volumes about their shared contempt. They'd seen countless young cultivators like him before—brash, overconfident, utterly unaware of their own limitations. A classic case of fearlessness born from sheer cluelessness.
Zane didn't take the bait. He'd learned long ago that proving himself through action carried far more weight than defending himself with words. Let them laugh. They'd understand soon enough.
He turned away from the three experts, dismissing them as thoroughly as they'd dismissed him, and addressed Kieran directly. "Kieran, I can repair these runes in two hours. What's the pay?"
The simplicity of his question cut through the tension like a blade. No posturing, no elaborate justifications—just a straightforward offer that hung in the air between them.
[AMBIENT: QUIET ARENA]
"Are you sure?" Kieran asked, though he wasn't as surprised as he should have been. Zane had a habit of pulling off the impossible. No one ever believed a warrior in the Soul Realm could do the things he did, but Zane always found a way. At this point, Kieran was just getting used to watching him perform miracles.
"I can give it a shot," Zane said with a laugh. "Even if I fail, it won't hold up the match, right?"
He had a point. Worst-case scenario, it was a two-hour delay. If he failed, the warriors were stuck waiting three days for the next battle anyway. What was two hours compared to three days?
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS APPROACHING]
[MUSIC: TENSE STANDOFF]
Just as Kieran was about to nod, Nate and the other two experts stepped in to stop him.
They didn't believe for a second that this kid could repair the runes. In their eyes, he couldn't fix them in two hundred hours, let alone two. The fact that Kieran was even considering it felt like a direct insult to everything they'd built.
The situation mirrored a master chef who had spent twenty hours perfecting an elaborate dish for his patron of many years, only to watch some street urchin waltz in and claim he could surpass it in mere minutes. Heat rose in their chests—a mixture of indignation and wounded pride. They were the best rune masters in the Heartlands, or so they'd believed until this moment. In their minds, any runic techniques from outside their exclusive circle were primitive at best, worthless at worst.
Nate's jaw tightened as he exchanged glances with his colleagues. Their reputations had been built over decades of painstaking work, countless failures, and hard-won breakthroughs. Now this upstart threatened to unravel it all.
Where did this kid with perfect brushwork even come from? The thought echoed in all three of their minds as they stood frozen in disbelief, their carefully constructed world suddenly feeling less certain.
A cold glint flashed in Kieran's eyes as he observed their reactions. This was precisely why he'd agreed to let Zane try. He wanted to knock Nate and his cronies down a peg, to remind them that mastery could emerge from unexpected places.
Those three had grown arrogant over the years, completely full of themselves. They demanded outrageous prices even from the World Merchants' Guild and acted so aloof, like they were the undisputed masters of the Heartlands. They seemed to have forgotten they were members of the Guild, and that the Runic Tower itself was built on the Guild's dime.
Now, maybe they would finally understand that they were no longer the only game in town.
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC DISCOVERY]
Zane's speed was incredible.
When he had first begun drawing runes, each stroke demanded careful deliberation. The craft drew directly from an expert's soul strength, consuming vast reserves of Soul Power with every line. In those early days, a single meditation rune could leave him drained for hours.
But Zane’s Soul Power was on a whole other level now. A soul tempered in the Fighting Soul Realm was astonishingly powerful. For Zane, channeling energy into divine textures now felt as natural as breathing. More importantly, he wasn't creating these patterns from scratch—he was merely tracing the original strokes etched into the stone tablet, restoring ancient craftsmanship to its former magnificence.
[SFX: LOW HUM]
A low hum resonated through the chamber.
The runic quill glided across the stone's surface with practiced grace, each movement precise yet effortless. Zane's hand never hesitated, never wavered. The strokes flowed from one to the next like water following its course, and in what seemed mere heartbeats, the first rune blazed complete before their eyes.
[MUSIC: MAGICAL TRIUMPH]
Golden light pulsed from the repaired pattern, casting dancing shadows across the stunned faces of the observers.
In that moment, Nate—renowned as one of the Heartlands' foremost runic experts, a man whose opinions had shaped Guild policy for three decades—could only stand motionless, his mouth hanging open. Words failed him completely.
The impossible had just unfolded before his eyes. He'd been outpaced by a damn teenager.
