Ep 179 - Underwater Monster
Word count: 1316
[AMBIENT: PEACEFUL MOUNTAIN SETTING]
[MUSIC: SERENE ADVENTURE]
Emberfin Lake sat tucked away on a hill in the southern reaches of the Skyreach Sect’s territory.
Compared to the towering mountains up north, the hill itself was nothing special. But the scenery was something else: breathtaking vistas stretched in every direction.
The Sect had spared no expense in crafting an elegant pathway to the summit, meticulously laying six-foot-wide slabs of blue stone.
Zane found himself pausing occasionally during his climb, captivated by the panoramic views.
[AMBIENT: TRAIL ENTRANCE]
At the trailhead, a guard in a brown uniform stood watch, his expression grim. He scrutinized Zane as the young man approached.
Zane wasted no time on pleasantries. “Hi there. I’ve come for Emberfin Lake.”
The guard’s eyes narrowed as they traveled over Zane’s white robe, a flicker of confusion crossing his face. "Emberfin Lake? Are you sure?"
White-robed disciples were typically the weakest, with barely any Sect Points to their name. And Emberfin Lake had some strict requirements for training.
Anyone who wasn’t strong enough wouldn’t just miss out on the benefits---they could get seriously hurt.
Zane nodded. “I’m well aware of the risks. I’m here to train.”
The guard leaned against his post, clearly skeptical. “Did your master send you to this fate?”
“She did,” Zane replied confidently. “Princess Isla herself informed me of the Sect’s secret attunement sanctums.”
The guard’s posture stiffened at the mention of the princess, and held out a hand. "Your Acolyte’s Sigil."
[SFX: SIGIL INSPECTION]
When he checked it, his eyes widened. The number of points Zane had was staggering. In all his years, he’d never seen an outer disciple with a balance that high---not unless they’d bought them with a boatload of crystal cubes.
"Still in an outer disciple’s robe, though," the guard thought. "So not from a noble family. Just rich, maybe."
He handed the sigil back. “You can’t go in right now.”
“Why not?” Zane asked.
“Someone’s already in there,” the guard said, pointing to a large clock nearby. “Booked a two-hour slot. See? Went in at eleven, he’ll be out at one. It’s only noon now. You’ve got an hour to wait.”
Zane glanced at the clock. Both hands pointed straight up to twelve, just as the guard said.
With a nod, Zane found a nearby stone bench and settled in to wait.
He figured he was in for the full hour. But less than thirty minutes later, a disciple came staggering down the path from the lake, drenched from head to toe.
Zane recognized him instantly. Leo Brandt — Nature Level, House Brandt, Dragon's Keep. He looked like he'd just been wrung out like a wet cloth.
"Zane." Leo blinked, surprised. "You're here too?"
"First time," Zane said.
Leo shook his head, still catching his breath. "A first-level Adept in there for two hours? I'd reconsider. I've got a younger brother who tried it at that level— came out with his Vitality flowing backwards. Took weeks to recover."
"Thanks for the warning," Zane said.
Leo studied him for a moment, then seemed to decide there was no point arguing. He nodded and walked on.
[AMBIENT: FOREST PATH]
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON PATH]
With the lake now free, Zane headed up the trail. After a short walk, the path opened onto a flat, misty clearing, and Zane saw the waterfall.
[AMBIENT: WATERFALL ROAR]
It wasn’t that high---maybe thirty or forty feet, nothing compared to the great falls of the Eastern Territory. But the sound\... The roar of it crashing into the pool below was deafening, like a stampeding army, a force so powerful it felt like it could shake the heavens themselves.
This was why it was one of the Sect’s secret attunement sanctums.
Zane walked to the edge of the deep pool and dipped his fingers into the crystal-clear water. The reason for the thunderous roar became instantly clear.
The water looked pristine, but it felt like something else entirely.
[SFX: FREEZING WATER]
An intense, biting cold shot up his fingers, so sharp it felt like they were being flash-frozen.
Zane hissed, quickly circulating his Vitality to force the chill from his hand.
On top of that, when he cupped some in his palm, he was shocked by its weight---it was absurdly heavy.
"They say that deep in the Frost-Bound Lands, there’s a substance called Heavy Water, ten times denser than normal," he thought. "Could this be it?"
If that was the case, then beyond the unnatural cold, the pressure beneath the surface had to be immense.
No wonder this place was considered such a challenge.
But Zane didn’t waste time on the shore. Two hours cost two hundred points---the equivalent of four hundred crystal cubes. Time was literally money here.
[SFX: WATER PLUNGE]
Bracing himself, he took a deep breath and plunged into the lake.
The Heavy Water was incredibly thick, almost viscous.
[AMBIENT: UNDERWATER STILLNESS]
[MUSIC: STRANGE UNDERWATER MOVEMENT]
Zane hit the surface with barely a splash, just a quiet ripple as he began to sink.
He descended with agonizing slowness, and at about three feet down, his descent stopped completely. The buoyancy of the Heavy Water was astonishing.
"Okay, adjust my breathing, try to go straight down," he thought. Zane angled his body, head pointed toward the bottom, and began to pull himself downward, hand over hand, against the water’s heavy resistance.
A warrior who’d reached the Stage of Inner Fortitude could go without breathing for a good while; their lungs had fundamentally changed, able to circulate air internally. The deep breath Zane had taken on shore would have to last him.
[AMBIENT: DEEP UNDERWATER PRESSURE]
For all its thickness, the Heavy Water was surprisingly clear. Even ten feet down, he could see his surroundings perfectly.
Zane had read up on Emberfin Lake. Some said it was bottomless. Legend claimed a master from the Soul Realm had once reached a thousand feet, but no one had ever gone deeper.
According to reports from disciples who’d braved its depths, the lake was divided into ten distinct layers, each a hundred feet deep. The first layer went down to a hundred feet, the second to two hundred, and so on, all the way down to the tenth layer, starting at the thousand-foot mark.
This was his first time, so he didn’t have a specific goal in mind---other than getting his money’s worth. Training here was expensive, and he wasn’t about to waste hundreds of crystals.
"Let’s see how deep I can really go."
Zane swept his hands, pushing against the syrupy water as he slowly descended. This was the core of the training: by forcing the body to adapt to the immense pressure and resistance, a warrior could fundamentally change how they generated power. Back on dry land, they’d feel impossibly light, their movements fluid and effortless.
[SFX: UNDERWATER CREATURE MOVEMENT]
[SFX: APPROACHING DANGER]
The murky waters parted as a sleek, amphibious figure darted through the depths with unnatural agility. Its scaly blue-green skin glistened in the dim underwater light, while gill-like slits pulsated along its neck.
The Gillwretch, a rare aquatic predator, fixed its bulbous yellow eyes on Zane. Its webbed claws gripped a midnight-black spear crafted from an unknown material. It moved with calculated precision, its muscular tail propelling it forward with explosive bursts of speed as it shot towards Zane, who was just barely able to stay out of its way.
[MUSIC: UNDERWATER CHASE]
After a short chase, the Gillwretch spun around and charged him again.
"This is bad," Zane thought, his brow furrowing. "If this keeps up, I’m not going to be able to fend it off."
[SFX: MAGICAL ENERGY SPARK]
Then, an idea hit him. A flicker of inspiration. Zane snapped his fingers. A spark of Corrupt Vitality shot from his fingertip, spreading with painstaking slowness through the Heavy Water.
Controlling the Corrupt Vitality here was nothing like on dry land.
The lake was a mess of tiny, hidden currents and micro-whirlpools that tugged the energy in every direction.
The currents quickly stretched the Corrupt Vitality thin, scattering it into a large, irregular net of energy.
[MUSIC: TENSE UNDERWATER BATTLE]
But it did nothing to stop the Gillwretch. The creature darted nimbly through the gaps, raising its black spear for another charge.
