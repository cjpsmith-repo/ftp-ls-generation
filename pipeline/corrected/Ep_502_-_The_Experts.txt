Ep 502 - The Experts
Word count: 1057
[AMBIENT: ANGRY CROWD]
[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]
The old man's face twisted as he listened to the roar of curses from the crowd. They were rabid for the tournament to continue, with no awareness of how much danger they could be in.
If we don't get these enchanted barriers fixed and fast, he thought, anxiety tightening his chest, hundreds of people could get killed. He could already imagine the carnage—spectators crushed, disciples torn apart by stray techniques. Then you'll really have something to curse us about.
The weight of responsibility pressed down on his shoulders. In his decades managing these trials, he'd never faced such a catastrophic failure. His reputation, carefully built over forty years of service, hung by a thread.
[SFX: WHOOSH OF FLIGHT]
[MUSIC: HOPEFUL RESCUE]
Three figures descended into the arena moments later, their robes billowing as they touched down and bowed before the old man.
These were the runic experts of the World Merchants' Guild, the finest masters in all the Heartlands. Their presence alone commanded respect; each had spent decades perfecting their craft, their fingers calloused from countless hours of inscription work, their eyes sharp from studying the minutiae of ancient patterns.
The Guild's wealth stemmed from a simple philosophy: acquire the best talent, regardless of cost. Top weapon-smiths, alchemists, and runic experts all drew generous salaries from Guild coffers. Many masters and independent cultivators eagerly sought positions there. While a sect might offer stability and a sense of belonging, the Guild provided something more tangible—resources that dwarfed what any traditional sect could muster. Access to rare materials, ancient texts, and collaborative opportunities with other specialists made the Guild an irresistible destination for ambitious craftsmen.
These three couldn't match Arnold, the legendary Son of Rune, whose mastery had elevated him to near-mythical status. But finding anyone in the Heartlands more skilled than this trio would be nearly impossible.
The Runic Tower in Oracle City housed the most comprehensive collection of rune knowledge in the region—nearly every pattern, inscription, and technique ever documented in the Heartlands lived inside those walls. Experts traveled from distant territories to study there, sometimes spending years poring over ancient tablets. Even Cedric from Cloud Hall had been sent to the Tower for several years to refine his understanding. These three men served as the Tower's masters. In the world of runes, their expertise set the standard by which all others were measured.
At the old man's command, the three experts nodded and split up to different sections of the arena.
[SFX: STONE SCRAPING]
They walked to the arena's edge and pried up the stone bricks. Beneath them lay a series of green stone tablets, each one covered in intricate runes that should have been glowing with protective energy.
[AMBIENT: DAMAGED ARENA]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]
Instead, the runes etched into them were a complete wreck. Many were scorched so badly they looked as though someone had tossed them into a furnace and left them to burn. The delicate lines that formed the protective patterns had been reduced to blackened scars across the stone's surface.
"The power load was just too much for these runes to handle," one of the experts murmured, running his fingers along the charred grooves. His voice carried a note of professional dismay—these were supposed to be among the most resilient barriers in the Heartlands.
Another expert knelt beside him, his frown deepening as he examined the damage more closely. "These six enchanted barriers are designed to support each other, distributing the load across the entire network. What kind of power could possibly burn through them all at once? Could someone at the Inner-Core Realm even accomplish this?"
"Impossible!" the third expert declared, shaking his head emphatically. His eyes widened as he traced the pattern of destruction. "There's no way a cultivator from the Inner-Core Realm caused this kind of damage. The energy signature alone suggests something far beyond that level."
Of course, anyone in Oracle City watching the tournament on their Scrying Slate already knew exactly how the barriers had been destroyed—they'd witnessed it firsthand. But the three experts had spent their lives buried in research, surrounded by ancient texts and theoretical frameworks. Even as Inner-Core cultivators themselves, they rarely paid attention to current events or the practical demonstrations of power happening right outside their tower walls.
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON PLATFORM]
A voice rang out from the high platform, cutting through the experts' debate. "Kieran, I can fix these runes in two hours. What would the World Merchants' Guild be willing to pay for it?"
The speaker was none other than Zane. He stood at the platform's edge, arms crossed confidently, a faint smile on his lips.
[MUSIC: CONFIDENT THEME]
Kieran glanced at Zane, then smacked his own forehead. In his panic, he'd completely forgotten about Zane's other talents. He remembered now—Zane wasn't just a fighter. He was a runic expert, and he had mastered the incredible perfect brushwork technique. The old man’s eyes lit up as he recalled Michael Nash raving about Zane's rune-drawing skills during the pre-qualifying matches.
"Are you… serious?" Kieran hesitated.
Zane was so damn gifted in so many ways that Kieran had completely overlooked his talent for runes. More importantly, he’d mastered perfect brushwork—a one-of-a-kind technique that could draw out the full potential of runic materials with flawless efficiency.
But runes came in different grades. The ones Michael Nash had shown them were only basic One- and Two-Star runes, so Kieran had assumed that was Zane’s limit. Drawing the advanced Three-Star, Four-Star, or even Five-Star divine textures took decades of practice. Zane was only eighteen. Kieran just couldn't see how a kid could possibly have that kind of skill.
[MUSIC: TENSE ATMOSPHERE]
Before Zane could answer, one of the experts released a dismissive laugh. It was Nate, the head of the Runic Tower. "I've heard my fair share of bragging," he sneered, "but this is a new level of stupid."
"Ha! Kids these days," another expert scoffed. "Pure nonsense."
[MUSIC: OMINOUS TONE]
The third expert leaned forward, his eyes narrowing as the temperature in the room seemed to drop. "You should know there's a price for speaking so recklessly, boy," he said, each word falling like a stone into still water. His tone carried the weight of someone who had crushed countless arrogant youths before—and wouldn't hesitate to do it again.
