Ep 777 - The Slave Seal

Word count: 1048

[AMBIENT: DESOLATE BATTLEFIELD]

[MUSIC: SOMBER STRINGS]

The battle was over — Wallace and Abel were dead, and the last surviving warrior from the York Divine Kingdom was still breathing only because he'd been the first to talk. 

Zane sighed. He couldn’t bring himself to feel sorry for Lord Wallace—not really. To become a warrior of the Soul Sea Realm, you had to be the best of hundreds of millions. It took a lifetime of resources and training to reach that peak, but only a single mistake to be wiped from existence. That was the life of a martial artist. You could climb all the way to the top, even become an Emperor, but you could never outrun your own fate.

A Soul Sea warrior was notoriously hard to kill; the death of one was enough to shake the seven divine kingdoms. But Lord Wallace and Master Abel had gotten careless. They’d let themselves get surrounded by nine others with no route for escape. In the end, their deaths were as swift as they were pathetic.

[MUSIC: TENSE ORCHESTRAL]

 Zane floated into the air, hands clasped behind his back. He came to a stop before the last survivor, a warrior from the Life-and-Death Realm who was only still breathing because he’d had the good sense to answer Zane’s questions.

Zane studied the man’s face. This was a powerhouse who had survived five Life-and-Death Crises—stronger even than Maddox. Yet here he was, paralyzed with fear before Zane, who was merely at the middle stage of the Spirit-Tribulation Realm. Zane knew, of course, that the man wasn’t afraid of him. He was afraid of the figures standing at Zane’s back.

"You… you promised you wouldn’t kill me,” the warrior stammered.

Zane nodded, a small laugh escaping his lips. “I did. I agreed not to kill you. But I can’t exactly let you stay in the Heartlands, and I definitely can’t let you run back to the York Divine Kingdom. So, what should I do with you? Any ideas?”

The man’s face went rigid. He clearly wanted to protest, to call Zane a bully, but what was the point? Zane’s promise hadn't been backed by a warrior’s oath, and he could be struck dead in an instant. After a moment of panicked thought, the warrior shook his head. “I can’t think of anything!”

From a short distance away, Dorian offered a faint smile. “Simple,” he said. “Just cripple his cultivation.”

The warrior's face darkened. "Crippling my cultivation is no different from killing me."

He meant it literally. He had survived five Life-and-Death Crises — the sixth was coming, and it was coming soon. He'd followed Wallace across the ocean partly for the opportunity, partly hoping Wallace would help him survive it. Without his cultivation, he'd face that tribulation as an ordinary man. He wouldn't last a breath.

Dorian's smile didn't waver. "Then killing you is the simplest choice."

The weather wasn't hot. A man who had survived five Life-and-Death Crises could sit calmly in a sea of fire. But sweat was running down the warrior's forehead.

The suggestion was simple, but utterly brutal. A Life-and-Death warrior with his power shattered was as good as dead anyway—a small price to pay for his life. Once you reached that realm, you were locked in a constant battle with the laws of the heavens. 

Zane watched the man's face. If it came to it, killing him was the safer option. Dorian and the others couldn't stay in the Heartlands forever. Once they returned to the main continent, who would be able to keep a fifth-stage Life-and-Death warrior in check? No one.

The warrior clenched his teeth. "I have another way. There's a method from the Divine Kingdoms — a Seal. Plant it in me. Whoever plants it becomes the master. The one who carries it cannot resist their master's will under any circumstances. The person enslaved keeps their own thoughts and memories. They simply cannot disobey." 

He didn't say the rest out loud. But the thought was there: perhaps one day, if Zane chose to, he could release the seal. Death left no such door open.

Desperate, the warrior fumbled inside his robes and pulled out a small, brown stone token. Zane recognized the crude divine texture etched into its surface—a slave seal. He glanced over at the Cobalt Dragon, an expert in the art, who gave the artifact a look of clear disdain.

The Cobalt Dragon's voice came quietly. It's a divine rune — but not one from the Dragon Clan's inheritance. The runes I passed to you all come from the True Dragon lineage. This one comes from the Human Clan. Only humans would develop a rune specifically designed to enslave other people.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL PIANO]

“Are you sure about this?” Zane asked, needing to be certain. He would be heading to the seven divine kingdoms soon, and having a guide from the Life-and-Death Realm could save him a world of trouble. But if this man was playing him, he could wreak havoc on the Heartlands the moment Dorian and the others were gone. That was a risk Zane couldn’t afford to take.

The Cobalt Dragon nodded. “It’s a common slave seal. Once it takes hold, he’ll be unable to resist your will. There’s only one condition: your soul must be stronger than his. If it isn’t, the seal will backfire.”

“I see.” Zane wasn’t worried. Lord Wallace’s soul had barely reached the Fighting Soul Realm; there was no way this warrior's soul was any stronger than his own. He turned back to the warrior. “Alright, then. I’ll accept your offer.”

The man hesitated, a flash of regret crossing his face. But what choice did he have? He grimly tossed the brown stone token to Zane.

Zane ran a finger over the divine texture, infusing it with his own Vitality. The seal pulsed with energy as he activated it and aimed it at the warrior.

[SFX: MAGICAL PULSE]

[MUSIC: DARK OMINOUS STRINGS]

 It shot forward, sticking to the man’s forehead with a faint glow. When the light faded, the warrior’s eyes were vacant. He bowed his head slightly. “Master, what are your orders?”

[MUSIC: SINISTER CRESCENDO]

 A wicked smile spread across Zane’s face. “I won't kill you. I want you to do it.”
