Ep 1088 - Pathetic

Word count: 1064

[AMBIENT: TENSE SILENCE]

The Lord of the Sunset World, being exceptionally sensitive to the resonance of other people’s Vitality, scanned Yolande and Zane but felt nothing—not a single ripple from either of them.

He gave an impressed nod. A warrior’s insight was everything. With a flick of his wrist, he sent the Nightshard Spear spinning through the air toward Zane.

[SFX: METALLIC WHOOSH]

Zane snatched the spear and ran his hand over the shaft, his fingers tracing the faint, blossom-like markings etched into the metal. He let a trickle of Chaos Energy flow from his palm, and the weapon drank it down without a sound.

[SFX: MYSTICAL ENERGY SURGE]

[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]

Instantly, the markings on the shaft flared to life, blooming into streaks of blood-red flowers under a crimson glow. They looked so vivid, almost glistening, and Zane could feel the weight of their dark, ancient history.

“Huh?” the Lord of the Sunset World said, his face a mask of confusion. “How is your Vitality so pure? The blossoms you’ve activated are incredibly bright.”

His own power was immense, the Vitality he’d gathered as a World Lord countless times greater than anything a kid from the Life-and-Death Realm could possess. Yet even he couldn’t awaken the spear’s Ancient Blood to this degree. How had Zane done it? The only explanation was that Zane also possessed a strain of Ancient Blood that was reacting with the spear. But the World Lord couldn’t sense any such aura from him. He was completely baffled.

Then again, he figured, every prodigy had their secrets. After all, Zane had somehow managed to fly thirty-six thousand miles on sheer physical strength. How? The World Lord had no idea, and he wasn’t about to start prying.

[AMBIENT: GRAND HALL]

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

The other leaders watched, a flicker of envy in their eyes. Their own weapons were just as powerful, but to see a kid at his level get his hands on something like that… it stung.

“Sir,” Yolande cut in, “can you tell us the reward for traveling thirty thousand miles?”

The Lord of the Sunset World just chuckled. “I’ll tell Zane when Passing the Torch is over.”

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS CHIMES]

Unlike the Tower of Sin, Passing the Torch was purely a trial, split into two parts.

[AMBIENT: DESCENDING FOOTSTEPS]

Zane and Layla dropped down from the platform together and rejoined the gathered warriors. Zane found a quiet corner and sat. Layla settled in beside him without a word.

A few of the Thirteen Palaces’ warriors exchanged glances. It clicked for them now — why, during the tablet trial, Layla had only ever allowed Zane to sit beside her. There weren’t many in this generation who’d earned that kind of standing. Zane, apparently, was one of them.

Layla’s expression stayed calm, composed — but underneath, something burned. She’d lost the Enchanted Barrier Tower. That much was done. But she refused to be a side character in this era. Maybe Zane was an obstacle placed in her path on purpose — what else could explain someone appearing who was even more monstrous than she was?

I will surpass you, she thought, eyes on his back. The resolve radiating off her was strong enough that nearby warriors glanced her way.

Zane didn’t look back. But the corner of his mouth curved, just slightly.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS DRUMS]

“So,” Zane said, “what exactly is the Illusion Battlefield?”

Layla glanced at him. “It only opens once every Evolution Era,” she said. “One Evolution Era is three point six billion years. When it opens, it’s filled with the successors of Realm Lords, Supreme Lords — every hidden genius in the universe takes part.” She paused. “Not a single living being in this current era has ever seen it.”

“Not one?” Zane said, genuinely caught off guard.

“Not one,” she confirmed. “An Evolution Era ends in a great extinction. Everything gets reshuffled. Even the Heavenly Dao collapses and reforms. That fragment of Heavenly Dao inside you? That’s a relic from the era before this one. Only a handful of the strongest beings in existence ever survive from one era into the next.”

“What’s so strange about that?” Layla said, chiming in. “They say the Illusion Battlefield always opens during a War of the Worlds. It’s probably a prelude to the world of chaos.” She said it like it was the most obvious thing in the world, but shot Zane a look as if to make sure he understood.

“I get it,” he said with a nod.

With that, everyone’s attention turned to the trial in the crucible.

[AMBIENT: ANCIENT PALACE]

[MUSIC: CEREMONIAL CHIMES]

The old man’s voice rang out over the gathered warriors. “Two hundred will enter the Illusion Battlefield trial together. When your name appears, step forward.”

One by one, golden names lit up above the warriors’ heads. Those called stepped forward and vanished in flashes of light, reappearing inside the ancient palace nearby. They settled to the ground, cross-legged, and went still — each warrior projecting a complete mirror image of themselves into the trial space.

On the old man’s command, a row of Scrying Slates were hung up so the crowd could watch.

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMUR]

[SFX: MAGICAL HUM]

The slates flickered to life, and Zane finally got a clear look at what was happening inside. The two hundred warriors weren’t fighting each other in some chaotic free-for-all; they were facing a series of individual challenges within the Illusionary Arena.

[MUSIC: TENSE DRUMS]

Soon, warriors started getting eliminated. One by one, their projections were defeated inside the arena, and their real bodies would open their eyes back in the crucible before they shuffled out, completely crushed.

As time went on, the challenges grew harder, and more and more people dropped out.

The last man standing made it through a total of eleven rounds before his projection was finally defeated.

[SFX: HEAVY BREATHING]

[MUSIC: VICTORIOUS HORNS]

When he opened his eyes and saw he was the only one left in the crucible, a smug look spread across his face.

But once all two hundred of them were out, the old man just shook his head. “Even the top performer this round only cleared eleven challenges,” he announced, his voice dripping with disappointment.

It was obvious he didn’t think much of any of them. He didn’t even bother to say the winner’s name. He only said one additional word: “Pathetic.”
