Ep 988 - Difficult Steps
Word count: 1069
[AMBIENT: ANCIENT LIBRARY]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]
Something was off. Their Master was a giant—there was no way he’d leave footprints this small. They had to have been left on purpose.
“But why leave these?” Saul wondered aloud. “They’re exactly human-sized. It’s like he knew this day was coming.”
First, thousands of books react to Zane, and now this rune-less one appears out of nowhere? It couldn’t be a coincidence. This was a setup.
“I agree,” the Eternal Scribe chimed in, though his voice wavered, as if trying to convince himself. “Our Master perceives all things through his Great Theurgy. He must have foreseen that Zane would enter The Archives and arranged this for him. We just... we didn’t understand his plan until now.”
Still, something about it didn’t sit right with Zane. He didn’t doubt the power of the owner of The Elysian Keep—a being who had transcended the universe would obviously possess something like the Great Theurgy. But the old man from the Celestial Position and Yolande had both told him the same thing: he was a man without a Destiny. No one in the universe could read his future. Even Vesper’s attempts wouldn’t be genuine.
That left two possibilities: either the Master’s power was so absolute that he could predict the future of a man without a Destiny, or this whole thing was just one massive coincidence.
Right now, none of that mattered. All that mattered was opening that book.
Pushing the thoughts aside, Zane flew toward the far end of the bookshelf. Faint footprints marked a path from the center of the shelf to its edge, leading straight to the enormous book.
[SFX: DEEP RUMBLE]
[MUSIC: TENSE ORCHESTRAL]
Dust swirled as Zane landed and took his first step, placing his foot directly into one of the prints. The moment it touched down, an immense gravitational force seized him. His feet felt welded to the shelf, completely immovable.
“What is this?” he grunted, his brow furrowed in concentration. He tried to flood his body with energy, to force his feet to lift off the ground.
[SFX: METALLIC CLANG]
But it was no use. The force pinning him was absolute; he couldn’t budge an inch.
[MUSIC: TENSE ORCHESTRAL]
[AMBIENT: ECHOING CHAMBER]
Seeing Zane trapped, the Eternal Scribe, Saul, and the three girls rushed to his side.
“You’re stuck?” Saul asked, his eyes darting to Zane’s feet, which looked completely normal.
Zane didn’t answer. Instead, he tried stepping backward.
His feet lifted instantly — so easily that the sudden release sent him stumbling back several steps before he caught himself.
He stood there a moment, looking at the prints.
“Difficult to advance,” the Eternal Scribe said quietly. “Easy to retreat.” He almost seemed to be speaking to himself. “That’s very much the Master’s style. I don’t know why he arranged it this way — but the principle is clear enough.”
Difficult to advance. Easy to retreat. Simple words. Zane had understood that principle since he was a boy. The hard part had never been understanding it.
A smile crossed his face. He stepped back onto the footprints.
The three women said nothing. They knew better than to speak now. But their worry was plain.
Cora watched him with wide eyes — his jaw set, his expression utterly focused. She found, to her own surprise, that she couldn’t look away.
[SFX: DEEP RUMBLE]
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC CRESCENDO]
Zane gave a grim nod. To move forward, he had to take the next step.
[SFX: HEAVY BREATHING]
[AMBIENT: OPPRESSIVE SILENCE]
Six thousand Dragon Scales.
His body screamed. Bloody wounds split open across his chest and back.
He tried to lift his foot.
It didn’t move.
Six thousand one hundred.
Another wound tore open.
Still nothing.
Six thousand two hundred. Six thousand three hundred.
With each increment, another gash ripped through his skin, another jet of blood. His clothes were soaked through.
The Eternal Scribe’s face twisted into a strange expression. “Where is he finding this power?” he muttered. “It’s already past the limit of his body…”
Saul said nothing.
Six thousand four hundred. Six thousand five hundred. Six thousand six hundred.
Gritting his teeth against the crushing weight, he fought to lift his leg. He moved slower than a child, as if he were carrying a mountain on his back.
“Six thousand seven hundred — rise!”
[SFX: HEAVY FOOTSTEP]
His right foot finally kicked free. It swung forward and landed on the next print, locking down immediately.
He didn’t stop. He wrenched his left foot up and drove it forward.
One step.
Two steps.
Three steps.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
He was moving, but at a terrible cost.
Four steps.
Five steps.
[MUSIC: INTENSE DRAMATIC STRINGS]
[AMBIENT: HEAVY BREATHING]
Six steps.
[SFX: FLESH TEARING]
[SFX: BLOOD SPRAY]
With every agonizing movement, new gashes tore open across his body. Blood sprayed from the fresh wounds in violent jets, ripping through his clothes.
One of the bloody projectiles shot straight for Isla. Tia was faster.
[SFX: ICE CRYSTALLIZING]
[SFX: IMPACT ON ICE]
A flick of her wrist summoned a crystalline ice shield just in time to block it. The impact left a deep dent in the magical ice, and Tia stared at it, her own fear reflected in its frosted surface.
Just how much power is surging through him? she thought, horrified.
Isla reached out a trembling hand to touch the dent. The blood had instantly frozen to the shield’s frigid surface, and she said nothing, her finger tracing the cold, crimson scar in the ice.
Somewhere in the back of her mind, a quiet voice told her she never should have pointed out those footprints. Even if she hadn’t, he would have found them eventually. But not like this. Not right now.
She kept her hand pressed against the ice and didn’t say a word.
Seven steps.
Eight steps.
Nine steps.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS LOW DRONE]
[SFX: LABORED BREATHING]
He was at his limit. Every ragged breath he took was a bloody mist in the air. The immense force was tearing him apart from the inside; his organs were starting to rupture.
Zane gasped, blood weeping from dozens of wounds. He’d only managed nine steps, but the path of footprints stretched on and on, all the way to the end of the shelf. It was a distance the Master had crossed in two or three strides.
For Zane, it was a journey of a thousand miles.
