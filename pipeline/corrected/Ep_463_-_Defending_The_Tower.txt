Ep 463 - Defending The Tower

Word count: 1035

[AMBIENT: OUTDOOR ARENA]

[MUSIC: TENSE ORCHESTRAL]
Zane stood on the sun-baked grassland and looked at the forty stone towers that rose from the earth like ancient sentinels.

The examiner had laid out the rules for the pre-qualifying test: a two-stage competition, beginning with tower defense.

Everything hinged on points. Scores would reflect individual performance, and only the top three competitors would advance to the main tournament. Zane glanced at his competitors' towers scattered across the grassland—each one identical to his own, each defender standing ready.

A large zero projected onto the side of Zane's tower—his current score. Perched atop the structure, a crystal ball waited to record every hit the tower sustained. Zane's jaw tightened. Twenty hits meant elimination. He couldn't afford mistakes.

[SFX: LOUD BELL TOLL]

A loud bell tolled from high above, its sound rolling across the grassland in waves. Zane drew a steadying breath, centering himself as he'd done countless times before battle. The familiar weight of Streamer at his side brought comfort.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS BATTLE]

[SFX: DISTANT CHITTERING]

As the ringing faded, a thick line of black dots swarmed the horizon. The chittering grew louder—thousands of tiny clicking sounds merging into an unsettling chorus.

Zane's eyes narrowed. "Here they come."

His pulse quickened, but his hands remained steady. This was just another trial, another obstacle between him and his ultimate goal. Marilyn's face flashed through his mind—the woman he'd sworn to kill. Every challenge, every victory brought him one step closer.

A sharp glint lit up Zane's eyes as he focused on the approaching swarm. Three-eyed spiders, their eyes glowing with a dim, unnerving light, were marching right for him. One alone wasn't a threat, but a horde this size was incredibly dangerous.

According to the rules, the spiders were here to attack him and his tower. More than twenty successful hits, and he'd be eliminated from the tournament.

To advance, Zane needed to slaughter as many creatures as possible while protecting his structure from their relentless assault.

[SFX: BOW DRAW AND ARROW RELEASE]

As the first wave of spiders scuttled into range, Zane held his breath, watching the bowman stationed atop his tower. The archer moved with practiced efficiency, drawing his bow in one fluid motion. The string sang as he released his shot.

[AMBIENT: BATTLE ARENA]

[MUSIC: INTENSE ACTION]

An arrow hissed through the air, punching through a three-eyed spider's carapace. The creature crumpled mid-stride.

The tower's automated defense proved impressive—the bowman possessed lightning reflexes, and every arrow found its mark with lethal precision.

Yet for all his accuracy, the archer could only eliminate one target at a time. A writhing black sea of spiders flooded the arena, their numbers overwhelming. Hundreds became thousands, their collective chittering rising to a deafening crescendo. The bowman's efforts, however skilled, wouldn't stem this tide alone.

Zane watched the swarm advance. He stepped forward, muscles coiling like springs ready to release. Time to join the fight.

[SFX: BATTLE CRY]

"Kill!"

[SFX: CHARGING FOOTSTEPS]

[MUSIC: EPIC COMBAT]

Zane charged forward, meeting the horde at the perfect moment.

Individually, the creatures were weak—barely more than manifestations of spiritual energy—but with at least a thousand swarming toward the tower, speed was everything. He and the bowman had to eliminate them swiftly. If even a few dozen slipped past their defense to reach the tower, he'd fail the stage.

His muscles coiled with anticipation. This was no different from the countless battles he'd fought to survive.

[SFX: SWORD SLASH]

[SFX: CREATURE DEATH]

With a single swing of his blade, he cleaved through more than a dozen spiders. Their bodies dissolved into motes of colored light before vanishing completely, leaving only the faint scent of ozone in the air.

Zane pressed forward, his sword becoming a blur of motion. Each strike found its mark with brutal efficiency. The familiar weight of Streamer in his hand grounded him, steadied his breathing.

His purpose burned crystal clear. He wouldn't let these mindless creatures stop him. Not after everything he'd endured.
His mind remained laser-focused on one thing: passing this test and securing his place in the main tournament.

[AMBIENT: QUIET OBSERVATION PLATFORM]

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL]

Watching from a viewing platform, the examiner frowned. Runic experts aren’t supposed to know how to fight. So what is he doing here? A flood of doubts filled his mind.

"I know him. That's him, all right," Michael Nash confirmed. "Unfortunately, I have no idea why he's in the Martial Tournament."

"No wonder he was so slow in the first round. Five minutes just to clear the spiders," another elder scoffed. "I'm afraid he won't make it to the fifth round after all."

"The fifth?" Michael Nash shook his head with a small smile. "I'm betting he makes it to the tenth."

Another elder grinned. "You're giving the kid too much credit, Elder Nash. The seventh round is the beast attack. I'd be surprised if he makes it past the sixth, let alone the seventh. My money's on him failing in the sixth."

The elders knew the magic array inside and out. Defending the tower wasn't easy. With each round, the beasts grew stronger. The first few rounds were a warm-up, but after the fifth, every task was at least twice as hard as the last.

Though the elders disagreed about which round would prove Zane's undoing, they shared one unshakable conviction: he would fail.

The reasoning seemed obvious. Everyone knew runic experts excelled at craftsmanship, not combat. Their fingers were trained for delicate brushwork, not brutal fighting. And Zane had only reached the Third Grade of the Soul Realm—a respectable achievement for his age, maybe, but woefully inadequate for this tournament.

Already, dozens of fighters at the ultimate level of the Soul Realm had breezed through the entrance exam, their movements fluid and confident. The top three from the previous pre-qualifying test had all achieved high levels of the Inner-Core Realm, their power radiating like heat from a forge.

The gap was staggering. Zane's cultivation level seemed almost laughable in comparison.

Several elders exchanged knowing glances, their expressions mixing pity with professional detachment. They'd seen countless promising young cultivators crash against the tournament's unforgiving standards. This boy would be no different, regardless of how impressive his rune-crafting might be.

One elder sighed, already mentally writing off the young man. "Talent in one discipline rarely translates to another. The boy should have stuck to his brushes."

How could someone so much weaker than the rest possibly pass?
