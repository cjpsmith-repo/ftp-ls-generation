Ep 158 - One vs. One Hundred
Word Count: 1115

[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL COURTYARD]
 Right now, though, Zane’s biggest worry was what this test was even about.
“Saul, you said an immortal set this thing up,” Zane said, his brow furrowed. “But I’m not even halfway to the Adept Stage. How am I supposed to pass a test like that?”
If the Legendary Immortal really had designed all the challenges, then forget about Zane—even the grand masters from the Eastern Territory would probably fail.
Saul just grinned and gestured around the courtyard. “Take a good look at everything here,” he said. “Notice how it’s all, what, a hundred times bigger than you?”
Zane glanced around again. He had to admit, Saul had a point. The place was clearly built for immortals. The walls soared thousands of feet into the air, making him feel like an ant crawling through it all.
“And don’t you think it’s a little weird that I showed up looking like this?” Saul asked, that big grin still plastered on his face.
The whole thing was weird. Ever since he’d stepped into the Elysian Keep, everything had been gigantic—except for the puppet, Saul, who was roughly Zane’s size.
“The test is all about finding the right successor, so it’s mostly a test of a candidate’s strength,” Saul explained, his tone encouraging. “But obviously, if the Immortal based the test on his power, no one would ever inherit the Elysian Keep. The test is designed to push your limits. That means the stronger you are, the harder it gets. Just remember: it’s a test of strength!”
But Zane had no idea how his strength was about to be tested in this bizarre world.
[AMBIENT: VAST EMPTY SPACE]
He trudged forward until a thick hemp rope came into view. Alongside it stood an assembly of bare-chested, extraordinarily muscular men. Their physiques appeared almost comically exaggerated—bulging biceps, chests like barrels, and shoulders that could carry mountains. Zane quickly tallied their numbers, estimating at least a hundred of these imposing figures arranged in formation.
"Hmph," growled their leader, a particularly massive specimen with a scar running across his chest. He thrust the weathered rope into Zane's hands, his expression betraying neither encouragement nor malice.
[SFX: ENERGY WHOOSH]
[MUSIC: MAGICAL TRANSFORMATION]
The instant Zane's fingers closed around the coarse fibers, an otherworldly sensation washed over him. His body suddenly felt weightless, as though gravity had relinquished its hold. The world around him blurred into a kaleidoscope of colors, reality itself seeming to bend and distort. A cold tingle ran down his spine as the transformation took place.
[AMBIENT: WINDSWEPT SUMMIT]
When clarity returned to his vision, Zane found himself perched precariously on a windswept mountain summit, still clutching the rope with white-knuckled intensity. Across a yawning chasm stood the assembly of muscular men on an opposing peak, their hands firmly gripping the other end of the rope. 
A vast, seemingly bottomless ravine separated the two mountaintops, its depth obscured by swirling mists. Above, ominous vultures circled in lazy spirals, their beady eyes fixed on Zane with predatory anticipation, as if they were already savoring the prospect of his fall and subsequent feast.
[SFX: BUBBLING LAVA]
 To make matters worse, the bottom of the ravine churned with molten lava.
“Looks like if I screw this up, or even just slip, I’m dead,” Zane muttered to himself.
The men across the chasm grunted in unison. They looped the rope over their shoulders and gestured for Zane to do the same.
*So the Test of Strength is a game of tug-of-war?* Zane thought, the reality of the situation dawning on him. *How am I supposed to beat a hundred guys by myself?* He swung the rope, wrapping it securely around his shoulder.
[MUSIC: TENSE BUILDUP]
 Zane gave the rope a firm tug and nodded at the men. The test was on.
“Heave-ho!”
[SFX: THUNDEROUS ROAR]
 With a thunderous roar, the one hundred intimidating men yanked on the rope with everything they had.
[SFX: BOOTS SCRAPING ROCK]
 Zane had braced himself, but the combined force was still overwhelming. He lost his footing, his boots scraping against the rock as he started sliding toward the cliff’s edge.
After getting dragged a good six or seven feet, Zane finally managed to dig his heels in. He planted his feet like a bull, halting the slide and finding a stable stance.
*This is way tougher than it looks,* Zane thought. He leaned back, throwing all his weight against the taut rope.
But the collective strength of a hundred men was immense. Despite his best efforts, he was still being dragged slowly but surely toward the cliff.
He hadn't unleashed the power of the Dragon Scales yet, relying purely on his own physical strength.
He gritted his teeth, his muscles straining and tendons popping. But even with everything he had, he kept losing ground, sliding inch by painful inch toward the abyss.
[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSE]
“This is it. This is the limit of my physical strength,” Zane realized, his heart pounding. "I'm only a few feet from the edge. If I don't use the power of the Dragon Scales, I'm going into that ravine!"
 “Come on!” Zane roared. The Dragon Scales in his mind flared with a green glow. A surge of power, like a jolt of pure electricity, shot through every muscle in his body.
[SFX: ENERGY SURGE]
 In an instant, his power multiplied.
“A hundred of them,” Zane thought, his mind racing to analyze the situation. “When I pull, they pull. The only difference is, every time they pull together, they let out a roar. Why do they always roar?”
*"That roar… it can't actually make them stronger. The Immortal wouldn't add something useless to the Dreamscape. It has to be here for a reason. Everything has a purpose."
The thought of that powerful roar sparked a memory—a story he’d read as a kid.
It was a story about the Ironrun River. There was a shallow part where small boats could pass, but big ships would get grounded. To get the big ships through, they had to be pulled by teams of men called boat trackers, who would walk along the shore, dragging the vessels through the shallow water with ropes slung over their backs.
But even with dozens of men pulling, their combined strength was inconsistent, not enough to move a ship that was stuck deep in the mud. To get everyone to pull at the exact same moment, they would sing work songs—rhythmic chants to keep everyone moving in sync.
The chant was used to synchronize their movements, combining all their individual strength into one powerful, unified pull.

[MUSIC: REVELATION THEME]
 Thinking about this, an idea suddenly hit Zane.
