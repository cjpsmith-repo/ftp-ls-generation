Ep 423 - Bidding War

Word count: 1128

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS REVELATION]

[AMBIENT: QUIET SHOP]

Okay, now Cedric's shock made sense. 

Still, in the Cobalt Dragon's eyes, Perfect Brushwork was nothing special. The upper world was just too vast, teeming with countless factions and races where talent like that was a dime a dozen. Perfect Brushwork was just the foundational skill required to master divine textures—a starting point, not a destination.

What had actually impressed the Cobalt Dragon was that Zane had managed to comprehend it in a single day. The ancient creature had witnessed cultivators struggling for years to grasp what this boy had absorbed in mere hours. That was why it had cursed out Cedric and the old man, calling them idiots who were completely unqualified even to discuss divine textures. Its fury stemmed not from cruelty, but from the frustration of watching lesser minds judge something they couldn't begin to understand.

But just because the Cobalt Dragon saw the skill as common didn't mean Cedric did. The gap between their perspectives was as vast as the distance between heaven and earth.

The truth was, Cedric couldn't even begin to grasp Perfect Brushwork. He had devoted his entire life to the runic arts, studied every text available in the Heartlands, and still this fundamental technique remained beyond his reach. In fact, no runic expert in the Heartlands could claim mastery of it. They were all children playing with toys, unaware that true craftsmanship existed on a plane they couldn't even perceive.

The technique had existed in the region’s history, but that was ages ago. Two millennia back, a runic expert named Arnold Shaw had unlocked its secrets, allowing him to activate the latent properties of materials as he drew runes. From that day on, he was known as the "Son of Runes."

[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT ORCHESTRAL]

Even now, many of his works survive. The most famous was the protective array guarding the Ethereal Spirit Sect—the very thing that made them the only fifth-grade sect in The Heartlands. That masterpiece of his skill had seen them through countless disasters, allowing them to ascend from a mere third-grade sect.

[SFX: TENSE SILENCE]

Cedric's gaze shifted between the glowing rune and Zane, his expression caught somewhere between awe and suspicion. "Be honest with me," he said, his voice dropping to a near whisper. "Who truly created this rune?"

A flash of irritation crossed Zane's face. He pressed his lips into a thin line, fighting the urge to snap. After everything he'd endured, this questioning felt like yet another insult. "Didn't I already explain this?" His tone carried an edge sharper than he intended. "Were you even listening to what I said?"

[MUSIC: CONFUSED TENSION]

"Who?" Cedric and the old man echoed, completely lost.

Zane felt his patience fraying. Seriously? He thought he’d been painfully obvious. He let out a heavy sigh and spelled it out for them. "I made this meditation rune. It's my own creation."

Their eyes snapped to Zane. In perfect, disbelieving unison, they shook their heads and shouted, "I don't believe you!"

"Believe what you want. I couldn't care less," Zane said, turning to the shopkeeper. "Boss, would you mind appraising this for me?" He didn't have time to argue with Cedric; it's not like he owed the guy anything.

The old man squinted, giving a sharp nod. He was a businessman, first and last. He didn't waste time on an item's origin, only what it would fetch. That was how you stayed in business.

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

"Appraise it? No, no, you can't sell this!" Cedric cried, stepping forward like a madman to stop him.

A flash of irritation crossed both Zane's and the old man's faces. The old man might be friendly with Cedric, but business was business, and acquaintances didn't pay the bills. Zane was here for one reason: to sell his divine texture and make some money. And now Cedric was, for some insane reason, getting in his way.



Zane cut through the standoff. "Fine. I'll sell it to whoever offers the most. That solves it, doesn't it?"
Both men stopped. Slowly, they turned to look at him.
"How much are you asking?" Cedric said.
Zane thought for a moment. The materials had cost him roughly the equivalent of twenty thousand low-grade crystals. "Double that. Forty thousand."
The silence that followed was deafening. Then both men started laughing — not mockingly, but with the slightly unhinged energy of people who had just realized they were about to spend far more than that.
[SFX: URGENT FOOTSTEPS]
"I'll buy it," Cedric said. "Whatever it costs."
He turned to the old man, cutting him out entirely.
Zane just shrugged. As long as he got paid, he didn't care who the buyer was. The old man, however, saw things very differently.

[AMBIENT: BUSTLING MARKETPLACE]

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

"What do you think you're doing, Cedric?" the old man snapped. "He came to my shop to do business, and you're just going to waltz in and poach my customer?"

"So what if I do?" Cedric shot back. "When you get right down to it, this market is Cloud Hall territory. I've got every right to do business here." As a runic expert for the Cloud Hall, Cedric held a high rank despite only being at the mid-stage of the Inner-Core Realm—placing him just below figures like Emanuel and Saint Morphen. He was on par with any of the other general elders.

But the old man didn't even flinch. "Cloud Hall territory? Are you insane?" he scoffed. "This market was built and is still sponsored by the World Merchants' Guild! Go on, I dare you. Go tell the head of the Guild that the Cloud Hall owns this place. If you've got the guts to say that to his face, I'll walk out of here and drop this whole thing right now."

Zane could hardly believe it. He never would have thought this little divine texture could fetch such a huge sum of money. But how could a simple meditation rune be worth so much?

In truth, the meditation function itself wasn't that valuable. What had Cedric and the old man in a frenzy was the technique behind it. This was a chance to study the lost art of Perfect Brushwork. For that alone, the rune was priceless.

The old man hesitated, his business sense kicking in. "F-Five," he stammered, already feeling reluctant. He was a merchant, and he knew his limits. Yes, the piece used Perfect Brushwork, but its true market value was unknown. A bad bid could cost him a fortune.

[SFX: DRAMATIC GAVEL STRIKE]

[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT HORNS]

But at that number, Cedric’s eyes lit up. A smug grin spread across his face. In his excitement, he seemed to have completely forgotten his initial plea to stop the sale altogether. 

"Ten! I offer you ten supreme Aether crystals!"
