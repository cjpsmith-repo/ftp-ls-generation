Ep 540 - Worry
Word count: 1138
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL VOID]
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
The woman in black robes stood motionless on the disc, her gaze locked onto the die suspended in the abyss below. Every spectator leaned forward, breath caught in their throats, as the die began its wild rotation once more.
The cube tumbled through the void, its faces blurring together in a dizzying display. Then, gradually, the spinning slowed, each rotation more deliberate than the last, until finally it settled into stillness.
[SFX: CROWD GASP]
A collective gasp tore through the assembled cultivators. The uppermost face displayed another six.
"Another six!" someone shouted, disbelief coloring their voice.
"Impossible," a nearby disciple breathed, his eyes wide with shock.
"How can anyone be this fortunate?" another muttered, shaking his head in wonder and envy.
The woman remained perfectly still, her expression unreadable beneath her hood, though a subtle shift in her posture suggested satisfaction. Around her, whispers rippled through the crowd.
[AMBIENT: CROWD WHISPERS]
By now, everyone understood they were pawns in some strange game within the Celestial Tomb of Vesper. No one knew Vesper’s reasons for it, but with everyone on the same starting line, the only thing that mattered was not falling behind.
Before her, the highest anyone had managed was a single six. But this mysterious woman, on just her first turn, had already earned a second roll. It was a spectacle, and no one could look away.
"Another six… who would've thought?" an onlooker mused, scratching his chin.
[SFX: MAGICAL MATERIALIZATION]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS CHIMES]
A moment later, six more discs materialized before the unfazed woman in black. The farthest one pulsed with a faint, golden glow. She barely glanced at it before striding across the newly formed path. The second her foot touched the golden disc, the disembodied voice boomed again.
[SFX: BOOMING VOICE ECHO]
"Throw the die again."
[SFX: COLLECTIVE GROAN]
A collective groan echoed through the arena. "Seriously? Again?" one of the warriors grumbled, slapping his forehead. The thought was unanimous: this woman's luck made even Tia's look ordinary.
"You think she's cheating?" someone whispered to their neighbor.
[AMBIENT: ANCIENT TOMB]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]
"No way. This is an immortal's tomb."
Zane sat up straighter. "Tell me."
He’d been racking his brain ever since the game started, trying to figure out why Vesper would create something that seemed to rely entirely on chance.
[MUSIC: REVELATION CHIMES]
"This might look like a game, but it’s really a Wheel of Fortune," the Cobalt Dragon stated.
Zane tilted his head, confused. "Wheel of Fortune? What do you mean?" he asked, his interest suddenly sparked.
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE FLUTE]
The Cobalt Dragon's tone was firm. "Think of it like this, Zane. To most warriors, luck is some mysterious, intangible thing. But it's a very real force. In The Heartlands, warriors are typically judged by two metrics: talent and willpower."
Zane nodded, following the dragon’s logic. It was true. A warrior had to constantly forge their willpower to grow stronger. Without it, even the most gifted prodigy would squander their talent on fleeting pleasures and empty pursuits. Potential and experience meant nothing without the will to see things through.
Because of this, every sect tests for talent first and willpower second when choosing new disciples.
As Zane processed this, the Cobalt Dragon continued, "And it's not a bad system. With those two criteria, the sects can reliably train disciples to the Soul Realm or even the Inner-Core Realm." He looked up, his gaze distant.
[MUSIC: ETHEREAL CHOIR]
"With a little luck, they might even produce an elder who reaches the Spirit-Tribulation Realm. But in the Upper World… if you only look at talent and will, you'll never find a true master."
Finding diligent, talented disciples was the easy part. But training them to the Inner-Core Realm, the Spirit-Tribulation Realm, or even the Life-and-Death Realm… that wasn't the ultimate goal. To reach the true peak, they all needed something more. A third factor had to be considered: luck.
[SFX: WHEEL SPINNING]
The scene playing out before them reminded the Cobalt Dragon of the Wheel of Fortune from the Upper World. It was a similar test, though that version wasn't fatal—unlucky participants were simply ejected from the trial.
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL REVELATION]
And suddenly, it all clicked into place. Vesper's purpose in recreating the Wheel was simple: he was searching for the luckiest warrior among them.
While the warriors here were at different levels, none of them were weak. And from what Zane had seen, they weren't unlucky either. Brent was fairly fortunate, but the Holy Maiden of the Divine Kingdom was on another level entirely—she'd managed three consecutive rolls on a single turn.
Zane shook his head with a wry smile. "Guess I'm not one of the lucky ones, then."
"You're wrong. In fact, it's the opposite," the Cobalt Dragon countered, his expression serious. "Luck isn't just about things always going your way. Sometimes, surviving terrible misfortune is its own kind of luck. Two steps forward. Consider what that reflects."
Zane fell silent. As a child, he'd had everything—the heir to House Lowell, wanting for nothing. Then overnight, it vanished.
"The two smooth steps reflect those easy years," he said slowly. "What comes next reflects everything after."
"Very possible," the Cobalt Dragon replied. "I can't be certain—Vesper designed this Wheel. But it fits."
[SFX: PULSING ENERGY]
The beam spun again, landing on Zane.
He stepped forward and punched the die.
One. The lowest possible roll.
Around him, smirks appeared.
[AMBIENT: CROWD WHISPERS]
Tia glanced back. "Take your time."
Zane nodded and looked down at the single disc before him.
Purple.
The smirks widened. No one had seen that color yet. But the killing intent radiating from it was unmistakable.
Zane thought of the Cobalt Dragon's words. This abyss measured fortune—and his had carried him this far.
He stepped onto the purple disc.
[SFX: WHOOSHING DESCENT]
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC TENSION]
Just then, something plummeted from the sky above Zane.
[SFX: ROARING FLAMES]
In the ensuing chaos, a faint voice cried out, "Fiery Star Flame!"
At the sound of those words, Maddox's eyebrow twitched. He shot a cold, mocking smile at Zane's tense figure. Looks like your luck just ran out, brat, he thought to himself.
Only someone who had faced that punishment could know its true horror. And so far, Maddox was the only one who had ever survived it. Every other warrior who had been subjected to the flame had been burned to nothing.
[MUSIC: DARK OMINOUS]
Glee glinted in Maddox's half-lidded eyes. He crossed his arms, certain Zane wouldn't survive. "I wanted to be the one to kill you," he muttered, a cruel smirk on his face, "but I suppose being reduced to ash is a fitting end for you." Though his tone was cold, a secret thrill shot through him at the thought of his rival's impending death.
Nearby, Tia and Elder Imogen exchanged a worried glance. They were both thinking the same thing. They had faith in Zane's uncanny fortune, but they didn't know if even he could survive the Fiery Star Flame.
[AMBIENT: TENSE SILENCE]
A knot of dread tightened in Tia's stomach as she stared, helpless, at the young man about to face his doom.
