Ep 461 - Haters Gonna Hate

Word count: 1042

[AMBIENT: CROWDED ARENA]

[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT HORNS]

[SFX: CROWD MURMUR]

The Old Man's announcement sent a shockwave of murmurs rippling through the crowd. No one could believe Zane had actually passed.

"What? He passed?" A warrior near the front gaped, his mouth hanging open in disbelief.

"Something's wrong with the lodestone!" another shouted, his voice sharp with indignation. "There's no way he could've passed otherwise! Are the World Merchants' Guild's standards really that low?"

A third cultivator laughed bitterly, shaking his head. "Is this a joke? Hell, maybe I should give it a shot." He gestured toward the lodestone with mock enthusiasm. "For all I know, I could pass too."

Scattered laughter erupted from the crowd, though the sound carried an edge of frustration rather than genuine amusement. Several warriors exchanged skeptical glances, their expressions twisted with a mixture of envy and wounded pride. A few crossed their arms defensively, as if Zane's success somehow diminished their own worth.

"The Guild must be desperate for participants," someone muttered darkly.

"Or they're playing favorites," another added, his eyes narrowing as he studied Zane with open hostility.

The vast majority of warriors gathered here were in the Soul Realm, somewhere between the seventh and ninth grades. They'd come to watch the pre-qualifying matches, sizing up the competition to see who they could beat and gauge their own chances. To their dismay, even being at the eighth or ninth grade of the Soul Realm felt a little weak, especially next to those who'd reached Consummation.

Not all of them came from powerful families. Many were independent fighters or from smaller factions, and most of them were broke. A few clutched a supreme Aether crystal, but they were hesitant to hand it over to the old examiner. The passing rate was just too damn low. It was tough for a Soul Realm warrior to get their hands on a supreme Aether crystal, and they weren't about to risk losing it for nothing.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL BUILDUP]

Then this kid—a third-grade Soul-Illuminated Realm cultivator, of all things—had stepped forward out of nowhere. The crackle of thunder and lightning from his seemingly weak punch hadn't appeared particularly powerful. Yet that single strike made the lodestone glow for a full five minutes.

The sight defied everything they understood about cultivation. Some warriors exchanged bewildered glances, while others stared at the still-glowing stone with their mouths hanging open. It seemed so unbelievable that many concluded the stone must be broken.

Who even was this kid? Zane. His name meant nothing to most of them—just another unknown face in a sea of hopefuls. Whether he passed or failed should have been irrelevant to their own prospects, but when the Old Man made the call, jealousy ignited in their chests like wildfire.

"How? How could he pass?" one warrior demanded, his voice cracking with indignation.

"If he can pass, any one of us should be able to!" another shouted, gesturing wildly at the crowd.

A grizzled fighter near the front spat on the ground. "So the Guild's just lost all its credibility. This is obviously rigged!"

"What are they thinking?" someone else called out, genuine concern mixing with their anger. "A third-grade Soul-Illuminated cultivator is going to get himself killed in the Martial Tournament!"

The protests spread through the crowd like ripples across water, each voice feeding the next until the chamber rang with accusations and bitter complaints.

[SFX: ANGRY SHOUTS]

[MUSIC: TENSION RISING]

As the complaints swelled, the Old Man's face darkened with a scowl. His patience had reached its limit. "Shut up!" he barked.

[AMBIENT: SUDDEN SILENCE]

The command cracked through the chamber like a whip. His voice carried such raw power that it made their ears ring, the sound reverberating through bone and marrow. Warriors who had faced death without flinching found themselves instinctively stepping back. His cold glare swept across the crowd, and every voice died in mid-protest. Even the most battle-hardened fighters felt their complaints wither under that glacial stare, suddenly remembering exactly who they were challenging.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS PLANNING]

Meanwhile, the true contenders in the hall remained motionless. Many had reached the Consummation of the Soul Realm. Most had broken through to the early-stage Inner-Core Realm, and a select few had already attained the middle stage. These were among the finest independent warriors in the region—earth-level talents or higher who had clawed their way to power without the backing of any major faction. Each carried scars, both visible and hidden, from battles that would have broken lesser cultivators.

Two warriors at the middle-stage Inner-Core Realm leaned against the far wall, eyes closed in meditation. Neither opened them, yet both sensed the disturbance rippling through the crowd.

One whispered to the other, his voice barely audible, "A little strange, don't you think? That kid's only at the third grade of the Soul-Illuminated Realm, yet he qualified."

His companion cracked open one eye, cast a lazy glance at Zane, then closed it again. A faint smile played at the corner of his mouth. "Yeah, third grade. Looks like he possesses some kind of exceptional talent, though. Perhaps a profound understanding of one of the Laws. The lodestone doesn't lie."

"You want to test him when the matches start?" Unlike his friend, this warrior seemed genuinely intrigued by Zane. He'd seen plenty of prodigies over the years, but something about this young man's stillness suggested depths worth exploring.

The other warrior shifted his weight against the wall, settling deeper into his meditative stance. "No. Tedious. Our goal is the main tournament. No need to create unnecessary complications. Besides, if he's truly talented, we'll face him eventually."

[MUSIC: QUIET DETERMINATION]

Despite the warriors discussing him as though he were some curious specimen, Zane stood motionless, his expression perfectly calm—as composed as he'd been before the test. The weight of their scrutiny meant nothing to him. He was a warrior, through and through. Certainly, he had his moments of doubt, times when his vow felt like an impossible burden threatening to crush him. But once he committed to a goal, he'd pursue it relentlessly, no matter how treacherous the path became.

And right now, he was determined to kill Marilyn.

That meant surviving these pre-qualifying matches first—and he would do whatever it took.
