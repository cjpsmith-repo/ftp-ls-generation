Ep 1152 - Good Luck

Word count: 1071

[MUSIC: TENSE ACTION]
The Snow Demon seized control, and the warrior’s body went limp, moving like a puppet on a string. He was strong—Spirit Supreme Realm strong—but the demon possessing him could barely tap into a fraction of that power. Lavender’s order had been clear, and Zane didn’t hesitate. 
[SFX: BODY COLLAPSE]
The moment the demon took over, the man inside ceased to matter.
The possessed warrior’s legs gave out. 
[SFX: SWORD SCRAPING GROUND]
He swayed, struggling just to lift his long sword as the three Nether God Shadows closed in. He wanted to fight back, but the demon couldn’t even channel the man’s Vitality. He stood no chance. The shadows descended, and it was over in an instant. 
[SFX: HEAVY IMPACT]
The warrior’s body crumpled to the ground, lifeless.
Not everyone made it out cleanly. A few warriors, lost in their illusions, had stopped controlling their descent. They drifted sideways in the freefall, and the abyss wall found them — ice spikes jutting from the stone, waiting. Some came away with cuts and gashes. Others hit hard enough that they had no choice. One by one, their escape runes flared, and they were gone.
With the threat handled, Zane’s attention snapped back to Freya. Lavender hadn’t seemed concerned, but Zane wasn’t taking any chances. Possession by a Snow Demon was a death sentence, and he couldn’t shake the fear that Freya was still trapped in the demon’s illusion.
[SFX: COLD ENERGY PULSE]
Just as he turned toward her, a pulse of cold energy flared from her clenched fist, followed by a trickle of blood from her fingertip. 
Her whole body twitched once, her eyelids fluttered, and then her eyes were open, perfectly clear. Seeing the concern on Zane’s face, she offered a small smile. “I’m fine.”
Zane’s eyes fixed on her wounded finger, his brow furrowed in confusion. “What was that?”
“A little insurance,” Freya explained, wincing slightly as she looked at her hand. “I was worried the Snow Demon might get its hooks in me, so I formed an ice spike out of my Vitality and held onto it. I gave myself five minutes to melt it… or it would have exploded.”
Zane understood immediately. Snow Demons trapped their victims in beautiful illusions—dream worlds designed to make them lower their guard before their souls were devoured. Freya’s trick was brilliant: a shard of pure pain sharp enough to shatter any fantasy the demon could conjure.
He found himself admiring her ingenuity. Freya wasn’t the strongest warrior he knew, but she was one of the smartest. She had a knack for these little tricks, the kind of cleverness that could mean the difference between life and death when things went wrong.
Around them, other warriors were beginning to stir, fighting their way out of the psychic assault. These were seasoned fighters with strong wills, but even so, most were still struggling. 
[MUSIC: DARK OMINOUS STRINGS]
A few, Zane noted grimly, weren’t struggling at all; they were already gone, their bodies now just hosts for the Snow Demons.
Below them, a second stone platform came into view.
[AMBIENT: QUIET COLLECTIVE EXHALE]
Every warrior who had held on through the vines, the blades, and the Snow Demons had done it for exactly this. The ones still falling — battered, bloodied, some still blinking away the last traces of the illusion — felt it the moment they saw it. A ripple of something moved through the group. Not relief. Anticipation.
Zane adjusted his angle and dropped toward the platform. Even landing with his full weight and momentum behind him, his feet met the ice without leaving so much as a scratch. The platform was coated in a layer of ice three inches thick — and whatever the ancient Supreme Lord had done to it, it held.
[SFX: BOOT ON ICE]
Not everyone made it. Cody had been angling toward the platform from the outer edge of the fall when his feet hit the ice and kept going. He scrabbled for purchase and found none. Lincoln watched him slide off without moving a muscle.
“Hope he’s lucky,” Lincoln said, his tone making clear he didn’t particularly care either way.
Cody vanished into the darkness below, into whatever the third level of the Endless Abyss held. Whether he used his escape rune or pushed on was his choice to make.
The remaining warriors didn’t wait. They scrambled for the cave entrance behind the platform, shouldering past each other, no one willing to be last. The first cave had already delivered beyond expectation. Whatever was in the second one, they weren’t going to miss it.
Zane now understood why Lavender had taken one look around the cave and left. Even a Divine-Class weapon that could last a million years would decay after hundreds of millions. These weren’t ancient treasures; they were piles of rust.
The weapons had been extraordinary once — at minimum second-grade divine tools, some first-grade, back when they were forged. That was why the warriors were still digging. Not because they expected to find something functional, but because even a first-grade divine tool reduced to scrap metal had a story behind it, and stories had value to the right collector.
But the other warriors, unwilling to leave empty-handed, didn’t seem to care. Maybe they feared they wouldn’t make it to the next cave; maybe they were just desperate. 
[SFX: METAL CLANGING AND SCRAPING]
They started digging through the heaps of metal, grabbing anything that looked halfway decent and stuffing it into their Storage Rings. Even if the weapons were useless scrap, maybe some collector back home would pay a few coins for a piece of history. After a moment, even Freya joined the search.
Zane, however, hung back. It wasn’t pride; it was a nagging doubt he couldn’t shake.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS MYSTERY]
Was this really it? Was this pile of scrap metal the great inheritance the Supreme Lord had left for them? It felt all wrong. The Supreme Lord had meticulously planned every detail of the Endless Abyss, scattering keys and clues across the entire universe. A being that powerful, that farsighted, would have known that any weapon, no matter how legendary, would turn to dust after hundreds of millions of years.
And yet, here the weapons were. 
As Zane watched the greedy scramble, a cold certainty settled in his stomach. Something wasn’t adding up. 
This wasn’t the answer. This was a distraction.
