Ep 1112 - The Pride of His Family

Word count: 1050

[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
The day Landry managed to grasp thirty percent of the Gods-Intimidating Strike, the entire palace had felt it. 
The explosive power he’d unleashed shook many of the resident leaders, and while those who were nearly on the level of World Lords weren’t exactly scared, they were definitely jealous. It wasn’t just the power that got under their skin; it was the fact that a warrior like Landry—someone from the Spirit Supreme Realm with no exceptional talent to speak of—was the one who’d managed it.
[MUSIC: TENSE BUILDING STRINGS]
Zane’s strike, on the other hand, was an almost perfect execution of the same technique.
By leaning on his own insights into Supreme Lord Sword’s intent, Zane had managed to draw out nearly ninety percent of its power. The force of it was staggering, almost unbearable.
[SFX: WHISTLING BLADE]
In response to his attack, a faint sliver of sword intent shot forward at an impossible speed. At first, it looked like nothing special. In fact, the moment it touched the endless silver-and-gold mountain, it simply vanished without a trace.
Zane stood perfectly still, his expression calm, but a flicker of confusion crossed his face. *What was that?*
For all the power he’d poured into it, the strike itself had seemed underwhelming. Had he misunderstood the technique after all?
No. Of course he hadn’t.
But something was missing. The Gods-Intimidating Strike demanded more than technique — it demanded a state of mind Zane hadn’t reached yet. His current grasp of emotionlessness was just that: a technique. A surface-level imitation of the real thing. 
He wasn’t too disappointed. Even at this level, the Gods-Intimidating Strike had one irreplaceable advantage: no buildup. Against an enemy, that alone made it formidable. Another trump card, even an imperfect one, was still a trump card.
What Zane didn’t know — what he couldn’t have known — was why someone like Yolande had once severed her own soul and split herself into a million clones. It wasn’t madness. It was method. 
Clones created that way carried incomplete emotions. They would reincarnate into the mortal world, live full lives, experience loss, joy, and death, and then be recalled — their memories absorbed. In this way, true emotional severance could be achieved from the inside out. It was a cultivation path unlike any other, and it would take her somewhere no one else could follow.
Zane quietly sheathed Thundergust and left the Illusionary Arena.
[SFX: DEEP RUMBLING GROAN]
The moment he was gone, the mountain groaned.
The colossal mountain wasn’t real, of course. It was an illusion formed from a powerful eighth-grade divine texture, reinforced by sixty thousand auxiliary ones, all modeled on the legendary Argent-Gold Demon Peak. 
If Zane tried to climb it, running at top speed from the base, it would take him a full year to reach the summit. 
And now, that mountain was groaning. 
[AMBIENT: DAWN AMBIENCE]
The rumbling continued for hours. Zane had entered the arena around midnight, and the sound didn’t stop until the sun began to rise.
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
The warriors of The Mist Palace were a dedicated bunch. For any cultivator, just getting the chance to enter was the kind of achievement that made you a legend back home. But once these so-called prodigies arrived, they quickly learned that their past glory meant nothing here.
[AMBIENT: BUSTLING TRAINING HALL]
Early that morning, a warrior named Gorman entered the Hall of Blades, his Maple-Leaf Spear resting on his shoulder. The pride of his family, his talent got him noticed by an eighth-grade sacred place, and from there, a tenth-grade one, where he’d even become the personal student of its lord. His master had pulled every string he could just to get Gorman into The Mist Palace.
[MUSIC: MELANCHOLIC STRINGS]
Gorman had arrived thinking his talent would make him a star. The reality had been a brutal wake-up call. He quickly began to feel small, insignificant. He’d been so proud of activating a lotus flower—a symbol of surpassing the heavens—only to find that countless warriors here could activate far more petals than he could. He was a nobody, not even qualified to participate in the Passing the Torch ceremony.
But a true talent knows when to swallow his pride. 
[MUSIC: DETERMINED RHYTHMIC PERCUSSION]
After a harsh adjustment period, Gorman had refocused, pouring all his energy into training against the simulated mountain. He’d come today to test a newly comprehended spear technique — something fierce and domineering that he was certain would leave a mark worth noting.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
[SFX: SHOCKED GASP]
Today, however, he walked into the Illusionary Arena and stopped dead. He stared in shock at a crack that stretched across the mountain’s face, a jagged line disappearing into the distance for thousands upon thousands of miles. He had no idea what could have possibly happened.
He flew toward it. Then down along it, searching for the end. He didn’t find one. He turned around and flew up — that took even longer — and still the crack stretched on beyond the limits of his sight. By the time he gave up, his shock had curdled into something closer to unease.
He had his own benchmark for impressive. A year ago, he’d happened to witness Jasper Reed — Cloudswallow Hall’s number one, ninth level of the Life-and-Death Realm at the time — casually drag his sword across the mountain and leave a mark a thousand miles long. A thousand miles, with a casual swing. Gorman had spent months trying to approach that.
Whatever had left this crack wasn’t in the same conversation.
[AMBIENT: QUIET CHAMBER]
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS]
Shaken, he left the arena and walked to a corner of the Hall of Blades where a tall, glowing screen was hanging. All attacks on the Argent-Gold Demon Peak were recorded and ranked there. Gorman didn’t know the exact formula it used, but it measured everything against the power of an early-stage Soul Realm warrior. It wasn’t a perfect system, but it was a reliable benchmark.
With a single spear strike, Gorman could hit a power level three times that of the baseline. It wasn’t a great score, but it was his.
[MUSIC: TENSE INVESTIGATIVE STRINGS]
Now, though, he just had to know who—or what—had left a mark like that.
