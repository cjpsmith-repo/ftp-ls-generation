Ep 1274 - The Ice Hall

Word count: 1048

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]
[AMBIENT: MOUNTAIN WIND]
Janet had a gift: she could always tell when someone was lying.
So, when she announced that Zane’s name was fake, all eyes snapped back to him.
Zane just smiled, not bothering to explain. It wasn’t something worth getting hung up on.
[SFX: CRYSTALLINE SHIMMER]
As they continued their climb up the mountain, an icy blue hall came into view. The massive structure seemed to be built entirely from blue crystals, shimmering with a cold, internal light. The closer they got, the more a refreshing chill washed over them, cutting through the mountain air.
Inside, the hall carried the same icy-blue color as the outside—floor, ceiling, walls, and pillars all cut from the same glassy stone, cool to the touch.
“This hall is beautiful,” Janet said, glancing up at the crystal overhead.
[SFX: BOOTS SCUFFING ON GRAVEL]
Coleman scuffed his boots on the ground, slowing to a stop and turning to Janet.
“So, how tough is this Third Hall of Causality?” he asked.
She stared at the shimmering hall, her expression grim.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
“Every hall is different, so it’s hard to say. But there are five in total, from the bottom of the mountain to the top. The last time I was here, our group had two Ace Talents. We cleared the first hall without losing anyone. Three people died in the second. Four more in the third. I died in the fourth.”
“That many died, even with two Ace Talents?” Ansel the ogre warrior said, his jaw hanging open. Among the eight of them now, there wasn’t a single one.
Even without a real fight, it was obvious Zane, Coleman, and Janet were the strongest in their group. Still, they all knew that even at their best, they were no match for an Ace Talent. Those guys were said to possess the greatest fortune in the universe, the kind of people who carried the fate of entire races on their shoulders. 
If a team with *two* of them couldn’t make it through, this place was far harder than any of them had imagined.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
That revelation sucked the air out of the group, and you could see the thought of turning back flicker across a few faces.
But Janet just offered a reassuring smile.“Don’t worry. Aren’t we doing better than last time? We’ll get through this as long as we all work together.” She put a little extra pressure on those last two words.
What she didn’t mention was that the two Ace Talents on her last trip had refused to cooperate. They were at each other’s throats the entire climb and never once bothered to save the others.
They hadn’t gotten far into the hall before the group came to a stop. Ahead of them, a deep ravine cut across their path—three thousand feet wide, streams of strange, glowing energy flowing endlessly along its bottom.
Ansel stretched his neck out over the edge to look down, then straightened up. “If we fall in, I’d bet we get sent straight back to the main city. How are we supposed to get across this?”
Under normal circumstances, distance wouldn’t matter—they could simply fly over a gap this size, or one a hundred times wider. But flying was forbidden inside the hall.
“We should be able to jump it,” Coleman said, eyeing the far side.
“Shouldn’t be a problem,” Zane agreed. Clearing three thousand feet with a running start wasn’t difficult on its own. But a hall like this wouldn’t set up an obstacle that simple. If they went in without thinking, jumping in blind, it would probably get someone killed.
Just like in the second hall, they pressed on toward the far end—only to find a wall of white light blocking the way forward.
“There’s something over here,” Janet said, pointing toward the corner of the hall.
Ten tall, icy-blue tablets stood there, each one sealed with a long hook and a coiled length of chain.
“Why are there so many hooks?” Ansel asked, frowning at them.
Zane walked over for a closer look, then reached out and struck the nearest tablet.
[SFX: STONE IMPACT]
Unlike everything else in the hall, the tablet turned out to be brittle—it shattered into fine, crystal-clear powder at his touch, and the hook inside dropped free.
He picked it up and gave the chain a hard pull. It didn’t budge. “Figures,” Zane said. “This part’s protected. Can’t break the chain.”
One by one, the others followed Zane’s lead, smashing their own tablets and pulling their own hooks free.
Ansel wasn’t satisfied with just one. He’d tried smashing the two leftover tablets—the ones meant for their dead teammates—but they wouldn’t even budge. They were completely indestructible.
It looked like each person could only get one hook. After everyone had taken theirs, they all turned to Janet again, waiting for a plan.
She just looked back at Zane with wide, innocent eyes and muttered, “I don’t know what to do next, either.”
A small smile played on Zane’s lips. 
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS CONTEMPLATIVE]
[AMBIENT: DEEP RAVINE ECHO]
He walked to the edge of the deep ravine and peered into the darkness on the other side.
It was a void, a blanket of black that swallowed all light. 
[SFX: FAINT MAGICAL GLOW]
But as Zane stared, his pupils began to glow with a faint green light, and the shadows across the chasm slowly started to resolve. He could just make out shapes moving in the murk.
“There’s something over there,” Zane said, his voice low.
“What is it?” Janet asked immediately. The silence that followed was thick with tension. With the rules of the hall still a mystery, one wrong move could get them all killed.
“I can’t see clearly,” Zane said, the light in his eyes glowing brighter. He leaned forward, focusing his vision, and then his expression twisted in alarm. 
[MUSIC: SUDDEN DANGER RISING]
“Watch out!” he yelled.
[SFX: HOOK WHISTLING THROUGH AIR]
The words had barely left his mouth when a hook shot out from the darkness across the ravine, flying straight toward them.
Everyone scrambled at Zane’s warning, but the hook was impossibly fast. 
[SFX: HOOK IMPACT FLESH]
It crossed the three-thousand-foot gap in an instant and snagged a human warrior named Cain.
The moment it sank into him, Cain went limp, completely helpless. 
[SFX: BODY DRAGGED ACROSS STONE]
He only had time for a single, choked scream for help before he was yanked off his feet and dragged into the darkness on the other side.
