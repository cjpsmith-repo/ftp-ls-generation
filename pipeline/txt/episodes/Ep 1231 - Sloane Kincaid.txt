Ep 1231 - Sloane Kincaid

Word count: 1098

[MUSIC: ETHEREAL MYSTERIOUS]
[AMBIENT: ANCIENT CITY WALLS]
The woman wore a purple silk robe with intricate embroidery, an ivory ribbon in her long, black hair fluttering behind her as she moved. She looked like she’d stepped right out of a painting.
She drifted along the edge of the city wall, her crescent-shaped shoes barely seeming to touch the ground. 
[SFX: ICE CRYSTALLIZING]
With each step, a platform of ice crystallized beneath her foot, holding her weight for a silent moment before shattering with a clear, musical chime as she moved on.
Zane watched her, his expression calm, but his body had already shifted into a ready stance. He knew better than to be fooled by appearances.
The official cultivation rankings were supposed to be the standard for a warrior’s strength, but here in the Upper World, that system was practically useless. A true powerhouse at the Soul Sea Realm could easily shred a standard warrior from the Spirit Supreme Realm—Zane had done it himself. Plenty of top-tier fighters from every race could pull off the same thing, which made the rankings little more than a suggestion.
[MUSIC: TENSE OMINOUS]
And every instinct he had told him this woman wasn’t a suggestion. She was a threat. The cold, sharp aura rolling off her felt immense, possibly stronger than anyone he’d faced since entering the Illusion Battlefield.
[SFX: CROWD MURMURING]
“The City Lord is here!” someone in the crowd murmured.
“That’s the lord of this city? She’s gorgeous. Looks like an angel.”
“What’s so strange about that? She’s from House Kincaid. They’re all supposed to look like that.”
On the city wall, even the most battle-hardened warriors stared, their usual scowls replaced with open admiration. She was just that captivating.
Zane felt a flicker of appreciation himself. In terms of sheer presence, the only person he knew who could match her was Lavender. But their energy was completely different.
Lavender carried herself with the untouchable grace of a goddess, but her every move promised violence. She was all sharp edges and coiled power, a dormant volcano with a fire burning just beneath an icy surface. 
This woman, on the other hand, was wrapped in an aura of calm and peace. Her chill wasn’t aggressive; it was like falling snow, something that made you want to quiet down, not fight.
But Zane could feel it, just under that frosty serenity: a sliver of killing intent. It was incredibly arrogant, the kind of intent that came from someone who believed they had the absolute right to take any life they chose.
And “any life,” he knew, included his.
“The one at the gate,” she asked, stopping a short distance from him, her voice as clear and cool as the falling snow. “Blue robes, black sword. You know the one.”
Zane raised an eyebrow. “You mean the guy who blew himself up rather than let me kill him?”
“Those points came to me,” she said, unbothered. “They were always mine to begin with.”
“How exactly does that work?” Zane asked. It was the part that had been bothering him since it happened.
She didn’t answer. Instead, her pale eyes stayed fixed on him. “You also killed Norton.”
Zane let out a short laugh. Two separate questions, when one would’ve done the job. “Yeah, I did. Let me guess — both of them yours?”
“You took half of Norton’s Illusion Points,” the woman continued, as though she hadn’t heard him. “I have to kill you three times to earn that back. After that, you’re free to leave.”
Zane understood then that talking to her wasn’t going to get him anywhere. It didn’t change what he was here for.
[SFX: SWORD DRAWN WITH METALLIC RING]
Zane’s sword, Thundergust, sang as he drew it. He held it level in front of his chest, his emotions carefully reined in.
“If you can beat me,” Zane said, “three times or thirty, doesn’t matter to me.”
The woman’s gaze dropped to the sword for a moment. She didn’t recognize it, but she could tell at a glance it was no ordinary blade. Something like surprise flickered across her calm features. “What’s your name?”
“Zane Jones.”
Something in her expression dimmed — a flicker of disappointment she didn’t bother hiding. There were more warriors with common surnames in the Illusion Battlefield than she could ever hope to search through by chance alone. This wasn’t the one she was looking for. Still, if the man she wanted had entered the Battlefield, he wouldn’t have stayed a nobody for long. She’d find him eventually.
“I’m not called City Lord,” she said. Her pale fingers opened slightly, and a ripple of light gathered around them.
When the light faded, five rings had appeared on her fingers — old, unadorned, strange against her refined bearing, joined together by a thin chain.
“My name is Sloane Kincaid,” she said. “Remember it. Now — die.”
[MUSIC: INTENSE RISING STRINGS]
[AMBIENT: HOWLING BLIZZARD]
As if summoned by that thought, the gentle snowfall thickened into a howling blizzard. 
[SFX: WIND SCREAMING]
The wind screamed, and through the heart of that swirling white chaos, Sloane Kincaid lunged.
One moment, she was an elegant lady strolling through the snow. 
[SFX: LIGHTNING CRACK]
The next, she was a bolt of lightning.
[SFX: RAPID FOOTSTEPS ON STONE]
In the blink of an eye, she closed the distance to less than ten feet.
Zane’s grip on Thundergust tightened. 
[SFX: SWORD DRAWN WITH METALLIC RING]
[SFX: WHOOSHING WIND]
[SFX: CRACKLING LIGHTNING]
He poured his Chaos Energy into the blade, and a twisting bolt of lightning shot toward her, crackling through the air. A razor-sharp wind blade followed right behind it, and in its wake, countless Nether God Shadows erupted into being, each one swarming Sloane with a different fighting style.
[AMBIENT: CITY WALL CROWD MURMURS]
On the city wall, the onlookers held their breath, their eyes glued to the fight. Most of them had either seen or personally experienced Sloane’s power, which was why they never dared to cross the city’s boundaries. They knew the only thing keeping them safe was the law she enforced.
“Something feels off,” one of the warriors whispered. “Is the City Lord going easy on him? She’s only supposed to kill him three times before he’s out.”
“She’s always a contradiction,” another replied. “Moody. I heard it’s because of the books she reads. The stories get to her.”
“Sometimes gentle, sometimes brutal. I gotta say, I find that kind of attractive.”
“Attractive?” his friend scoffed. “She’s a top talent from House Kincaid. You really think you have a shot?”
