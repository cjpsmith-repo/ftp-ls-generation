The upcoming fight was all anyone could talk about, spreading like wildfire through Misty Peak. Zane versus Tristan.
[AMBIENT: QUIET MEDITATION ROOM]
Zane, meanwhile, sat cross-legged on his bed, doing his best to tune out the gossip. He focused on his meditation, practicing the Psyche Spike. His showdown with Tristan was just days away, and backing out now wasn't an option. He'd look like a total coward.
'If I could only master this technique—transforming my essence into a piercing weapon as described in the manual—I might stand a genuine chance against him,' Zane contemplated, his fingers tracing the ancient characters on the page.
"Transform your soul into a spike," he whispered to the empty room, frustration edging his voice. "Yet how does one mold something without physical form?"
The soul existed beyond tangible reality—an ethereal reflection of one's self-perception. This explained why, during deep meditation, a person's spiritual essence naturally assumed their physical appearance without conscious direction. Zane's inner being mirrored his external self perfectly, from his determined eyes to his battle-scarred hands, a spiritual echo of flesh and bone.
This meant that as long as he saw himself as Zane, his soul would remain locked in that form. It couldn't become a spike, or anything else for that matter.
"The only way to change my soul's form is to completely forget myself," he reasoned.
[SFX: SPIRITUAL ENERGY SURGE]
As he wrestled with the paradox, an idea struck him. In that instant, a sharp, translucent gray spike shot out from between his eyebrows.
He'd done it. He'd finally transformed his soul into a weapon.
Because the spike was forged from his very soul, it was invisible to anyone but him. There was no way to defend against an attack you couldn't see.
Zane practiced for a while, getting a feel for the Psyche Spike. Its range was only about three feet—a far cry from the "three thousand feet" the adAriasd manual promised, where no one could defend against it.
Still, he'd cleared the first major hurdle: learning to forget himself. From here on out, things would get easier. A proud smile touched Zane's lips. The match in three days had been a weight on his shoulders, but now, he was actually starting to look forward to it.
[MUSIC: ANTICIPATORY BUILDUP]
The next three days flew by. Fights in the battle ring were always a huge deal at Misty Peak. It was a chance for the crowd to witness real combat, to feel an energy that regular training sessions just couldn't replicate.
Many pledges claimed they learned more from watching one good fight than from weeks of practice, using what they saw to break through their own plateaus. Besides, the daily grind was a bore. A good grudge match was pure entertainment.
But what made this match irresistible was the massive gap in power between the two fighters. One was at the Core Strength Level, the other a High-tier candidate.
If Tristan was a lion, Zane was a mouse.
They weren't even in the same league. Everyone expected it to be a quick, brutal, and humiliating victory for Tristan.
[AMBIENT: CROWD MURMURS]
[MUSIC: TENSE SHOWDOWN]
Tristan arrived at the battle ring early, flanked by his usual crew.
But today, a middle-aged man stood with them. His clothes were simple—a plain gray tunic and a bandana—but the emblem of the Eight Sigils embroidered on his sleeve made everyone take notice. No one dared show him the slightest disrespect.
That emblem belonged to the House Sterling, one of the Seven Ruling Houses of the Empire.
Even as a servant, a man representing the Sterlings was not someone any pledge at the Skyreach Sect would ever dream of crossing.
The servant leaned in and whispered to Tristan, "My master's orders are clear. That boy dies today."
Reputation was everything at Misty Peak. If Zane backed out, his would be ruined. The crowd was already getting restless.
"Looks like Zane's wussing out!" someone shouted. "What a joke. All that big talk and he gets cold feet. A guy like that has no future here!"
"Come on, let's go," another grumbled. "Nothing to see here. Might as well go train. Figures Misty Peak just got another loser."
[AMBIENT: RESTLESS CROWD DISPERSING]
Two hours dragged by. Most of the crowd had lost patience and started to drift away. The long wait had put Tristan on edge, his short fuse burning ever shorter.
"Tristan," Miles, the Sterling servant, demanded coolly. "You said the boy would be here. Where is he?"
Tristan's lips curled into a taut, artificial smile. "Rest assured, Miles. No one would have the audacity to leave me waiting."
Miles cast a contemptuous glance. "Ensure you're not squandering my valuable time. My master expects results, not excuses."
"I wouldn't dream of disappointing," Tristan replied, the words barely escaping his mouth when a flicker of movement caught his peripheral vision.
[AMBIENT: SUDDEN SILENCE]
[MUSIC: TENSE ANTICIPATION]
The crowd fell silent as Zane appeared at the entrance, his silhouette framed against the light. He moved with deliberate steps toward the ring, his face betraying no emotion. Something about his calm demeanor sent an unexpected chill down Tristan's spine. The confidence he'd cultivated all morning suddenly wavered as Zane's eyes locked with his across the distance—those weren't the eyes of someone who feared defeat. They were the eyes of a predator who had already calculated every move of the hunt.
As Zane approached the ring, the air itself seemed to grow heavy with tension. What had he been doing these past hours? And why did he now radiate such unsettling certainty?
