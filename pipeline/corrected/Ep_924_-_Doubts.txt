Ep 924 - Doubts

Word count: 1147

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

When they heard the name “Black Star,” it all finally clicked for the Demi-Immortals.

The Black Star wasn’t on any list of the Upper World’s most powerful weapons, mainly because it was just a pickax, not something you’d bring to a fight. But it was still a divine-class item, which meant that in the right situation, it was unbelievably useful. You could definitely call it a treasure, the kind of thing that would easily fetch a king’s ransom.

A scholarly-looking man named Delroy gave a slight nod. “That explains it. No wonder she broke my enchanted barrier. I was wondering how someone from the Lower World could have pulled that off. Turns out it wasn’t her own strength at all.”

He was proud of his skills, and for good reason. He’d earned a Reaper Title from the Tower of Illusion—the highest-ranked bronze title you could get. That alone was enough to make him supremely confident. As far as he was concerned, no warrior from the Lower World should have been able to shatter his barrier, which was woven with a Law of Causality to give it a kind of “relative defense.” Who from down here could understand how to wield a force like that?

The thing is, while Delroy was mostly right, he still underestimated the warriors of the Lower World. He was right that they didn’t understand the Law of Causality, but that didn’t mean they couldn’t use it. 

Years ago, a man named Maddox from the Heartlands had used his Nameless Saber to summon the Life and Death Wind, a powerful manifestation of that very law, pulling it straight from the River of the Netherworld. Maddox had no idea what he was doing; he just did it.

Zane’s situation was pretty much the same. He didn’t understand any of it, but he had used the power locked inside the Black Star to smash Delroy’s barrier to pieces.

“Enough talk,” the burly man in the group said, thinking it over. “We should just grab the Ancestral Dragon and get out of here. No, wait.” He trailed off. “It’s already hatched and thinks this woman is its mother. The imprinting can’t be undone, so there’s nothing we can do. I guess we head back to the Upper World and figure something else out.” 

To the Demi-Immortals, the figure cradling the Ancestral Dragon was a young woman — none of them knew it was Zane inside Brianna's body.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

But Vorn was sharper than the others. He caught the look on Dorothea’s face and knew something was up. “Dorothea, did you find something?” he asked. “What aren’t you telling us?”

Only then did Dorothea nod. “Yes. I made a few important discoveries.” She paused, then added with a small smile, “Very important discoveries, in fact.”

The way she emphasized the word “important” made everyone turn to face her, their curiosity piqued.

Before Dorothea could continue, the young man in black spoke up. “Dorothea found the whereabouts of the Tower of Crystal,” he said. “It's here — lost in this Lower World.”

[SFX: CROWD UPROAR]

The reaction was immediate.

“The Tower of Crystal? One of the three training towers?”

“It's been lost for years — are you serious?”

“Dorothea, is this true?”

They looked to Dorothea for confirmation. She nodded. “It's almost certain.”

“Vorn,” the burly man said, his earlier frustration giving way to something closer to excitement, “our luck on this trip isn't bad after all. The Evil Dragon gave us trouble, but the Tower of Crystal — that's beyond price.”

Delroy agreed. “The two towers we have are overcrowded as it is. A third would be an enormous find. Dorothea, how did you learn its whereabouts?”

Dorothea pointed at Zane. “From her.”

[SFX: SCYTHE HITTING GROUND]

The burly man drove his scythe into the earth and took two steps toward Zane, his voice dropping to something dangerous. “You. Tell me where the Tower of Crystal is. Now.”

Zane looked at him and said nothing.

The burly man took another step. “Kid, if you know what's good for you—“

“The Tower of Crystal,” Dorothea said, cutting him off, “is not the important thing I wanted to tell you.”

Silence.

Delroy stared at her. “Not important? What could be more important than a lost training tower?”

Dorothea pointed at Zane again. “She told me she entered the Tower of Crystal and took the Talent Tablet test. And while she was inside—“ she paused— “she saw my father's name on it.”

“Orson Garrison?” Vorn said.

Dorothea nodded. “His name could only appear in the first row. That can't be faked. Which means she was genuinely inside.”

The others exchanged glances. Among the seven of them, only Dorothea and the burly man had no title from the Talent Tablet. Dorothea was only on this mission because of her father's standing — and now his name was the key to everything.

“So what?” the burly man pressed. “What does it matter that she was inside?”

“It means,” Dorothea said, her voice steady, “that Zane has obtained the golden celestial rank.”

[SFX: GROUP LAUGHTER]

The moment the words left her mouth, a wave of laughter broke out among the other six men.

Delroy had the strongest reaction, his lips twisting into a condescending smirk. “Dorothea, have you lost your mind? Do you even hear yourself?”

Even Vorn just shook his head slightly. “Dorothea, you’re still too inexperienced. How could you be naive enough to believe something like that?”

“Ha! Dorothea, that’s ridiculous!” the burly man laughed. “You’re smarter than to fall for that kind of nonsense.”

They just couldn’t bring themselves to believe a word of it.

In truth, even Dorothea had her doubts. It was, after all, just a claim with nothing to back it up. But she was basing her trust on her own judgment, and she didn’t care what the others thought.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]

“Vorn, the Tower of Sin has been lost for a very long time,” she said, her tone firm. “From what I know, it was only open for a short while before it vanished, and no one ever managed to get a title from it. But this woman was the one who brought up the ‘golden celestial rank.’ If she hadn’t seen it with her own eyes, how would she even know that term exists? That can’t be a coincidence, can it?”

Delroy snorted. “What if she just picked up some information about our race somewhere? Or maybe someone she knew got a golden title?”

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC REVELATION]

“Someone else? No, that’s impossible,” Vorn said, shaking his head. He knew the numbers. There were a hundred thousand supreme worlds in the heavens, and the most talented prodigies from every single race had thrown themselves at the Demi-Immortal trials. In all of recorded history, only six living beings had ever obtained the golden celestial rank. Even if there were a million great worlds below, it was statistically impossible for anyone from this generation to have earned one.
