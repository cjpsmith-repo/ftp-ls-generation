Ep 418 - Thirty Percent Discount

Word count: 1072

[AMBIENT: QUIET DUSTY SHOP]

[MUSIC: MYSTICAL MYSTERY]

The shopkeeper wasn't exactly looking down on Zane—not in the cruel, dismissive way some merchants treated customers beneath their station. It was subtler than that, a judgment born out of decades of experience.

From where the old man stood behind his cluttered counter, everything about the kid felt wrong. The way he carried himself, the items he'd selected, even the uncertain hesitation in his voice when he'd first entered the shop—it all pointed to someone who didn't belong here, who didn't understand what he was getting himself into.

The old man had been selling raw materials his entire life. As a well-known figure at the World Merchants' Guild’s Cloudhaven Branch, he knew every top magic array and rune expert in the city. The most famous weaponsmiths and alchemists? He was on a first-name basis with most of them. He’d even befriended a few magic array masters from Cloud Hall, so he knew a thing or two about runes himself.

Magic arrays were built from runes, and learning to draw them was no simple feat.

Normally, young apprentices were taught in person by a mentor who provided all the necessary tools from the get-go. A beginner buying their own supplies was strange. And once they had some real practice under their belt, they’d need better runic quills anyway, not this starter junk.

It was obvious Zane was a total rookie, probably struck by a sudden whim to learn runes. With his limited knowledge, he likely thought it was just doodling with a fancy pen. The old man had seen it a hundred times: some kid gets a wild idea and decides they’re going to master a new skill overnight. In reality, very few ever stuck with it. To him, if they didn't have the discipline for the craft, the materials they bought would just end up collecting dust. That was exactly why he couldn't be bothered with Zane's request.

But Zane ignored his dismissive attitude and nodded seriously. "Yes, I want to learn runes. I want to start with some simple ones. Here's my list of materials."



He slid the list across the counter. The old man glanced at it and his expression curdled. "Four-star materials — to practice your brush?" He stood up, waving Zane toward the door. "Get out. I don't have time for jokes."

"You're open for business," Zane said. "I'm offering to pay."

"Not to you. Out."

He turned back to his heartstone. Zane had no idea what to do next — this was the biggest shop in the market, and the old man simply wasn't going to budge.

[SFX: MAGICAL WHOOSH]

Just then, the Cobalt Dragon’s voice echoed in his mind.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL TENSION]

Don't leave, Zane! I have a way to make him sell you the materials.

"Really? What's the plan?" Zane thought back, his eyes widening.

The Cobalt Dragon explained. See that stone in his hand? It's a piece of Ancient Phoenix Heartstone, and the text on it is in the Ancient Phoenix script. There's no way that old man can read it. If you tell him what it says, he might just change his tune.

"Great idea," Zane thought, rolling his eyes. "One small problem: I can't read those words either."

The Cobalt Dragon seemed to sense his frustration. I know you can't, Zane, he sent back calmly, but I can.

The dragon's lifespan was something Zane couldn't even begin to wrap his head around. He didn't just know the rare Ancient Phoenix language; there were precious few languages in existence that he didn't know.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]

A thrill shot through Zane. He knew he had to play this smart. He walked back to the counter, but the old man wouldn't even spare him a glance. 

The old man was completely absorbed in the intricate script carved into the heartstone. These artifacts were incredibly valuable to both historians and collectors, but they were almost impossible to appraise. The text only made it more precious. It was said that the heartstones of the Phoenix clan were passed down from the ancient Phoenix people themselves. If someone could decipher the characters, it could be a monumental breakthrough for their craft.

But deciphering them was another story. The old man had been studying this single piece for over a year and a half, cross-referencing it with every ancient tome in his personal collection, and had only managed to identify a few words.

When Zane approached again, the old man’s face tightened with irritation. He opened his mouth to scold the boy, but the words died on his lips. 

Taking a deep breath, Zane began reciting the words the dragon fed him. "Use the fire born from stars as a sacrifice in the Ashen Lake, praying for prosperity in the next life."

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC REVELATION]

His scowl melted into pure, slack-jawed astonishment the moment he heard Zane reciting the very words he had already managed to decode.

He was speechless, completely floored. His mouth hung open as he watched Zane’s lips move. Shaking himself out of his stupor, he shot to his feet, listening intently.

[SFX: DRAMATIC PAUSE]

But just as he leaned in, Zane stopped talking and offered a small, knowing smile. He had him. "I can tell you the rest," he said slowly. "But on one condition."

"What is it?" the old man asked, his voice tight with anticipation.

"I want to buy all the materials on my list," Zane said firmly, an innocent smile playing on his lips. "And I want a thirty percent discount."

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

The old man fell silent, his face a war of emotions. The materials Zane wanted weren't cheap. He was only a steward for the World Merchants' Guild; he didn't have the authority to cut a deal like that. Any discount would come directly out of his own pocket.

But his burning desire to understand the text was far stronger than his concern over the loss. He weighed his options for a split second before the thirst for knowledge won out. Gritting his teeth, he looked at Zane with a pained expression.

"Okay," he said, the word tasting like defeat. "You've got a deal."

He’d already done the math. The discounted price was only a little below his cost, and he was certain that knowing what was written on that stone would be worth far more than the few coins he’d have to pay to cover the difference.

But nothing could answer his main question: How the hell did this kid know how to read it?
