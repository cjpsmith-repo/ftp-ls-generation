Ep 1240 - Who Killed Me?

Word count: 1021

[MUSIC: DARK OMINOUS STRINGS]
[AMBIENT: DISTANT BATTLE SOUNDS]
The Illusion Battlefield was a meat grinder. It was dotted with countless sanctuaries, sure, but traveling between them was a long and treacherous journey, so most fighters just hunkered down in their local zones, hunting each other for points. Everyone had a strategy.
Sloane Kincaid’s was particularly effective. She’d seized control of a sanctuary and was essentially collecting Illusion Points like taxes. Every warrior in her city had to pay tribute, no exceptions. But holding a single city wouldn’t have been enough to get her rank as high as it was. 
Her real ace in the hole was a rare technique: One Soul, Two Bodies. Her duplicate, a woman named Laney, was off in another supreme world entirely, gathering points in Sloane’s name. Every point Laney earned was credited to Sloane’s total.
Before the Illusion Battlefield even opened, House Kincaid had debated whether to keep Sloane whole or let her split. Fused into one complete body, she’d have the strength to stand against top-tier family geniuses on equal footing. Split, she’d collect points twice as fast, but at a real cost to her raw power. In the end, the choice had been Sloane’s alone to make, and she’d chosen to split.
That was how Sloane had climbed to the seventh spot on the massive, shimmering pyramid of names. It was a staggering accomplishment, and all of House Kincaid was bursting with pride. After all, only two people from the Thoughtless Minds faction had ever broken into the top ten. One was Nigel, the former number one, and the other was Sloane. Now that Nigel’s rank had mysteriously plummeted, she was the only one left.
The head of House Kincaid was thrilled. He’d been crowing for days that the family finally had a worthy heir.
[SFX: DRAMATIC WHOOSH]
And then, Sloane’s name started to freefall. It dropped from seventh place all the way into the seven hundreds.
The Kincaid patriarch stared at the pyramid, his face turning to stone. 
“Someone just killed Sloane,” he said, his voice cold enough to freeze the air.
Unfortunately, those outside the Illusion Battlefield couldn’t see what was happening inside. All they could do was speculate. Who could possibly have the power to kill the star prodigy of House Kincaid?
“Whoever took down Sloane is no nobody,” one of the House Kincaid elders growled. “I want to know who had the nerve to provoke my niece.” The great houses were known for their pride; to them, Sloane was untouchable. An attack on her was an attack on them all.
[SFX: HAND SLAMMED ON TABLE]
“I told you we should have let them merge,” another elder fumed, slamming his hand on a table. “This One Soul, Two Bodies technique just leaves her vulnerable. Why bother with such a complicated method?”
The patriarch raised a hand, and the room fell silent at once — even the elders sat up straighter. “Sloane is already very good,” he said evenly. “Being killed once means nothing. She’ll climb again. For all we know, she wasn’t even killed — there are dangers scattered throughout the Illusion Battlefield that have nothing to do with an opponent. Those who fall in there can be revived. She has plenty of chances left.” 
Several elders nodded slowly. He wasn’t wrong — Nigel himself had led the rankings once, vanished entirely, and was already climbing back. Early standings meant little in the end. Sloane deserved the same patience.
[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSEFUL]
[AMBIENT: QUIET SANCTUARY]
Elsewhere, in a sanctuary of her own, snow fell around Laney — black instead of the white that always drifted around Sloane. 
Same face, same voice, but nothing else about her matched. 
Where Sloane collected tribute and played the long game, Laney simply killed. She didn’t check a warrior’s point total or weigh what she’d gain from a kill — anyone caught outside the city gates was cut down where they stood, no exceptions, no calculations. As the primary body, Sloane carried the strategy. As the secondary, Laney only had to carry the blade.
It worked, in its way — plenty of cultivators had died under that saber since the Battlefield opened. But plenty more had simply run, banding together and slipping past her all at once rather than risk staying. She couldn’t catch everyone. In the end, her total came in well short of Sloane’s steady tribute — brutal, but inefficient.
The warriors breathed a collective, silent sigh of relief as they watched Laney prepare to leave. The sooner that plague was gone, the better.
But just as she was about to depart, she noticed something that made her stop cold. Her own ranking was nosediving.
*Who killed me?*
[SFX: TELEPATHIC WHOOSH]
Laney closed her eyes, tapping into the telepathic link she shared with Sloane. A moment later, she had her answer.
*A warrior named Zane Jones.* The name echoed in her mind. *I can’t cross supreme worlds to hunt him down, not right now. But a guy with this much power? Our paths will cross eventually. It’s only a matter of time.*
As she stood there, a sharp, dangerous focus burned in her eyes.
[MUSIC: ETHEREAL RESURRECTION]
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL ENERGY HUM]
In yet another sanctuary, a green ball of light pulsed, slowly forming a human figure inside. It was Sloane, reviving just moments after Zane had killed her. 
[SFX: LIGHT SHATTERING]
A second later, the light shattered, and she stepped out. Her jaw was tight with frustration as she stared toward the sanctuary’s exit.
“Miss Kincaid!”
“Miss Kincaid!”
Norton and Keith had been waiting beside the revival point. When Norton had first lost to Zane, he couldn’t accept it. It felt impossible. 
[MUSIC: SOMBER REALIZATION]
But now, seeing that even Sloane had been defeated by the same guy, the massive gap in their strength finally slammed home.
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON STONE]
Sloane just gave them a slight, sharp nod and started walking toward the city gate without a word.
[AMBIENT: MURMURING CROWD ON WALL]
On the city wall, every warrior was staring down at Zane, their eyes wide with a mixture of shock and awe. This kid had actually done it. He’d killed Sloane Kincaid.
To these Soul Sea Realm fighters, who had long ago accepted their own limits, the idea was unthinkable. Sloane was a legend, supposedly unbeatable. No one in the entire sanctuary was her match, let alone capable of killing her.
And yet, Zane had.
