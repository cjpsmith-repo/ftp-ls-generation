Ep 920 - Question Time

Word count: 1025

[AMBIENT: QUIET CHAMBER]

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]

Dorothea looked at Zane, her expression laced with skepticism. “A tower?” 

For her Demi-Immortal Race, there were three towers dedicated to training, and a warrior earned recognition for the Demi-Immortal Race by passing the Talent Tablet test within one of them. The problem was that one of those towers had been lost ages ago. When Zane mentioned a tower out of the blue, it suddenly made his story feel a little less like a fabrication.

“What's it called?” she pressed, her brow furrowed.

“The Tower of Sin,” Zane replied, his gaze still glued to the Ancestral Dragon Egg. He was desperate for it to hatch. 

[SFX: CRACKING SOUND]

A spiderweb of new cracks was spreading across its surface, tracing intricate patterns over the upper half.

Dorothea shook her head. “The Tower of Sin? No, that's not right. That's not the name of the lost tower.”

A small smile played on Zane's lips. He knew he wasn't lying, which gave him the confidence to keep her talking. “It has fifteen floors,” he said, holding her gaze. “I took the Talent Tablet test on the tenth. I can still remember the names in the first row—Abigail Corbin, Orson Garrison, Cliff, and Fire.”

“Orson Garrison!”

[MUSIC: TENSE REVELATION]

The moment the name left his mouth, Dorothea’s smile vanished, her face hardening into a serious mask. “You mean the Tower of Crystal,” she stated, her tone leaving no room for argument. “Okay. I believe you've been there.”

“The Tower of Crystal,” Zane repeated, the name rolling around in his mind. It clicked. He remembered “Tower of Sin” was the name used by the people on the main continent. Of course, the Demi-Immortal Race would have their own name for one of their sacred training grounds. 

Its real name was the Tower of Crystal.

The reason for her shock, however, was a name Zane had just casually dropped: Orson Garrison.

Her full name was Dorothea Garrison. Orson Garrison was her father.

“Where is the Tower of Crystal?” Dorothea Garrison demanded.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

Zane's heart did a nervous little flip-flop. In his effort to stall for time, it seemed he’d stumbled into a minefield. The Tower of Sin was as vital to the main continent as the Violet Heart Sanctuary was to the humans. While the Heavenly Feather Sanctuary was thriving, the Violet Heart Sanctuary was still comparatively weak. If these people found the tower and seized it, it would be a complete catastrophe for humanity.

Dorothea watched his hesitation and smiled coldly. “The ascension passage of this world is sealed. The Tower of Crystal still has to be here somewhere. I don't need you to tell me — I can find it myself.” 

She turned the question back on him. “But let's talk about your talent. What row was your name on? Fourth? Fifth?”

Zane said nothing. Dorothea took his silence as confirmation.

“You said you earned a title,” Dorothea said, her eyes fixed on him. “So, what was it?”

“Actually,” Zane said, “my name wasn't in any of the rows.”

Dorothea's expression dimmed. “A name that didn't even make the first row, and you're calling yourself a Demi-Immortal? That's not how it works. Only warriors ranked in the first two rows qualify for recognition. Below that, your name gets recorded — but that's all. You're not a member.”

She paused, as if deciding whether to bother explaining further, then continued. “One of our own, Amber, stood in the second row. Within two hundred years, she became the leader of the Violet Heart Sanctuary and led it to suppress the Ogres' Sacred Land entirely. That's what the second row means. Warriors below that — however talented they might seem down here — simply don't qualify.”

Amber never broke through to the Soul Sea Realm — not because she lacked the talent, but because the ascension passage of this world was sealed. If it had been open, even among the Demon Night of the Upper Realm, she would have climbed far higher. 

That was the ceiling this world had placed on its greatest warriors. And Zane was claiming to sit above all of them.

She looked at him with something approaching pity. “If your name wasn't in the first row, I'd stop claiming membership if I were you.”

[SFX: CRACKING SOUND]

A new ring of cracks appeared around the middle of the egg. Zane kept his eyes on Dorothea.

“My name wasn't in any of the rows,” he said again. “It was above them.”

Dorothea stared at him. “Above them.”

“Above all of them. Above Abigail Corbin, above Orson Garrison — above every name on the tablet.”

Dorothea's composure cracked. Not the full collapse that came a moment later — just a hairline fracture, a flicker of something she couldn't quite suppress. If he was telling the truth, the implications were staggering.

For a moment, she said nothing. Then: “And your title?” She watched him carefully. “Gold, silver, or bronze?”

In the universe, any warrior who earned a title was considered absolutely favored — a being the heavens themselves had marked. The gap between titles was small, but it existed, and in a fight for your life, that gap could mean everything.

Zane blinked. He hadn't known there were other titles. He'd only ever seen one name above the rows.

“None of those,” Zane said. “If I remember right, it was the gold 'Celestial Paragon.'”

[SFX: MAGICAL SWIRL]

The instant he said it, the flower embedded in Dorothea Garrison’s right eye began to swirl. Its roots were buried deep within her socket, and watching it spin, Zane couldn't help but wonder if it was painful.

He had no idea that his words had just sent a seismic shock through her entire being.

[MUSIC: INTENSE SUSPENSE]

“How… how is that possible?” she whispered, her voice trembling with disbelief. “Celestial Paragon! A Gold Celestial Paragon!”

[AMBIENT: TENSE SILENCE]

She knew Zane had to have been to the Tower of Crystal. He'd seen the names on the Talent Tablet. But the only way he would have even known a title like “Celestial Paragon” existed was if he’d seen the words carved into the tablet himself, a distinction reserved for those who had actually earned it. 

She knew this for a fact. And that was precisely why she couldn't believe it.
