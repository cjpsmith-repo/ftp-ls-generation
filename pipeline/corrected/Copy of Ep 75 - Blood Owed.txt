Zane smiled to himself, taking in Leo's words. Max, on the other hand, looked completely lost. He was dying to ask what they were talking about, but he could tell this wasn't the time to interrupt. So he kept quiet.
"Once I'm back at the Skyreach Sect, I'm just gonna lay low and focus on my training," Leo said. "But hey, Zane, if you want to find me, come to Sunflower Ridge."
Sunflower Ridge was the fourth-ranked of the Sentinel Peaks at the Skyreach Sect. As one of the best peaks, it was where most of the kids from House Brandt went to study.
"So, Sunflower Ridge, huh?" Zane replied. "When I get back to the Sect, I'll definitely stop by."
Zane had never seen the point of sucking up to the Great Houses. He believed a man had to forge his own path to get anywhere in life—relying on someone else's power just wasn't his style. But after spending some time with the young heir of House Brandt, Zane found that he genuinely liked the guy. He could see them actually becoming friends.

Zane pulled Shane away from the main camp. "Come on. Let's get a drink."
In a tavern near the barracks, Shane worked through multiple cups of wine without a word. Zane let him drink. When the silence had stretched long enough, he raised his own cup.
"Shane — I'm sorry. The route wasn't changed to hurt your unit. Their real target was me. Your men died because of my enemies."
Shane's fist tightened around his cup. He stared at the table for a long moment. "Don't take that on yourself. The real villain is Randall. He broke military law and got my brothers killed. That debt is his — and I'll collect it myself."
He looked up at Zane. "Do you have a plan?"
"Yeah," Zane said. "I do. As long as I stay here, they'll come for me again. When they do — I'll finish it."

Shane didn't say a word on the way back from the main camp. He was grateful to Leo and Zane—they'd risked their own necks to save him—but a dark cloud hung over him, heavy with the deaths of his men. As a battle-hardened soldier, Shane had seen his share of death. He'd learned to grow numb to it over time. He hadn't even felt this hollow when he'd watched his own brother fall on the battlefield.
But this was different. This wasn't the chaos of war. He knew Randall had deliberately marched the Emerald Task Force into a death trap.
Zane made a silent vow: he would hunt down whoever was behind this, no matter the risk. He'd do it for Shane, for Rex, and for every other soldier who'd been sacrificed.

Over the next five days, Shane reorganized the Emerald Task Force and got back to their mission of hunting the Razor swarm. Imperial regulations required soldiers to turn in half of their collected Razorwing Cores, but the official count was whatever Shane reported.
As a thank you, he made sure nearly ninety percent of their haul went straight to Zane.
Shane and his men were pros when it came to hunting the Razor swarms, and with Zane and his Shattered Dagger in the mix, their hunts became brutally efficient.
The whole operation ran smoother, and their haul was bigger than ever.
All told, those five days of hunting netted Zane a massive haul. Adding to what he already had, he now sat on about four thousand regular Razorwing cores and over two hundred from their commanders—worth five hundred Points total.
Once he threw in the Matriarch's Core, he was looking at a grand total of one thousand five hundred Points.
For a recruit who'd been at the Skyreach Sect less than a month, one thousand five hundred Points earned purely through hustle was insane. If the other new guys found out, they'd probably cry themselves to sleep over how unfair life was.

Early on the sixth day, Zane was getting ready to hunt with the Emerald Task Force. They headed deep into the jungle and had already taken down two Razorwing swarms. As the soldiers were harvesting the crystal cores, Shane walked over to Zane. "You said as long as you were here, they'd make another move on you. It's been a few days, and... nothing. What gives?"
True to his word, Shane hadn't reported Randall, trusting Zane's promise of revenge. He knew storming into the main camp with an accusation and no proof would just blow back on him. The higher-ups would never take the word of a task force captain over an officer like Randall.
Zane had told him to be patient, that they'd make a move eventually. But days had passed with no sign of anyone coming after him, and Shane was getting antsy.
"Guess they have more patience than I gave them credit for," Zane said, prying the Alpha's Core from a Razorwing leader and tucking it into his Storage Ring. "Don't worry. We'll just keep hunting. We can wait them out."

A moment later, three men emerged from the jungle, with Liam from Misty Peak in the lead. Behind him stood two others, one dark-skinned, one fair-skinned. Zane recognized them from the yard back at the military camp; he'd been impressed with their weapon skills even then.
At the sight of the newcomers, the men of the Emerald Task Force gripped their weapons, instinctively moving into a defensive formation.
But Zane held up a hand, stopping Shane. "Stay out of this," he said, his voice low and quick. "They're here for me. This is my fight."
Zane stepped forward, bracing himself. The Emerald Task Force had already lost too many men on his account. He refused to let them get dragged into his mess again.
Stryker and Graves let out a pair of unsettling chuckles. "Oh, you're not the only one dying today," Stryker sneered. "The whole Emerald Task Force is going down with you."
"I'd advise you to kneel and end your own life," Graves taunted with a malicious grin. "Once you're in our grasp, death will come slowly, accompanied by your screams."

The menacing promise lingered heavily as Graves and Stryker unleashed their Life Force, which erupted outward in a violent surge. The swirling Eclipse Haze materialized between them, then transformed into twin massive pythons that lunged across the terrain toward Zane with frightening speed.
"They've manifested their Life Force," Zane realized, twisting his body with precise timing to evade the simultaneous attacks.
Though Zane trusted his physique—now refined into a living Soul Armament—he recognized the need for caution. Never before had he confronted cultivators at the Nature Level who could release their Life Force externally, and the stakes had doubled with two such opponents facing him simultaneously.
The true test of his transformation was about to begin, and there would be no second chances.
