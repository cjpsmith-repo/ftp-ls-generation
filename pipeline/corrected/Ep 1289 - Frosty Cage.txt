Ep 1289 - Frosty Cage

Word count: 979

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS ETHEREAL]
[AMBIENT: BUBBLING LAVA SEA]
The flame kittens weren’t very big, but you had to figure that, as Law Creatures born in a sea of raging fire, they were terrifyingly powerful. Why else would the lava giants eat them? 
Zane was on board with the plan to use one to attract a lava giant’s attention. Actually getting his hands on one, though—that clearly wasn’t going to be a walk in the park.
Not far off, the surface of the fire-sea rippled. 
[SFX: LAVA BUBBLE POPPING]
[SFX: SOFT KITTEN PURR]
A bubble of molten lava swelled and popped, and a tiny flame kitten tumbled out, letting loose an adorable, soft purr. Zane and the others could only stare, wide-eyed. They had to catch one of those things, but this was the Fourth Hall of Causality. Nothing was ever easy here.
“I can try,” Janet said, her cool gaze fixed on the kitten. “But no promises.”
[SFX: WOOD MATERIALIZING]
With a graceful flick of her wrist, a piece of dark wood appeared in her palm. Though she was a master of the Wood Law, she was also skilled with ice. 
[SFX: ICE CRACKLING ON WOOD]
The wood began to twist in a hypnotic dance as a layer of frost bloomed across its branches. When she’d entered the Illusion Battlefield, she had left the Divine Weapons from her clan, House Kincaid, behind. It wasn’t that they didn’t have any; she just didn’t need them.
[SFX: MYSTICAL RESONANCE]
This piece of wood was her true weapon. It was called the Lofty Wood, a treasure said to be on par with any Divine Weapon. Legend claimed the universe itself had created the tree it came from, giving it a divine destiny to reach for the heavens. This very piece had been inside Janet’s body since birth—a sacred treasure so rare that her family had once considered removing it. Now, it was a part of her.
House Kincaid had once attempted to draw the Lofty Wood out of her—only to find it wouldn’t budge. It had rooted itself to her, will and all, and refused to let go. 
When they pushed the extraction anyway, the sky answered: a twelve-colored dragon of lightning tore across the heavens and shattered the family’s entire mourning hall to splinters. After that, House Kincaid never tried again. To this day, no one—not even Janet—knew why the wood had chosen her, or why the heavens had answered so violently on her behalf.
“Using ice against fire, I think I can trap one of these things,” Janet said, studying the kitten. “But someone has to lure it close enough first.”
All eyes turned toward the mouth of the Hall—where Ansel, who’d been hiding just outside since his last run-in with one of these things, had finally crept back in.
“Me? Absolutely not.” Ansel’s whole head shook back and forth. Then he caught the looks the others were giving him, and his shoulders slumped. “Fine. Fine! If no one else will, I’ll—”
“I’ve got it,” Zane said, stepping forward before Ansel could finish. His body gave him an edge none of the others had—if anyone could take the heat of an angry flame kitten, it was him. Zane was the one holding the group together at this point, and nobody argued.
He walked to the edge of the lake and, almost lazily, flicked a piece of gravel up off the ground and into his hand.
[SFX: LAVA BUBBLE POPPING]
Another bubble burst nearby, and a second kitten floated up, blinking at nothing in particular.
[SFX: GRAVEL THROW]
Zane flicked the gravel at it. The stone melted before it even made contact—but the kitten noticed anyway, its flat little eyes drifting toward him without much interest.
Everyone held their breath.
The kitten looked him over, unimpressed, and slipped back beneath the magma without so much as a flicker of temper.
Zane blinked. Ansel, watching from a safe distance, looked like he’d seen a ghost all over again—if anything, more than the first time. How was that fair? The same kind of creature that had chased him halfway out of the Hall couldn’t be bothered to so much as hiss at Zane. Whatever these little things were, they clearly had personalities of their own—and tempers to match.
No matter. There were plenty more where that one came from.
Even though Zane wasn’t an ice specialist, he’d trained all his Original Laws at the same time. 
[MUSIC: TENSE RISING]
[SFX: ICE CRACKLING]
The ice awl he formed, powered by his Chaos Energy, was incredibly potent. But the little flame kitten didn’t even flinch. It just barreled straight toward the three icicles, its seemingly soft body radiating intense heat. 
[SFX: INTENSE HEAT WAVE]
[SFX: SIZZLING STEAM]
Before they could even make contact, the icicles sizzled into wisps of steam and vanished into the dry air.
The flame kitten, it turned out, had a nasty temper. After incinerating the attack, it didn’t slow down one bit. 
[SFX: FLAMES ROARING]
[SFX: ANGRY CAT MEOW]
The flames covering its body flared as it locked onto Zane, the one who’d attacked it, and charged with an angry “Meow!”
“Fall back!”
This was all part of the plan, so Zane kept his cool and began a strategic retreat. The kitten, however, was getting bigger with every second. 
[SFX: CREATURE GROWING]
[SFX: FLAMES INTENSIFYING]
The tiny thing was suddenly the size of a flame leopard.
Janet raised an eyebrow at the sight and took a step back. 
[SFX: ICE CRACKLING AND EXPANDING]
She flicked a finger, and the Frosty Cage she’d placed on the ground began to shake and expand. The cage she’d originally woven from the Lofty Wood was now far too small, and she had to resize it on the fly to account for the kitten’s sudden growth spurt.
[SFX: FIERCE FELINE SHRIEK]
“Meow!” the fiery beast shrieked, viciously chasing Zane.
[SFX: RAPID WHOOSHING MOVEMENT]
The two of them moved like a blur, covering a hundred feet in a split second. Zane shot past the Frosty Cage Janet had made.
“Now!”
Janet’s expression turned serious. She spread her fingers, her mind connecting with the Lofty Wood. 
[SFX: ICE CAGE SPRINGING OPEN]
The Frosty Cage sprang open, shooting straight for the flame creature hot on Zane’s heels.
