Ep 1317 - Falling Apart

Word count: 1042

[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSEFUL]
[AMBIENT: DESOLATE BATTLEFIELD]
Slade’s mind raced, his glare fixed on Zane.
Everything was falling apart.
According to his clan’s predictions, the first stage of the Illusion Battlefield was supposed to be wrapping up. It was a simple weeding-out process, designed to filter out the majority of warriors. The intel suggested only about fifty people from each of the fifty supreme worlds would make it through—a total of two thousand five hundred fighters.
Out of trillions of warriors, those two thousand five hundred represented the absolute elite of the Soul Sea Realm - children of World Lords and Supreme Lords, untouchable Ace Talents, genius-level saints. The predictions, drawn from fragmented ancient records, weren’t perfect but proved reliable enough. Slade had known about the cities and central hall before ever entering this place.
Slade had hunted relentlessly, climbing to fourth place - high enough to guarantee advancement. His plan was simple: hide in the city and wait.
But that plan had gone completely off the rails when the rules of the Illusion Battlefield suddenly changed. No one, not even his own clan, had seen this coming. The rule that banned killing inside the cities was just… gone. Maybe it was never really a rule, or maybe their ancient records were missing a page. Either way, he was seriously regretting his choices. If it weren’t for Hailey, he never would have picked this fight with Zane in the first place.
And now his Saint-Killing Spear was in the man’s hands. How was he supposed to fight him?
[SFX: WEAPON MATERIALIZING]
[SFX: METAL RINGING]
A flash of crimson light burst from Slade’s Storage Ring. Backed into a corner, he pulled out his last resort: the Crane Crying Spear. 
It was a rare, low-grade Divine Weapon, but it was nothing compared to the spear Zane now held. He knew he couldn’t win, but the pride of an Ace Talent wouldn’t let him just roll over and die. There was still a sliver of hope, a tiny chance he could pull this off if he gave it everything he had.
“Dance of the Blood Crane!” he roared, thrusting his spear forward.
[SFX: ENERGY BEAMS SHOOTING]
Beams of bloody light shot straight for Zane.
Zane, however, remained perfectly calm. He activated the Eight Smoky Melodies, all that time sparring with the Supreme Lord Sword, having honed his skills to a razor’s edge.
Rather than draw the Thundergust, he raised the Saint-Killing Spear itself in his free hand and pointed it at Slade. He’d never trained with a spear in his life, but at his level, that hardly mattered—grass, branches, anything could become a weapon in the right hands.
The moment he took hold of it properly, he felt something looking back. A will, buried deep in the weapon, pressed against his mind—starving, desperate, and utterly fixated on him. The spear was alive, and it had been waiting a long time to be fed.
He turned the thought over as he dodged Slade’s next strike without effort. This spear had belonged to the Queens of Demon Night. Whatever powered it wasn’t only Vitality—there was faith bound up in it too, and he had none to offer. So he offered the only other thing he had: a thread of Chaos Energy, drawn up from deep in his own reserves and poured straight into the weapon.
[SFX: CRACKLING ENERGY]
The spear answered with a sound like popping embers, and just as quickly, swallowed everything he’d given it. Still hungry. Zane frowned, unbothered, and kept pouring—his own reserves ran deep enough that emptying them wasn’t a real concern.
A few feet away, Slade caught the sound and faltered mid-strike, staring at the spear like it had grown a second head. “What’s going on with that thing?”
Zane didn’t answer. When the hunger in the weapon finally eased, he gave it a light shake and thrust it forward.
A spear-shadow took shape at the tip—vast, and growing—dwarfing anything Slade had ever managed to summon from the same weapon, on Zane’s very first try with it.
Slade’s face went white. “That’s—that’s impossible!” He knew exactly how this spear worked: the size of that shadow scaled directly with how much energy was poured into it. He could have emptied every last drop of his own reserves into the weapon and never come close to matching it.
“Nothing’s impossible,” Zane said quietly, and drove the spear forward.
[SFX: DARK ENERGY SURGE]
The shadow of his spear bloomed outward, an overwhelming torrent of darkness. The moment Slade saw it, he knew. There was no blocking that.
Run. It was the only thought left in his head.
But there was nowhere to go. Zane wasn’t about to let him get away.
[SFX: ENGULFING DARKNESS]
In the end, Slade had no choice but to let the spear’s shadow swallow him whole.
[SFX: SOUL LIGHT WHOOSH]
A red light, the vessel of Slade’s soul, shot back toward the city. The rules had changed, but revivals still happened in one central place.
[MUSIC: DARK OMINOUS STRINGS]
Zane watched it go, a flicker of annoyance in his eyes. Still no Illusion Points from that, he thought. Slade has three Exemption Medallions. I’ll have to kill him four times just to get his points.
[SFX: RAPID FOOTSTEPS ON RUBBLE]
He followed the red dot, charging straight into the city, ready to kill Slade the second he rematerialized.
[AMBIENT: CHAOTIC BATTLE]
Inside, the fighting had escalated into pure chaos. It seemed like everyone was locked in a desperate battle—the powerful warriors just back from the hall, the other Ace Talents, even the regular fighters were all at each other’s throats. The city, once a sanctuary protected by Celestial Law, had been reduced to a pile of rubble in less than five minutes.
[SFX: MASSIVE EXPLOSIONS]
[SFX: BUILDINGS COLLAPSING]
Explosions rocked the ground as buildings crumbled into dust.
[SFX: ENERGY BLASTS WHOOSHING]
Blasts of Law Powers crisscrossed the air, and if you weren’t careful, you could get hit by a stray shot and die without ever seeing your attacker.
[SFX: MAGICAL RESURRECTION HUMS]
On the other side of the city, glowing orbs of light began to appear, marking the spots where fallen warriors were about to be resurrected. And next to each orb, a pack of fighters waited like vultures. They were there for their defeated enemies, ready to kill them all over again.
This was the grim fate of the weak: an endless cycle of death and resurrection, their Illusion Points drained from them until there was nothing left.
