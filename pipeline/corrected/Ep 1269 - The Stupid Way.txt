Ep 1269 - The Stupid Way

Word count: 1236

[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSEFUL]
[AMBIENT: ANCIENT STONE CHAMBER]
[SFX: HEAVY STONES ROLLING]
Just over thirty seconds later, the pillars of light winked out, and in the sudden quiet, the massive stones began to roll again.
Now that everyone understood the rhythm of the trap, a little of the tension drained from the room. The stones were predictable, at least, sticking to the grid lines etched into the floor. All they had to do was stay off the active paths, and they’d be safe when the light show started again.
[SFX: STONES SCATTERING CHAOTICALLY]
The stones scattered, no longer moving in a predictable line but rumbling in every direction across the hall. The eight surviving warriors scrambled with them, a desperate dance from one safe square to another.
“Here! This spot’s clear!”
“No, don’t! A huge one’s coming this way—it’ll be blocked in a second!”
“Over here, move!”
Once they found new footholds, they locked in place, bracing for the next phase. Before, when the stones were aligned, there had been a single, continuous safe row. Now, with the boulders strewn about, their safety was fractured into isolated islands.
[SFX: MASSIVE STONE RUMBLING]
As the stones rumbled past, Zane didn’t rush to find a safe spot like the others. 
Instead, he darted between the moving monoliths, his gaze sweeping over each one as it passed. Grimacing faces had appeared on the boulders ever since the short warrior from The Hallowed Concord had attacked one, and Zane was sure they were the key.
“It has to be the faces,” he muttered, his eyes scanning the chaotic field.
[SFX: HEAVY BOULDER IMPACT]
[MUSIC: DISCOVERY REVELATION]
The instant the boulders ground to a halt, Zane’s eyes flashed with discovery. *There.*
“Is that one… different?”
It was almost imperceptible, but the grimace on a nearby boulder wasn’t black like the others—it was a dark, dried-blood red. 
[SFX: STONE GRINDING ON STONE]
But just as Zane moved to get a closer look, the boulder shifted, rolling over and pressing the red face flat against the stone floor. There was no way he could lift it now.
[SFX: DEEP MECHANICAL HUM]
A low hum filled the air, and a moment later, light shot out from all four sides of every massive stone. 
[SFX: ENERGY BEAMS CRACKLING]
The forty-three stones unleashed a blinding, crisscrossing web of one hundred and seventy-two beams. Zane had no choice but to give up his investigation and leap to a nearby safe square, his mind racing. He needed a trap, something like a divine texture. He knew exactly what to design, but carving one himself right now was out of the question.
[SFX: MULTIPLE STONES ROLLING]
The beams held for about ten breaths before finally dimming and dying out, and the boulders began rolling once more. Zane’s eyes stayed fixed on the one that mattered — and as it rolled, it flipped, turning the dark red face straight down into the floor.
“Gone again,” he muttered, jaw tight. He’d finally caught a real clue, and now it had slipped away from him a second time.
[SFX: HEAVY STONES SETTLING INTO FORMATION]
The stones drifted back toward one side of the hall, clearly falling into formation again. “We’re missing a piece here!” one of the Demon Night warriors called out, and everyone abandoned their scattered footing to converge on the single safe line left once the boulders locked into their row.
“Did you find anything?” Janet asked, watching Zane’s expression.
“At the bottom of one of them,” he said. “There’s a face down there — darker than the rest. Dried-blood red instead of black.”
Janet’s eyes lit up. “Then we just need to find that one and smash it!” She’d seen something like this before, she explained — a different hall, a different trial, but the same basic shape: thirty-three stone pillars, and somewhere among them a single dragon carving to find and shatter.
Zane shook his head. “It’s not that simple. That red face isn’t just hiding by accident — it keeps changing position every time the stones move. Even if we find it, it’ll be buried again before we can touch it.”
Janet’s nose wrinkled as she turned that over in silence.
“Whatever the case,” the Demon Night warrior said, “let’s at least find the thing first.”
Everyone nodded.
As they grew more comfortable with the boulders’ rhythm, the group stopped retreating to safe squares between rounds and instead pressed closer to the stones themselves, hunting.
“There! This one — it’s here, I can see it!” the Demon Night warrior shouted, triumphant for a moment. “But it’s already being crushed under—”
[SFX: ENERGY BEAMS CRACKLING]
Before anyone could reach it, the beams fired again, and Zane was forced back before he could confirm the sighting with his own eyes. Still, it was enough. The red face was real, and it was exactly where he’d guessed. But the math of the problem was staggering: forty-three boulders, six faces apiece — two hundred and fifty-eight ghost faces in all, and only one of them mattered.
When the boulders lined up again and the group gathered, no one had an answer.
“There’s a stupid way to do this,” Zane finally said.
“Let’s hear it,” the Demon Night warrior said. By now, no one questioned him outright, so long as it didn’t put their lives on the line.
“Whoever here can rig a trap — now’s the time,” Zane said.
The short warrior tipped back the brim of his round hat with a faint smile. “I can.” He waved a hand, and three finger-thick purple branches appeared, planting themselves upright on the floor as he let them fall.
“Won’t work,” Zane said, shaking his head before the man could even explain.
“This is Amethyst Thunderoak. When it detonates, the force—”
Zane wasn’t listening. There wasn’t time for a lecture, and the branches would be flattened the instant a boulder rolled over them anyway.
While some of the warriors were still just trying to survive, Janet and the short warrior caught on to what Zane was planning. 
[SFX: HEAVY STONE ROLLING]
As a boulder rolled, the side with the dark red grimace would eventually have to face down. They could pepper the entire floor with traps, and as long as the key face landed on one of them, they could trigger them all at once.
It was a brute-force approach, maybe even a stupid one, but it was their only shot.
“I can do that,” Janet said with a thin smile. “I’ll summon the Lofty Wood.”
[SFX: SHIMMERING MAGICAL MATERIALIZATION]
She flicked her fingers, and a faint, shimmering texture materialized on the squares in front of and behind her. 
[SFX: THORNS ERUPTING FROM STONE]
A silent command, and razor-sharp thorns erupted from the pattern on the floor—one of her signature skills.
“I can help, too,” the short warrior grunted. 
[SFX: HANDS CLAPPING TOGETHER]
He clapped his hands together, and a crackle of thunder shot from his palm, striking the ground beside him. 
[SFX: LIGHTNING CRACKLING INTO STONE]
A lattice of raw lightning burned itself into the stone square.
Since they had no idea where the marked boulder would land, they’d have to cover every single tile in the hall. It would be a slow, exhausting process. 
But with no better ideas, they got to work. As they dodged the rolling stones, the two of them began laying their traps, one square at a time.
