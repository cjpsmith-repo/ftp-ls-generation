Ep 658 - The Red Dot

Word count: 1090

[AMBIENT: OPPRESSIVE FOG]

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

"I—I can't take this anymore!"

The silence of their trek was shattered as Garrick screamed, his voice raw with panic. The oppressive fog had everyone on edge, a crushing weight that was grinding them down. For Garrick and some of the others, it was just too much; they were on the verge of breaking completely.

Fabian, Jarrod, and Esther, however, remained calm. They had passed through the Gate of Many Lives, a trial that had forged their spirits into something stronger. The others hadn't, and without that tempering, their resolve was brittle. Lacking any confidence in what lay ahead, their terror was a palpable thing in the mist.

"If we stop, we die here," Malcolm said, his voice flat. He was in no mood for a breakdown.

"We're never getting out!" Garrick shrieked back, his face a mask of wild desperation. "We're going to die in this godforsaken place!"

Malcolm rounded on him, raising his fists. "Keep talking like that, and I'll send you to Hell myself."

The murderous look in Malcolm’s eyes was enough to make Garrick’s mouth snap shut. In a situation this grim, that kind of talk was poison, a surefire way to push everyone over the edge.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]

[SFX: EERIE WIND]

Just then, one of the Demon Night’s top War Lords pointed ahead into the gloom. "Look! What is that?"

Everyone’s head snapped in the direction he was pointing.

[AMBIENT: DISTANT HUMMING]

There, suspended in the distance, was a single red dot of light. It hung in the thick haze like a bloody sun rising through morning mist.

The fog was so dense they couldn't see more than a thousand feet in any direction, so how was this light cutting through so clearly? It was impossible, yet they could all see it perfectly.

[SFX: MAGICAL SHIMMER]

Zane tilted his head back, a bright, jade-green light flickering in his eyes. Even his Magic Vision couldn't pierce the strange fog, but as he focused, the red dot sharpened slightly.

"That light… it reminds me of the Dark Nether Death Light, don't you think?" Zane murmured.

Esther nodded grimly. "You're right. All the death light in this cave is likely radiating from that red dot."

A wave of ice-cold terror washed over Fabian. "That means we haven't been getting away from the source at all," he stammered. "We've been walking straight toward the core."

[MUSIC: DARK SUSPENSE]

A collective chill passed through the group. They had no idea what lay at the core, but it was painfully obvious it had the power to kill them all effortlessly.

"Then we just move away from it!" Malcolm said, his eyes lighting up with a flicker of hope. "We have the red dot as a reference point now. We just walk in the opposite direction until we're out. It's better than wandering blind, right?"

Everyone seized on it. The moment they had a direction — any direction — their pace quickened, a surge of desperate energy pushing them forward.

Zane let the others go ahead. He already knew what the Crystal Butterfly was telling him. The ring was getting hotter, not cooler. But he said nothing. They needed to find out for themselves.

Sure enough, it wasn't long before Garrick let out a strangled cry and pointed upward. "That — that red dot — it's getting bigger!"

Everyone looked up. The distant point of light had swelled from a dim speck to the size of a fist.

"We're walking straight toward it," one of the Twilight Group War Lords said, his voice shaking. "We were walking away. How is it getting closer?"

Esther bit her lip. She had no answer.

"There's only one explanation," Malcolm said, his voice tight with forced control. "There's an invisible force in the fog dragging us toward the core. We don't get to choose the direction. It's choosing for us."

A cold, faint smirk touched Yolande's lips, an expression so typical of her it was infuriating.

Malcolm snapped. "Is there another way or not? Just spit it out!"

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

Yolande seemed completely unbothered by his growing anger. "We can't walk out of the fog," she said.

"Is there a way to break it?" Fabian and Esther asked almost simultaneously.

"There is," Yolande said. "But you can't do it."

Esther frowned. "What do you mean?"

"This fog is actually an array," Yolande explained slowly. "It contains the power of Spatial Law. To walk out of it, you need to be proficient in Spatial Law above the third level."

"Third level," Jarrod said, with a short, bitter laugh.

There were two people in the group with any grasp of Spatial Law: Zane and Jarrod. Zane was halfway through the second level — already an exceptional pace for his rank. Jarrod hadn't finished the first. Neither of them was anywhere close to the third. And there was no question of simply sitting down to cultivate; by the time either of them reached that level, the fog would have long since killed them all.

"So where is it?" someone asked. "The array plate — where do we find it?"

She shook her head. "I've never been to the Dark Nether Cave before. This is my first time. But my guess is that the plate is somewhere in the core."

"Okay, wait," Malcolm said, staring at her with suspicion. "If you've never been here, how do you know all this?"

Yolande blinked, then dropped her gaze to the fog-swirled ground. "I'm not telling you."

Malcolm forced his hands to unclench, pushing down his rage with a deep, shuddering sigh. "Fine. We keep moving. If we can't find a way out, we head for the core. Who knows? If we get lucky, we might even find some rare treasure before we die."

[AMBIENT: OMINOUS WIND]

[MUSIC: SOMBER ORCHESTRAL]

No one argued. At this point, they had no other choice. A grim resignation settled over the group, and with it, a strange sense of calm. They were ready to face whatever came next. If they were going to die here, they might as well go down fighting.

[SFX: PULSING ENERGY]

[MUSIC: RISING DANGER]

As they pressed on, the red dot in the sky swelled, growing from a distant point to the size of a dinner plate. They could feel the power of the Dark Nether Death Light intensifying with every step, a silent, deadly radiation that sliced right through the fog. This was it—a life-or-death test.

All that mattered now was surviving the light.
