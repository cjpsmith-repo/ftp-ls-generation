Ep 965 - Mysterious Power
Word count: 957
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL WIND]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]
In the Violet Core Realm, Cora had barely been there an hour — and already, the sky was falling.
It was always a headache when the swords weren’t returned promptly. But this was different. They were being pulled down from the Southern Sky Region en masse, and the sheer scale of it caught the purple-robed woman’s attention.
Could someone be practicing the Colorful Sword Array? she wondered. It was a special technique from the Violet Core Realm, one that required a warrior to command eleven thousand colossal swords at once.
[SFX: RUMBLING CRESCENDO]
But that thought was shattered in the next moment. The number of swords plummeting from the sky just kept multiplying.
[SFX: METALLIC RAIN INTENSIFYING]
At first, only one or two swords rained down. Then dozens. Then hundreds, thousands, and soon, tens of thousands.
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC ORCHESTRAL SWELL]
There had to be over a hundred thousand of them now. The sea of great swords that usually filled the sky of the Southern Sky Region was visibly shrinking.
[SFX: SHARP GASP]
“Why are so many coming down? Is someone eating them?” The woman’s expression soured.
Sure, the Violet Core Realm had a staggering number of swords, but it also had plenty of warriors with the Violet-Core Physique to borrow them. It was a normal sight to see a constant stream of blades flying back and forth.
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL WIND]
[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
But no one ever tried to summon all ninety-nine-point-nine billion of them at once. To do that, you’d need a First-Rank Violet-Core Physique at the very least—the legendary Black-Violet Physique. But warriors at that level were so powerful they didn’t even need the swords. While the blades could obliterate an entire world of chaos, they weren’t a serious threat to the realm’s strongest masters. For them, the swords were mostly for show, a flex.
If two masters were fighting, the one who could summon a million swords could easily wipe out their rival’s entire army of followers.
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
Still, it was almost unheard of. She remembered about a year ago when the swords had been summoned away on a similar massive scale. No one ever found out who did it, but the number was enough to put every master in the Violet Core Realm on high alert.
Search parties had been sent across the entire realm. They found nothing. The woman responsible had already slipped away through a space node before anyone could reach her — and without her staying in the realm, tracing her was nearly impossible.
The consensus was that it could only have been someone with a high-rank Violet-Core Physique—a Third-Rank Blue-Violet Core or a Second-Rank Pure-Violet Core, at a minimum.
[SFX: SWORD RESONANCE]
The search had come up empty — though some argued it could simply be a wealthy warrior who’d forged enough low-grade Relic-Class weapons to fake the physique.
“Could it be the same person?” the purple-robed woman muttered.
She stomped her foot. The skyship banked hard and swept downward.
[SFX: SKYSHIP DESCENT]
[MUSIC: URGENT STRINGS]
The swords were still falling — hundreds of thousands of them — and as her ship plunged through the Southern Sky Region, their trajectories kept crossing hers. She had to wrench the ship sideways again and again to avoid being skewered.
“Whoever this is,” she muttered through gritted teeth, “does she have any idea how much work it is to put these back?”
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC CRESCENDO]
But this was different. Many masters in the Violet Core Realm could control over a million great swords at once, all of them top-grade Relic-Class weapons.
The true super masters could command as many as eleven million. For them, even Divine weapons were nothing special, but since they couldn’t find eleven million of those, they’d have to settle for mixing a few into the array.
[AMBIENT: WHOOSHING WIND]
[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
As the ship broke through the lower clouds, she saw it.
Millions of great swords were arranged below her in a vast, flowing river — a continuous loop stretching across the sky in every direction. The blades at the far end of the river didn’t stop or vanish. They curved, changed direction, and rejoined the stream, like fish folding back into a current.
[SFX: DEEP RESONANT HUM]
What stopped her cold wasn’t the number. It was the silence.
Not a single sword struck another. Millions of blades, flowing and turning and redirecting — and not one collision. Not one sound. The level of control required was beyond anything she had seen.
[SFX: FORMATION SHIFT]
Then, without warning, every sword in the river snapped to attention simultaneously, straightening into perfect parallel lines across the entire sky.
A beat of silence.
“Turn.”
[SFX: MASSIVE WHOOSH]
The millions of blades pivoted as one and began to rotate, slowly at first, then building — a circular vortex the size of a continent, spinning in absolute silence.
She had watched warriors spend centuries mastering a fraction of this. And this girl had done it on instinct, for fun, with a dragon at her feet.
[SFX: SKYSHIP WHOOSH]
With a soft whoosh, the purple-robed woman angled her skyship, gliding past a dense cluster of the descending blades. And there, down below, stood a woman in white.
Cora had rolled her sleeves up to the elbow. Her slender fingers moved constantly, waving toward the sky in small, precise gestures. She was smiling. She glanced down at the Ancestral Dragon beside her.
“Nice, right?” she said.
“Gagoom!” The dragon’s little tail went wild.
Cora laughed. “Again, then.”
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
The thought that hit her was deeply unsettling: the girl was just playing with them.
She’s just a kid, barely at the Inner-Core Realm, she thought, stunned. How is she doing this?
[MUSIC: DARK CONTEMPLATIVE]
A bitter wave of jealousy washed over her. In the upper world, a warrior at the Inner-Core Realm was no different than a newborn—they hadn’t even crossed the true threshold of martial arts.
Yet here this girl was, commanding the swords as if they were an extension of her own body, all with a smile on her face. It just wasn’t fair.
