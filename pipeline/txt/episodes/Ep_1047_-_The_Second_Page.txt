Ep 1047 - The Second Page

Word count: 1121

Ryder’s plan was simple. Get within five feet of the red dots, then unleash his full power. He figured that with the gravity still steady, he could make a break for it and snatch the weapon.

Legend had it that a master once used this very trick to snag a Grade-One Divine Weapon.

But Ryder was at his limit. It was a struggle just to climb another inch. All he had left was sheer willpower to keep from falling.

[MUSIC: INTENSE DRUMS]

After a few agonizing seconds, he managed to push himself a few feet higher. He was running on fumes. If he didn’t go all out now, he might not get another shot. That thought alone was enough. He gritted his teeth, and the Vitality inside him erupted in a massive surge.

[SFX: WHOOSH]

His slow ascent instantly became a blur as he shot upward.

But the moment his power flared, the gravity from the dome didn’t just increase—it multiplied.

[SFX: DRAMATIC STING]

“I’ve been tricked!” he yelled.

How could this happen? Weren’t they supposed to have a grace period before the gravity ramped up? Why did it spike the second he went for it?

[AMBIENT: HALL MURMUR]

At the table below, a sneer crept across the white-eyebrowed elder’s face. “That’s what you get for trying to game the system instead of relying on your own strength.”

In truth, Ryder hadn’t been tricked at all.

The thing was, the exploit had been real. The dome’s divine gravity matrix was designed to constantly read a warrior’s condition, but there used to be a tiny delay in its response. Clever warriors had discovered they could coast toward the dome, holding back their power, then unleash it all at once to take advantage of that lag.

The window was less than a blink of an eye, but it was just enough time for a master to soar high enough.

Naturally, the Hall of Arms couldn’t let a loophole like that stand. They never officially banned the move, but they quietly patched the divine gravity matrix, closing the exploit for good.

And that was the crucial detail Ryder didn’t know. He’d bet everything on a trick that no longer existed.

His burst of Vitality had shoved him a few feet higher, but the crushing gravity was just as strong. He was still several feet short of the nearest red light. To make matters worse, the speck of light seemed to taunt him, drifting a little closer. It was only a foot away now, but for Ryder, that gap felt like an impossible chasm.

“It’s all or nothing!”

A flash of crimson crossed Ryder’s eyes. He bit down hard on his tongue, blood filling his mouth, and threw himself upward for one final, desperate push.

He had nothing left to give. But he gave it anyway — reaching deeper than he thought possible, dragging up every last reserve he had.

The dome responded instantly. Every ounce of extra power he pushed out, the gravity matched and exceeded. He gained inches. Painful, costly inches.

One foot. Half a foot. One inch.

His fingertips touched the surface of the nearest red orb. Half an inch more and it would be his.

Then the orb moved.

The dots of light in the dome weren’t fixed. They drifted freely — like living things, like they had a will of their own. The one beneath his fingers simply shifted sideways, calm and unhurried, out of reach.

That was what broke him.

[SFX: CRASH]

His strength gave out all at once. He dropped, arms flailing, grabbing wildly at whatever lights he could reach on the way down.

[SFX: HEAVY IMPACT]

He hit the floor hard. When he looked at what he’d caught on the way down, the blood drained from his face. A Grade-Three Divine Weapon. Worse than Matthew.

He threw it aside without a word.

[AMBIENT: HUSHED HALL]

The crowd murmured.

“He almost had it. Just bad luck in the end.”

“He pushed himself to the limit and still came up short. That had to hurt.”

The white-eyebrowed elder looked at Ryder. “Another try?”

“No.” Ryder’s voice was flat and cold. He’d spent enough. He had nothing to show for it and he knew it. He turned to Matthew. “We’re leaving.”

[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]

Intrigued by the selection process, Zane stepped forward to see if there was a better option for him.

“Of course,” the white-eyebrowed elder nodded, unrolling the picture scroll and handing it to Zane.

The first page showed sixteen Grade-One Divine Weapons. Ryder had been eyeing four of the swords.

Zane skimmed the pictures. Besides the four swords Ryder had been looking at, there were sabers, spears, bows, and even a quiver of arrows.

A quick glance confirmed that none of these required the extra hundred Sovereign Stones.

Zane looked up at the elder. “I just want to see the scroll. I’m not committing to the extra hundred yet.”

The elder nodded. “That’s fine.”

Ryder, who had been heading for the exit, stopped. A cold smile crossed his face. He stayed where he was. He wanted to watch.

Zane flipped the scroll over and found a second page.

“Huh? There’s a second page?” A look of curiosity crossed Zane’s face.

Only three weapons were pictured there.

Seeing Zane’s expression, Ryder’s lip curled into a sneer. “Don’t even bother with the second page. You can’t get those.”

Zane shot Ryder an indifferent look. “And why do you care? I’ll look at whatever I want.”

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

Ryder was already fuming about being forced to settle for a Grade-Three Divine Weapon. And hearing some new kid from The Mist Palace talk down to him like that sent a surge of killing intent through him.

Matthew, standing beside him, chimed in, “Ryder, that kid has a serious attitude problem.”

Zane felt the wave of killing intent but didn’t so much as flinch. He just shot Matthew a dismissive glance before turning his attention back to the second page.

The white-eyebrowed elder just smiled. “The Hall of Arms has its rules. You want to fight, you take it outside.”

Ryder wasn’t stupid enough to start a fight in here, of course. He just wanted to rattle the new guy.

[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL HALL]

The elder then turned to Zane. “The weapons on the second page were left behind by the palace’s former leaders. If you’re willing to pay an extra one hundred Sovereign Stones, you can choose one of the three. I’ll have it placed in the dome for you.”

Zane could feel every eye in the hall on him — the elder’s quiet curiosity, Ryder’s cold smirk, Matthew’s barely concealed contempt — and he hadn’t even made his choice yet.
