Ep 1202 - In The City

Word count: 1024

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS ETHEREAL SYNTH]
[AMBIENT: VAST DIMENSIONAL SPACE]
The moment Zane opened his eyes, the world he knew was gone. 
His consciousness had been projected into another reality, a sprawling dimension called the Illusionary Arena.
The place was immense, as vast as fifty supreme worlds stitched together and divided into fifty distinct sections, each one a battlefield in its own right. Warriors from across the universe were scattered among them, and Zane found himself in a large city inside one of these realms. The streets were packed with combatants from countless different races, but they all had one thing in common: every single one of them was at the Soul Sea Realm.
[AMBIENT: BUSTLING CITY]
Zane got to his feet, taking in his surroundings. The Illusion Battlefield was so realistic it could easily pass for a genuine world. It was a place usually sealed off from the rest of the universe, opening only on rare occasions for contests like this one. But this was no human city. The buildings were all capped with black and red roofs, and a faint crimson light pulsed from the cracks in the walls. 
[SFX: MAGMA BUBBLING]
The stone itself seemed to have magma flowing just beneath its surface.
“An Ogre city,” Zane murmured, his eyes scanning the architecture.
The placement of the warriors wasn’t random. Combatants from the hundred thousand supreme worlds were grouped by proximity. Since Zane’s home in the Northern Sky Region bordered Ogre territory, it made sense he’d been dropped here. He looked around, confirming his theory. 
The streets were dominated by Ogres, each one as tall and solid as a small mountain. Only a handful of humans and other races were mixed into the crowd. A few of the humans here had simply drawn the short straw—the placement carried a degree of randomness, and they’d been unlucky enough to land in Ogre territory by chance rather than proximity.
[MUSIC: TENSE BATTLE PREPARATION]
The moment the nearby Ogres spotted Zane, their faces soured into hostile glares. He paid them no mind. It didn’t matter what corner of the arena he’d landed in. This was a contest for the strongest young warriors in the entire universe, which meant he had trillions of opponents to overcome. Human, Ogre, or otherwise—it made no difference. All that mattered was defeating every last one of them and being the sole victor.
Zane might have been ready for the fight, but the other humans looked like they were already defeated. The ones who, like him, had been dropped into the heart of Ogre territory looked utterly miserable, their shoulders slumped in despair. 
“All human warriors, get your asses over here!” a rough voice bellowed. 
[AMBIENT: CROWD MOVEMENT]
A cold voice suddenly rang out over the entire city, carrying none of the warmth it usually had. “Flying and fighting are prohibited in all major cities. Those who violate the rules will die immediately. There are four hours remaining before the city gates open.” It was the mysterious girl—but ever since the Battlefield had opened, she sounded like an entirely different person, reciting the rules without a trace of feeling. Zane listened closely, committing every word to memory.
Hundreds of Ogres gathered like a small mountain, closing in to encircle the warriors of other races. Near Zane, more than ten human warriors went pale with fear. Even though fighting was supposedly banned within the city, they fell in line anyway, walking over without protest. Zane didn’t move an inch—he simply ignored the order, same as he ignored everything else.
As one hulking Ogre took a menacing step toward Zane, he didn’t even flinch.
“Human brat, do you know whose territory this is?” the Ogre snarled. “If you don’t listen to us—”
“Scram,” Zane said, cutting him off cold.
[SFX: HEAVY FOOTSTEPS STUMBLING BACK]
The Ogre froze, then stumbled back two steps as if shoved by an invisible force. He paled, then flushed a deep, embarrassed red. When he finally met Zane’s gaze again, there was a flicker of raw fear in his eyes.
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS CALM]
Nearby, a warrior in a cloak took a long, slow drag from his pipe, blowing out a perfect smoke ring. His eyelids were heavy, as if he hadn’t noticed a thing.
“Careful,” the cloaked warrior said, almost lazily. “Word is their leader’s got a real pedigree. Cross him, and it could get troublesome.”
Zane allowed a thin smile. “Maybe you’re the troublesome one.”
The cloaked warrior huffed out a short laugh, and the two shared a look of understanding. 
The Illusion Battlefield had drawn every young elite in the universe, but elites came in every shade of strength. The weak were grass, fit only for grazing. The merely strong were sheep. But the true powers here were wolves—and even among wolves, there was a Wolf King. 
Zane and the cloaked man recognized exactly what the other was. Between wolves, there would always be a reckoning eventually.
Just then, another voice boomed through the crowd. “What do you think you’re doing?”
The speaker was an Ogre warrior decked out in polished silver armor. Zane’s eyes narrowed, assessing the gear. It was a rare treasure, a Low Grade-Two Divine-Class artifact, with defensive capabilities easily on par with a Grade-One weapon.
Another Ogre pointed at Zane and the cloaked man. 
“Thane! It’s these two humans! They’re refusing to follow your orders!”
[MUSIC: TENSE CONFRONTATION]
A tall, powerful Ogre named Thane pushed his way out from the crowd. He loomed over Zane and the cloaked warrior, his expression cold. 
“I’ll give you two one last chance,” he growled. “Get in line and obey me.”
Zane and the cloaked warrior exchanged a quick glance. To them, this posturing Ogre was about as threatening as a sheep.
A small smile played on Zane’s lips. “Obey you? And what, exactly, is in it for us?”
“My mercy,” Thane responded coldly. “I’ll only kill you twice before I let you go.”
He delivered the line as if it were a generous gift. Warriors didn’t truly die in the Illusion Battlefield; getting killed just meant you’d revive back in the city. 
In Thane’s mind, killing them only twice was an act of profound kindness. If he really wanted to be cruel, he could hunt them down and kill them again and again, forever.
