[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]
The tension was palpable as Bob and Leo stood facing each other, their postures ramrod straight with hostility. 
The other disciples had fallen silent, forming a loose circle around the confrontation, their eyes darting nervously between the two men. No one dared to intervene, especially not when Bob was involved.
 The last time Bob got into a fight, it was right here on the shore of the lake. But back then, his opponent was just some kid from a minor noble family. The lesser houses couldn’t hold a candle to the Seven Great Houses, and sometimes, they just had to take the disrespect. It was no big deal for an heir of the Seven Great Houses to rough someone up. Hell, if their families even bothered to apologize, the lesser house would probably act like it was an honor. He'd nearly killed the poor kid.
Leo was a young lord from House Brandt, one of the Seven Great Houses. But he wasn’t a typical arrogant jerk like most of them. Leo was actually one of the decent ones. Since both Leo's and Bob's families were counted among the Seven Great Houses, a fight between them would be a huge mess. It would absolutely spark a conflict between the two powerful families. And that was a problem, because things between House Pauling and House Brandt were already tense.
But Bob? He was a total hothead.
[MUSIC: MYSTICAL UNDERTONES]
 Every warrior had their obsession, a core drive. For some, it was Possession—an all-consuming need to own and control. That drive gave them the power to shatter their limits. Others believed in Yielding, thinking that giving a little ground now would gain them an advantage later.
Bob was all about Possession. The word “compromise” just wasn't in his vocabulary. He’d been like that since he was a kid. If he wanted something, everyone else had to get out of his damn way.
Backing down from a fight would mess with his focus so bad it would actually stunt his growth as a warrior. So even though Bob knew fighting Leo was a bad idea, he wasn't about to back down. He didn’t care about the consequences. All he could think about was putting Leo in his place.
But Leo wasn't one to back down, either. There was a gap in their skill levels, sure, but as a member of the powerful House Brandt, he had a stubborn streak of his own. Leo was kind, but he wasn't a pushover. And he hated bullies.  He was sick and tired of Bob's temper holding everyone hostage. 
[AMBIENT: TENSE CONFRONTATION]
Finally, Steward Carter stepped forward, desperate to avoid an all out war.  “Whoa, hold on! Listen, you two, let’s all just calm down and talk this out. It doesn’t have to get this tense." The two young lords were squared up and ready to throw down, deaf to reason. With the tension ratcheting up, Steward Carter could feel the sweat beading on his brow.
According to the rules of the Skyreach Sect, fighting between disciples was forbidden, unless it was a formal challenge.
Still, these two were from the Seven Great Houses. With powerful families at their backs, they weren't afraid of causing trouble. If this wasn't handled carefully, Steward Carter knew he’d be the one taking the fall. No matter who won, the losing family would take it out on him."Forget it. House Brandt has bec—"
[SFX: SPLASH]
[AMBIENT: WATER ENVIRONMENT]
The argument was interrupted by the sound of water cascading across the stone platform. 
All eyes turned toward the source of the disturbance as Zane emerged from the Heavy Water, soaked to the bone. Droplets glistened on his skin as he gracefully stepped onto the platform, seemingly unaffected by the crushing depths he'd just navigated. His expression remained composed, though his eyes quickly assessed the tense standoff before him.
"What's happening here?" Zane asked, his voice cutting through the silence. He glanced between Leo and Bob, immediately sensing the hostility radiating between them.
Steward Carter's eyes widened in surprise. The young man had either returned from the water at the perfect moment to interrupt the brewing conflict.
Bob didn't really care how Zane had stayed under for so long. What really ticked him off was that Zane, a fighter barely in the Adept Realm, wasn't showing him the proper respect. In Bob's mind, his rude warning had been for Zane's own good.
Carter leaned in, lowering his voice. "You're friends with Leo, right? Maybe you could talk him down. Give everyone a way out of this."
His reasoning was simple: Zane wasn't a Great House heir. He had no family backing, no political skin in the game. The smart move for someone like him was to stay out of it — persuade his friend to stand down, let Bob have his way, and walk away clean. Carter had seen plenty of outer disciples make exactly that calculation.
He'd misjudged the situation entirely.
Hearing this, Zane just shook his head. "Leo's my friend. When a friend's in trouble, you help them out. Simple as that. Besides, I hear you were trying to run me out of Emberfin Lake. Is that right?"
[MUSIC: TENSE CONFRONTATION]
Zane's sharp tone made it clear he was standing with Leo. Having drawn his line in the sand, Bob saw no reason to hold himself back. If Zane had reached the Soul Realm, Bob might've thought twice. But Zane was just a beginner in the Adept Realm.
That's what really got under Bob's skin: this kid Zane was a nobody, from no important family.  He wasn't worth the energy it would take to flatten him.
Bob was at the Consummation of the Adept Realm; he could barely find a decent challenge anymore. So he just laughed. "Yeah, you heard right. So what? Every time I come to Emberfin Lake to train, people know to get out of my way."
"Is that so?" Zane retorted, not even trying to hide his hostility.  "And who are you supposed to be? Why should I move for you? Keep dreaming, loser."
[SFX: CROWD GASPS]Steward Carter exhaled heavily, pinching the bridge of his nose. "Oh, for heaven's sake," he muttered, shoulders slumping in resignation. The young man's defiant response confirmed his fears—this wasn't going to end peacefully. If a fight broke out, they were all in for it. Steward Carter could feel a headache coming on.Just a little while ago, when he'd let Zane into Emberfin Lake, Steward Carter had pegged him as a calm, polite kid. He never would have guessed that the seemingly mild-mannered cultivator had such a short fuse.
No one moved a muscle as they waited to see what Bob would do next.
