Ep 1275 - Warriors In The Darkness

Word count: 1058

[MUSIC: DARK SUSPENSEFUL]
[AMBIENT: ECHOING CAVERN]
[SFX: METAL HOOK SNARE]
[SFX: BODY DRAGGED]
The hook snared Cain, and he was gone—yanked into the suffocating darkness before he could even finish his scream.
“Ah—!”
The sound was cut short. One moment the warrior was there; the next, not even a shadow remained in the gloom.
[SFX: MAGICAL LIGHT BURST]
A split second later, a shower of golden light erupted from the blackness across the ravine, only to be instantly swallowed up again.
That could only mean one thing: Cain was dead. Those were his Illusion Points, and someone had just absorbed them.
“Where did the points go?”
“It looks like they were absorbed!”
“Impossible!” Janet’s face was a mask of shocked terror. “Is there another warrior in this hall? Someone who could have killed Cain and taken his points?”
She was right to be terrified. Here in the Illusion Battlefield, only other warriors could absorb Illusion Points. Wild animals and fearsome beasts could kill you, but they couldn’t take your points. 
If Zane had been the one to kill Cain, Cain’s points would have flown directly to him, no matter the distance. It was the same principle Sloane had used when she gave her subordinates those golden suicide pellets; they contained a trace of her own consciousness, so when they were used, the system registered it as her kill.
If a warrior was killed by a beast out in the wilds, their points would just float there, waiting for the next person to come along and claim them. But Cain’s points had been absorbed almost instantly.
[MUSIC: RISING TENSION]
There was only one explanation. 
There were other warriors hiding on the other side of that ravine.
They must have been the ones who hooked Cain, dragged him over, and killed him.
But that didn’t make any sense. Every time this hall opened, only ten warriors were allowed to enter. So how could there be more of them on the other side?
“Be careful,” Zane said, his voice low. 
[SFX: MAGICAL ENERGY PULSE]
He tightened his grip on the hook in his hand, his eyes shifting to a mottled green as he activated his magical sight. He peered into the oppressive blackness, but it was like staring at a curtain woven from the Dark Law itself, perfectly shielding whoever was hiding inside.
The hooks themselves couldn’t hurt anyone directly—Zane had already worked that much out from what happened to Cain. But once the hook caught hold, it dragged its target in with a pull nobody could fight against.
“Now!” Zane hissed, and hurled his own hook across the ravine, aiming for one of the shadows he’d caught sight of.
[SFX: HOOK WHISTLING THROUGH AIR]
The shadow slipped aside before it landed—whoever was over there had done this before. 
Zane yanked the hook back, jaw tight. “Missed.”
Just as the hook returned to his hand, his eyes went wide. “Watch out—Hailey!”
The warning came a half-second too late. A hook shot out of the dark and snagged the woman from the Demon Night, the same invisible pull already dragging her toward the ravine’s far edge.
[SFX: HOOK WHISTLING THROUGH AIR]
Zane didn’t hesitate. He whipped his retrieved hook back out—not at the shadow this time, but straight at Hailey herself—and hauled her back before the darkness could swallow her whole.
“That was close,” Zane muttered, breathing out.
Janet let out a breath of her own, relief plain on her face.
“It’s the power of the Dark Law,” Coleman said grimly.
He held his own hook in one hand and his Blade of Hallowed Censure in the other. “They came prepared.”
Zane kept scanning the darkness, his gaze finally locking onto a few blurry figures across the chasm. 
Hailey gave Zane a small nod, gratitude flashing across her face before it hardened into fury. “There really is someone over there.”
She ripped the Moonburst Flamebow free and tore into her own reserves, a flame vortex boiling to life in front of her. Another hook lashed out at her from the dark—she twisted aside without breaking her draw and loosed the string.
[SFX: FLAME ARROW WHOOSH]
The vortex screamed across the ravine.
[SFX: MASSIVE FIRE EXPLOSION]
For one bright instant, the flames lit up the far side—and Zane, Janet, and the rest finally saw them. Ten warriors, standing in a neat line, each holding a hook, sneering back across the gap.
One of them—clearly their leader—looked straight at Zane with open contempt and dragged a finger across his own throat. The Dark Law swallowed what was left of the fire whole, and the far side dropped back into black.
“Ten of them, and not one dead the whole way here,” Janet said, voice tight. “We can’t take this team lightly.”
[MUSIC: IMMINENT DANGER]
The horrifying truth clicked into place. The hall hadn’t just presented them with a challenge; it had pitted two full groups against each other.
Those ten warriors on the other side must have passed through the first two halls as well, but not a single one of them had died. They still had all ten members. These guys were no joke. Worse, they’d already used the Dark Law to set an ambush, putting Zane’s group at a huge disadvantage.
“Does anyone know the Dark Law?” Janet asked, her voice strained. It was an uncommon skill, and she’d never mastered it.
Zane hadn’t either. He’d received the Baptism by Laws, but he’d never focused on something so sinister.
Hearing her question, Coleman let out a cold laugh. “We don’t need the Dark Law. Watch this.”
He clapped his hands together softly. 
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS ETHEREAL]
[AMBIENT: THICK MIST SWIRLING]
A low, resonant hum vibrated through the air, and a thick mist began to billow around their feet. A hazy, shimmering light radiated from his body, spreading out in every direction.
The mist didn’t feel strange to Zane or the others standing in it, but its effect was meant for their enemies. Across the ravine, it would look as if countless figures had just appeared out of nowhere. Coleman had created a swarm of illusions that blended in with their group of seven, making it impossible to tell who was real and who was just a mirage.
“Let’s see them try to hook us now,” Coleman sneered.
The mist didn’t hide them completely, but it was more than enough to confuse their opponents. 
[MUSIC: STRATEGIC TENSION]
It was clear now: the Third Hall of Causality was a battlefield, and only one team was going to walk out. Hidden within the swirling illusions, they began to plan their counterattack.
