Ep 1080 - A Prize Worth Fighting For

Word count: 1030

[AMBIENT: TOWER INTERIOR]

[MUSIC: INTENSE ORCHESTRAL]

The three-thousand-mile mark in the Enchanted Barrier Tower was a meat grinder.

In the first tier of warriors, not a single person had been eliminated, but that was the easy part. The second tier was a bloodbath, with seventy percent of the contestants knocked out. The third tier had it even worse; only three hundred warriors out of three thousand—a scant ten percent—managed to push past that stage. For the ones who did, just covering that distance was a victory in itself. The Alliance would invest more resources in them now, which made the prize of a low-grade Divine Weapon feel like little more than a pat on the back.

After that three-thousand-mile mark, everyone, in every tier, had slowed to a crawl.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

Everyone except Zane. He just kept his pace.

Anyone paying close attention would have realized his speed hadn’t changed one bit since the very beginning. More than that, he still hadn’t used an ounce of his Vitality to shield himself. He was taking the full force of the brutal Law Storm with nothing but his own physical strength.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS PIANO]

Only Yolande had been watching him the entire time.

But it wasn’t long before someone else noticed, too.

[AMBIENT: PALACE OBSERVATION HALL]

An old man in simple cotton clothes, one of the palace leaders, had been observing the first-tier warriors when his gaze drifted back to the second tier. Something caught his eye.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

“Huh?” The old man’s eyes lit up. “That young man isn’t using any Vitality to protect himself.”

He squinted, and his expression went rigid. “Wait a second. Isn’t that the kid who was late? The one at the very back of the pack? When did he catch up to the second tier? He’s already past three thousand miles.”

To a man of his stature, three thousand miles wasn’t much of an accomplishment. And since Zane’s performance had been so lackluster at the start, the old man had completely written him off. If it weren’t for the bizarre fact that Zane wasn’t using his Vitality, he probably never would have noticed him at all. But here was the kid who’d been dead last, suddenly catching up as if he’d teleported. The sight was, to say the least, surprising.

“Is that the same guy?” another palace leader asked, leaning in. “The one in last place who refused to use his Vitality?”

“That’s him,” the old man confirmed. “How interesting. I never expected him to make it to the front of the second tier.”

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]

“It’s more than interesting, it’s strange,” another leader added, their attention now fixed on Zane. “He’s covered three thousand miles and still hasn’t shielded himself. He must have incredible confidence in his raw strength.”

“The Mist Palace has some fascinating people this time around. Besides Layla, the one in second place is also from the Mist Palace.”

“Sixteen thousand miles,” one of the palace leaders said. “I’m afraid she’ll be picked up by Silverwood Pavilion after this.”

“When I went through this tower, thirteen thousand was my limit.”

[SFX: SOFT CRACK]

[MUSIC: RISING TENSION]

Past sixteen thousand miles, Layla’s body became wreathed in green light. A crack appeared between her eyebrows, and a pale green stone the size of a fingertip emerged from it.

The old man stood up abruptly. “The Wrath of the Ocean.”

“She has it? Elder Dunn hid this well.”

The old man sat back down. “No — if she had it, Silverwood Pavilion would know. Elder Dunn doesn’t know either.”

Yolande said nothing. She had always assumed Zane would surpass Layla. Now she wasn’t sure.

The Wrath of the Ocean wasn’t just any gift. It was one of the most powerful and unique talents in existence. Warriors who possessed it were extraordinarily rare across the entire universe, and what made it truly frightening was that its Law of Causality didn’t just exist within its bearer — it grew alongside them. A Divine Weapon’s Law of Causality was fixed the moment it was forged. This was something else entirely.

[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]

Layla’s speed doubled. Seventeen thousand miles. Eighteen thousand — Supreme Lord Illusion’s record, broken. She didn’t stop. Every thousand miles drew fresh exclamations from the palace leaders.

Twenty-one.

Twenty-three.

Twenty-five.

She finally slowed at twenty-eight thousand miles, advancing two hundred more before stopping.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

Suddenly, a ripple of shock went through the observers as the true nature of the grand prize began to circulate.

“Oh my God,” someone breathed. “If I’d known, I would’ve fought with everything I had.”

[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL ATMOSPHERE]

The prize wasn’t just some weapon; it was a Tenth Tribulation Introducing Pill. A legendary item, it could trigger a Celestial Tribulation, much like the Tribulation Flower Zane had once swallowed back on the Divine Kingdom Continent. The difference was that a Tribulation Flower could only help a warrior face up to nine Celestial Tribulations. For the true geniuses of the Upper World, that was the expected benchmark; their sheer talent alone was enough to anger the Heavens and draw nine calamities down upon them. Any warrior who broke through to the Soul Sea Realm from only the eighth grade was barely considered a true talent.

But the Tenth Tribulation Introducing Pill was something else entirely. It was a Grade-Nine pill, leagues above the common Grade-Seven Tribulation Exterminating Pill. Its purpose wasn’t to survive a trial, but to create one. It could trigger the legendary tenth Celestial Tribulation—the Perfection Tribulation. Passing that tenth trial would refine a warrior’s foundation to a state of absolute perfection, a priceless advantage for their entire future.

“Zane has also crossed eighteen thousand miles,” the old man said.

The palace leaders turned. “If not for Layla, that would be the only story anyone told about this event.” A beat. “But he’s stopped.”

Yolande watched Zane, motionless in the storm. She didn’t know what he was doing.

[AMBIENT: TOWER INTERIOR]

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS]

Down in the tower, Layla had been moving forward with the same unbroken focus the entire time.

Jasper was right on her heels, but he was a wreck. His clothes were in tatters, and two deep, bloody gashes ran down his legs. He looked absolutely miserable.

When Layla’s figure finally emerged at the entrance of the tower, a faint smile on her lips, the warriors of the Thirteen Palaces looked at her differently — not as a peer, but as something above them.
