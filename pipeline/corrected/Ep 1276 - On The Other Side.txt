Ep 1276 - On The Other Side

Word count: 1077

[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSEFUL]
[AMBIENT: MISTY CHASM]
On the other side of the chasm, a group of ten warriors stood in the darkness, looking dazed but still on high alert as they stared into the thick mist across from them. 
To them, the fog was unnaturally dense as it rolled out, creating countless illusions—people, snakes, bugs, every creature imaginable. In just a few moments, the seven figures on the other side had vanished completely.
[SFX: HOOK WHOOSH]
Someone in the darkness let a long hook fly with a *whoosh*. It shot across the three-thousand-foot gap like a spear, plunging straight into the fog. 
But the hook only snagged an illusion. The figure it had latched onto dissolved into nothing, and the hook came sailing back empty. 
[SFX: HOOK WHOOSHING BACK]
In response, a hook shot out from the other side, mirroring the move. The strategy was simple: hook an enemy, drag them across the abyss, and let your team gang up on them for a quick kill. It was exactly what had happened to Cain—he’d been hooked and pulled over without a chance to fight back, bursting into a cloud of Illusion Points before being sent back to the main city.
Now, with one side using a Dark Law and the other hidden by the fog, both teams were completely concealed. 
[SFX: MULTIPLE HOOKS FLYING]
The long hooks flew back and forth across the ravine several more times, but not a single warrior was caught.
“Interesting,” the leader of the ten warriors murmured. “That’s Illusive Fog. One of them must be from The Hallowed Concord.”
He was a stick of a man, thin and frail, but his eyes held a sharp glint, like two knives hidden behind his calm expression.
“They must’ve passed the first two Halls of Causality, too,” another warrior laughed coldly. “They’ve already lost two teammates, and we just killed another. That means they’re down to seven. Ha! Not so tough after all!”
“Larkin could take on all seven by himself, right?” someone else exclaimed in the darkness. “This Third Hall of Causality is going to be a piece of cake.”
[MUSIC: OMINOUS BATTLE DRUMS]
[AMBIENT: ECHOING STONE HALL]
To pass the Third Hall, one side had to eliminate every warrior on the other. The entrance for Zane’s group was the exit for these ten, and their entrance was Zane’s exit. The only way through was to be the last team standing.
“Of course! Larkin is an Ace Talent! Who could possibly match him?” another warrior chimed in. “If we sent him over, those guys would be dead in a flash!”
“But they’re using the Illusive Fog for cover,” someone else said, a bit hesitant. “It’ll be hard to snag them with the hooks.”
“Heh, they can’t break my hold either,” a voice growled from within the shadows—the one who’d woven the Dark Law himself.
“What’s the use if you can’t break it? Are we afraid of them?” A massive shape shifted in the darkness, easily ten feet tall. “Get rid of this broken thing. It’s so dark I can’t see a damn thing.”
“I set up the Dark Law to protect everyone,” the caster shot back, unconvinced.
“I don’t need your protection. I’m standing right here. Let’s see who dares hook me.”
“Enough,” Larkin said quietly. “Drop the Dark Law around Damon.”
“Brother Larkin—are you sure?”
“If they spot him, they’ll try to hook him over. Once they do, they’ll come for me next.” Larkin’s tone didn’t waver. “Just don’t underestimate them. Anyone who’s made it into these Halls isn’t weak.”
Damon only laughed. “They’re cowards, hiding in that fog because they’re too scared to fight in the open. I’ll crush them the second I step out. What is there to fear?”
“Don’t underestimate your opponent,” Larkin said. “Go over first. Then turn your hook on me.”
No one argued further. Larkin had earned that kind of authority the hard way—in the Second Hall of Causality, he’d pulled all ten of them through a situation that should have killed them, relying on nothing but his own strength. Even Damon, who bowed to no one, deferred to him now.
[SFX: DARKNESS PEELING BACK]
A section of the shadows on the far side caved inward, and a massive figure stepped forward into view.
[SFX: TENSE MURMUR]
Meanwhile, Zane’s group had caught a name that gave them pause: Damon. Ansel stood staring across the ravine at the massive figure, his mouth hanging open. “That really is Damon,” he stammered. “Supreme Lord Demondawn’s only son—one of the most formidable warriors the Ogre race has ever produced. What is *he* doing here?”
“How strong can he really be?” Coleman asked, unconvinced. He’d been thinking their opponents wouldn’t be too hard to handle as long as they weren’t facing an Ace Talent from a famous race or faction. “There’s no Ace Talent among the Ogres by that name.”
“He’s not an Ace Talent,” Ansel explained. “He doesn’t have the destiny for it. But his strength and talent are just as good as any Ace’s.”
To become an Ace Talent or a saint-level genius, a warrior needed the destiny to lead the world of chaos. But that didn’t mean everyone with that destiny actually qualified. Ansel’s point was simple: the Ogre on the other side couldn’t be an Ace Talent because fate hadn’t chosen him, but with his level of strength, his power was nothing to scoff at.
[SFX: TENSE SILENCE]
Hearing this, Coleman, Janet, and the others grew serious. Across the ravine, the Ogre stood there confidently, just waiting for them to make a move.
Zane gripped the hook in his hand, his eyes fixed on the Ogre. His gaze dropped from his opponent down into the deep ravine, where he saw a river of illusory energy flowing. No matter how strong this guy was, he couldn’t escape the Celestial Law woven into the hook. The moment he was pulled over, he’d be on the defensive with no way out.
But he was practically radiating confidence. He had to be counting on something.
As Zane was turning this over in his mind, Damon’s voice roared across the three-thousand-foot gap. 
“Hey! You bunch of cowards over there! I’m standing right here, and not one of you has the guts to make a move? You’re pathetic!”
He let out another booming laugh. “Why don’t you just give up? Get rid of that mist! Come on over, kneel, and beg me for mercy. I might even let you die a little faster!”
He spread his arms wide.
“Go on! Throw your hook! Pull me over! I promise I won’t even fight back!”
