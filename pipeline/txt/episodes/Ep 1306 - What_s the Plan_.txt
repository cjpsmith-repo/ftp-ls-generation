Ep 1306 - What’s the Plan?

Word count: 1065

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS ETHEREAL]
[AMBIENT: MAGICAL CHAMBER]
[SFX: MAGICAL SWIRLING DUST]
[SFX: ENERGY ABSORPTION]
A fine powder, like a dusty mist, swirled in the air before funneling into the Teleportation Token.
“Did it work?” Zane asked, his voice tight with excitement. His heart was hammering against his ribs. He’d been so sure everything would just vanish when the Illusion Battlefield collapsed—all his items, gone. But if he could actually keep the Teleportation Token… this was better than he’d ever imagined.
[SFX: SOFT MAGICAL CHIME]
A moment later, a mark no bigger than an ant appeared on the corner of the token. It was real. He could take it with him.
Of course, there was no guarantee it would actually *work* outside this realm. If it was useless, he’d have wasted a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity for nothing. But it was a gamble he was willing to take. If it paid off, he could travel anywhere in the universe.
“Huh? What’s that?” Coleman asked, leaning in to get a better look at the small token in Zane’s hand.
[SFX: STORAGE RING ACTIVATION]
Zane snatched his hand back and tucked the token into his Storage Ring. “It’s nothing.”
Coleman just stared, his eyes wide with disappointment. “So secretive,” he muttered.
Zane just shrugged and ignored him. His own decision was made. But as it turned out, he wasn’t the one in their group facing the toughest choice.
That was Janet.
The Lofty Wood was part of her—a living thing fused with her own body. Taking a copy of it should have been the easiest decision in the world. And yet, she was cradling the little flame kitten, her resolve wavering. 
[SFX: SOFT PURRING]
She gently rocked the creature, its warmth a solid, living thing in her arms, and she wanted so badly to take it with her.
The kitten was a high-level Law Creature, a rarity even in the World of Divine Flame and notoriously hard to catch. But her Lofty Wood was a divine treasure, a legendary wood from beyond the heavens. She had the chance to duplicate her most valuable asset, and she was hesitating over a tiny, purring ball of fire.
“Janet, why are you still thinking about it?” Zane asked, noticing her pained expression after he’d put his token away.
Janet pursed her lips. “I’m trying to decide what to take.”
“What’s there to even think about?” he said with a laugh. “You’ve got to choose the Lofty Wood.”
Janet didn’t answer right away. She just held the kitten a little tighter, her expression unreadable. Then, all at once, her face firmed—and she crushed the pink dot in her hand.
[SFX: CRUSHING CRYSTAL]
[SFX: MAGICAL SHIMMER]
The powder didn’t drift toward the Lofty Wood at all. It settled over the kitten’s fur instead, leaving a faint mark behind.
Nearby, Hailey watched the whole thing without comment, her expression giving nothing away—just the faintest smile.
Jeffrey’s jaw dropped. “You’re crazy!”
Everyone stared, a little heartsick on her behalf—the pink dot was precious, and she’d spent it on a kitten that, however rare, was worth nowhere near what her Lofty Wood was. 
But Janet didn’t look the least bit sorry. She just smiled and nuzzled the kitten, which let out a soft, contented meow, as if it understood exactly what had just happened.
Before they parted ways at the exit, Janet turned to Zane, smiling. “One condition—you’re not allowed to attack any of us.”
“Seconded!” Coleman blurted immediately.
“Then let’s go,” Hailey said, already moving toward the door of light.
No one argued. After watching Zane operate through the entire Hall of Causality, all of them had quietly reached the same conclusion: none of them stood a chance against him. Even Jeffrey, who respected almost no one, couldn’t help admiring him. Zane just smiled and nodded.
“Wait—when we leave the Hall, do we go back to our own main cities?” Jeffrey asked.
Janet shook her head. “No. The exit’s fixed to one main city in the whole Great World. Every warrior who clears the Hall ends up there.”
Jeffrey’s brow furrowed. “So that means—”
“That’s the whole point,” Janet said. “Anyone who can clear the Hall of Causality is already near the top of their world. Funnel them all into one place, and the collisions only get worse.”
A spark of something sharp and eager crossed Jeffrey’s face.
It wasn’t hard to see why. Even Coleman—the weakest of their six—ruled his own main city outright. If that was the floor, then whoever they ran into out here would be standing on the same ground they were.
[MUSIC: TENSE ANTICIPATORY]
[AMBIENT: BUSTLING CITY STREETS]
[SFX: SHIMMERING PORTAL]
Zane stepped through the shimmering door of light, his eyes scanning the main city. He knew what was out here. The warriors who passed the Hall of Causality’s test were the best of the best, true elites just a step below Ace Talents. The ordinary fighters in the sanctuary were basically nobodies now.
[AMBIENT: DISTANT COMBAT ECHOES]
The first wave of experts to emerge from the hall had already torn through this city, forcing the strong to flee and leaving the weak behind to gawk at the chaos. 
Some of the experts had moved on to other sanctuaries or gone off to explore, but plenty had stayed, figuring the exit of the hall was the best place to hunt for other powerful warriors. Stealing someone’s Illusion Points just came down to skill and strength. 
The onlookers who remained were mostly the city’s own leftover cannon fodder—cultivators without the standing to ever earn an iron certificate, let alone the millions of Illusion Points it took to even attempt the Hall—and they knew better than to run. Whoever came through that door next would decide who played sheep and who played wolf.
He turned to the others as they emerged behind him. “Jeffrey, Janet, what’s the plan?”
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
Jeffrey’s eyes glinted with focus. “I need to process some things. I’m going to find a quiet place to meditate.”
[AMBIENT: QUIET MEDITATION SPACE]
The Tome of Abiding Past wasn’t just a monastic scripture; it was a cultivation method. It reminded Zane of the Gods-Intimidating Strike—another text that countless warriors had spent millennia trying to decipher, but that only he had ever truly mastered. Like the Strike, the Tome was common knowledge in The Hallowed Concord, but almost no one could actually understand it.
