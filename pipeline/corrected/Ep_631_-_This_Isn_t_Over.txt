Ep 631 - This Isn't Over
Word count: 1182
[AMBIENT: TOWER INTERIOR]
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
On the top floor of the Tower of Sin, a group of high-ranking Ogre War Lords were gathered, their faces masks of dejection. Even as their brethren continued to line up for a pointless challenge against Zane on the eleventh floor, the leaders knew it was over. The struggle was pointless.
Back when Zane was a mere War General, he’d already taken down Boris with ease. Now that he’d ascended to a second-level War General, he was more than a match for any ordinary War Lord. But more importantly, Zane wasn't an idiot. So what if they somehow managed to beat him in the arena? He held the real power now—the Tower of Sin itself—and he could use it to kill anyone he wanted. Rules were for the weak, a tool to keep them in line. Did they really expect the one with all the power to play by their rules? Impossible.
[MUSIC: RISING TENSION]
The Ogres’ time in the tower was clearly running out. Many had already started packing, preparing for the long trip home.
“We still have one last chance,” Maurice said, his voice cutting through the gloom. “Who’s willing to stay?”
“And do what? Keep fighting a losing battle?” a top-level War Lord shot back, his tone thick with annoyance.
Maurice just stared coldly at him. Sometimes his comrades could be so dense.
Another War Lord’s eyes lit up.
“He’s right,” he said, a slow smile spreading across his face. “There is one last chance.”
Maurice let out a loud laugh. “And it’s a good one, too.”
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
Understanding began to ripple through the room, and a spark of hope returned to their eyes. In less than a month, Zane would begin kicking the Ogres out of the tower for good. But they had an ace up their sleeve. Years ago, they had hidden two War Sages in a secret passageway beneath the Tower of Sin, a place where they could safely avoid the deadly Dark Nether Death Light.
That same light, which flooded the Dark Nether Cave, was known to weaken periodically. It was only a matter of time before Zane, hungry for power, would venture inside to explore. If not this year, then the next. If not next year, then the one after. The lifespan of a top-level War Lord was long. They could afford to wait. They could even continue their training in the secret passageway.
Many of the War Lords present, Maurice included, had been actively suppressing their own power to keep from breaking through to the War Sage level, which would trigger the Cursed Land’s deadly spell. But if they hid in the passageway, they could finally let go.
[AMBIENT: QUIET CHAMBER]
[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]
The logic was simple: the longer the suppression, the stronger the rebound once the breakthrough finally came.
"I've already suppressed my cultivation for several years," Maurice said. "Breaking through to War Sage won't be difficult. Who else can say the same?"
One by one, voices answered.
"I can."
"And me."
"My breakthrough is long overdue."
Maurice looked them over. Then his gaze settled. "Garrick. Timothy. You two stay."
Garrick and Timothy — ranked eighteenth and twenty-second among all seat controllers — said nothing.
"I can't remain myself," Maurice said. "I'm the leader. My absence would be noticed." His eyes were hard. "Whether or not our clan ever returns to this Tower depends on the two of you."
A cold, dense killing intent settled over both their faces.
"As long as that brat sets foot in the Dark Nether Cave," Garrick said quietly, "we'll tear him apart."
[MUSIC: TRANSITION CHIMES]
[AMBIENT: TOWER ENTRANCE]
Half a month passed in a blink.
Creatures from every race gathered at the entrance to the Tower of Sin and watched. The Ogres filed out in a long, thin line — War Fighters, War Generals, top-level War Lords — one after another. Their faces were tight with fury and shame. Zane had made it plain: starting tomorrow, any Ogre found inside the Tower would be killed on sight.
"That human actually did it," someone in the crowd murmured. "He pushed the entire Ogre race out of the Tower. By himself."
"Almost feel sorry for them."
"Don't. You wouldn't say that if you'd spent years under their boot."
The line moved north, toward the two teleportation arrays at the edge of the Cursed Land — one leading to the Flaming Place, one to the Dread Place. Most walked with their eyes forward, unwilling to look back. Only a handful of the top-level War Lords knew the truth: this wasn't finished. It was only finished if Zane survived the Dark Nether Cave.
[AMBIENT: TOWER PLATFORM]
[MUSIC: QUIET RESOLVE]
Zane watched from the platform, his expression calm.
The Tower of Sin was an amazing place to train. Zane was already a sixth-level War General. He figured he had plenty of time to get stronger before the Dark Nether Death Light weakened, which, according to his information, would be in about three to four months. As far as he knew, it happened every year, though the timing was never exact. But once the period began, the light would be harmless for at least a month.
I’m getting that inheritance from the Dark Nether Cave, he thought, his eyes narrowing with a flash of pleasure.*
[MUSIC: DARK AMBITION]
And then it won’t be long before I can return to The Heartlands.*
As he looked around, his gaze fell on Maurice, who was walking away. The Ogre turned his head, and a sinister smile stretched across his face.
A chill went down Zane’s spine, and he frowned.
Looks like the Ogres won’t give up that easily, he thought.
[SFX: ETHEREAL WHOOSH]
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL TOWER]
A moment later, Zane let his body sink, merging with the very essence of the tower. Since the whole structure was embedded upside down in the Cursed Land, what felt like the top floor was actually the first. He spiraled downward, a phantom passing through the first floor, the second, the tenth, all the way to the fifteenth.
Default victories and promotions were meaningless now. He had banished the Ogres, and with them, the old rules of the Tower of Sin.
[AMBIENT: VAST HALL]
[MUSIC: AUTHORITATIVE THEME]
Standing in the center of the vast hall on the fifteenth floor, Zane’s voice rang out, calm and clear.
"All seat holders, please come forward. It’s time to make some new rules.”
