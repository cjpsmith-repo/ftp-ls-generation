[MUSIC: TENSE STANDOFF]

Alex's condescending words hung in the air between them, sharp as drawn steel. Around the hall, disciples leaned forward, sensing the shift in atmosphere. Alex's tone carried no bluster—only cold certainty.

Zane met his eyes without flinching. "I wouldn't be standing here otherwise."

"Good." A thin smile crossed Alex's face, though it never reached his eyes. "I respect warriors who understand what they're walking into—even if they're walking toward their own defeat."

The words settled between them like frost on steel. Alex's gaze remained steady, measuring, calculating. But he could never rattle Zane. Even in families as small as the Lowells, friction ran deep. Rivalries festered. Ambition turned brother against brother.

Alex had clawed his way up from a third-rate house where survival meant proving your worth daily. Though heaven-level talent flowed through his veins, he'd learned early that natural gifts meant nothing without relentless effort. House Gresham was a major power—a behemoth where talented disciples were as common as autumn leaves. You couldn't coast on potential alone if you wanted to carve out a name for yourself. You had to seize it with both hands and refuse to let go.

Every scar, every sleepless night, every moment of doubt he'd conquered—all of it had forged him into the warrior standing before Zane now.

So despite viewing Zane as a weakling who'd stumbled into prominence through luck and spectacle, Alex refused to lower his guard. The world overflowed with warriors who'd been killed by opponents they'd underestimated—enough corpses to fill Hell ten times over. Alex was too intelligent, too disciplined to commit such a rookie mistake.

Zane's eyes brightened at Alex's words, and a genuine smile crossed his face. "Not bad," he said, his tone carrying unexpected approval.

The response caught Alex completely off guard. His brow furrowed in confusion. "What's that supposed to mean?" The praise felt wrong somehow—discordant with everything he'd expected from this confrontation. Had he misheard? Was Zane mocking him?

Alex's jaw tightened as he studied Zane's expression, searching for the trap hidden within those two simple words. In his experience, compliments from rivals were poisoned daggers wrapped in silk. Yet something in Zane's bearing suggested otherwise—a straightforwardness that felt almost foreign in the cutthroat world of cultivation politics.

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC TENSION]

Zane gave a confident chuckle. "You're smarter than the rest of those morons, I'll give you that. You understand that a lion uses its full strength, even when hunting a rabbit. So, I guess that makes you worthy of being my opponent."

[SFX: SUDDEN SILENCE]

Alex froze.

He'd expected Zane to say something weak, to maybe even beg for his life. If Alex had been in a good mood, he might have even spared the kid.

He never imagined Zane would compliment him. It was the complete opposite of what he'd expected, and Alex was having a hard time processing it.

Arrogant people usually have something to back it up. What did this kid have? Zane was only at the Fourth Level of the Soul Realm—a lamb to the slaughter.

Alex could handle a stronger opponent looking down on him. In the world of warriors, might makes right. If he was looked down on for being weak, he deserved it.

[MUSIC: RISING ANGER]

But being disdained by someone weaker? That was an insult Alex just couldn't stomach.

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMUR]

"Did you hear that?" a spectator gasped. "This kid—what, Fourth level of the Soul Realm?—just said Alex was 'qualified' to be his opponent!"

Another fighter barked out a laugh. "That's the funniest thing I've heard all day."

"Since when did Soul Realm newbies get so damn cocky at the Martial Tournament?" a third voice chimed in, dripping with contempt.

Zane's comment ignited the crowd. Oracle City's local fighters erupted in indignant shouts, their voices overlapping in a cacophony of disbelief and outrage. They'd seen countless arrogant cultivators pass through these tournaments, but this level of audacity from someone so young struck them as particularly galling.

Alex's face flushed crimson, the veins at his temples pulsing visibly. His jaw clenched so tightly that the muscles in his neck stood out like cords. "Great," he snarled, his voice low and dangerous. "You managed to piss me off with a single sentence. Now you get to deal with my wrath!"

[SFX: MAGICAL SWORD HUMS]

A sharp, crystalline hum cut through the air as two middle-grade Mythic weapons—one shimmering green, one brilliant gold—began to vibrate in his hands. The blades sang with anticipation, resonating with their master's killing intent.

[SFX: MAGICAL BARRIER DISSOLVING]

[MUSIC: INTENSE BATTLE THEME]

The arena's enchanted barrier vanished. The battle began.

"One move is all I'll need! Let me show you the Apex of Sword Intent!"

[SFX: SWORD WHOOSH]

[SFX: ENERGY CRACKLING]

Alex swung his swords, unleashing a cyclone of purple and gold sword intent that twisted together as he charged straight at Zane.

"Gresham's Blade Dance!"

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

But the dance was over before it started.

[SFX: SUDDEN IMPACT]

[AMBIENT: SHOCKED SILENCE]

The warriors in the stands fell silent. It had all happened in a flash, too fast for anyone to process. Even Alex had no time to react. Before darkness took him, his last conscious thought was a bewildered question: What just happened?

What Alex didn't realize was that he hadn't made a mistake. He was simply outclassed—and by a mile.

[AMBIENT: HIGH PLATFORM WIND]

Up on the high platform, Brent, a disciple from the Ethereal Spirit Sect, blinked. "Wow, he's really strong. I probably couldn't have beaten him back when I was at the Fourth level of the Soul Realm."

"Not necessarily," Philip, the eldest disciple from the sect, said, shaking his head. "I misjudged him. Zane used raw physical power—he's a body refiner. He must have some kind of secret technique that lets him shatter Alex's sword intent like that. If I remember right, you'd already grasped half of the Spatial Law back when you were at the Fourth level of the Soul Realm, hadn't you? He wouldn't have stood a chance against you then."

Being able to grasp half of the first level of the Spatial Law while still in the Soul Realm was an achievement that would put even old masters like Saint Morphens of Cloud Hall to shame.

"I'm not so sure about that," Brent replied, knowing his own limits.

Philip continued, "Brent, you're the most talented warrior in the history of The Heartlands, not to mention the only saint-level genius in our sect. Comparing yourself to a guy like Zane is a good way to lose your edge."

[MUSIC: DETERMINED THEME]

Brent grinned. "Alright, I hear you. But still, if that brat keeps winning, I just might have to teach him a lesson!"
