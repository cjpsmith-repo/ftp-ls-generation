[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMURS]
As Johanson stood motionless, silently reciting ’The Assassin’s Ten Commandments’ to himself, Zane dramatically collapsed onto the ground with an exaggerated sigh. His eyes twinkled with mischief as he called out to the referee, "Hey, Ref! Since my fancy opponent refuses to make a move, do you mind if I just sit here and save my energy?"
The referee in the arena stared at Zane, bewildered by his behavior. A flicker of uncertainty crossed his face—after all, every warrior possessed their unique fighting style, and the rules didn’t explicitly forbid sitting down mid-match. He shifted uncomfortably, at a complete loss for how to address Zane’s unusual question.
[SFX: CROWD LAUGHTER]
Zane had deliberately pitched his voice to carry throughout the arena, making sure every spectator could hear him. A ripple of laughter spread through the crowd, punctuated by scattered applause and a few supportive shouts.
Of all the fights in the All Peaks Competition, Zane’s matches had swiftly become legendary for their entertainment value. His unpredictable tactics and irreverent attitude generated more excitement than nearly any other competitor combined, eclipsing even the renowned Finn in terms of spectator enthusiasm.
Given that Zane was only at the Second Level of the Adept Realm, the skill gap between him and the other contestants was massive. Every disciple was dying to see how he’d handle himself in the tournament.
Plus, Zane’s last match against Adrian had been an absolute rollercoaster. The disciples were hungry for another wild fight.
[AMBIENT: RESTLESS CROWD]
This battle, however, was failing to live up to expectations. From the moment the combatants stepped onto the stage, an oppressive silence had fallen between them. They merely stood facing one another, each launching only a single lackluster attack that barely disturbed the air.
Not a single meaningful blow had been exchanged, no techniques displayed, no power demonstrated. The anticipated clash had devolved into an exercise in motionless tedium, leaving the once-eager crowd shifting restlessly in their seats. There was no denying it: so far, the fight was unbelievably dull.  Zane’s comment echoed the collective frustration rippling through the audience, his words dripping with deliberate accusation.
His casual posture contrasted sharply with Johanson’s rigid stance, making it abundantly clear who he blamed for turning what should have been an electrifying confrontation into this unbearable stalemate.
[AMBIENT: CROWD COMPLAINTS]
"What the hell? What’s with this Johanson guy?" one disciple complained. "Zane’s just sitting there on the ground, and Johanson doesn’t have the nerve to attack him!"
"Is this even a fight? I’m about to fall asleep," another disciple muttered. "Seriously, a personal disciple of a saint? He must be all talk if he can’t even face Zane! He’s not one of the top five, he’s more like bottom five!" a third one scoffed.
"Right? Maybe he’s the most cowardly personal disciple ever," another chimed in. "He finally shows up and doesn’t even have the guts to fight? Why doesn’t he just forfeit? I’m getting tired of waiting for some action!"
Zane’s taunt was all it took. The crowd’s grumbling about Johanson grew louder and more dissatisfied.
Watching it all unfold, the other personal disciples, including Patrick and Tracy, just smiled.
They knew exactly what Johanson was doing. His ambition was to become a master assassin, and assassins don’t fight head-on like some brawler. Asking an assassin to do that was like telling an archer to charge headfirst into cavalry.
Assassins specialized in the one-hit kill. Johanson might only attack once, but that single strike would decide the entire match.
That’s what made fighting an assassin such a drag to watch. Anyone who understood this knew that a silent battle was far more dangerous than a flashy one. This wasn’t a contest of flashy moves; it was a war of wills.
From the start, Johanson had played his part perfectly. At least, in the eyes of Tracy, Patrick, and the other top disciples, his performance was flawless. He had concealed his presence so well that neither Zane nor even they could get a read on him.
Johanson was the leopard, and Zane, his antelope.
[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSE]
What none of them could see was that Johanson, buried somewhere in the shadows, was barely holding himself together. The crowd's insults rang through the arena and into his hiding spot like needles. He wanted to rush out and silence every last one of them. It was all Zane's fault.
Then Zane, still sitting on the ground, closed his eyes.
[SFX: EERIE STILLNESS]
The sheer audacity of it — resting, eyes shut, as if Johanson weren't even worth staying awake for — sent a fresh wave of fury through him. And then, as if that weren't enough, Zane turned his head slightly and seemed to look directly at Johanson's hiding spot.
Johanson's heart lurched. He clamped down hard on the surge of emotion and went back to reciting the Assassin’s Ten Commandments. Restrain. Restrain. But the crowd kept going, and Zane kept sitting there with his eyes closed, and the anger kept building.
He couldn't take it anymore.
An almost tangible wave of killing intent washed over Zane.
[SFX: DARK ENERGY PULSE]
But this killing intent was different. The force of his very soul was woven into it.
Even a warrior in the Soul Realm would have trouble withstanding Johanson's attack. Zane was only in the Second Level of the Adept Realm. There was no way he could handle a killing intent laced with a soul attack.
[MUSIC: TENSE ANTICIPATION]
Johanson had done his homework on Zane. He'd watched the entire fight against Adrian and had noted the detail Adrian mentioned—that one of Zane’s trump cards was a soul attack.
But Zane was just a kid in the Grade Two of the Adept Realm. How strong could his soul possibly be?
Johanson, on the other hand, had consumed a soul-nurturing herb every single day since childhood. The price of a single one of those herbs was astronomical, costing over a hundred crystal cubes.
Although the Empire had destroyed the Shadow Group, the organization was still incredibly wealthy, and they had poured most of that fortune into Johanson, their only hope.
Even the heirs to the Great Houses couldn’t afford a soul-nurturing herb every day. Sure, their families had the money, but they’d never pour all their resources into one person. The risk was just too high.
But Johanson’s situation was unique. He was the Shadow Group’s only hope in nearly three hundred years—the only true prodigy to emerge from their ranks.
Having nourished his soul with those herbs for so many years, Johanson was confident it was far more powerful than that of any warrior at his level.
That’s why he gave Zane’s supposed soul attack no thought at all. In his mind, there was no comparison between their souls.
[SFX: DARK ENERGY PULSE]
He was certain his killing intent alone could reduce Zane to an empty, soulless husk.
Let’s see how you handle this, Zane Lowell, Johanson scoffed.
