Ep 754 - Law of the Phoenix

Word count: 1069

[AMBIENT: BATTLEFIELD]

[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]

In most wars between factions, the real fight came down to a handful of elite warriors. As long as he was left standing, Maddox was more than enough to win this war on his own.

But winning the battle didn't mean he controlled the war. Typically, warriors were expected to fight their direct counterparts. Maddox had already won the fight between the top brass. But then, in the blink of an eye, Cora had jumped in and wiped out every last mid- and low-level warrior from the Corrupted Divine Kingdom. 

His face twisted with rage. "You're asking to die, woman!" 

This time, he didn't hesitate. He shot his hand toward Cora, ready to crush her with a single move.

[SFX: MAGICAL WHOOSH]

Cora just blinked, a flicker of contempt crossing her face. She reached out, pulled Zane and Tia close, and in the next instant, all three of them vanished.

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

"The Violet Power Realm?" Maddox sneered. "Not as impressive as you think!"

He understood the Violet Core Physique—he'd helped Marilyn activate hers. But Marilyn could manage only a hundred swords. Cora, supposedly early Inner-Core Realm, had conjured tens of thousands. Impossible.

[SFX: ENERGY SURGE]

Still sneering, Maddox thrust his arm toward the spot where she'd vanished. He figured she was hiding in the Violet Power Realm, connected to their world by a spatial anchor. If he could shatter that anchor, he could drag her right back out. He'd seen Zane do exactly that to Marilyn at the Martial Tournament — using the Law of Space to pinpoint the anchor and force her into the open.

He expected to do the same. His hand closed on empty air.

He frowned. How is that possible?

[SFX: RAPID IMPACTS]

He threw another punch. Then another. Dozens in quick succession, each one targeting a different point in the space around him, searching for the node. His sharp eyes swept the void.

Then they lit up. There — front left.

He rushed toward it.

The node vanished.

"Idiot," Cora's voice drifted from nowhere.

[MUSIC: RISING FRUSTRATION]

Maddox said nothing. He wasn't going to bicker with her. But the node was gone again, and for the first time since the battle had started, something close to agitation was creeping into his chest.

He couldn't leave empty-handed. He'd brought over a hundred thousand elite warriors to Oracle City—all annihilated in seconds by a girl in a white dress. After such a catastrophic loss, who would dare join him? Zane was his only remaining prize.

Cora's cool voice echoed from nowhere. "I thought you said I was asking to die? Or was that just talk? Come on. Give it a try."

[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL VOID]

[MUSIC: ETHEREAL STRINGS]

Inside the Violet Power Realm, the sky was an endless field of giant swords, suspended in perfect rows from horizon to horizon. The rules here were entirely different from the outside world — a broken realm with its own logic, its own silence.

Cora set Zane down on the ground and laid him flat. Then she reached over and pulled Tia away from him. She wasn't a generous woman. Even with Tia's body cold and motionless, seeing the two of them wrapped around each other sent a slow, unwelcome feeling rising in her chest.

She knelt beside Zane and took his hands. They were cold. She held them anyway.

"Lavender," she said, then stopped. She tried again, more carefully. "Sister Lavender."

[SFX: SOFT ENERGY]

After a moment, small points of red light gathered and took shape. Lavender looked at Cora briefly, then turned to Zane. Her face was tight with worry. "What is it?"

"Is he going to die?" Cora asked. Her voice came out smaller than she intended.

"He's dead," Lavender said flatly.

Cora's mouth opened. She started to speak and couldn't find the words. The grief hit her all at once, sudden and messy.

Lavender watched her for a moment. Then she snorted. "I lied to you. It's hard to say right now. All we can do is wait."

[MUSIC: FRAGILE HOPE]

Cora gripped Zane's hand tighter and didn't let go.

The Celestial Fragment was still glowing faintly against his chest. It wasn't a cure — it wasn't designed to be. What it carried was something far more specific. The Life and Death Wind ran on a single thread of causality: life is the cause, death is the effect. To break it, you needed a counter-law — a different thread of cause and effect running in the opposite direction.

Law of the Phoenix: death causes rebirth.

No one had ever truly destroyed a phoenix, because their existence was governed by that single indestructible law. Death was not an end for them. It was the beginning of the next life. Only a stronger thread of causality could ever unravel it.

Whether the fragment held enough of that law to reach Zane, neither Lavender nor the Cobalt Dragon could say.

[SFX: ENERGY PULSE]

A faint energy flowed from the Celestial Fragment and into Zane's body, his consciousness latching onto it. He was in some kind of trance, analyzing the raw information carried within that power.

The sheer volume of it was overwhelming.

[MUSIC: COSMIC WONDER]

A single Cosmic Age. Three-point-six-billion years of history, all recorded. Everything that had ever happened across a hundred thousand worlds, countless stars, and the vast outer lands — it was all there. 

His own world only had a written history stretching back a few tens of thousands of years. Before that, nothing but a blank slate. And that was just a tiny fraction of three-point-six-billion years.

And this wasn't even the whole of the heavens — just a single fragment. Worse, he'd only managed to absorb a minuscule fraction of its energy.

What am I supposed to know? Why am I supposed to know it? his consciousness wondered.

He was utterly alone. There was no one to guide him, no way to reach the Cobalt Dragon or Lavender. He had to figure this out by himself.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS ETHEREAL]

As he kept sifting through the data, he started to sense something else — an emotion, buried deep within the fragment.

The heavens have emotions? The thought struck him as bizarre.

He remembered Lavender telling him this wasn't a piece of their heavens, but a remnant of the one that came before. And from this tiny piece, Zane could feel an overwhelming sense of helplessness and sorrow.

They said the heavens completed a cycle every three-point-six-billion years. If that was true, then why did the last one shatter?

Who broke it?

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL CRESCENDO]

The question burned in his mind as he sank deeper into the fragment's power.
