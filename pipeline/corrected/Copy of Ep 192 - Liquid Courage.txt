Ep 192 - Liquid Courage

[AMBIENT: MOUNTAIN CLIMBING]
[SFX: GROWING TENSION]
Zane tried not to think about the dozens of people who'd gathered to watch him climb, who might now be watching as he plummeted to his death. 
"How the hell is this cliff face so smooth?" he muttered through gritted teeth, his fingers searching desperately for any imperfection in the seemingly flawless surface.
Despite its appearance from a distance, certain sections of the mountain wall remained untouched, pristine stone. However, the more frequently traveled portions told a different story. As disciples from Misty Peak climbed the wall again and again, their persistent hands and determined feet gradually wore away at the stone, leaving behind subtle marks that, with the passage of time, deepened into tiny hollows and crevices.
Through diligent practice and painful trial, disciples learned to identify these nearly invisible holds. Mastering this skill allowed them to ascend ever higher across the vertical terrain. The most popular climbing routes had evolved into an intricate network of these deepening pockets, a secret map known only to those who had paid for the knowledge with sweat and blood.
To the untrained eye gazing up from below, the cliff appeared impossibly sheer and forbidding. Yet up close, the reality revealed itself—a surface delicately pockmarked with small indentations, each one a potential lifeline. Some veteran disciples, having conquered the face hundreds of times, had committed every single divot to memory, the pattern etched into their minds as clearly as the stone itself.
[MUSIC: TENSE DETERMINATION]
Experience was everything on this wall. And Zane, who was just muscling his way up, had none. Everyone watching figured he was in for a rough time.
Flynn had made it one hundred fifty-five feet up by being agile, using every tiny crack and foothold he could find. It was a known fact that far fewer climbers reached the summit than started, so the higher you went, the fewer footholds there were. They also grew fainter, harder to spot the farther up you climbed.
Though Zane lacked the veteran climbers' intimate knowledge of the rock face's subtle handholds, he possessed something far more valuable: extraordinary speed. Where others relied on meticulous familiarity with each weathered depression, Zane's advantage lay in his lightning-quick reflexes and explosive movement. 
As he ascended, a fierce determination gleamed in his eyes—the same resolve that had carried him through countless impossible situations since his father's murder. The familiar warmth of the Phoenix Crystal pulsed through his veins, transforming what should have been a novice's clumsy ascent into something that left onlookers speechless.
[SFX: RAPID CLIMBING SOUNDS]
 The energy from the Phoenix Crystal was a game-changer. His hands slapped against the rock face in a blur, his feet finding holds with the same blinding quickness.
Zane knew that with his current strength, he could just carve his own grips into the stone and haul himself to the top. But that would be cheating, so he held back. His control was so precise that his restraint was invisible. As he slapped and stepped, his speed, impossibly, began to increase.
[SFX: CROWD EXCLAMATION]
 "Whoa, look! Zane's speeding up again! No way!" shouted one of the outer disciples from below.
"Are you serious? How is that even possible?" another called out, completely baffled. "That makes no sense!"
Normally, disciples got slower the higher they climbed. They were strongest at the bottom, using their initial burst of energy, but with nothing to push off of mid-air, their momentum would die, and the climb would become a grueling crawl.
You can't just ignore the laws of physics.
But Zane was already halfway up the wall, and he was still accelerating.
What did that mean? It meant Zane didn't need to build momentum from the ground. He didn't need a running start. He could climb with the same explosive energy whether he was a hundred feet up, three hundred, or even three thousand. It was as if there was no difference to him between scaling a vertical cliff and sprinting on flat ground.
For Zane, this test wasn't a challenge at all.
[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT ASCENSION]
 He blew past the hundred-foot mark, then one-fifty, accelerating all the while.

He didn't stop at one-fifty. He kept going.
Two hundred feet. The old handprint was somewhere below him now.
When he finally felt like stopping, he drew back his arm and slammed his palm flat against the stone.
[SFX: STONE CRACKING]
The impact left a deep, clean print in the rock face. Then he let go and dropped straight down, landing without breaking stride.
He walked over to Flynn and held out the weighted vest.
"Thanks for the loan," Zane said pleasantly. "I've embarrassed myself."
Flynn took it without a word. His face said everything.
[SFX: CROWD CHEERING]
Even though he wasn't one of their own, the onlookers couldn't help but marvel at his incredible performance.
[AMBIENT: QUIET SECLUDED AREA]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS UNDERTONES]
Later, at a secluded pavilion, Zane reached into his pack with an easy, fluid motion. He pulled out a small cup. No one else was around, and it was unlikely any other disciple from Misty Peak would recognize the liquid anyway. Still, Zane discreetly covered the cup with his hand before passing it to Dustin.
[SFX: SHARP INTAKE OF BREATH]
 Dustin stared down at its contents, and his eyes went wide. "Celestial Essence?!" he whispered, his voice tight with shock.
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS REVELATION]
 He remembered it clearly. When Zane had auctioned off the crystal core, someone else had been selling Celestial Essence. A tiny amount had sold for one hundred and twenty thousand crystals. If one of the bidders hadn't dropped out, the price would have just kept climbing.
"This looks like at least as much as what sold at The Blackwood Auction House!" Dustin's voice trembling with excitement. He had no idea where Zane could have gotten his hands on something so rare. "How many drops do you have there?"
Zane smiled calmly. "Fifty-five droplets." 
Taking a deep, shaky breath, Dustin nodded. "Well, that explains the secrecy. If I were you, I'd be even more careful."
Zane chuckled. "I wouldn't be this worried if this was the only cup I had. I need your help, Dustin. I want you to help me auction this off, but in batches, over time."
Dustin froze. "Wait, what are you saying? In batches? You mean… you have more than this?" he asked, sucking in a sharp breath of cold air.
Celestial Essence was an incredibly mysterious substance; no one even knew how it was formed. The batch from the auction had been a fluke—a chance discovery by a few warriors.
But Zane was implying he had more. So much more that it had to be sold off slowly, piece by piece.
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC TENSION]
 What in the world did that mean?
