Ep 1307 - Your Little Friends

Word count: 1058

[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
Jeffrey’s meditation didn’t take long—by the time he rejoined the others, everyone was already talking about what came next.
After what he’d learned at the First Beacon, Jeffrey was itching to put it all into practice. It was the only thing on his mind—even more important than racking up Illusion Points.
“Guess it’s time for me to head out,” Hailey said, her tone making it clear she had no intention of sticking around the City of Ogres. “I’ll see you around.”
And just like that, their team was done. It had been a short run, but they were all grateful for it. All good things, right?
[SFX: SOFT PURRING]
As everyone else was saying their goodbyes, Janet just stood there, quietly stroking the little flame kitten in her arms. When the chatter died down, she looked up and found everyone’s eyes on her. She met Zane’s gaze, saw the unspoken question about what she was going to do next, and tilted her head with a smile. “I’ll just follow you,” she said.
“Why?” Zane asked, the confusion plain on his face. He’d never been good at hiding what he was thinking.
Janet’s smile just got sweeter. “No special reason,” she said. “I just want to watch you beat up other warriors, that’s all.”
Zane was used to being on his own, but he didn’t really mind the company. He had a feeling the Illusion Battlefield was about to change in a big way, and he wanted to get in as many fights as he could before it did. Arguing with Janet would be a waste of energy he needed for the endgame, so he just let it go. She could tag along.
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS UNDERTONE]
A small crowd of cultivators drifted over, humans mixed in with a few other races—but the one leading them was Demon Night. Green, curly hair, slender eyes like willow leaves, a blood-red mole between his brows, and a voice dripping with something oily and casual.
“Well, what a coincidence,” he said. “Isn’t this Phantom?”
Hailey went rigid. She turned, her expression gone dark. “Slade... it’s you.” Her voice shook.
He strolled straight up to her and, in front of everyone, slid his hand along her cheek without a shred of hesitation.
[MUSIC: DARK TENSION]
Hailey didn’t fight him off. She just stood there, her face twisting, murder plain in her eyes.
“Been a few months,” Slade said, shaking his head, his eyes traveling inappropriately over her body. “Still haven’t grown any bigger. Disappointing.” 
“You bastard!”
Something in Hailey finally cracked.
The whole team had fought side-by-side with Hailey in the Hall, but they knew next to nothing about her. She kept her past private, and they respected that. What had just happened made one thing obvious, though: whatever hold Slade had over her, it was real—why else would she put up with the way he was acting? 
Zane didn’t care about the details. All he knew was that Hailey was his friend. She’d had his back, and now that she was in trouble, he was going to have hers. It was that simple.
He started to move, but then stopped. 
Jeffrey and Janet were already stepping up to stand with Hailey. A second later, even Coleman and Ansel—usually the most timid of the bunch—moved forward to back her up.
Slade watched them line up behind Hailey, his eyes scanning over each of them with pure disdain. 
He sized them up in a second and dismissed them just as fast. His gaze landed back on Hailey, trying to stare her down, before it dropped to her chest again. “So,” he drawled, looking at her out of the corner of his eye, “these are the little friends who made it through the Hall with you, huh?”
[MUSIC: TENSE CONFRONTATION]
Before Hailey could answer, a hand landed on her shoulder. She turned to see Zane, his face a complete blank as he stared past her at Slade. 
He gave her a slight nod, a silent order to step back, and then moved forward to put himself between them. He was the one who answered Slade’s question. “Yeah,” Zane said. “You got a problem with that?”
“I see.” Slade’s eyes widened in mock surprise, a sneer spreading across his face. “So what’s this? All of you planning to play hero for her?”
“Not all of us. Just me,” Zane said, his voice flat. “And this isn’t about her. I just don’t like you. You talk too much, and you’re annoying. I figure it’s time someone shut you up.”
Zane’s expression never flickered. He sounded perfectly calm, but the words were pure ice. 
“Shut me up?” Slade barked out a laugh. “Are you serious? You’re a funny guy, you know that?”
Slade’s crew found it hilarious, too, breaking out into the kind of laughter you’d hear in an asylum. They were a mixed bunch—humans, Demon Night, and Twilight folk—a temporary team just like Zane’s, cobbled together in the Hall. And now they all joined in.
“If you want to die so bad, just step outside the city,” one of them jeered. “I’ll be happy to do it for you. Nice and slow.”
Another one snorted. “I don’t think there’s a single Ace Talent in their whole group.”
“Ha! You’re right! Just a bunch of cowards.”
“Don’t get your hopes up, Slade,” another chimed in. “I bet they won’t even leave the city. They’ll just hide in here like the rats they are.”
Slade’s sneer sharpened. “Later, outside the city, I’m going to kill you three times over,” he told Hailey, “and take every last one of your points while I’m at it.” Then his eyes slid back to Zane. “As for you—I wonder if you’ve even got the nerve to leave the city and fight me at all.”
The insults finally got to Janet and Jeffrey. 
[MUSIC: SOMBER PROTECTIVE]
Their faces hardened, and Jeffrey took a half-step forward, ready to fire back, but Hailey put a hand on his arm, stopping him.
“Please, don’t,” Hailey said, her voice low. “I can handle him. Just leave this to me.” She tried to force a reassuring smile. “Slade’s an Ace Talent from my own race. He’s on a whole other level… and he’s the son of Supreme Lord Gunter.”
 She didn’t want to drag them into this.
