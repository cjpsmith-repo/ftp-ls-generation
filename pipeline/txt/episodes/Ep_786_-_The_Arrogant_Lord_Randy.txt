Ep 786 - The Arrogant Lord Randy

Word count: 1006

[AMBIENT: TENSE ARENA]

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

The first round had taught the remaining Spirit-Tribulation warriors two crucial lessons: be fast and stay under the radar.

Everyone was sizing each other up again, mentally cataloging the heavy hitters. The consensus was clear: gang up on the strongest fighters first. This put the famous, top-tier warriors in a bind. Their reputations, usually a source of pride, had just become targets on their backs. In a free-for-all like this, the best strategy was to seem like no threat at all—to hang back and simply survive.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS DRUMS]

At the top of everyone's hit list was Lord Randy. Though he wasn't from the Crystal Martial House, he was a prince of the Harlen Divine Kingdom — younger brother to the Crown Prince himself — and had already reached the Soul Sea Realm before the age of twenty, a feat that put him in a class of his own. The question hanging in the air was simple: if all twenty-nine other warriors ganged up on him, could he actually win?

Lord Randy, of course, wasn't blind to the dozens of calculating stares fixed on him. Their intentions were practically written on their faces.

[SFX: CONFIDENT SNORT]

[MUSIC: HEROIC BRASS]

 He snorted. “Tricks are for the weak,” he muttered. He was representing his title on this journey to the Sacred Feather Sea. He had to crush every obstacle and pass this test, or he’d bring shame upon the royal family. A pack of second-rate fighters wasn’t about to be the thing that stopped him.

Zane took in the scene with a detached sort of interest. As a complete unknown at the middle stage of the Spirit-Tribulation Realm, he was already flying under everyone’s radar. He had every intention of keeping it that way.

[SFX: AMUSED CHUCKLE]

 “Looks like the kids have figured out the game,” one of the Soul Sea Realm masters chuckled from the sidelines.

“I just hope we get some real talent this time,” another added. “Last year’s batch was the worst the kingdom’s ever seen. If that happens again, we’re in real trouble. The Crystal Martial House needs to deliver.”

Their concern was justified. The martial houses were funded by the divine kingdoms for one purpose: to train elites capable of retrieving treasures from the Sacred Feather Sea. A bad year meant a smaller cut for the kingdom, and no one wanted that. 

[MUSIC: TENSE DRUMS]

 Round after round rolled by. The second was faster than the first, the third faster still. By the seventh round, the pattern was locked in — the strongest fighters went out first, the careful ones survived, and the circle shrank on anyone foolish enough to wait too long.

The three Soul Sea masters watched each batch with quiet attention. When the seventh round's names were called and "Luo Tianxing" was among them, the masters exchanged a look.

"Solid foundation," one said. "Strength comparable to late-stage Spirit-Tribulation, maybe more."

"He gives me a faint pressure," another agreed. "He won't be beaten easily."

"I think he can actually defeat a late-stage Spirit-Tribulation warrior outright," the third said.

It was a generous assessment — in the four Divine Kingdoms, a Spirit-Tribulation warrior beating a Life-and-Death Realm expert was practically unheard of. Even so, they were still underestimating him.

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMUR]

 Zane stepped forward when his alias was called. The warriors around him gave him a brief glance and looked away. Middle-stage Spirit-Tribulation, unknown face — no threat. They had bigger problems.

The last name on the list was Randy's.

The twenty-eight warriors already in the circle couldn't help but tense. Everyone knew what it meant. There was only one play: unite and take him out first. But when Randy stepped calmly to the center of the circle — the same spot that had gotten the gorilla warrior eliminated in round one — and looked around at them all with quiet contempt, that plan started to waver.

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC CRESCENDO]

 "There will be one winner in this round," he said. "Me. I'll drive all twenty-nine of you out myself."

A ripple of unease moved through the circle. United, they might have a chance — but might wasn't the same as would. And if they committed to a combined assault and it failed, they'd have spent everything on one throw.

Zane shook his head slightly, watching the others’ reactions. He had to give him credit—Lord Randy was sharp. He knew his reputation made him an unavoidable target, so trying to lay low would be pointless. Instead, he was leaning into it, weaponizing his arrogance to intimidate everyone else into backing down. It was a classic power play. If his opponents were too afraid to work together, they’d never be a real threat. That kind of supreme confidence wasn't just an act; it was rooted in genuine strength. When everyone already knows you can back it up, they don't question the attitude—they just fear the consequences.

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMUR]

[SFX: BATTLE GONG]

[MUSIC: INTENSE COMBAT THEME]

 A moment later, the next round began.

A low buzz of speculation rippled through the fighters.

“So, who’s out first? Think anyone’s actually dumb enough to go for Lord Randy?”

“Not a chance. He’s way too strong. That’s a suicide run.”

“My money’s on that new kid. Middle-stage Spirit-Tribulation? Seriously? What a joke.”

“Yeah, look at the guy. He’s built like a twig. One solid hit and he’s going flying!”

“Oh, look! Someone’s already taking the bait. Guess that’s a wrap for him.”

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMURS]

[MUSIC: TENSE DRUMS]

 The sentiment spread through the group, a shared smirk at the thought of an easy first elimination. 

[SFX: BATTLE CRY]

 The warrior who’d singled him out barreled forward, yelling, “You’re first on the chopping block, pipsqueak!”

A flash of scorn crossed Zane’s eyes, but he smothered it just as quickly. He didn’t bother fighting back. 

[SFX: QUICK FOOTSTEPS]

 As the warrior charged, Zane simply darted along the edge of the red circle, sidestepping the attack with almost casual ease.
