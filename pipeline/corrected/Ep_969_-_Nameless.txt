Ep 969 - Nameless
Word count: 1115
[AMBIENT: COZY INTERIOR]
[MUSIC: GENTLE STRINGS]
Zane’s eyes tracked Cora as she landed — she looked like a different person from the exhausted figure who had flown in moments ago.
Cora practically floated into the room, a serene smile playing on her lips. In a sweet, melodic voice, she asked the old man, “Sir, would you care for a cup of mulled cider?”
The old man chuckled, his eyes crinkling at the soft timber of her voice, and gave a warm nod.
His wife was the kind and considerate Elder Apeiron. Cora had just become her student the night before, so treating him with respect was the right thing to do. Still, she hoped Zane wouldn’t catch on that she was trying to get a leg up in her training.
Pleased, Cora went to prepare a pot of cider. She poured a cup and handed it to the old man, whose face lit up with delight.
He let out a booming laugh. “You’re a good kid! None of my other students are half as thoughtful as you!”
He was sure his wife would be grateful for such a talented student. And seeing her sweet, well-mannered nature only confirmed he’d made the right choice.
But his ability to read minds also showed him the quiet bitterness she carried from Tia and Zane’s wedding the day before. He felt a pang of sympathy for the lovesick girl, which was why he’d made a point to praise her so highly in front of Zane—for all the good it might do.
Cora smiled gratefully before preparing another cup. She offered it with a steady hand to Zane, then stepped back, giving the two men space to talk.
Zane took a small sip, the warm cider doing little to ease the tension in his shoulders. “Yesterday, I was grateful to receive your—”
The old man waved a hand. “Vorn and the others have already returned to the Demi-Immortal Race. That green pill will double your cultivation speed with half the effort. And don’t worry about the Tower of Sin — we’re leaving it here. It’s not particularly useful to us anyway.”
Zane blinked. He hadn’t asked any of that yet.
He tried again. “About the Tower of Sin agreement—”
“Already handled.”
A beat of silence. Zane glanced at Cora. She had a hand over her mouth.
He made one last attempt. “Could you at least tell me why—”
“No reason. Fateless people walk their own path. That’s all there is to it.”
Zane closed his mouth and took a long sip of cider. Cora gave up trying to hide her laughter.
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE PIANO]
The old man’s tone shifted, growing heavier. “I’ve given it a lot of thought. Anyone without a set destiny has to forge their own path. You have the talent and strength to command the world of chaos, but it’s not your birthright. The choice has to be yours. Nobody should interfere with a man’s fate or his future.”
Zane took another sip of cider in silence. It was almost the same thing Yolande had told him not so long ago.
She’d been willing to go to war with the Demi-Immortal Race to keep Kade by her side—and Kade was the one who had actually earned the Silver Berserker Rank. Yet, she’d sworn she wouldn’t meddle in Zane’s affairs. Even after he’d obtained the Celestial Fragment, she hadn’t batted an eye, leaving the decision entirely in his hands.
[MUSIC: MELANCHOLIC PIANO]
Zane was truly free to choose. But instead of feeling liberated, a seed of doubt lingered. He felt adrift, wishing someone—anyone—would just point him in the right direction.
[MUSIC: WARM INSPIRATIONAL]
He hadn’t voiced any of this, but the old man patted him on the shoulder, a comforting weight. “You can’t walk the same path as the legends of old, nor can you predict the paths of those to come. No one has the right to point the way for you—not even a Supreme Lord! But remember this: whether I take you to the Demi-Immortal Race or not, whether your blood is changed or not… you are already one of us. I trust you haven’t forgotten the promise you made in the Tower of Sin.”
[SFX: CHAIR SCRAPING]
With that, the old man stood and waved the porcelain cup in his hands. “May I have another cup of mulled cider?” he asked Cora.
[MUSIC: PLAYFUL FLUTE]
Cora smiled and walked over to refill his cup. After the old man downed his third cup in a single gulp, he glanced at the Ancestral Dragon weaving around her feet. “Oh, I almost forgot. We’ve decided to leave the Ancestral Dragon here with you. Okay, I’ve got to run! See you next time!”
Zane stayed quiet, figuring it was useless to try to get a word in.
So it was Cora, not him, who replied.
She smiled gently. “Sir, what is your name? If you don’t mind my asking.”
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL WIND]
[SFX: ETHEREAL WHISPER]
The old man tilted his head, a playful smile on his lips. His mouth moved, but no sound came out. Instead, a faint, invisible ripple of Vitality washed over Cora, carrying a single word.
[SFX: MAGICAL FLASH]
He flashed and was gone.
Cora understood. Nameless. The old man’s name was Nameless. No wonder he’d been so cagey about it with Zane.
Zane turned to Cora. “What did he tell you? What’s his name?”
Cora’s mouth curved. “I’m not telling you.”
“What? You have to tell me.”
“No,” she said simply, her eyes bright with mischief, and took a slow step backward.
[SFX: QUICK FOOTSTEPS]
Zane crossed the room in two strides, caught her by the wrist, and pulled her in. She laughed — a rare, unguarded sound — and stopped trying to get away.
He never did find out.
[AMBIENT: PEACEFUL MONASTERY]
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
In the days that followed, Zane split his time between his training, Tia, and Cora. About two weeks later, he stepped out of Cloud Hall and gazed toward the east.
The Eastern Territories.
Four years had passed in a flash since he’d first set foot on the Cloud Road.
For most warriors, four years was a blink of an eye. But he’d only been twenty back then. Four years was a fifth of his life, a period of time that had turned his entire world upside down. He had been on the move from the moment he left home, never finding a moment’s peace. Now, with a rare pocket of calm, he knew what he had to do.
[MUSIC: DETERMINED ORCHESTRAL SWELL]
“It’s time to go back,” he muttered to himself. “Time to go back to the Skyreach Sect.”
