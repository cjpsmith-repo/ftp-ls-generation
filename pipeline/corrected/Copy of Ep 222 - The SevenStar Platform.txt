Ep 222 - The Seven-Star Platform
Word count: 1060

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS MAGICAL]
[AMBIENT: BUSY MARKETPLACE]
Zane reached into his storage ring and produced a small crystal vial filled with luminous golden liquid.
Dustin's eyes widened at the sight, his gaze transfixed on the precious substance that had caused such a bidding frenzy at the auction house. His fingers twitched with barely contained excitement as Zane casually handed him the bottle containing one hundred drops of the rare essence.
Turns out, Zane had more than enough Celestial Essence on him. He'd started with over six hundred drops. After giving fifty to Dustin for the auction, he still had more than five hundred. Forging the Streamer had cost him another hundred or so. Now, with the bottle of one hundred he was holding, Zane had a solid four hundred drops left.
"So, since it's so popular, should I split this into two batches of fifty, or sell all hundred at once? What do you think?" Zane asked curiously.
"All at once! Definitely all at once!" Dustin blurted out, then immediately clapped a hand over his mouth. His chubby head swiveled around, checking if anyone had overheard. The last thing he needed was for word to get out that Zane was his supplier.
Zane had to chuckle at how cautious Dustin was. "Relax," he said. "Nobody's paying attention to us." Zane's spiritual senses were way stronger than any other outer disciple's from Misty Peak---including Dustin. Even with his eyes closed, he could tell if someone was eavesdropping.
"If a weaponsmith wants to upgrade a low-grade mythic weapon to a medium-grade one, fifty drops of Celestial Essence might not cut it," Dustin explained. "So when they're forging, no smith is gonna hold back a single drop. They just throw everything they have into the furnace at once. The more Celestial Essence they use, the better their chances."
He continued. "For example, using fifty drops to upgrade a low-grade mythic weapon only gives you, like, a twenty percent chance of success. But if you use a hundred drops, the odds jump way up."
[AMBIENT: BUSY CROWD]
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE]
"Ugh, why are so many people here?" Zane muttered to himself, his spirits sinking. He'd already traveled for days to get here, so he figured he might as well wait and practice.  He hadn't expected a line this long. With a sigh, he trudged to the back of the line.
The Seven-Star Platform was built by the Skyreach Sect about six years ago. The story goes that a northern clan discovered a massive glacial heartstone, a hundred feet high and three hundred feet wide. When the Skyreach Sect heard about it, they bought it for a fortune.
The heartstone was so gigantic that even the Sect's strongest masters couldn't move it in one piece. They ended up having to cut the massive stone into six pieces, load each one onto a giant Skycruiser, and bring them back to build the Seven-Star Platform.
After that, the Sect's magic array masters laid enchantments inside the platform. Powered by the glacial heartstones, it became a place where disciples could dramatically improve their swordsmanship.
While the platform could be used for practicing with all kinds of weapons, like spears and staves, it didn't take long for people to realize it was most effective for sword skills. And since most disciples in the Skyreach Sect were swordsmen anyway, the Seven-Star Platform eventually became dedicated almost exclusively to sword practice.
If you showed up with a saber, you wouldn't even get in the door.
[AMBIENT: CROWD MURMURING]
As Zane got closer, he saw the crowd was split into two lines. One was incredibly long; the other, much shorter. He frowned. "Why two lines? Is this like the entrance exam, where kids with pull from the noble houses get to cut?"
The long line had at least a hundred people, all waiting for the disciples currently inside to finish. It was obviously going to be a brutal wait. On top of that, the All Peaks Competition was just around the corner, so nearly every disciple in the Sect was training their butts off, making the lines even longer than usual.
What Zane didn't know was that some people in that line had already been waiting for a full day---some even longer.
The other line, though, was moving pretty quickly, with only a dozen or so people in it.
Realizing this, Zane didn't hesitate and walked right over to the shorter line.
"Excuse me," Zane said to the person in front of him. "Any idea why this line is so much shorter?"
[SFX: CONTEMPTUOUS SCOFF]
The disciple in the black robe turned, looking Zane up and down. He took in the white robe and sneered, "You'd be better off in the other line."
"Why? Are outer disciples in white robes not allowed here?" Zane asked bluntly, having already noticed that everyone in this line wore black.
The man scoffed. "You notice the kind of people in this line? This is for those entering Level Eight, Nine, or even Ten of the Platform. Anyone without that kind of strength belongs over there, in the line for Levels One through Seven."
"Oh, I see," Zane said, finally getting it.
The others in line started snickering. They probably figured this white-robed disciple was in way over his head. Once the Seven-Star Platform roughed him up a bit, he'd learn his place.
Zane heard them, but chose to ignore it. As for the other disciple's "advice," he just smiled. "I appreciate the warning," he said firmly, "but I think I'll give it a shot."
Realizing his "kindness" was being ignored, the disciple in front of Zane just shook his head. "Don't say I didn't warn you," he muttered. "You're biting off way more than you can chew."
A few minutes later, a group of disciples emerged from the Seven-Star Platform, each one of them radiating skill. They were all swordsmen, carrying their blades on their backs instead of tucking them away in a Storage Ring.
Many serious swordsmen did this, believing it helped them stay connected to their blade's intent by never letting it out of their sight.
Zane noticed they all wore the same runic symbol on their chests. They had to be elite inner disciples from one of the Thirty-Three Peaks.
Once that group left, Zane's line started moving, and disciples began to enter the platform one by one.
They had no idea who Zane really was. But they would, very soon indeed.
