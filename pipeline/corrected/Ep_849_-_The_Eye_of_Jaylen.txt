Ep 849 - The Eye of Jaylen

Word count: 1012

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]

The crystal cube was still glowing between Zane's fingers, every eye in the clearing fixed on it.

Zane had no idea what the stone was worth. He figured it was just a simple trinket, something he could give Fiona as a thank-you for her gift. But from the looks on everyone’s faces, this was clearly more than just a pretty rock.

Could it actually be valuable? he wondered.

Seeing Zane’s silence, Randy swallowed hard. “Zane, are you sure about this?”

“Of course,” Zane said, his tone suggesting he was giving away a piece of scrap metal.

“But this thing… its value is immeasurable,” Randy pressed, trying to find the right words. He wanted to scream that the Ancient Heartstones were nothing compared to this cube, but he didn't want to be blunt and make things awkward.

Zane just waved it off. “I came here for the Ancient Heartstones. This cube doesn’t matter to me. Here you go, Fiona!”

[SFX: CRYSTAL WHOOSH]

He tossed the shimmering stone to the princess with a casual flick of his wrist.

A few of the other warriors instinctively reached for it, but the throw was too perfect.

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC CRESCENDO]

It flew in a clean arc straight into Fiona’s hands, well out of anyone else’s reach.

Meanwhile, Fredrick and his friends were scowling, their jealousy practically radiating off them. But they couldn't just walk over and snatch the stone from a princess. The laws of the Union of Seven Divine Kingdoms were far stricter than those in the Heartlands, and they knew better than to test them. A Martial House warrior would have been another matter — but Zane's strength had made that option equally unappealing.

Fiona caught the cube neatly. “Zane, do you really not know what this is?”

He just shrugged and shook his head.

“Nope.”

A bright, chiming laugh escaped her. His casual dismissal was almost unbelievable. “You really aren’t from here, are you?” It was just a passing comment, a small observation based on his cluelessness, but it made Zane smile. He was starting to suspect he’d dug up something truly special.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS REVELATION]

“This is the Eye of Jaylen!” Fiona explained, her expression turning serious. “It’s far too precious. I can’t accept this.” She tossed the cube back to Zane before nodding at Randy. “Lord Randy, it would be better if you explained it to him. The Harlen Divine Kingdom knows more about this than anyone.”

Randy smiled, grateful for her kindness and tact. He turned to Zane and began his explanation. “Even the best weapon refiners in my kingdom, the Harlen Divine Kingdom, can only forge semi-divine weapons. If we used every resource we have, we could still never hope to forge a true Divine-Class weapon.” As the kingdom most famous for its forging, his words carried immense weight.

[MUSIC: ANCIENT LEGEND]

“It wasn't always that way. A long time ago, a Life-and-Death Realm warrior pulled one of these out of this very mine. The weapon smiths couldn't figure out what to do with it at first — it couldn't be destroyed by blade or flame, and it had strange properties no one understood. It sat unused for years. Then a craftsman named Jaylen finally cracked it. He used the stone to forge the first true divine weapon the four kingdoms had ever seen. In his honor, the stone was named the Eye of Jaylen.”

Randy paused. “Seven Eyes have been found in this mine. Seven divine weapons exist in the four kingdoms. That's the entire supply. Not one crown prince among the four kingdoms owns a divine weapon — not Fredrick, not Callan, not our own Crown Prince. The value of what you're holding is roughly equivalent to a divine weapon itself.”

He glanced at the crowd around them. “And every one of them knows it.”

Zane listened, a small smile playing on his lips. He understood. But even so, his heart was calm. A divine weapon was a worldly possession. His own strength was what mattered.

[SFX: STORAGE RING ACTIVATION]

He tucked the Eye of Jaylen into his Storage Ring without ceremony.

“I'm going back in,” he told Randy.

Randy nodded, then added, “Stay below the thirtieth floor. Above thirty is Life-and-Death Realm territory — the leaves are too hard for Spirit-Tribulation warriors to break through. Above forty, only Soul Sea masters can operate. Don't let anyone goad you into going higher than you need to.”

[MUSIC: CHALLENGE THEME]

[SFX: FLAMES ROARING]

As if on cue, Fredrick glanced over with a cold smirk. “You beat the Little Monster, and you're still scared of the thirtieth floor? Interesting.” He hoisted a high-grade Mythic pickax onto his shoulder — its head blazing with twin jets of flame — and bounded up the pyramid, disappearing through a door on the thirtieth floor.

Randy watched him go, then looked back at Zane. “Don't.”

Zane wasn't listening. He wasn't going to be baited by a trick that obvious. There wasn't much time left. Finding Ancient Heartstones was more important.

[SFX: WHOOSH]

So, without wasting another moment, he leaped back up to the twenty-ninth floor.

[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT STRINGS]

His persistence paid off. After a few tries, he hit a massive cluster. He knew that Ancient Heartstones usually appeared in groups, so finding one often meant finding dozens more nearby. He kept jumping through the narrow doors and falling out of the light circles—ten, twenty, even thirty times—scouring every inch of the floor. By the time he was done, he’d gathered two to three hundred Heartstones and stuffed them into his Storage Ring.

It still wasn’t enough.

He’d turned over every stone on the twenty-ninth floor and knew it was tapped out. A quick search of the twenty-eighth floor yielded a small pile, but the twenty-seventh gave him nothing. Time was ticking away, and he’d only managed to gather about a quarter of what he needed.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS DRONE]

[AMBIENT: ECHOING CHAMBER]

His gaze drifted upward toward the thirtieth floor. Randy’s warning echoed in his mind—that it was too difficult for even Spirit-Tribulation Realm warriors.

[MUSIC: DETERMINED PERCUSSION]

But Zane knew he wasn’t an ordinary warrior. He might just have a shot. He hadn’t been interested in exploring it before, but with the lower floors picked clean, there was nowhere to go but up.
