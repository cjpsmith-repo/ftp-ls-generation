Ep 1154 - The Number One Guy

Word count: 1104

[MUSIC: MAGICAL REVERENCE]
“The number one weaponsmith in the universe.”
It was a hell of a title, and even though the younger warriors gathered around had never heard the name Livingston, you could see the claim blew them away. 
[SFX: MURMURING CROWD]
The little automaton seemed furious at their disbelief, but it just stuck its nose in the air, defiant.
Zane was just as stunned as the rest of them.
“Number one weaponsmith in the universe?” one of the warriors scoffed. “Get over yourself. You’re just some clunky automaton.”
“Yeah,” another chimed in. “Everyone knows the best weaponsmith in the universe is Delroy from the Demi-Immortal Race.”
He had a point. In the world of warriors, weaponsmiths were a huge deal. Every major clan had their own, but the top-tier ones were practically legends, their creations capable of shifting the rankings on the Cosmic Spirit Index. The title of “number one” wasn’t something you just threw around—it had been settled for a long time. The top ten Divine-Class weapons on the Spirit Index had all been forged by Delroy. No one even dreamed of questioning his title. So, for this little machine to make such a claim felt less like bragging and more like insanity.
[SFX: METAL CLANKING]
“I’m not bragging!” Livingston shrieked, its metal arms flailing. “I *am* Livingston! Go ask your elders! They’ll know! I...”
Its voice, once booming, began to fade, the words turning to static. 
[SFX: MECHANICAL SPUTTERING]
[SFX: POWER DOWN HUM]
It sputtered, its movements slowing to a halt until it froze mid-gesture, totally silent. The Sovereign Stone Zane had given it was spent.
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS APPROACHING]
[SFX: JINGLING JADE STONES]
“Fascinating little thing,” a human warrior said, stepping forward with a smile. He held out a handful of Sovereign Stones—at least ten of them. “I’ll take it back to the Magnolia Sanctum for study.”
He wasn’t talking to Zane; he was talking to the powered-down automaton. Smart move. Since the automaton seemed to be a thinking, living being, it was technically a free agent. The guy was trying to buy its loyalty right out from under Zane’s nose, probably figuring Zane wouldn’t make a scene over it.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS TENSION]
[SFX: ICE CRACKLING]
He only made it a few steps before his leg froze to the ground, trapping him in place.
Zane just looked at him, his gaze cold and completely flat. There was no threat in it, no killing intent, but it was enough. 
The warrior didn’t dare move another inch.
Zane smiled faintly and pressed several more Sovereign Stones into the groove on the puppet’s back.
[SFX: AUTOMATON POWERING UP]
The lights came back on, and so did the voice — mid-sentence, like it had never stopped.
Livingston stared at Zane for a moment, then his gaze drifted to the pile of decayed weapons behind him.
“Since you call yourself the number one craftsman in the universe,” Zane said, “does that mean you actually know how to craft?”
“Of course!” Livingston jabbed a metal finger at the mountain of artifacts. “These are all my creations. Every one of them. What’s wrong with—”
He stopped.
[MUSIC: SOMBER SHIFT]
“Why?” His voice cracked, the bravado gone in an instant. “Why are they all dead?”
Livingston shuffled over to the pile and dropped to his knees in it, gathering the ruined weapons into his arms like something precious. He was openly grieving — and not performing it, either. The sound coming out of him was real.
Zane’s eyelid twitched. He’d found himself a lunatic.
“He’s lost his mind,” Lavender said into Zane’s thoughts, not unkindly.
“No,” the Cobalt Dragon said. “He hasn’t.”
Zane raised an eyebrow. “What do you mean?”
“Every weapon he forges, he believes, has a soul. A creature of his own making. He says they’re tied to his bloodline, the same as a child would be.” Whether Livingston really was the universe’s greatest craftsman or not, the dragon couldn’t say. But he recognized the grief. It was real.
Livingston picked one of the daggers out of the pile, cradling it like something fragile. “This one - only a second-grade Divine Artifact, but I bound its Celestial Essence myself and stabilized it with divine runes. It should have held for at least three hundred million years before the essence finally dissipated.” His voice dropped. “It’s already gone. How long has it actually been?”
[SFX: CROWD MURMUR]
A murmur ran through the gathered warriors.
“Three hundred million years…”
“Can a Supreme Lord even live that long?”
“I’ve heard they can. Their lifespans aren’t like ours.”
Even among warriors used to thinking in centuries, the number was staggering. But Zane’s eyes narrowed slightly. Three hundred million years was just Livingston’s own estimate — and Livingston didn’t actually know how long he’d been buried down here. The real number was almost certainly higher. Possibly much higher.
“I don’t get one thing,” Zane said. “If you’re a craftsman, why are you a puppet?”
Livingston’s voice went cold and flat. “What would you know? A craftsman is never going to get far on the Martial Path. This body is just a shell to me — I built it myself. It’s the only way to immerse fully in the craft. The only way to buy more time to do it.”
He’d turned himself into this on purpose.
[MUSIC: CLEVER SCHEMING]
Zane’s mouth curved, just slightly. There was a workshop in the Elysian Keep — not accessible yet, but it would be, eventually, once his cultivation carried him further down the Path of Extinction and he claimed the token to open it. If Livingston really had forged weapons at this level, his skill had to be real, whatever the actual ranking. And the fact that he was a puppet, with no clan, no politics, no allegiances to anyone — that made him exactly the kind of craftsman Zane wanted.
“So,” Zane said, “do you still want to forge weapons?”
Livingston’s voice boomed back to life, full of conviction. “Of course! I was born to forge! If I couldn’t forge, I’d have rusted away a long time ago!”
Zane was a little taken aback by the sheer passion in its voice. “Well, in that case,” he said, “I know a workshop where you can really show off your skills.”
“A workshop? Ha!” Livingston scoffed, striking a prideful pose. “There isn’t a workshop in the entire universe worthy of my talents.” It paused for a dramatic beat. “But I suppose it will have to do.”
The automaton was so obsessed with its craft that it couldn’t be picky, but it had to get the last word in. Zane didn’t bother arguing. 
[SFX: METAL OBJECT PICKED UP]
He just picked up the small metal body and tucked it into his bestiary bag. Living beings couldn’t survive in his Spatial Sanctum, but since Livingston was a soul bound to a machine, the bag would have to do for now.
