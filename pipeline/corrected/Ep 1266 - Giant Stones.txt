Ep 1266 - Giant Stones

Word count: 1092

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS CONTEMPLATIVE]
[AMBIENT: QUIET CHAMBER]
Zane had no idea just how much Janet idolized Sloane.
The Thoughtless Minds comprised four great clans: Sloane’s House Kincaid,Janet’s house, House Vance, and House Valerius. These dynasties - the most famous human families in the universe, surpassing even the Supreme Lords’ lineages - formed the pillars of the human race. Through strategic marriages and alliances, they held sway over thousands of worlds within the Thoughtless Minds.
Janet could still vividly picture Sloane standing at the top during the last great competition, long before Nigel had even made a name for himself. 
In her eyes, no one from the younger generation of human warriors even stood a chance against Sloane.
Nigel’s fame was a recent thing, all because he’d merged with the Celestial Fragment and become a saint-level genius. But if Sloane could truly master The Godly Way of the Five Elements, she’d be a saint-level genius, too. And if that happened, Janet was certain Nigel wouldn’t be a match for her at all.
Among all the Soul Sea Realm warriors, no one could defeat Sloane. At least, that’s what she had always believed. 
So, when Zane casually mentioned that Sloane had lost to him, she was floored.
She just stared at him, completely unable to process what he’d said. She knew the Five Elements fusion he was using was a secret technique from House Kincaid, but that wasn’t nearly enough to convince her. Sloane might have taught it to him for some reason, but that didn’t mean she would lose to him.
Zane was confused by her reaction at first, but then a slow smile spread across his face as he started to understand. “What? You don’t believe me?”
[MUSIC: TENSE QUESTIONING]
Janet leaned in, her eyes fixed on his as she asked carefully, “When you saw Sloane, what color was the snow behind her?”
“Is that important?” Zane asked, baffled.
“What color was it?” she pressed.
“White.”
“Pure white?”
He was starting to get annoyed, but she wasn’t letting it go.
“Yes,” he said, throwing his hands up a little. “One hundred percent pure white.”
A slow sigh of relief escaped her lips, and a wide grin finally broke through her disbelief. 
“Oh. No wonder.”
“No wonder what?”
“I guess you did beat Sloane,” she said, her smile widening. “But only because she was holding back. Technically, she was only using sixty percent of her strength, at most.”
It all clicked for Zane, the understanding dawning on his face.
[MUSIC: SOMBER REVELATION]
“There’s more to it than that,” Janet continued, watching his face carefully. “Sloane doesn’t fight with just one body. She has two — bound together as one soul. What you saw was only half of her. If she ever fully merges with the black snow, that’s her true strength. You haven’t really beaten her until you’ve beaten that.”
One soul, two bodies. Black snow.
Zane’s eyebrows lifted slightly. Sloane had already been the toughest opponent he’d faced in the Illusion Battlefield — and she hadn’t even been at full strength.
Going by what Janet had said, this “black snow” existence was close to Sloane’s equal.
“Then I hope we get the chance to fight again,” Zane said, a faint smile crossing his face.
“You will,” she said, nodding. “And when you do, you’ll understand just how frightening she really is.”
[MUSIC: STEADY MARCH]
They pressed on, climbing higher into the mountain. About thirty minutes later, they reached the halfway point.
[AMBIENT: SHIFTING STONE ECHOES]
Waiting there was a second hall — several times larger than the first, both in height and width. The other eight warriors stood outside it already, clearly waiting. 
Not for Zane. For Janet. She’d led them through the first hall, and now that she wasn’t stepping forward first, no one else seemed willing to take the lead.
“Every hall is different,” she said, studying the entrance. “Passing through doesn’t come down to strength alone — it comes down to reaction, judgment, reading the room. This one will be harder than the last.”
She stepped through without waiting for an answer. The others fell in behind her.
The first hall had been almost insultingly simple — nothing but bare walls and twin beams of white light shooting from either side. Even cultivators well below the Soul Sea Realm could have cleared it carefully. That simplicity was exactly why it had only paid out one hundred Illusion Points.
This hall was different. The floor and walls shimmered with faint gold patterning, giving the whole space an air of luxury. At its center sat an enormous square stone — thirty feet tall, thirty feet wide, thirty feet long — its surface carved with dense, unfamiliar characters that belonged to no race anyone recognized.
The group studied it from a few hundred feet back, turning it over from every angle without finding an answer. Eventually, all eyes drifted back to Janet.
She continued sizing Zane up. If he’d truly beaten Sloane using only half her strength, he matched her own level - assuming she pushed herself to the limit. But strength was only part of any real fight. She’d have to test him herself. Not here, but before the Illusion Battlefield ended.
Feeling everyone’s attention on her again, she said, a little irritably, “This hall changes every time. I’ve never been through this one either.”
“Well,” one of the other cultivators said with a thin smile, “since no one else wants to go first — allow me.”
[SFX: MAGICAL SHIMMER]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS WONDER]
As they were talking, a shimmer of light appeared before one of the other warriors in their group, coalescing until it took the shape of a person.
“A sword spirit,” Zane said, his eyes lighting up.
“Damn, that’s a solid one,” someone else muttered.
[MUSIC: TENSE FOREBODING]
“Good,” another replied. “Use it to scout ahead. We still don’t know the rules of this hall, and one wrong move could get us all killed. Too bad we don’t have any puppeteers with us.”
The warrior’s sword spirit was impressive, but Zane noticed it wasn’t quite perfect. Compared to his own, which could easily pass for a real person, this one had a faint shimmer, a subtle flaw that gave it away if you looked too closely. Still, creating a spirit this solid was no small feat.
Once it was fully formed, the sword spirit glided toward the center of the hall, and every eye in the room followed it. The path from the entrance to the middle of the chamber was dead silent. 
[SFX: STONE RUMBLING]
But just as the spirit drifted near a massive, unassuming boulder, the stone began to tremble.
[SFX: CRUSHING IMPACT]
Then, with a violent lurch, it rolled forward, crushing the sword spirit flat in an instant.
