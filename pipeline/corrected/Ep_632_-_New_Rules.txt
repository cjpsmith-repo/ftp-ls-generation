Ep 632 - New Rules
Word count: 1063
[AMBIENT: GRAND HALL]
[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
With the Ogres gone from the Tower of Sin, a power vacuum had opened up. Twenty-six seats were suddenly up for grabs, and the big question on everyone’s mind was simple: who was going to get them?
The seat holders from every other race were already scheming, but it was just for show. They all knew the decision was Zane’s to make. He could do whatever he wanted with those twenty-six seats. As things stood, the only power he really had to consider was the Demon Night. After all, his own Violet Heart Sanctuary was still technically under the protection of the Heavenly Feather Sanctuary. If the Demon Night hadn’t stepped in, the Ogres would have wiped them completely off the map.
While the Tower of Sin was a huge deal, everyone knew that true power came from a sacred place, a home base built up over decades, centuries, or even millennia. The other leaders figured Zane would throw some seats to the Demon Night as a thank-you and give the rest to the Humans. Honestly, as long as he didn’t touch their seats, they’d call it a win.
[AMBIENT: TENSE HALL MURMURS]
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
The highest-ranking War Lords filed into the hall, their eyes fixed on Zane, faces tense with fear and awe. Who could have imagined a human rewriting the Tower's rules? Every race had once fought to the death for a single seat. This man had expelled the Ogres and seized twenty-six at once.
Many admired what he'd accomplished—something they couldn't have dreamed of achieving. With his current power, Zane could expel every race from the Tower, even the Demon Night. But he wouldn't.
The Tower was merely a means to an end; their sacred places mattered most. Making enemies of everyone would unite them against the Violet Heart Sanctuary.
[AMBIENT: COUNCIL CHAMBER]
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
Zane had been thinking about the Tower’s rules, too. The system that had evolved over the years wasn’t half bad. Anyone who entered had to walk a razor’s edge between life and death. Only the absolute best could climb floor after floor, earning more resources, Lucky Light, and points. The weak were weeded out, sometimes becoming nothing more than fuel for the Tower itself. It was just a small-scale version of the real world.
"How should the rules be changed?" a peak War Lord of the Giant Clan asked.
Zane smiled faintly. "The rules from the first floor to the fourteenth floor stay exactly as they are."
"And the fifteenth?" another War Lord pressed.
"All seats on the fifteenth floor remain reserved. I was the one who drove out the Ogres, so the twenty-six seats are mine to allocate." He paused. "One more thing. The seats held by the Humans and the Demon Night will be permanent. No rotation."
[SFX: CROWD ERUPTION]
The hall erupted.
"Permanent seats? They'd hold them forever?"
"That's too much—"
Zane let them talk. He understood their concern. Under the existing rules, seats on the fifteenth floor weren't fixed. Every cycle, seat holders competed to keep their positions. The strong took more; the weak gave theirs up. It was a system that had always favored the Ogres and the Demon Night, who could produce enough elite fighters to dominate every round. The Humans, by contrast, had clawed their way to four seats — and even those weren't safe. At their lowest points, the Humans had held just one seat, sometimes none at all.
Making those seats permanent meant the Humans would never fall that far again.
Zane had thought this through carefully. He knew he wouldn't stay in the Tower forever. Once he claimed the inheritance from the Dark Nether Cave, he'd return to The Heartlands. That might be months away, or a year — but no more than three. The Violet Heart Sanctuary wouldn't be strong enough to stand on its own by then. The Demon Night might look after the Humans for Lavender's sake, but that was a thin thread to hang everything on.
Permanent seats were the answer. And there was another layer to it: by locking the Demon Night's seats as well, Zane was tying their interests directly to the arrangement. If anyone wanted to challenge the permanent seats, they'd have to go through the Demon Night first. The Demon Night wouldn't hesitate.
"There are pros and cons," Zane said calmly, cutting through the noise. "The downside is that you can't compete for the permanent seats held by the Humans or the Demon Night. The upside is that neither of them will be expanding their count either. Those numbers are fixed."
[MUSIC: TENSE PERCUSSION]
“Does anyone have a problem with that?” Zane asked.
The other War Lords kept their mouths shut. Arguing was pointless, and besides, Zane’s logic was sound. Between the Demon Night and the Humans, they would hold nearly fifty seats, leaving the remaining seventy-something for everyone else. It was pretty much the same balance of power as when the Ogres were around. Back then, they were all constantly worried about the growing might of the Ogres and the Demon Night, who seemed destined to swallow up every last seat.
But Zane’s new arrangement actually put a cap on the Demon Night’s expansion, fixing the number of seats for both them and the Humans. For the smaller races, this wasn’t a bad deal at all.
[MUSIC: RESOLUTION THEME]
“No objections,” one leader finally said, and a murmur of assent followed.
“I have none either.”
[MUSIC: DECISIVE ORCHESTRAL]
Of course, Zane wasn’t really asking for their permission.
He gave eight of the Ogres’ twenty-six seats to the Demon Night, bringing their total to twenty-four. The other eighteen went to the Humans, giving them twenty-two seats and making them the second-strongest power in the Tower. As for how Esther Sterling and Fabian planned to hand out those seats among their own people, Zane figured that was their business, not his.
[AMBIENT: QUIET CHAMBER]
Esther Sterling took the news calmly. An extra eight seats was nice, but it wasn’t enough to get her worked up.
[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT STRINGS]
For the four original human seat holders, however, it was a completely different story. Fabian and Jarrod looked like they were on cloud nine. They had just been handed eighteen seats out of nowhere.
The only question now was how to divide them up.
