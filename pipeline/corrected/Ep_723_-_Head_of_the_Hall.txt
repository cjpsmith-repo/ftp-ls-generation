Ep 723 - Head of the Hall
Word count: 1043
[AMBIENT: WIND AT HIGH ALTITUDE]
[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
As the newly appointed head of Cloud Hall, Tia gained absolute authority the moment she ascended to the Life-and-Death Realm. But “head of the hall” was just a title. To truly lead, she had to command the respect of the elders, stewards, and disciples with undeniable strength. In terms of raw power, only Elder Imogen ranked above her, and that was a gap Tia had just closed.
Several noble houses had seen her ascension trial as a window of opportunity. It was a brilliant strategy, really—strike while she was at her most vulnerable. They had mocked her for the timing, all while sharpening their knives in the shadows, believing that for all her power, she was still just a young woman, too naive to see their schemes.
[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT STRINGS]
Unfortunately for them, Zane had shown up just in time, saving both Tia and Cloud Hall from their little coup. And now that Tia had broken through to the Life-and-Death Realm, any thought of rebellion was pure fantasy.
The house lords, who had once plotted with such confidence, were now paralyzed by the shock of her sudden, terrifying surge in power.
[SFX: WHOOSHING WIND]
[AMBIENT: SKY ATMOSPHERE]
Leaning forward, Tia began to descend, long, crystalline feathers of energy trailing behind her like the tail of an icy phoenix. Zane, Saint Morphen, and the Spirit-Tribulation elders followed in her wake, a silent procession leaving the floating fortress of Cloud Hall. They flew toward the spot where the warriors from the noble houses hovered, trapped between the sky and the ground below.
[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]
[SFX: SUDDEN SILENCE]
Tia stopped, her gaze sweeping over the lords, as cold and sharp as glass. "Emanuel is dead," she announced, her voice dangerously calm. "He was executed for his part in this. Now… I wonder how the rest of you plan to explain your rebellion. Don't even think about telling me you had nothing to do with it."
[AMBIENT: WIND HOWLING]
[MUSIC: TENSE ORCHESTRAL]
Cloud Hall floated high above the city of Cloudhaven, a city it had long protected, ensuring the prosperity of the very houses that had just tried to tear it down. They were once allies; now they were mortal enemies.
The lords stared back, utterly speechless. They had families, legacies—they had risked everything on a victory they thought was guaranteed. Hearing her words, they finally understood what a pathetic joke their confidence had been. Their roots ran too deep in Cloudhaven to flee. They were trapped, and they knew it.
The head of House Calder was the first to lower his head. "I'm at your disposal, Hall Master."
"I know my leadership has been a thorn in your petty little hearts," Tia said, her voice dripping with contempt. "But let me make one thing perfectly clear. Cloud Hall is an independent sect; it doesn't belong to any of you."
Somehow, over the years, the houses had convinced themselves that Cloud Hall was theirs to command. They seemed to forget that while they had contributed to its founding, their contributions paled in comparison to the cost of maintaining it.
The other heads followed House Calder's lead, one by one, until all of them had bowed.
Tia's expression didn't soften. "Since you have no objection," she said, "your four houses will be removed from Cloud Hall. Exterminated."
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC REVELATION]
The word landed like a blade. Every face in the group went white — the house lords, their warriors, and even the elders standing behind Tia. Saint Morphen's eyes went wide. No one had seen that coming.
The heads of the four houses were speechless. They had expected punishment. They had not expected this. Even knowing that rebellion was unforgivable, they had assumed their deep ties to Cloud Hall — their disciples, their elders, the generations of history woven into the sect's foundation — would protect them from the worst. To exterminate them all would tear Cloud Hall apart.
But Tia's face was stone.
"Hall Master." Saint Morphen stepped forward carefully. "With the Corrupted Divine Kingdom closing in, Cloud Hall cannot afford to lose this many people. Perhaps—"
Tia let him finish. Then she let the silence stretch just long enough to make everyone uncomfortable.
"Saint Morphen is right," she said at last, her voice cooling from fury to ice. "Since that's the case, I'll put your lives on my tab. For now." She looked across the assembled lords. "But your captured elders will need to be ransomed. One thousand top-grade Aether crystals per person."
The relief on the lords' faces lasted exactly as long as it took to do the math. Twenty-plus Spirit-Tribulation warriors. Over twenty thousand top-grade Aether crystals. More than the combined savings of all four houses.
Their expressions crumpled.
[SFX: CRYSTAL HUMMING]
“The protective array that shields this entire city runs on supreme Aether crystals,” she continued, her voice cutting through their daze. “Thousands of them. That was nearly half of our entire treasury. You seem to have forgotten that when you decided we were disposable.”
The four houses were wealthy, their businesses thriving under Cloud Hall’s protection. But their wealth was a different beast entirely. To fund a rebellion with over twenty Spirit-Tribulation warriors would require a fortune upwards of twenty thousand supreme Aether crystals. Even if they liquidated every asset they owned, it was an impossible sum.
[AMBIENT: TENSE CHAMBER]
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
"But that's… that's far too much!" the lord of House Ward choked out, his face ashen. "House Ward could never gather that many Aether crystals."
A cruel sneer touched Tia's lips. "That's a shame. But where there's a will, there's a way. I suggest you figure it out." She paused, her eyes locking onto his.
"Because if you fail to deliver, I will personally kill every last one of you."
The lords exchanged frantic, desperate looks. On the surface, her demand seemed like a chance at mercy, but they all knew what it really was: a way to bleed their houses dry, seizing every bit of wealth they had accumulated over generations. For a warrior, money was survival. Without it, they were nothing.
As the crushing reality settled over them, Zane drifted forward to hover beside Tia, a pleasant smile on his face.
[MUSIC: DECEPTIVELY LIGHT PIANO]
"If you're a little short on cash," he said cheerfully, "I might have a solution for you."
"What is it?" the lord of House Ward asked, latching onto the sliver of hope.
Zane chuckled. "You can pay in installments."
