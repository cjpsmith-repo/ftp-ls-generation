Ep 1106 - Not Dying Yet

Word count: 1050

[AMBIENT: ETHEREAL WHISPERS]
[SFX: SOFT CHEWING]
The Luck-Devouring Snakes swirled through the air, the only sound a soft, persistent chewing. They weren’t tearing at Zane’s flesh, but at something far more vital: his very fortune. With every passing moment, his expression grew darker.
[SFX: CRACKLING PAPER]
[MUSIC: OMINOUS DRONE]
It was a dry, rustling sound, like insects chewing through old paper—a quiet, relentless erosion of his luck. The snakes themselves were ghostly, seemingly not of this world, and Zane was utterly powerless against them. Even the Cobalt Dragon, a presence deep within his mind, let out a psychic sigh of resignation. 
Zane’s fortune was so immense that it had drawn this terrifying trial, and since ancient times, no scripture had ever recorded a way to survive it. Neither the Dragon nor Lavender could see a path forward. It was an unstoppable act of Heaven, something no one could resist.
Panic began to set in, a cold knot tightening in his gut. What could he even do? 
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
But as the moments stretched on, a strange calm washed over him, smoothing the frantic edges of his thoughts. He executed a sharp mid-air flip, settled into a cross-legged position, and landed silently back where he’d started, his face now a mask of pure concentration.
Zane hadn’t given up. Since the day he’d committed himself to the path of martial arts, the thought of surrender had never once crossed his mind. No tribulation, no opponent, could ever make him yield. These trials weren’t meant to be simple death sentences; if they were, the universe would be empty of warriors. The fact that the heavens had sent one this powerful meant there had to be a deeper meaning to it all. He just needed to calm down and watch, not panic.
*No matter how bad my luck gets,* he told himself, *I’m not going to die from this just yet.*
[MUSIC: QUIET RESOLVE]
And even if it did get bad—so what? Luck had carried him through more than a few miracles, sure. But luck had never been the only thing carrying him. Take it away, and the road ahead just got harder. That was all. Harder wasn’t the same as impossible.
So what, he thought again, and this time it landed differently—not as a denial, but as something settled. Something in him shifted again, deeper this time. Whatever this tribulation cost him, he’d find a way to make it count anyway.
[SFX: CONTINUOUS CRACKLING]
The dry crackling continued as the ethereal snakes slithered around him, feasting on his fortune as if it were the finest delicacy.
Across the platform, Layla’s eyes met Zane’s—and her heart slammed against her ribs without warning. There was something in his gaze she couldn’t name, layers of feeling too tangled to read. What was happening to him? She didn’t understand it, but she couldn’t look away either.
She wasn’t the only one. Up and down the gathered ranks of the Thirteen Palaces, every warrior who happened to catch Zane’s eyes felt the same thing—hearts pounding, a strange unease they couldn’t explain.
[SFX: SLOW CLAP]
The Lord of the Sunset World broke the quiet with a slow, deliberate clap. “Look at that. This kid knows full well he can’t fight the Luck-Devouring Snakes—and he’s using them to forge his mind instead. His foundation just took a real leap forward, mark my words.”
Yolande said nothing. She only smiled, faint and private—but the smile told its own story. Where the Lord of the Sunset World wore his admiration loudly, hers stayed quiet. Both of them were impressed all the same.
“He’s keeping his composure,” one of the palace leaders noted from afar. “Zane is really something else.”
“Maybe he’s already figured out a way to beat this,” another suggested.
A third leader scoffed. “Forget Zane. Even the supreme lords of the highest realms can’t stop these things once they appear. The trials they face are just on a much grander scale than this.”
[MUSIC: TENSE ORCHESTRAL]
For all that, the Luck-Devouring Snakes didn’t care one way or another about Zane’s newfound calm. The feast wasn’t over—not by a long shot—and they kept right on gnawing, exactly as before. And Zane, for his part, didn’t care either.
The leaders of the Thirteen Palaces were among the most powerful beings in existence; they were not easily surprised. But as a sudden gust of wind swept through the clearing, even their expressions shifted. Warriors at their level could summon hurricanes ten thousand times stronger with a single thought. This was different. No one could tell where this wind came from or how it had formed. It was just… strange.
While they were still trying to make sense of it, a warrior in the crowd pointed behind Zane and shouted, “Look! What is that?”
There was nothing there, which was precisely what made the situation so bizarre.
The wind howled, kicking up dust and blurring the edges of the world. But as it whipped past Zane, everyone noticed something odd. The wind seemed to part, flowing around an unseen obstacle directly behind him. 
[SFX: ETHEREAL SHIMMER]
The dust it carried caught on the edges of this invisible shape, tracing a faint, shimmering outline in the air.
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
[SFX: OMINOUS WIND]
It was the silhouette of a giant.
Or, you could call it a silhouette. It was a massive, human-like form made of nothing, defined only by the wind that couldn’t pass through it. And that’s when everyone saw it: the Luck-Devouring Snakes were gnawing right along this invisible outline. 
[SFX: GASPING CROWD]
[AMBIENT: TENSE ATMOSPHERE]
They weren’t just eating some abstract concept of luck—they were chewing on an invisible giant.
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]
Zane heard the murmurs ripple through the crowd, but his expression remained unchanged. When he finally turned to look, however, his composure gave way to a flicker of pure, unadulterated curiosity.
*A giant’s silhouette?* he thought. *What the hell is this?*
If the snakes were eating the giant, did that mean this *was* his luck? And why would his luck take this shape? For all his hard-won calm, Zane couldn’t help but feel completely baffled by the sight.
And more importantly, how long had it been there, trailing him like a shadow he never knew he had?
