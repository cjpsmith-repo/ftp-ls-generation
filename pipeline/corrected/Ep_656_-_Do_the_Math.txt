Ep 656 - Do the Math

[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL ENERGY HUM]

[SFX: FLOWERS BLOOMING RAPIDLY]

The fog had risen fast — what had been a thin haze around their feet was already swallowing the horizon, and Malcolm was still standing between them and whatever came next.

[SFX: FLOWERS BLOOMING RAPIDLY]

Esther met Malcolm’s gaze, and a wave of pure vitality pulsed from her. In an instant, hundreds of flowers erupted from the barren ground, weaving themselves into a protective dome around them. It was her Myriad Flowers Domain—a power that should have been impossible for anyone who hadn't reached the Soul Sea Realm, but one she could summon all the same.

Malcolm scoffed. “You think a few flowers can stop me?”

[SFX: POWERFUL WING FLAP]

[SFX: EXPLOSIVE DESTRUCTION]

With a single, powerful flap of his wings, he strode through the air and threw a lazy punch. The dome of petals didn't just break; it exploded into nothing. A smug grin twisted his lips as Esther’s expression hardened. There was a time when no one could touch her, not even Maurice. Now, she couldn’t even withstand a single one of Malcolm’s attacks.

But Esther seemed unfazed. She simply waved her hands, and a new wall of flowers bloomed to shield them. “Malcolm,” she said, her voice unnervingly calm, “have you noticed something different about the air?”

“Different?” He squinted, his eyes sweeping over the thin mist coiling around their feet.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]

[AMBIENT: EERIE MIST]

Then his focus snapped, and the arrogance drained from his face, replaced by a dawning horror. “This is…”

[SFX: RUNNING FOOTSTEPS]

[MUSIC: TENSION RISING]

Just then, Timothy, Garrick, and the other two members of the Twilight Group scrambled over. “Malcolm, this is bad!” Timothy yelled, his voice cracking with panic. “I think we’ve stumbled into—”

“The fog,” Malcolm finished for him, his own voice tense with horror.

[AMBIENT: EERIE CAVE WITH ECHOING DRIPS]

[MUSIC: OMINOUS DRONE]

Fog wasn't unusual on the main continent, especially in the thaw between winter and spring. But here, in the Dark Nether Cave, it meant only one thing: you were in the core. You were already lost.

[SFX: DISTANT RUMBLING]

The reason why the fog even existed was a mystery, but everyone in the Tower of Sin was grateful for it. The Dark Nether Cave was flooded with a lethal Dark Nether Death Light year-round, a force that could melt any living thing—no matter how powerful—into a puddle of goo. Only the native monsters could survive it.

But for a brief period each year, that thick fog would rise from the cave's core, and it was the only thing in existence that could weaken the death light enough for warriors to enter. It was their one window of opportunity. Without it, this place was a tomb.

And now they were standing inside the very source of it.

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

Malcolm refused to accept it. He yanked a sheepskin map from inside his coat and spread it open, scanning it rapidly. "Impossible," he said. "We shouldn't be in the core. The map doesn't show us anywhere near it."

Fabian and Esther pulled out their own charts and checked. A few breaths of silence passed.

"He's right," Fabian said slowly. "According to the map, this isn't the core region."

Esther's expression was unreadable. "No," she said. "But the core region has expanded."

That landed like a stone dropped into still water. Unlike the clear boundary between the outer and inner circles — a line the cave's monsters wouldn't cross — the core had no fixed edge. Wherever the fog spread, that was the core. It moved. It grew. And right now, it had grown around them.

Lavender said nothing. She was watching Yolande with a faint, unreadable smile — calm in a way that had nothing to do with courage and everything to do with knowing something the others didn't.

[AMBIENT: TENSE ATMOSPHERE]

[SFX: MAGICAL HUMMING]

Zane barely noticed. His hand had gone to his Storage Ring. It was hot — hotter than it had been during the entire chase. When he checked the Crystal Butterfly, it had transformed from a pale flame into a roiling ball of fire, its light straining toward the fog ahead.

The inheritance was in there. Whatever the Immortal had left behind, it was waiting for him somewhere inside the core.

He said nothing. He filed the information away and kept his face still.

[AMBIENT: TENSE ATMOSPHERE]

Esther turned back to Malcolm. "Now," she said evenly, "do you still want to fight?"

Malcolm's jaw worked. His grip tightened on his blade. He had a plan — had always had a plan. Kill Zane, then retreat to the Tower. Cycle in and out every ten days to avoid the curse. Grow stronger, floor by floor, until no one in the Tower could touch him. In time, even the Ogres' Sacred Land would have to treat him with respect.

The fog had not been part of that plan.

[SFX: BLADE DRAWN]

"Kill Zane first," he said, raising his blade and pointing it directly at Zane. "Then we talk about the fog."

Esther's eyes went flat. "You've swallowed the fruit, and you still think this ends cleanly? If we fight to the death right now, neither side walks away." She held his gaze. "Do the math, Malcolm."

A long silence, then finally, Malcolm lowered the blade. Barely.

"Fine," he said, his voice cold and hard. "We deal with the fog first." He looked around at the thickening white. "We retrace our steps. The path back was flat and clear. We go the way we came."

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

"A truce is a great idea," Garrick said quickly, almost tripping over the words.

Malcolm shot him a look that could strip bark from a tree. Garrick's mouth closed.

Not that Garrick needed telling. He'd been running through his own private nightmare ever since the fog appeared. If he'd known it would come to this, he never would have said a word to Malcolm. He would have taken his chances with the Shadow-maws and the Frost Wolves. At least that would have been quick.

[AMBIENT: THICK FOG]

[SFX: EERIE WIND]

The two groups formed a tense, reluctant line and started back the way they came. But the mist that had been a thin haze only moments ago had become a suffocating, blinding white wall. The fog was so thick you couldn't see your own hand in front of your face.
