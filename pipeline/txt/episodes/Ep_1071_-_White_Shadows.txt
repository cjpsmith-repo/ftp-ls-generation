Ep 1071 - White Shadows

Word count: 1123

[AMBIENT: ANCIENT STONE CHAMBER]

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]

Countless warriors had spent years in this place, but none had ever unraveled its mysteries. Zane didn’t expect to be the one to crack the code, especially not in the short time he had.

Honestly, he wasn’t even trying to. He was just curious about the white sword shadows left behind by the legendary Supreme Lord Sword.

When the Supreme Lord Sword had carved his poem into the rock, he’d poured all of his insight into the words themselves. For over a million years, those inscriptions had been releasing the faint, white sword shadows, though not everyone could see them. Fortunately, if Zane focused, they came into view.

He’d found he could resist the shadows with his own sword intent, but out of sheer curiosity, he decided to try blocking them with his actual blade.

[SFX: SWORD WHOOSH]

To his surprise, the moment he moved, the shadow dodged his sword and kept rushing toward him. It seemed they couldn’t be blocked.

But Zane wasn’t going to give up that easily.

He tried again, this time holding back his own sword intent; he knew from experience the shadows would just devour it. One wrong move, and he could get seriously hurt. He had to take this seriously.

[MUSIC: INTENSE PERCUSSION]

[SFX: METALLIC CLANG]

Lifting his five-foot-long sword, Zane lightly deflected an incoming white shadow. It ricocheted off his blade, spun in midair, and shot back toward him.

This was exactly what he’d been hoping for. As it closed in, he swung Thundergust and flicked it away again.

[SFX: RAPID SWORD STRIKES]

And again. And again. And again.

He repeated the motion, his blade tracing arcs of blue light in the air as he batted the white sword shadow back over and over. Only three seconds had passed, but he’d already deflected it seven times.

[SFX: GLASS SHATTERING]

After the seventh strike, the white shadow suddenly stopped its assault and shattered.

Zane’s eyes widened. “It broke?”

The moment it shattered, something strange happened. Every other white shadow that had been radiating outward from the rock in all directions — dozens of them, firing constantly — vanished. All at once. Gone.

Zane stared at the rock. The poem still loomed over him, silent and still. He didn’t know what he’d done, but something had changed.

The shattered shadow dissolved into hundreds of faint, shimmering sparks.

[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL SHIMMER]

“Is that the sword spirit?” he wondered aloud.

“Sword spirit?” Lavender’s voice echoed from within him. “Why can’t I see anything?”

“I don’t know,” Zane admitted.

It seemed that even Lavender couldn’t perceive the white sword shadows or the sparks they became. He was the only one. And as the shimmering lights drew closer, he had the sinking feeling they weren’t the sword spirit at all.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS TENSION]

Something felt terribly wrong.

Behind him, deep in their meditation pits, Landry and Damian felt it too — though for a completely different reason.

The heart-piercing sensation was gone.

It had been there every single day for sixty thousand years. For twenty thousand. Constant, unrelenting, as reliable as the sun rising over Willow Island. And now, between one breath and the next, it had simply stopped.

The two men looked at each other.

“What’s going on?” Damian’s Vitality carried the thought across to Landry. “That’s impossible.”

“Could it be the kid?”

“Impossible. Impossible.”

“Then explain it.”

Neither of them could. They’d watched Zane dancing around with his sword, batting at nothing, and written it off as theater. But this — this they couldn’t explain away.

For Lavender, the sword spirit had become little more than a convenient shell to hide in; her own soul was far beyond it. Still, watching from within, she couldn’t help but wonder just how Zane had managed to strengthen it. Had something happened while he was just swinging his sword at nothing?

Then the rock moved.

After a long, still pause, the poem’s carved surface seemed to shudder — and then every white shadow it had been holding back came screaming out at once. Dozens. Hundreds. All of them aimed at a single point.

At Zane.

[SFX: RUSHING WIND]

He’d stirred up a hornet’s nest. He’d shattered one shadow and now the rest were coming for him all at the same time. Every single one. He didn’t stop to think — he ran.

[MUSIC: FRANTIC CHASE THEME]

His speed was extraordinary, but the shadows were faster. He made it a few hundred feet before they caught him — pouring into his body from every direction at once, each one driving that heart-piercing pain deeper and deeper until it wasn’t pain anymore, it was everything, filling his chest until there was no room left for anything else.

[SFX: BODY HITTING AIR]

Zane’s eyes rolled back. His body went limp, and he dropped from the sky.

[SFX: RUSHING MOVEMENT]

Lavender shot out of his body before he hit the ground, caught him, and set him down gently in the desert sand. She straightened up and looked at her hand for a moment, then back at Zane.

The sword spirit felt different. Sharper. Stronger. She turned it over in her mind — she’d been living inside it so long it was practically wallpaper, but something had shifted. Whatever Zane had been doing out there with his sword, it had worked.

She was still thinking about it when he stirred.

[AMBIENT: QUIET TENSION]

“Lavender?” he called out.

“It looks like your sword intent got sharper just now,” she told him.

Zane just scratched his head. “I’m not really sure.” The memory of the white shadows flickered through his mind, and a shiver of fear went down his spine just thinking about it.

[MUSIC: LIGHT TRIUMPHANT]

“I’ve become stronger!” Lavender announced with a smile.

Her wording wasn’t quite right. It was Zane’s sword spirit that had grown stronger, not her, but she said it for the fun of it. Zane just murmured, “Ah,” still unable to shake the weird, foreboding feeling. The aura radiating from the sword spirit felt sharper, more forceful, and it wasn’t coming from Lavender’s soul—it was from the spirit itself.

Still, he managed a weak smile back at her. “You’re right. It seems I can sharpen my sword intent by using those white shadows.”

Even though he’d already mastered his sword intent, this proved it wasn’t at its absolute limit. Back on Killing Sword Mountain, all he’d done was stick his sword into the ground. This was a whole new level.

[MUSIC: OMINOUS FOREBODING]

Strengthening his sword spirit was good, but the memory of the white shadows still left him deeply unsettled.

He had a feeling that the path to becoming stronger was going to involve a lot more suffering.
