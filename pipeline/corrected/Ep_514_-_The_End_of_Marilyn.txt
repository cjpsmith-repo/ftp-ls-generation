Throughout the history of the Heartlands, no one had ever approached Arnold's legendary mastery of runes. The protective magic arrays he designed centuries ago remained vital to the Ethereal Spirit Sect's defense, their intricate patterns humming with power that had never gone out.
Guarded by just six warriors who understood its workings, the array could stop any attacker, regardless of their strength. It had once repelled an assault by warriors from the Spirit-Tribulation Realm—beings whose mere presence could shatter mountains.
For lone fighters who answered to no one, a protective array was of little consequence. But for the established sects, it represented a treasure more valuable than even a Relic-Class weapon. Such arrays meant survival. They meant the difference between prosperity and annihilation.
Sadly, Arnold's flawless brushwork died with him. Though other sects had tried desperately to copy the Ethereal Spirit Sect's arrays, pouring fortunes into the effort and sacrificing countless hours, their results had always been disappointing imitations—pale copies of the original genius.

This explains the crowd's shock when Zane, with just over a hundred hastily drawn new runes, strengthened the arena's barriers enough to block a punch from a Life-and-Death Realm warrior. Disbelief rippled through the assembled masters like a physical wave.
Had he truly drawn those patterns himself? And if so, what kind of symbols could possibly possess that much power? The questions hung in the air, unanswered, as the spectators stared at the glowing barriers with a mixture of awe and fear.
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS REVELATION]
What the crowd didn't realize was that Zane hadn't drawn runes at all. He had woven divine textures—a far more potent art that allowed him to so drastically amplify the arena's barriers.
Of course, none of the leaders from the Heartlands could comprehend the power of divine textures. The concept was entirely foreign to them.

But with the limited materials available in the Heartlands, recreating the Grand Celestial Array was impossible. Besides, Zane still lacked the skill to inscribe even a nine or ten-star rune.

Maddox was stunned as the barrier simply absorbed his attack. All he could do was watch as Zane pressed his sword to Marilyn’s neck.
"I don't usually negotiate with ants," Maddox told Zane. "But I'll make an exception for you. What do you want?"
Maddox assessed the standoff. If Zane truly intended to kill Marilyn, he would have done it already. No, the kid was dragging this out for a reason.
[MUSIC: DARK DRAMATIC CRESCENDO]
Zane looked up at Maddox, his eyes blazing with cold fury. "Let Elara go," he said, his voice like ice.
The demand actually stunned Maddox, which was no small feat. He wracked his brain—why that request? And then, it clicked.
"I remember now," Maddox murmured, a flicker of recognition crossing his face. "I've seen you before."
And it was true. He'd seen Zane back at the Skyreach Sect. He should have placed him sooner, but given his own status, he’d never bothered to give the boy a second thought.
[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

The other sect leaders exchanged uneasy glances.
Lady Darkmere spoke in a carefully measured tone, "Lord Maddox, this isn't truly a matter of life and death between you. The boy simply wants his sister back. Surely that's... understandable."
The elders' motivations were plain enough. If they could convince Maddox to release Elara, they would have every right to approach Zane afterward—to ask him to construct a  Protecting Array for their sects. An array of that caliber could accelerate their growth from a mid-tier sect to an upper-tier one within a generation.
But Maddox wouldn't hear of it.
He tightened his grip and pulled Elara forward, his massive hand closing around her throat. "Zane," he called out, "release Marilyn, and once I've taken what I need from your sister, I'll return her to you. Unharmed. You have my word."
A beat of silence. Then Zane lowered his head—and began to laugh.
It started low, then rose into something raw and furious.
"What's so funny?" Maddox demanded.
Zane leveled a finger at him, his voice shaking with barely contained rage. "You take everything from a person—everything—and then promise to return her unharmed? That's your word? Unharmed?" He laughed again, the sound hollow and sharp as breaking glass.
Maddox's expression darkened. "Choose carefully. If you push me, I will make her suffering last. And when I'm done, there won't be enough of her left to bury."
His grip on Elara's neck tightened. She didn't react—couldn't. Her senses had been sealed completely. She couldn't hear the threat, couldn't feel the hand at her throat. She simply stood, motionless, like a statue of herself.
"Suffering?" Zane's laughter faded. His eyes went still and cold.
