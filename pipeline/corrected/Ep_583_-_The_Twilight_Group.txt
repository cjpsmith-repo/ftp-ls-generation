Teresa was still fuming, unable to stomach her loss.
Zane couldn't help but smile. He figured he'd be happy to give her a rematch, should their paths cross again on the third or fourth layer of the tower.
He lingered in the arena for a while, relieved when no one else stepped up to challenge him. It meant he could kick back for the rest of the day and snag a win by default, without a single fight.
[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT DRUMS]
A full day passed. When Zane returned to the arena, no one challenged him for a third straight day, and just like that, he qualified for the third layer. He felt ready for whatever the next level would throw at him.
[AMBIENT: TOWER INTERIOR ECHO]
The layout of the Tower of Sin was simple enough: the first few floors, up to the fifth, were crawling with War Fighters. But the higher you climbed, the more War Generals you’d run into, until by the fifth layer, they were all you saw.
Word of Zane's arrival rippled through all five layers. Third-layer fighters scrambled to avoid him, recognizing his reputation. Only the bravest—or most foolish—dared challenge him.
As Zane ascended, he drew increasing attention. Human War Generals from upper floors descended to observe, though few War Fighters risked facing him. The sparse fights he encountered ended swiftly and decisively.
Three days on the third layer. Zero challenges. Zane advanced to the fourth without throwing a single punch.
[AMBIENT: BATTLE MARCH]
[MUSIC: OMINOUS DRUMS]
When allied forces from different races marched on the ogres' sacred place with fire in their eyes, the Ogres finally realized they'd overplayed their hand. It had been a reckless mistake. With no way to fight off the siege, they grudgingly opened the doors to the Tower of Sin again, even forced to give up some of their seats.
[MUSIC: DARK ORCHESTRAL]
After the uprising, the power dynamic in the Tower of Sin became a smaller version of the political landscape on the main continent. But humans were in a tough spot, easily shoved aside by the stronger races, leaving them with little influence in the tower. The Ogre race, however, were a powerful and undefeated race; no one in the Tower of Sin dared to cross them.
Even though the Ogres had lost their monopoly, they still controlled a huge chunk of the Tower.
[AMBIENT: LARGE STONE CHAMBER]
The large chamber was sparsely decorated—rough, and anything but elegant. A few Ogres were scattered around the room, some sitting on the floor, others sprawled out carelessly. They seemed to be in the middle of a heavy discussion.
[SFX: HEAVY FOOTSTEPS]
[MUSIC: TENSE DRUMS]
A moment later, the sound of loud, heavy footsteps echoed through the hall. An Ogre War Lord in a cloak appeared at the entrance, breathing hard. "The order to assassinate Zane is out!"
[SFX: CHAIR SCRAPING]
Boris shot to his feet. "Assassinate the kid?"
The other Ogres glanced at him with disinterest. Another Ogre stepped forward. "Boris, you have no right to speak here!"
"When that kid reaches the tenth floor, I'll end him myself," Boris said through gritted teeth. "Why do we need an assassin?"
The cloaked War Lord stared at him, expression stony. Boris was a saint-level genius now—bold enough to challenge a War Lord. Any other War General would have stayed silent.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
"I understand how you feel, but this order comes from the top," the War Lord said, his voice deceptively calm, barely masking his irritation. "If this kid makes it to War General, stopping him will be a hell of a lot harder. Are you so sure you can take him?"
Boris growled, furious that his own kind doubted him. “I know I can kill him with my bare hands!”
The War Lord sneered at Boris's defiance. "I'm sure you do. But it doesn't matter. Zane will be killed by the Twilight Group's death squad," he declared, then huffed and stormed out of the room.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
The Twilight Group, the sixth-ranked race on the main continent, earned their name by how they operated: they hid during the day and struck only after sunset.
They weren't the strongest race in a straight fight — but they didn't need to be. Assassination was in their blood. They could end a life without a sound, without a trace, before their target ever knew they were there.
And they weren't operating alone. For generations, the Twilight Group had lived under the Ogres' protection — and in return, they did the Ogres' dirty work. Every major race in the Tower knew the arrangement. As long as the Ogres didn't go too far, nobody said a word.
In all the years the Twilight Group had operated inside the Tower of Sin, not one of their targets had walked away alive.
Boris stood alone in the chamber after the others had gone. He unclenched his fists slowly. He'd meant every word — he believed he could beat Zane. He'd been looking forward to it.
But now that chance was being taken from him. He stared at the floor, jaw tight.
Somewhere in the back of his mind, a thought took shape that he'd never expected to have: he almost hoped the assassination would fail.
[MUSIC: MELANCHOLIC PIANO]
Back on the second layer, when Teresa found out Zane had already qualified for the fourth, her hope began to drain away. If he kept climbing at this breakneck speed, she'd never catch him. By the time she qualified for the third layer, she'd heard he was already entering the fourth. She frowned, realizing that by the time she reached the fourth, he'd probably be on the fifth.
She let out a frustrated sigh. Someone had to slow him down. Otherwise, she'd never get her rematch.
[AMBIENT: QUIET TRAINING HALL]
On his first day on the fourth layer, Zane was disappointed, but not surprised, when no one stepped forward to challenge him. He just shook his head with a wry smile. Nobody even made eye contact anymore. He went ahead and rented a secluded practice room to keep up his training.
But something else was starting to grate on him. He hadn't fought anyone in three straight days, which meant he hadn't earned any Lucky Light or a single point. His mood soured even more when he realized he'd have to dip into his own Aether crystals to pay for the practice room.
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL TENSION]
[AMBIENT: EERIE SILENCE]
The moment Zane stepped into the practice room, he froze. The hair on his arms prickled as the air inside shifted, growing heavy.
His eyes swept the room, suspicion tightening his gut. He could swear something was off.
