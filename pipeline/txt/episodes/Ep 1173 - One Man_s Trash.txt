Ep 1173 - One Man’s Trash

Word count: 1118

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS WONDER]
[AMBIENT: VAST ECHOING HALL]
Zane had never seen anyone react to the Elysian Keep quite like Livingston did.
Livingston threw himself onto the pale brick floor like he was being reunited with a long-lost lover. 
[SFX: SOFT METALLIC CLANK]
He lay there for a moment, his eyes drifting across the wide-open expanse of the Elysian Keep, before a soft *clank* brought him back to reality. He scrambled back to his hands and knees and began examining the details etched into the floor, which stretched for over a hundred feet in every direction.
“No way,” he breathed, and a moment later, a high-pitched shriek of excitement echoed through the hall.
[SFX: CLAW TAPPING ON STONE]
He tapped a claw against the floor, his voice trembling with disbelief. “This is Imperial Vigorite. *Genuine* Imperial Vigorite. I’ve only ever seen one piece in my entire life, and it was barely the size of my palm.”
Livingston had lived an exceptionally long time—longer than some of the Supreme Lords—and he knew just how impossibly rare the material was. But here it was, an entire plaza paved with priceless Imperial Vigorite. The sheer, unbelievable wealth of it was staggering.
A faint smile touched Zane’s lips as he watched Livingston’s frantic display. It was nice to finally have someone around who understood what they were looking at. 
Zane had always known the Elysian Keep was special, but he’d never paid much attention to the floors and walls. The thought that he’d been walking on a fortune this whole time was a shock.
[SFX: MAGICAL MATERIALIZATION WHOOSH]
Just then, Saul appeared out of thin air, greeting Zane with a respectful nod. “Welcome back, Master,” he said, his voice calm and even.
Before Zane could reply, Livingston was already rushing forward.
“You’re an automaton?” he gasped, clambering all over Saul, looking him up and down. 
[SFX: METALLIC TAPPING]
He tapped the automaton’s legs, then his chest. “You really are a puppet! Unbelievable! I never thought anyone could make a puppet this perfect.”
Livingston had always been intensely proud of his weaponsmithing skills, but he knew in an instant that he couldn’t hold a candle to whoever had created Saul. Though the puppet body Livingston had made for himself was crude and ugly, it housed a perfect law of life that had allowed his soul to survive for centuries without fading. He’d believed he was the only one in the world capable of such a feat. 
But looking at Saul’s flawless construction, he finally understood what it felt like to be completely outclassed. His own life’s work suddenly seemed like a child’s toy.
Saul’s expression remained placid. “Please stop touching me.”
But Livingston was too fascinated to stop. How could he?
Zane stepped in. “He’s a weaponsmith,” he told Saul. “From the previous cosmic era.”
A strange look crossed Saul’s face. He studied Livingston for a moment — the ugly, clanking frame, the erratically flickering head-lights — and then spoke with the measured tone of someone delivering a verdict. “Surviving a full cosmic era using only the materials available at the time… to create a puppet body capable of housing a soul this long without fading.” A pause. “Ugly. But not without talent.”
[SFX: INDIGNANT METALLIC RATTLE]
“Ugly—?!” Livingston sputtered.
“The materials were inferior,” Saul continued, unruffled. “You made the best of what you had. That is all.”
[SFX: METAL CLATTERING]
Suddenly, his body seized up, and he collapsed with a clatter of metal. 
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS RUSHING]
Zane rushed over, pulling a few Sovereign Stones from his pocket. 
[SFX: STONE SLOTTING INTO GROOVE]
After helping Livingston into a sitting position, he slotted the stones into a groove on the puppet’s back.
[SFX: MECHANICAL WHIRRING RESTART]
A moment later, Livingston could move again. 
As the group moved deeper into the Keep, Livingston fell behind, his head turning in every direction. Every railing, every pavilion post, every floor tile — supreme treasures, each one. He reached out to touch a railing and pulled his hand back. Reached again. Stopped. After a while, the sheer density of it broke something in him, and he went quiet. Not peaceful — numb. There was only so much wonder a mind could hold before it simply gave up trying.
It was in this dazed state that he spotted the passages leading off from the central pavilion — a library, an alchemy room, and then—
The first thing he did was point a shaky claw toward a sealed archway. “Why can’t I go into the Armory?”
Zane just shrugged. “Simple. I don’t have the right to get in, so you don’t either.”
[SFX: BODY SLUMPING AGAINST BARRIER]
“Argh!” Livingston let out a miserable groan and slumped against the invisible barrier. He stared longingly at the Armory door, his gaze so intense it looked like he was trying to burn a hole straight through it. “How do I get in?”
“It comes down to luck,” Zane answered honestly. He was determined to find a way through the Keep’s true path, but since he only had three of the master tokens, he couldn’t make any promises about unlocking the Armory.
Realizing there was no clear path forward, Livingston was crushed. 
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS DRAGGING]
He scrambled to his feet to follow Zane, dragging his feet and glancing back at the sealed door with every step. “Luck? What kind of luck?”
Saul, who had taken the lead, answered without looking back. “With your pathetic weaponsmithing skills, you wouldn’t be qualified to control the Armory even if you did manage to open it.”
“How is that possible?” Livingston sputtered, his voice cracking with indignation. After centuries of believing he was the greatest weaponsmith alive, he wasn’t about to let some puppet insult him. People could question his sanity, even his very existence, but never his craft. How could anyone who didn’t know what he’d sacrificed dare to doubt him?
He opened his mouth to argue — and stopped.
His gaze drifted back to the pale bricks in the walls. Radiant Sand. Imperial Vigorite on the floors. Every surface in this place was built from materials he had spent his entire existence dreaming of touching. 
If this was the standard — if the master who built this place considered those materials ordinary — then what did that make his own carefully sourced, painstakingly gathered supplies?
Trash. By comparison, genuine trash.
His mouth closed. For once, he had nothing to say.
A faint, knowing smile touched Saul’s lips. 
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
[AMBIENT: ANCIENT LIBRARY]
He pointed toward another grand hall. “My master’s Archives contain a vast collection, including many books on weaponsmithing,” he said, his tone softening just enough to sound magnanimous. “You are free to read them.”
