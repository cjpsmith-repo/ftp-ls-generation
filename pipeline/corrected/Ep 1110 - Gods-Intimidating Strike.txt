Ep 1110 - Gods-Intimidating Strike

Word count: 1064

[AMBIENT: WIND HOWLING]
[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
On Willow Island, the warriors didn’t waste a second. As the sword intent swirled in the clouds above, the sword masters had a clear advantage, their instincts helping them decipher the technique far faster than the others. 
But the saber and spear wielders weren’t about to let an opportunity like this slip by. The core principles were universal enough that they, too, could learn something from the ghostly strikes in the sky.
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
Zane just stood there, staring up at the heavens, lost in thought. Then, a slow smile spread across his face as it all clicked into place.
*So that’s why it’s been so hard to control my sword intent since I refined it,* he thought. *Because you’re not supposed to control it at all.*
[SFX: DRAMATIC IMPACT]
[MUSIC: INTENSE PERCUSSION]
The realization hit him like a physical blow. This wasn’t just a move; it was a last resort. It was designed to kill in a single, all-or-nothing shot. You had to lay everything on the line in that one thrust—your focus, your energy, even your own life. An extreme sword intent like that could only be channeled through an equally extreme technique.
Normally, the first step to mastering any skill was learning to control it. The more control you had, the more power you could wield.
But Supreme Lord Sword had skipped that part. He’d abandoned all sense and reason, unleashing his full power without a single thought for control.
“The only way to fight a True God is to go all in, to throw reason out the window,” Zane muttered to himself. “Is that why they say he’s on the same level?”
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL WIND]
[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]
If Supreme Lord Sword had been there to hear him, he would have been deeply impressed. A young man who’d only just broken through to the Soul Sea Realm had managed to figure it all out from a single move. Because Zane had already touched upon that same sword intent, he was learning from the display in the clouds at a speed no one else could match.
[AMBIENT: EERIE SILENCE]
For a time, Willow Island was completely still. Every warrior was fixated on the sky, determined to squeeze every last drop of understanding from the fading sword clouds.
[SFX: TIME PASSAGE CHIMES]
Two hours passed. Then twenty. Then two full days.
[AMBIENT: OCEAN WAVES]
[MUSIC: ETHEREAL CHOIR]
It wasn’t until the third day that the clouds finally dispersed, revealing a stunning purple sky where the blue of the heavens reflected off the waters of the Orange Sea Realm.
A few warriors remained rooted to the spot, having only grasped a fraction of the technique’s power. 
The palace masters wore faint, satisfied smiles of their own. Their cultivation ran so far ahead of the Thirteen Palaces’ warriors that they’d taken in plenty from the sword cloud without even trying hard.
[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]
“Millions of years,” one of the palace masters said slowly, almost to himself. “Supreme Lord Sword’s words have sat carved into that rock for millions of years. Countless sword cultivators have come here and failed to crack them. So why now? Why today, of all days?”
No one had an answer.
A short distance away, Yolande’s gaze settled on Zane. Before Passing the Torch had even begun, Zane mentioned seeing strange white sword shadows—and he’d shown up several hours late as a result. Could the two things be connected?
The official in the cotton robes seemed to land on the same thought at the same moment. “Zane,” he said, “before Passing the Torch started—were you meditating out here?”
Layla’s head turned sharply. That was right—she remembered now too. He had been late.
Zane gave a helpless shrug. Sure, he’d absorbed those white sword shadows earlier. But as far as he could tell, that had nothing to do with whatever had just happened in the clouds. He had no better answer to give them.
But one man, an old warrior from the Spirit Supreme Realm, had somehow understood thirty percent of it.
[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]
The other leaders watched him, their faces grim. How could some old-timer comprehend so much more than they, the powerful and talented lords of their realms? It made no sense.
Of course, none of them knew that this old man, Landry, had spent sixty thousand years sitting by that huge rock, trying to decipher the words Supreme Lord Sword had left behind. All those years of study had finally paid off. He and Damian had gained more from this than even the Lord of the Sunset World.
Both of them were carrying Grade-Three Divine Weapons—the kind of blade most warriors of the Thirteen Palaces would have outgrown decades ago. Whatever Landry was about to do, he’d be doing it with about the weakest sword on the entire island.
Damian’s sword told its own story. Tens of thousands of years of polishing hadn’t kept it pristine—if anything, the care had worn it thin. Hairline cracks ran the length of the blade now, the price of a lifetime spent trying to keep something whole that was always going to wear down anyway. Sword cultivators tended to love their swords like that.
[SFX: SWORD WHOOSH]
A sharp *whoosh* cut through the air as Landry tested his new insight. The strike he unleashed held only thirty percent of Supreme Lord Sword’s original power, but the result was terrifying. No warrior from the Spirit Supreme Realm could ever dream of wielding such force.
“The Gods-Intimidating Strike!” someone shouted, recognition dawning across the crowd all at once—they’d all just spent three days staring at nothing else. “He’s got thirty percent of it!”
His cracked sword pierced the massive boulder. For a moment, nothing happened. 
[SFX: STONE CRACKING]
[SFX: BOULDER COLLAPSE]
Then, the stone, which was larger than a mountain, began to crack and crumble, collapsing into a pile of dust and rubble.
[MUSIC: EMOTIONAL STRINGS]
The sight overwhelmed Landry. Tears streamed down his face, hot and sudden. After being stuck in this place for thousands of years, he had long ago given up any hope of achieving anything more in his life. Like all the other warriors who had chosen to stay, he knew it was a dead end. All he could do was meditate and hope for a miracle.
Most of the others had already given up or been driven off. Only he and Damian had stubbornly remained, paying a huge price just for the privilege of staying. They had been hopeless, simply waiting for old age to finally claim them.
But it hadn’t. And now, this had happened.
