Following the tense confrontation between Zane and James, everyone's appetites had awakened with fierce intensity. Their bodies, having burned through adrenaline and anxiety, now demanded nourishment.

They quickly identified the best restaurant that was still open and led the way with confident ease, pointing out landmarks and sharing brief anecdotes as they walked.

"This way cuts the journey in half," he declared proudly, gesturing toward a narrow passage fragrant with the mingled scents of nearby kitchens. The group followed, their footsteps quickening as delicious aromas promised relief for their empty stomachs.

Throughout the meal, Ethan put away drink after drink but remained completely sober. If anything, the more he drank, the more energized he became. It had been a while since they had all gotten together like this, even though they were all on Misty Peak. They ended up talking late into the night.

Adepts usually had a high tolerance for alcohol, especially if they made a conscious effort to control it. But maybe Ingrid was just so happy to be with Zane that she'd let her guard down and had a little too much. The two girls she'd brought along were walking with Rhys and Ethan, lost in their own conversation.

Even though it was late, the nightlife in the Capital was just getting started. Lanterns on the rooftops lit up the street so brightly it was almost like daylight, casting long shadows of the people strolling by.
Zane couldn't help but steal glances at Ingrid, her face captivating under the moonlight. The longer he looked at her, the faster his heart began to beat.

"Hey, you still haven't answered me," Ingrid said, her voice a little softer now. "Why didn't you come to my peak to see me?" Coming from someone of her status, it was a bold question. But she was happy, and a little tipsy---a dangerous combination. This was just how Ingrid was, though; when she liked someone, she couldn't get them off her mind.
If she'd been sober, she never would have found the nerve to ask. The alcohol had given her just enough courage.
Zane was quiet for a moment. "I was busy," he said finally. "I forgot."
Ingrid looked at him. A flicker of something crossed her face — disappointment, maybe. But then it softened. At least he wasn't making excuses.
As enchanting as she was, Zane couldn't stop his mind from churning over the events of the evening. His thoughts drifted to his own weapon. The broken throwing blade was only good for sneak attacks; it was useless in a straight-up fight.
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC ANTICIPATION]
The All Peaks Competition was coming up soon. It would bring together all the top disciples of the Skyreach Sect, and there would be no shortage of powerful, talented fighters.
Zane had been preparing for this competition the entire time. Fortunately, it was scheduled for after the Midwinter Festival, so he still had plenty of time to get ready.
As for a weapon, Zane knew he had to find the one that was right for him.
The most common weapons were slashing knives and swords. In the Eastern Territory, however, most people preferred swords.
Swords were considered the king of all weapons. They could be used for both slashing and thrusting, and the training manuals contained more techniques for swordsmanship than for any other skill. Knives were powerful, too. They weren't good for thrusting, but their thicker blades made them brutally effective for slashing.
Besides knives and swords, there were other options like bows, hammers, axes, and spears.
After thinking it over for a moment, Zane decided on a sword.
He remembered his father had once wielded a top-tier Mythic sword called the Green Wind-Rider Sword. Unfortunately, it had been lost. Since his father had been a master swordsman, Zane figured he might have a natural knack for it, too.
Before they parted ways, Dustin pulled Zane aside.
"Those fifty drops of Celestial Essence you gave me — I'm staying in the Capital a few extra days to arrange the auction properly. Last time one drop went up, we had buyers coming to us for weeks afterward asking for more. This time I want the right people in the room before it goes to the block."
Zane nodded. "I'll have more when you need it."
Dustin's eyes lit up. "That's what I was hoping you'd say."
[AMBIENT: BUSY PAVILION]
Zane said his goodbyes to his friends and rushed to the Weapon Pavilion. As usual, the place was buzzing with business.
When Zane walked in, the clerks didn't give him a second glance. But the middle-aged shopkeeper who had helped him before spotted him immediately, his face lighting up.
The last time Zane was there, he had bought over two hundred swords at once. The massive purchase had left a lasting impression on the shopkeeper, who had remembered Zane ever since. The commission from that deal alone had been huge.
So when he saw Zane again, he couldn't hide his excitement and hurried over to greet him.
