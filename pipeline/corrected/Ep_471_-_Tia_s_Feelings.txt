Ep 471 - Tia's Feelings

Word count: 1027

[AMBIENT: WIND RUSHING]

[MUSIC: URGENT ORCHESTRAL]

[SFX: RUNNING FOOTSTEPS]

Zane didn't take any time to admire his newly upgraded defensive tower. The moment the process finished, he was already sprinting toward Stoney's territory, his boots pounding against the earth.

The sixth wave of beasts was supposed to be fiercer than the last—everyone knew that. But worry never crossed Zane's mind. With Lily guarding his tower—now a full level higher than any competitor's—he knew his territory would be secure. She had never failed him before.

[SFX: SKITTERING INSECT LEGS]

[AMBIENT: OMINOUS RUMBLE]

His instincts were right. Moments after Zane disappeared from view, a swarm of ten-foot-long purple crystal centipedes came skittering into his territory. Thousands of tiny legs propelled their crystalline bodies across the ground in a frantic, zigzagging charge. Sunlight glinted off their translucent carapaces, creating an almost hypnotic display of violet light that belied the creatures' deadly nature.

The six archers positioned in Zane's tower drew their longbows in perfect unison.

[SFX: ARROWS SWOOSHING]

[SFX: CRACKLING FIRE]

Six fire arrows streaked into the sky, then arced gracefully downward, plunging directly into the heart of the centipede swarm. Each projectile trailed a tail of flame that painted bright orange streaks across the air.

[SFX: EXPLOSIONS]

A series of explosions tore through the swarm with devastating efficiency, instantly vaporizing more than a hundred creatures. Fragments of purple crystal scattered like deadly confetti, glittering as they fell. The archers were already nocking their second volley before the echoes of the first had faded.

[AMBIENT: TENSE OBSERVATION HALL]

[MUSIC: MOUNTING DRAMA]

Watching the slaughter, the examiner threw his hands up. "I knew it! I knew he’d pull something like this and break the balance of the entire competition!"

Michael Nash just cracked a wry smile. A level-five tower was supposed to be fighting these purple crystal centipedes. A level-six tower, on the other hand, should have been facing the seventh wave. But Zane had jumped the gun, upgrading early. His tower was so overpowered that the centipedes didn't stand a chance.

The old man’s mustache twitched with amusement. "Well, he played by the rules to do it. What are you going to do about it?"

The examiner’s face was red. For him, the integrity of the magic array was everything, and in his eyes, Zane had just spit on it. He stewed for a moment, then snapped, "What if we just remove him from the array?"

Michael Nash and the old man exchanged a look, both subtly shaking their heads. "I don't think that's a great idea," Michael said carefully. "But you're in charge of the array. It's your call."

The examiner’s glare was fixed on the Scrying Slate, his eyes narrowed as he watched Zane tear through Stoney’s territory, poaching points. That was the last straw. "Alright, I've made my decision," he declared. "I'm kicking him out!"

He barked an order at his subordinates. The entire magic array was managed from a massive central platform, but there were smaller mobile consoles for direct interventions. At his command, several attendants wheeled a table-sized control platform over to him.

[AMBIENT: MAGICAL HUMMING]

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL CRESCENDO]

The examiner stepped up to the console and placed his hand over a complex, glowing rune. With a single command, he could eject Zane from the simulation.

"Wait, what?" Tia murmured from where she'd been standing by herself, observing the entire trial. She'd arrived shortly after the examination began, drawn by curiosity about how Zane would perform against competitors from across the Heartlands. Her confusion was evident.

The examiner, noticing her reaction, switched the Scrying Slate's view back to Zane's territory. Throughout the examination, Tia had watched in silence, her expression carefully neutral as she monitored Zane's unconventional approach. Now, however, uncertainty flickered across her features.

[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT STRINGS]

"Look," he said, pointing. "He left his sword spirit to defend his base while he ran off to another player’s territory to steal their points. Now his tower is a level ahead of everyone else. The rest of the waves will be a complete cakewalk for him!"

If Zane's tower maintained its one-level advantage, he would sail through the first phase of the pre-qualifiers. The realization struck Tia like a thunderbolt. After a moment of stunned silence, she felt a smile bloom across her face, impossible to suppress. The worried lines on her forehead vanished, and her eyes curved into happy crescents.

She caught herself quickly, forcing her features back into careful neutrality. As Cloud Hall's leader, she couldn't afford to display such open delight—not here, not in front of these officials who already questioned her every decision. They expected composure and formality, the dignified bearing of someone worthy of her position. Such a stark contrast to the person she became when alone with Zane, when she could let her guard down and simply be herself.

But inside, her heart soared. Zane always discovered the most brilliantly unconventional solutions to impossible problems. It was one of the qualities that had first caught her attention, back when she'd been watching him from the shadows.

[MUSIC: RISING TENSION]

Her joy, however, was tangled up with anxiety. A part of her desperately hoped Zane wouldn't pass the pre-qualifiers. The main tournament wasn't merely a competition—it was a meat grinder that chewed up talented cultivators and spat out broken bodies. Zane possessed saint-level talent for cultivation, a gift so rare it appeared perhaps once in a generation. But his actual power remained dangerously low for the Heartlands. He needed to hide in the shadows, concealing his potential until he grew strong enough to defend himself against those who would destroy him out of jealousy or fear.

But she also knew Zane's nature. Once he set his mind to something, nothing could stop him—not reason, not danger, not even her pleas. She had a feeling he would pass these matches no matter what. Another part of her—the part that believed in him—felt secretly thrilled at the prospect of watching him prove everyone wrong.

These conflicting emotions had been warring inside her all day, which is why she’d come to watch him in the first place.

Still, she never could have predicted this.
