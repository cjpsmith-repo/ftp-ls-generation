But Isla wasn't about to let it go that easily. "Eagle Peak?" she repeated, her voice sharp as she turned to another master. "Master Jasper! Your Core Apprentice initiated a fight and assaulted another student. According to the rules, what is the proper punishment?"
Master Jasper stepped forward from the group of masters. His eyes swept over his apprentices before he spoke. "According to the Sect's rules," he said, his voice flat, "the penalty is a deduction of one thousand Points."
[SFX: CROWD GASPS]
Upon hearing these words, the blood drained from the tall apprentice's face, leaving him ashen and visibly shaken. Losing a hundred Points would have been troublesome enough, but a thousand? That was utterly catastrophic—a punishment that could derail his entire cultivation journey.
Points functioned as the very lifeblood of the Skyreach Sect, a currency more precious than gold to those seeking advancement. Apprentices required them for virtually everything—from accessing training grounds and cultivation chambers to purchasing instructional manuals and acquiring rare cultivation resources. The tall apprentice's hands trembled slightly as he contemplated the long, arduous path ahead to recover from such a loss.
Acquiring Points proved notoriously difficult, a fact that every student learned through bitter experience. While apprentices could officially earn Points by completing missions and tasks for the Sect, the rewards rarely matched the effort expended.
Many resorted to simply purchasing them outright, particularly those from wealthy backgrounds. The exchange rate had once been reasonable—one Crystal Cube for one Point—but in recent years, inflation had struck mercilessly, driving the price to two Crystals for a single Point.
The tall apprentice swallowed hard, mentally calculating the fortune this punishment would cost his house, and the disappointed faces he would soon have to confront.

After the ruling, Master Isla turned and walked straight toward Zane. "There's something off with your Student ID," she said. "Let me take a look."
She took the card in both hands, studied it for a moment, then handed it back. "My mistake," she said lightly. "Everything's fine." She smiled and walked away.
Zane stared at the card, then pocketed it. He had no idea what that was about. But right now, getting through The Gauntlet was all that mattered.
