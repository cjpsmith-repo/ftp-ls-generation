[MUSIC: BATTLE PREPARATION]

“The second group!”

The man in the simple cotton clothes stretched out a hand, casually making another mark on the list and calling out two hundred names.

[SFX: MAGICAL WHOOSH]

As the names floated out like glowing embers, the chosen warriors stepped forward and entered the crucible.

[SFX: BATTLE SOUNDS]

Most of them managed to survive the first six rounds of challenges in the Illusionary Arena, but after that, the difficulty shot up. Warriors started dropping out, defeated and eliminated. The rounds ticked by—seven, eight, nine, ten—and with each one, the number of eliminated fighters grew.

Even so, a few powerful contenders in the second group showed some serious talent. One of them, a warrior named Clay, lasted for an astonishing nineteen rounds before he was finally taken down.

This time, the man in cotton clothes seemed almost satisfied. “In this group,” he said in his usual dry tone, “Clay held on for nineteen rounds. Not bad.”

A faint smile finally broke through Clay’s otherwise blank expression. He shot a glance at Zane and Layla, waiting for a reaction. After all, anyone with enough talent to enter the Thirteen Palaces was bound to have a little pride. Before joining the Passing the Torch, Clay had been supremely confident in his own strength. But his performance in the Enchanted Barrier Tower had been a disappointment, nowhere near Zane and Layla’s level. He knew getting first place would be tough, but he’d never expected the gap between them to be that huge.

[MUSIC: TENSION RISING]

Then came the third group, and with it Jasper Reed of Cloudswallow Hall. He’d taken a beating in the Enchanted Barrier Tower and was still recovering from it — but by the tower’s standards alone, he’d have ranked among the top three. Word was, he was the best Cloudswallow Hall had.

He fought well — for a while, it looked like he might post one of the strongest scores of the day. But in the end, he lasted only seventeen rounds before he was eliminated.

For Cloudswallow Hall’s top warrior, seventeen rounds was a disappointing result. When Jasper left the Illusionary Arena, he looked dejected. Zane couldn’t help shaking his head at the sight. Jasper was stubborn enough, but his confidence had always leaned on other people’s belief in him rather than his own footing — and that kind of foundation was easy to shake. Layla barely reacted at all.

While the third group’s names floated out, Layla still hadn’t found hers. She reached up, slipped off one of her heart-shaped earrings, and rolled it slowly between her fingers. This time, she didn’t put much force behind it — just enough to feel it crumple in her palm.

With over four thousand fighters going in batches of two hundred, it was going to take more than twenty groups to get through everyone. Zane settled in for a long wait. Layla, seeming to have a good sense of how this was going to play out, didn’t even bother looking up for her name.

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMUR]

[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]

As more warriors entered the Illusionary Arena, fighters who’d managed only five or eight thousand miles in the Enchanted Barrier Tower suddenly posted impressive results. Different tests revealed different strengths. The tower had measured endurance and understanding of the Laws, but some warriors couldn’t harness their full power amid the Law Storm’s chaos. In direct combat, they could finally demonstrate their true capabilities.

Because of this, the rankings were in constant flux. Warriors from the Cold Palace and Gold Palace were posting good scores, some even lasting twenty rounds. It was an impressive display, and the palace leaders were beaming with pride. After all, their students’ achievements reflected well on them. But whenever their eyes drifted to Zane and Layla, a flicker of annoyance would cross their faces. With the two top contenders still on the sidelines, nothing was decided yet. It was still anyone’s game.

[MUSIC: TENSION BUILDING]

Several hours later, it was finally time for the last group.

This group was smaller, with only one hundred and sixty-three warriors. The man in cotton clothes didn’t even need to call them; they knew it was their turn. They’d been waiting all day, and the air was thick with tension. This was their chance.

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

But when the man marked the list, and the last names were selected, Zane and Layla still weren’t among them.

Zane stared, shocked and confused. What was going on? Beside him, Layla tilted her head, her expression just as puzzled.

The man in cotton clothes, still hovering in the air, seemed to notice their expressions. “You two are in the next group,” he announced.

“Are you serious?” Zane groaned. The old man’s face was perfectly calm, as if this was the most normal thing in the world. He was just being stubborn, insisting on putting Zane and Layla in a group all by themselves.

[AMBIENT: WIND OVER STONE]

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]

As the last fragment of light fell away, the words covering the massive rock face peeled off, leaving only a few jagged lines around the edges. Landry and Damian stared in awe at the now-barren stone, their breath held in anticipation. After a long moment, they both stepped back.

For two days and two nights, the cracks had spread across the boulder’s carved characters, slow at first, then faster and faster. Landry and Damian had barely moved the whole time, watching without blinking — to them, those two days felt longer than ten thousand years.

Neither of them knew what Zane had done, but he’d clearly triggered something, which meant whatever mystery the old Supreme Lord Sword had left behind here might actually be real. If it wasn’t, the disappointment alone could kill them on the spot. But if it was, even grasping ten or twenty percent of it would change everything.

Landry had never been the talented kind. Even giving everything he had, the best he could hope for was a deputy city lord’s seat in some minor holy land. People who came to stare at this rock generally fell into one of two camps — those who believed they were brilliant enough to crack it, and those who were ordinary and simply refused to spend their whole lives that way. Landry was the second kind. So was Damian. These two days were the most important of their lives.

[MUSIC: SLOW TENSION BUILD]

[SFX: STONE CRUMBLING]

Piece by piece, the carved characters had crumbled and slid down the stone, shattering at the base. Every time a fragment fell, Landry scrambled to gather it up like it was something precious — even though he already knew, after staring at this rock for sixty thousand years, that the stones were just stones.

There was no reaction. No hidden secret revealed.

A bitter smile touched Damian’s pale lips. He let out a deep, shuddering sigh. “From now on,” he said, “we can be free.”

“Free? Ha!” Landry’s laugh was hysterical, echoing off the blank stone. “Sixty thousand years! Sixty thousand years! I’ve been sitting here for sixty thousand years!”

He had been there three times longer than Damian. Back when Willow Island was first turned into a site for the Passing the Torch, every warrior stationed before the rock had been forced to leave.

But Landry had fought for the right to stay. And he had won.
