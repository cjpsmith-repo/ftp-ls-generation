Ep 1183 - A Grain of Sand

Word count: 1020

[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
Even with Supreme Lord Armond unleashing his hottest flames, the rate at which the Star of Golden Sand burned slowed down. The star was proving stubbornly resilient. Its surface was soft sand, but that gave way to ten miles of soil, which in turn rested on a layer of hard rock. Deeper still was the mantle, far tougher than any ordinary stone, wrapped tight around the star’s very core—an object of incredible sturdiness and astonishing weight. 
[SFX: DEEP RUMBLING]
A piece of it no bigger than a hand weighed as much as an entire mountain.
It was clear Armond’s flames weren’t going to burn through the entire star anytime soon.
“Leave the Crown of Destiny to me,” Supreme Lord Magnus said, seizing the opportunity. “Someone in my clan desperately needs it.”
“Someone in your clan?” another Supreme Lord scoffed. “Don’t be ridiculous. You’re not the only one with a claim to it!”
The truth was, there were countless World Lords in the boundless universe, but far too few Crowns of Destiny to go around. 
[SFX: DISTANT ECHOING TOKEN]
Nearly twenty Supreme Lords had already arrived, and with Zane’s Roaring Token still echoing through the cosmos, more were on their way. It created a rather large problem: who, exactly, would get the crown? It couldn’t be split in half, much less broken into pieces and shared.
Many of the assembled Lords were secretly on guard. They all knew it: if no agreement could be reached, this would almost certainly end in a fight.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
“Fools,” a different Supreme Lord’s voice cut through the bickering. “All you care about is this Crown of Destiny, but not one of you has stopped to consider what matters most. This is the one hundred and twenty-ninth Crown. If there really is someone who carries the Heavenly Destiny, have any of you thought about what that means?”
[SFX: HEAVY SILENCE]
His words fell like a stone, plunging the group into a sudden, unnerved silence.
A single Cosmic Age could only sustain one hundred and twenty-eight supreme lords. That number had always been fixed. The appearance of a one hundred and twenty-ninth was completely unprecedented. No one knew what would happen. Could it trigger the collapse of the heavens? Would it bring this great era to an abrupt end? The risks were immeasurable, too heavy to even comprehend.
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS APPROACHING]
The heavy silence was broken by the arrival of another of their rank.
“Ranell! You’re finally here!” Supreme Lord Addison called out. He had been the first to arrive, but the growing crowd of his peers had slowly pushed him to the edge of the gathering.
[SFX: EERIE RATTLING]
Ranell was hard to miss. Not because of his size or his power — but because of what was trailing behind him. Thousands of shadow-shapes swarmed at his back, each one skeletal, each one gnawing. They crawled over one another, a writhing mass of cursed forms, their teeth working ceaselessly against the thick shell of Ranell’s protective essence. They couldn’t break through. But they didn’t stop.
Addison stared. “Brother — what is all that behind you?”
Ranell’s expression soured. “Don’t ask. I ran into the Bone Curse Clan on the way here. They came at me like madmen, tearing through space to chase me down. I killed a good number of them.” He paused. “Apparently, that was the wrong move.”
[SFX: UNEASY MURMUR]
The other Lords exchanged glances. Every one of them knew the Bone Curse Clan’s reputation. It wasn’t that the Supreme Lords feared them — a Bone Curse warrior was no threat to someone at this level. The problem was what happened when you killed them. Each one that died released a cursed skeleton that latched onto the killer and could not be removed. Not by force, not by any technique a Supreme Lord possessed. They simply followed, gnawing, for years.
Ranell had killed enough of them that he’d brought thousands along for the journey. None of the other Lords said anything. It wasn’t their problem. Their eyes drifted back to the burning star.
[AMBIENT: CRAMPED SPACE]
Inside the Elysian Keep, Zane watched the star shrinking and felt a flicker of unease. There wasn’t much left to hide in.
Saul’s face was, as always, unreadable. “It’s too conspicuous to stay as a stone now,” he said. “I’ll make us smaller.”
[SFX: TRANSFORMATION HUM — RAPID SHRINKING]
The stone that housed the Keep shrank fast — past pebble, past splinter — until it was nothing more than a single grain of sand. Then it burrowed inward, driving itself into the very center of the star’s core.
[MUSIC: EPIC CELESTIAL TRANSFORMATION]
[AMBIENT: COSMIC VOID]
[SFX: STAR MELTING AND SHRINKING]
The star continued to burn. 
[SFX: GLASS SOLIDIFYING]
Its outer layers melted away, fusing the sand into a solid, glassy substance that glowed like molten metal. It shrank from a hundred miles in diameter to ten, then to one. Soon, it was no bigger than a thousand feet across, the fire finally reaching the core. The eighty-thousand-mile Star of Golden Sand was gone. 
[SFX: FINAL COLLAPSE]
All that remained was a small, black object, about the size of a person’s fist, hanging in the void.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS STRINGS]
The sight of it made the Supreme Lords’ faces fall.
[SFX: CONFUSED MURMURING]
“Where’s the Elysian Keep?”
“He couldn’t have escaped, right? A Spatial Transference? Impossible. We would’ve seen it!”
[AMBIENT: TENSE CHAMBER ATMOSPHERE]
A wave of confusion rippled through the assembled lords. None of them could understand how the miniature Elysian Keep had vanished right under their noses. Could it be inside that fist-sized, black star core?
Supreme Lord Armond’s eyes narrowed, a cold sneer twisting his lips. 
“Keep burning the core,” he declared. “The Elysian Keep is interesting, I’ll give it that. It’s hiding inside the core itself.”
And he was right. The miniature Elysian Keep had transformed into a single grain of sand and burrowed into the very center of the impossibly hard stellar remnant. As long as they kept the heat on, it would eventually be exposed.
[SFX: ROARING FLAMES]
[AMBIENT: INTENSE MAGICAL ENERGY]
Another Supreme Lord stepped forward. “Armond, let me add to that.” A second fire dragon took shape in his hand, circled once, and merged with Armond’s. The moment they fused, the combined flame more than doubled in power.
It shrank from the size of a fist to the size of a pebble, and then finally vanished completely, leaving behind a single grain of sand, slowly turning in the empty space.
