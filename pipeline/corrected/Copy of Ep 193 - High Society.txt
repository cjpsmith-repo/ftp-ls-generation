Ep 193 - High Society
Word count: 1107
[AMBIENT: QUIET SECLUDED AREA]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS TENSION]
Zane sat across from Dustin, their voices hushed in the private corner they had claimed. The revelation about his stash of Celestial Essence still hung in the air between them.
Dustin knew this could only mean one thing: Zane had a ton of Celestial Essence. More importantly, he might actually know how to make it.
Zane nodded. “Dustin, I want to sell the Celestial Essence through an auction at the Auction House. It seems like a win-win for your family, right?”
Dustin nodded enthusiastically.
“But you see my concern, right?” Zane said, his expression serious. “My worry is the pressure this is gonna put on your family if we do this a few more times. Can you guys handle that kind of heat?”
[SFX: DEEP HEAVY SIGH]
Dustin let out a deep, heavy sigh.
Zane had every right to be worried. After all, in this world, nothing was ever a sure thing.
[MUSIC: TENSE POLITICAL INTRIGUE]
The Blackwoods were powerful, no doubt---politically and financially---but they were still a rung below the Seven Great Houses. Auctioning off Celestial Essence once was one thing. Other lucky warriors had stumbled on a stash before. But if the Blackwood family started auctioning it off every month? That would raise more than a few eyebrows. It would draw a lot of unwanted attention.
It would point to only one conclusion: one of their clients either had a massive stockpile of Celestial Essence or, even more unbelievably, knew how to mass-produce it.
And once the Great Houses caught on, they’d put unbearable pressure on the Blackwoods. It’d only be a matter of time before the family cracked and gave up the source.
Dustin, however, saw a clear path forward. The Auction House had been in the game for years, and protecting their clients' identities was their number one rule. It could all be handled discreetly.
Dustin kept his face deadpan. "Before we get to the fifty-five drops — your concern about the family taking heat. I want to address it."
Dustin leaned forward. "The Auction House has been doing this for years. We have a method. The seller leaves the items at a predetermined location. Two unrelated couriers — who don't know each other or the seller — deliver a slip to our people. We collect the items without ever knowing who consigned them. When the sale clears, payment goes into an anonymous token, left back at the same location. The seller collects it on their own time. No names. No faces. No trail."
Zane was quiet for a moment. "And your family never knows who the seller is?"
"Not unless they want to lose every anonymous client they've ever had," Dustin said. "Which they don't."
Zane exhaled. That solved the problem he'd been turning over for weeks.
“Still, I have to ask again,” said Dustin. “Are you sure you want me to sell all fifty-five drops of this for you? 
Zane gave him a dismissive wave. “Ha! That many crystals might be a fortune to someone else, but not to the third son of the powerful Blackwood family. I trust you, man.”
He knew Dustin was playing the long game; he wouldn’t risk a future partnership for a quick payday. Besides, Zane had already let it slip that he had a lot more where this came from. Dustin would have to be an idiot to steal these fifty-five drops and blow his chance at a much bigger score down the road.
And if Dustin did cross him? Zane would cut him off completely. He'd make sure Dustin wasn’t safe even on Misty Peak---the guy would be looking over his shoulder for the rest of his life. The fallout from pissing Zane off would cost a hell of a lot more than half a million crystals.
[SFX: MAGICAL STORAGE SOUND]
Dustin took the vials of Celestial Essence from Zane, carefully tucking them into a beautiful emerald ring on his left hand---a Storage Ring.
Once it was secure, Dustin let out a long breath. “Selling this much... It’s going to cause a huge stir.”
The reason Celestial Essence was so precious---so valuable---was that you could apply it directly to your weapons.
For example, if you applied enough of it to a top-grade Spirit Weapon, you could trigger a fundamental transformation. There was even a chance it could evolve into a Mythic Weapon.
It was a long shot, requiring a ton of time, effort, and even more Celestial Essence, but a Mythic Weapon was worth the gamble. The owner could make a massive profit, even after factoring in the costs. That's what made the essence priceless.
[AMBIENT: MOUNTAIN PATH]
[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON ROCKY TERRAIN]
With the deal struck, Dustin stopped his training and shucked off his weighted suit. The two of them headed back toward the main peak to meet up with Rhys and Ethan.
“Hey, Zane! Dustin!” Ethan called out, a warm smile spreading across his face. “I was just looking for you guys.”
“What’s up?” Zane asked, looking confused.
Dustin clapped Zane on the shoulder, grinning. “You forgot, didn’t you? Ethan's family is throwing a party. He invited us a few days ago.”
Coming from a minor family---one with even less clout than House Moon---Zane didn’t know the first thing about the Great Houses or their traditions. Dustin, on the other hand, got it. He could see how trapped and unhappy Ethan was, crushed under the weight of his own family’s name.
“Ah, man, I’ve been so swamped, it totally slipped my mind. My bad,” Zane said. “The party’s today?”
Ethan nodded.
[AMBIENT: ELEGANT PARTY]
[MUSIC: CLASSICAL BACKGROUND]
At the estate, all the guests were respected and important members of society. A butler moved through the crowd, a professional smile plastered on his face as he catered to their every need, his manners impeccable.
[SFX: BUTLER APPROACHING]
But the moment he laid eyes on Ethan and his friends, his whole demeanor shifted. The practiced smile vanished, replaced by an arrogant sneer. “Young Master,” he said, his voice dripping with condescension. “What are you doing back here?”
It was obvious the feeling was mutual. Ever since his mother died, the mansion had stopped feeling like a home anyway. “What’s wrong?” Ethan shot back, his voice sharp. “Am I not allowed in my own house?”
[MUSIC: TENSE UNDERTONE]
The butler just shook his head. “Oh, you’ve got it all wrong, sir. It’s just... now that you’re an outer disciple of the Sect... whether you come home or not is hardly of any consequence, is it?” he added with a slight, knowing chuckle.
The butler put a heavy, pointed emphasis on the words “outer disciple.” The message was clear: even a declining House Moon still looked down on a mere outer disciple. He was warning Ethan not to get any ideas about his status in the family having improved one bit.
