Ep 964 - A Thousand Swords
Word count: 1101
Cora had said her quiet “okay,” and somewhere across the hall, the wedding celebration was still going.
The old man rubbed his forehead, trying to wrap his head around Cora.
His wife was the supreme master of the Violet Core Realm—a title countless warriors would kill for the chance to study under. Cora, though, was a different story entirely. He’d been the one to push her, to convince her to become his wife’s apprentice. It had taken ages to get her to agree, but in the end, she had.
He had a few words for Zane, too, but now just wasn’t the time. He let it go.
[AMBIENT: NIGHT CRICKETS]
[MUSIC: SOFT ROMANTIC STRINGS]
The celebration lasted deep into the night. It was well past midnight when the last of the guests finally trickled out, leaving Cloud Hall shrouded in silence.
[AMBIENT: QUIET BEDROOM]
Tia sat stiffly on the edge of the bed. The sheets, woven from exquisite Golden Silk, felt like a whisper against her skin.
Tia wasn’t old, not by any stretch, but she was definitely getting a later start than most. The sheer newness of it all made her jittery, even a little scared. Her nerves made her want to talk, to fill the quiet space between her and her new husband.
“Who was that old man we met today?” she asked, her voice barely a murmur.
Zane shook his head and offered a small smile. “I’m not really sure.”
The old man hadn’t introduced himself, but he was staying at Cloud Hall for the time being. Zane had a hunch he was someone important—maybe a master sent by the Celestial Paragon, or even one of the humans. After all, the White-Haired Priest had surely reported back to the upper world by now.
Tia’s bright eyes went wide. “You don’t know who he is? Then why on earth did you take his pill?” She tried to look stern, but the playful glint in her eyes gave her away. It was adorable.
It was enough to melt Zane’s heart. He sat down on the bed next to her. “Well, you saw him,” he said, shrugging. “He basically forced it down my throat.”
Honestly, he had no idea what the pill was for. His body felt perfectly normal, but maybe the effects just hadn’t kicked in yet. He figured all he could do was wait and see.
Tia turned to him, her brow creased with worry. “Do you feel alright? Any discomfort?”
Zane looked at her — the nervous set of her shoulders, the way she was trying so hard to seem composed — and felt something loosen in his chest. He reached over and pulled her gently into his arms. “None at all,” he said quietly.
He smiled and leaned in and kissed her.
She tensed for just a moment — then didn’t. The stiffness went out of her all at once, and she let herself be held.
The candles burned low. The night outside was very still.
[AMBIENT: COLD NIGHT WIND]
[MUSIC: MELANCHOLY STRINGS]
On the other side of Cloud Hall, Cora sat alone in the dark.
Out the window, through layers of cloud, she could just make out the last thin sliver of a quarter moon. The Ancestral Dragon had settled quietly at her side — it could sense something was wrong, and for once it wasn’t making a sound.
They sat like that for a long time.
Finally, Cora rubbed her shoulders against the cold and looked down at the dragon. “It’s not fun here,” she said. “I’ll take you somewhere better. Want to come?”
“Gagoom.” The dragon’s tail flicked, and it shot straight into her arms.
[SFX: MAGICAL SURGE]
A purple aura curled up from Cora’s body. Black-violet light swirled in her eyes. Then, without a sound, she stepped sideways through a space node — and was gone.
[AMBIENT: ETHEREAL WIND]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS WONDER]
The Violet Core Realm opened up around her, vast and dazzling, purple light blazing in every direction. The realm was incomplete — fractured long ago — but even a broken realm of this kind was countless times larger than the entire Lower World.
The Ancestral Dragon went wild with delight. It launched itself off her arms and went spiraling through the open space, crying out in pure joy.
Cora watched it for a moment. The old man had said his wife would find her here. She looked around. Nothing. No one.
“He lied,” she muttered, lips pursing.
But then the image came back to her — Zane somewhere far above, in the Upper World, and her already there waiting for him. She thought of Tia, and her expression hardened.
“Fine,” she said, to no one in particular. “I’ll practice on my own.”
A resolute look settled over her face — fierce and completely unlike her usual self. She wasn’t obsessed with martial arts. She was obsessed with Zane. It came to the same thing.
[SFX: SWORD WHOOSH]
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC ORCHESTRAL]
A faint shimmer in the air, and then, with a silent tear in the fabric of the sky, a gigantic sword descended.
Then another.
And another.
[SFX: MULTIPLE SWORD WHOOSHES]
[AMBIENT: RUMBLING SKY]
Soon, a torrent of countless swords rained down upon the Violet Core Realm.
[AMBIENT: ETHEREAL WIND]
[SFX: METALLIC RESONANCE]
Nine hundred and ninety-nine blades in total, they blotted out the stars, arranging themselves into a precise, deadly formation. Normally, such swords would vanish after a single strike, returning to the ether from which they came.
But with Cora now standing within the Violet Core Realm itself, the swords she summoned remained, shimmering with terrifying potential.
Each blade was an extension of a warrior’s will, drawn from all across the realm.
[AMBIENT: SKY WIND]
[MUSIC: SERENE STRINGS]
In the Southern Sky Region, a grand skyship inlaid with precious jewels glided through the clouds. A woman stood at its bow. She appeared to be in her thirties, with a timeless beauty and a fair, delicate face.
Her purple robe billowed in the wind, whipping past the ship.
[SFX: REALITY TEAR]
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC CRESCENDO]
The serene moment was shattered as the sky above her split open, and the massive swords began to fall.
The woman’s brow furrowed. “What is happening?”
[SFX: SPATIAL DISTORTION]
The gigantic swords of the Violet Core Realm could slice through space itself, arriving at their destination in an instant. It was a power that bent the laws of causality, allowing any warrior with a Violet-Core Physique to call upon them, no matter where they were—in this world, or any other.
Who in the world was doing this?
