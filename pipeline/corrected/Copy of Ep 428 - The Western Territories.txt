Ep 428 - The Western Territories

Word count: 1173

[AMBIENT: BUSY STREET CROWD]

[SFX: HEAVY DOOR CLOSING]

Zane followed Michael Nash inside. The doors closed, silencing the street completely.

[AMBIENT: ELEGANT HALL REVERB]

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS ORCHESTRAL]

He'd stepped into another world. The World Merchants' Guild wasn't merely lavish—it whispered of centuries-old elegance. 

Damn, Zane thought. This place is incredible.

He'd grown up in House Lowell and walked Cloud Hall's floating peaks, yet this felt different. The Guild's opulence settled into your bones with quiet confidence, reminding you that while kingdoms rose and fell, the Guild endured.

Standing in that magnificent hall, Zane felt humbled. These people dealt in power that transcended cultivation realms.

And they'd invited him inside.

Michael's greeting him personally signaled they were rolling out the red carpet. The old shopkeeper had submitted Zane's seven rune works to headquarters. The perfect brushwork quality had sent shockwaves through the Guild, prompting them to invest in him regardless of his Cloud Hall affiliation.

[SFX: SHARP HAND CLAP]

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS APPROACHING]

After Zane sat, Michael clapped sharply. Two maids appeared with steaming mulled cider.

Michael got straight to business. "Are you here to sell runes or purchase materials?"

"Sell a few runes, but I mostly need materials and information," Zane replied.

Michael slid a gold and crystal nameplate across the table. "Our branch commissioned this for you. You'll receive a twenty percent discount on all materials at any Guild branch, anywhere. We'll also purchase your runes at premium rates."

[AMBIENT: QUIET OFFICE]

[MUSIC: CONTEMPLATIVE STRINGS]

Zane picked it up with a wry smile. The Guild wasn't in the business of charity—they were investing in him and would expect returns. Still, Zane admired their style. It's always a pleasure doing business with smart people. Besides, I need a reliable contact right now.

Zane handed over his divine textures. "They're all one-star, but when activated, they pack the punch of ordinary two-star textures."

Michael examined the unique patterns, nodding. He'd suspected Zane was heir to a lost school of runecraft. Now, that guess was confirmed.

Michael channeled energy into the sheets. Seeing the flawless, flowing power within the brushwork, a broad smile spread across his face. He handed them to an aide for appraisal.

He didn't care much about one- or two-star textures. But as Zane grew stronger… the idea of the kid one day drawing four, five, or even six-star divine textures was staggering.

"Besides selling your work, what materials are you looking for?" Michael asked casually. He wasn't nervous; here wasn't a material in The Heartlands he couldn't acquire. With Zane's current cultivation level, his needs shouldn't be too exotic.

[MUSIC: TENSION RISING]

But his confident smile faltered once Zane started talking. "I need two portions of Celestial Ichor, a five-thousand-year-old Amethyst Thunderoak, a thousand-year-old Ashenroot, and…" 

The materials were all top-tier treasures that surfaced once a decade, if that. Even when the Guild acquired them, powerful old-timers snatched them up immediately.

"It's not that we don't want to sell them," Michael said with a wry smile, "but those items are incredibly precious. The best I can do is promise to notify you the second we hear anything."

Zane nodded. He'd figured as much.

Michael kept going. "However, regarding the Amethyst Thunderoak - I did just receive a report about that..." 

Zane smiled at the older man's shift in tone. "How much for the information?"

Nash chuckled, though his expression carried genuine warmth. "This tip isn't exactly a secret. Consider it a gift between friends." 

He burned a sending charm with practiced efficiency. Moments later, an aide delivered a carefully rolled map. Nash handed it to Zane, his smile fading into concern. 

[SFX: MAP UNFURLING]

[MUSIC: OMINOUS UNDERTONE]

"However, if you're planning to go after the Thunderoak yourself, I really wouldn't recommend it."

[AMBIENT: DISTANT WIND]

[MUSIC: DARK FOREBODING]

When a treasure that rare appeared, it inevitably drew swarms of warriors like moths to flame. Beyond the competition, Twilight Peak itself was notoriously dangerous—a place where even experienced cultivators had vanished without a trace.

In Nash's eyes, Zane's talent with runes was genuinely unmatched, perhaps once-in-a-generation. But his Innate Realm cultivation remained nowhere near strong enough to survive that kind of brutal free-for-all. The thought of such promise being snuffed out over a single treasure troubled him more than he cared to admit.

Nash studied Zane's expression for a long moment, reading the determination flickering behind those eyes. He sighed, recognizing the futility of his warning—there was no changing Zane's mind. The kid couldn't get out of there fast enough. 

After Zane departed, Michael frowned and muttered to himself, "Are the people at Cloud Hall complete idiots? How could they let him go wandering off by himself?" 

Any other faction would have locked a rune master like Zane away in protective custody. The World Merchants' Guild understood the value of rare talent, but apparently Cloud Hall did not.

[AMBIENT: DESOLATE WIND]

[MUSIC: WEARY TRAVEL THEME]

A month later, five thousand miles southwest of Cloudhaven, Zane crossed the West Mountain Pass into the Western Territories.

His flying chariot had crashed two weeks ago after encountering an unexpected storm. Finding a decent courser in these desolate lands proved impossible—the few horses available were half-starved nags barely capable of carrying their own weight. So he trudged on foot, the endless road draining his energy with every weary step.

[AMBIENT: VAST EMPTY PLAINS]

The journey tested his resolve in ways combat never had. Each day blurred into the next—walking until his legs ached, sleeping under cold stars, rationing what little food he'd brought. The isolation gnawed at him. Out here, stripped of Cloud Hall's resources and his friends' companionship, he felt the weight of his own insignificance pressing down like the vast sky above.

[SFX: DISTANT WIND]

The Western Territories were a vast, empty expanse where you could travel a hundred miles without seeing another soul. Finally, a small town shimmered into view. A weathered sign announced it: Stonefall.

[AMBIENT: BUSY TAVERN]

[SFX: DOOR CREAK]

Zane ducked into the nearest tavern.

[AMBIENT: RESTAURANT INTERIOR]

A waiter with a long, thin braid met him at the door. "Right this way, sir!"

Zane nodded and followed him upstairs.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS STOP]

[MUSIC: OMINOUS TENSION]

On the second floor, a prickle of unease ran down his spine. The room was nearly empty, yet every waiter—and the manager behind the bar—stopped to watch him. Their eyes followed him with strange, predatory stillness.

Why are they all staring? he wondered, but a low rumble from his stomach interrupted the thought.

The braided waiter returned with a menu. "Sir, these are our house specials. Care to try any?"

Zane barely glanced at it. "Bring me one of everything. I'll try all the specials."

[SFX: SUBTLE MOVEMENT]

[MUSIC: DARK SUSPENSE]

The waiter's smile widened. He nodded, then shot a look back at the manager, giving a subtle gesture. Silent, unnerving communication passed between the staff—a ripple of shared glances.

Zane sat calmly, but a knot of dread tightened in his gut. 

He had a bad feeling this meal was going to be more than just a letdown.
