Ep 1147 - Find Me

Word count: 1018

[MUSIC: TENSE BATTLE]
When Zane decided to meet the ogre warrior’s hammer head-on, most observers would think he’d lost his mind. 
In a fight like this, a Soul Sea Realm warrior had absolutely no business trying to match an ogre’s brute strength. The smart play was to dodge, weave, do anything *but* stand your ground.
But just as the massive hammer came whistling down, a cold light flared along the edge of Zane’s sword, Thundergust. 
[SFX: SWORD ENERGY FLARE]
[SFX: AURA EXPLOSION]
A wave of pure Malice exploded from him, a suffocating force that completely smothered the Ancient Evil Ape aura rolling off the ogre. Then, as everyone watched, frozen, Zane brought his five-foot blade down on the giant hammer.
[SFX: MASSIVE IMPACT THUD]
It wasn’t the clean ring of metal on metal. It was a dull, sickening thud that seemed to suck the air out of the clearing.
What happened next left everyone speechless.
[SFX: SHATTERING METAL]
[SFX: HAMMER SPLITTING]
The Earth Law protecting the dark golden hammer simply shattered. Zane’s slash carried a relentless momentum that didn’t just stop the ogre’s attack—it split the massive war hammer clean in two. 
And it didn’t stop there.
[SFX: WET IMPACT, BLOOD SPRAY]
The blade kept moving. The ogre — body the size of a small mountain, hammer nearly ten feet long — was split in two just the same. Blood sprayed in every direction. The warriors nearby threw up their Vitality in a hurry, shielding themselves from the worst of it.
[MUSIC: COLD SILENCE]
Zane held the sword horizontal for a moment. The blade shivered once, and the blood vanished from it instantly. Then, without a word, he put it away — straight into his Storage Ring, onto a rack he’d built there specifically for the purpose.
The ogre’s hammer had been poured full of everything he had. If it were placed in the Lower World, not to mention a mountain, even a continent would have been smashed in half.
Zane, however, didn’t even seem fazed. 
He crouched beside the ogre’s Storage Ring, erased the spiritual imprint with a firm press, and knocked it against the ground. Everything inside was scattered across the stone floor — rare materials, miscellaneous items, and over a hundred Sovereign Stones catching the dim light.
He didn’t look at any of it. He found the Ice Lotus in the pile, picked it out, stood up, and walked over to Freya.
“Here.” He held it out. “And — if something like this happens again, don’t try to handle it yourself. Just find me.”
The look in Freya’s eyes couldn’t be described as simple excitement anymore. He’d said it so casually. Like it was the most natural thing in the world. But she knew exactly how much those words weighed.
Power wasn’t new to her. But there was something about the way Zane operated that she couldn’t quite name — the absolute lack of urgency, the absence of any posturing. 
He’d just split an ogre in half and was now standing here handing her a flower, like one thing had nothing to do with the other. Like it had all been completely inevitable from the start.
[MUSIC: HEROIC TRIUMPHANT]
From the moment he’d snatched the Storage Ring to when he’d plucked the icy lotus from the ground, he’d been completely relaxed, his guard down. He meant every word.
The other warriors who’d been planning to jump him were suddenly paralyzed. They just stood there in silence, watching him, the courage draining from their bodies. Looking at the ogre’s corpse, they knew exactly what would happen if they made a move. Zane’s strength was demonic. Next to him, their Spirit Supreme Realm power felt like a joke. A deep, primal fear began to set in.
It wasn’t just fear of losing. It was something more unsettling than that. They’d had a plan. And now, looking at what was left of the ogre, they understood exactly what that plan would have cost them. The thought sat in their chests like a stone.
The remaining ogre warriors were seething. Even so, not a single one of them dared to say a word.
But the moment Zane turned away, the muttering started — low and furious, passing between the surviving ogre warriors as they gathered up their dead.
“The Saint Lord won’t let this go.”
“This human leaves the palace sooner or later. The Saint Lord will be waiting.”
“He’ll answer for this.”
[MUSIC: GENTLE HOPEFUL]
“Let’s go,” Zane said, turning to Freya with a soft smile. “Refining this icy lotus should help you.”
Freya nodded, quickly stowing the lotus in her own ring before following him. As she passed the dead ogre, her eyes fell on the treasures scattered across the ground—rare items and precious materials, along with hundreds of Sovereign Stones that were worth a small fortune. She hesitated. Zane had killed the ogre, so all of this was technically his. Leaving it seemed like such a waste.
But Zane hadn’t bothered, and so she wouldn’t either. It just felt right.
The other warriors filed out quietly — the Demon Night first, then the humans, one by one. Nobody spoke. The ogres were last, still carrying their dead.
[MUSIC: OMINOUS DESCENT]
Back through the wind tunnel, around the far side of the cave, and then there it was again — the mouth of the Endless Abyss, yawning open in the dark.
Zane didn’t slow down. He jumped.
Freya followed without hesitation. The others came after, one by one, until the cave was empty.
[AMBIENT: ETHEREAL VOID RESONANCE]
Lavender’s voice echoed in Zane’s memory, explaining everything in painstaking detail. He nodded along, the strange concept of this place, the Endless Abyss, finally starting to click.
[SFX: MYSTICAL CHIME]
“You might not have realized it,” Lavender had told him, “but with everything you’ve been through, you’ve just completed your first lap of the loop. Now, the second one begins.”
“So how many did you clear?” Zane had asked.
“Four,” she’d answered flatly. “The challenges on the fourth lap got too dangerous, so I had to quit.”
Even back then, at the Spirit Supreme Realm, Lavender had been a queen who commanded the respect of the Supreme Lords of the Demon Night. She didn’t lack resources, but she hadn’t been willing to risk her life for it.
[MUSIC: DETERMINED RISING STRINGS]
“But you can’t give up,” she’d said, her voice turning serious. “Not this time.”
