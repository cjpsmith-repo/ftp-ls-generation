Ep 39 - Disqualified

[SFX: MAGICAL ENERGY PULSES]
Every eye was locked on Zane, drawn to the incredible energy radiating off him. Even the disciples from the Great Houses, gathered a ways off, couldn't help but look over. It was an involuntary reaction, a magnetic pull.
"Ha, Caleb's pathetic," a young man in white from one of the Great Houses whispered, flicking open a green fan. "Brought a whole crew to jump some nobody. Guess he never learned that even a cornered rat will bite. Looks like the kid's gearing up for one last stand."
"So what? You're reading too much into it," another member of a Great House said with a dismissive nod. "The kid's in a tough spot, sure, but he doesn't have the juice to fight back. He's only at the Core Strength Level. No way he can touch someone that much stronger. Then again, Caleb really is something, stooping so low to mess with a Core Strength kid."
The bodyguard beside him, however, gave a respectful nod toward Zane. "Mister Garrett," he said quietly, "you'll be in The Gauntlet later. I'd keep an eye on that one."
"Why?" Garrett asked, his tone patient. "What am I supposed to be seeing?"
Garrett knew his bodyguard wouldn't speak up without a damn good reason; things were rarely as simple as they seemed. His man was a veteran of the Nature Level, with a strength to match. More than that, he was fresh from the brutal battlegrounds of The Crucible—a place that made a man an expert at reading people. Garrett trusted his judgment implicitly.
The bodyguard continued, "I can't put my finger on it, but I've got a bad feeling. He might only be at the Core Strength Level, but that kid is dangerous. We need to be careful."
Hearing this, Garrett's eyebrows shot up. A smirk played on his lips. "Well, this just got interesting," he mused. "Looks like Caleb might have bitten off more than he can chew."
The alliances between the kids from the Great Houses were fragile at best—they were anything but a united front. 

Caleb, meanwhile, hadn't moved. "You know," he said, almost pleasantly, "death is actually the easy way out. I have other ways to make you suffer. Sure you don't want to reconsider?"
"Not a chance," Zane said. "I told you — my life isn't yours to take."
That was all the answer Caleb needed. He gave the faintest nod.
A tall young man behind him didn't wait for more. "Why are we still talking?" he snapped, and launched himself at Zane, fist driving straight for his head.
[SFX: FIST CUTTING THROUGH AIR]
The crowd tensed. The attacker was at the peak of the Marrow Refining Level — a full stage above Zane — and faint runes flickered across his knuckles, the mark of a powerful fist technique. Everyone watching braced for the crack of a shattered arm.
Zane didn't dodge. He threw his own fist straight back.
[SFX: FISTS COLLIDING — DULL THUD]
The impact landed with a muffled crack. Zane didn't move an inch — feet planted, body absorbing the force as the warm current surged through him and dissolved the blow. 
The attacker stumbled backward three steps, nearly going down, his hand hanging limp at his side.
Nearby, Ethan's jaw dropped. He'd known Zane was stronger than he looked — but this?
A wave of quiet, fierce satisfaction rolled through the crowd of regular recruits. Someone had finally hit back.
Caleb's group didn't take it well. They closed in fast, surrounding Zane from all sides, ready to swarm.
Zane's eyes narrowed. If they rushed him together, he'd go down — but he wasn't going down alone.
Just then, a booming voice cut through the tension. "What in the hell do you think you're doing? This is the day of The Gauntlet, not some back-alley brawl!"
[SFX: CONVOY ENGINES]
[AMBIENT: ARMORED TRUCKS ARRIVING]
All heads turned to see a convoy of thirty armored trucks rolling in. The man who'd shouted stood on the lead Skycruiser. He wore ragged overalls that were a stark contrast to his square, serious face. This was Wyatt, the supervisor of The Gauntlet.
[SFX: BOOTS ON GRAVEL]
Wyatt leaped from the Skycruiser and marched straight toward the commotion. "Who was fighting?" he demanded.
"It was him!" Perrin shot out, stepping forward and jabbing a finger at Zane. "He was the one fighting, sir!"
"Takes two to tango. Who's the other one?" Wyatt asked.
"That would be me," the tall young man from House Warrick admitted, not looking the least bit concerned.
[SFX: GAVEL STRIKE]
Wyatt nodded grimly. "Alright, then. You're both disqualified from The Gauntlet!"
The color drained from Zane's face. This couldn't be happening. It was completely unfair—he was just defending himself. "Sir, I didn't start it!" he protested. "They forced my hand! Why am I being disqualified?"
"I don't care who started what," Wyatt spat. "You fight here, you get kicked out of the Skyreach Sect. No exceptions!" He had no interest in hearing Zane's side of the story, making his decision without a second thought.
Caleb spoke up. "Supervisor, I'm a Core Apprentice of the Skyreach Sect, not a participant in The Gauntlet."
"Core Apprentice?" Wyatt grunted. "Which peak?"
"Eagle Peak, sir," he replied.
"Hmph. Master Finch of Eagle Peak is here today. As a Core Apprentice, your punishment is a one-hundred-point deduction from your peak," Wyatt declared.
A smug smile spread across the young man's face. He was completely relaxed. Everyone knew that while a hundred points was a huge blow for a regular student, for an apprentice from a Great House, it was nothing. A slap on the wrist.
[AMBIENT: CROWD MURMURING ANGRILY]
They'd lost a hundred points, but they'd gotten exactly what they wanted: Zane kicked out of the Skyreach Sect. A murmur of anger rippled through the thousand-plus students watching. It wasn't even a slap on the wrist. Wyatt was clearly in the pocket of the powerful.
[MUSIC: APPROACHING DREAD]
[SFX: SKYCRUISERS DESCENDING]
[AMBIENT: ENGINE NOISE]
Just then, a fleet of Skycruisers descended, carrying all thirty-three peak masters of the Skyreach Sect. After The Gauntlet, these masters would select apprentices who met their standards, choosing the best talent to ensure their peaks thrived. The competition for recruits was fierce.
Zane knew just how screwed he was. Wyatt was obviously siding with the Great Houses. But he couldn't let them destroy his future over something he didn't even start.
"Supervisor, I didn't start this," he pleaded. "You have to believe me. Everyone here saw what happened. I was forced to defend myself! It's not fair to kick me out like this!"
"Not fair?" Wyatt shot back, his face a blank mask. "Tell me this: were you, or were you not, fighting?"
"Yes," Zane answered, his voice tight. He had fought. He'd had to. If he hadn't, he'd be dead.
[SFX: SHARP VOICE CUTTING THROUGH AIR]
A sharp, clear voice cut through the air. "That's ridiculous!"
Hearing the ice in her tone, Wyatt's smug smile faltered for a second. "Master Novak," he said, recovering quickly, "it's a minor issue, surely not worth your time."
"If it's so minor, then let the boy speak," Master Novak snapped. "You," she said, her finger pointing directly at Zane. "Tell us what happened."
"They started it," Zane said, his voice ringing with conviction as he pointed at Caleb and his crew. "They cornered me for no reason. One of them charged and threw a punch, so I fought back!"
Wyatt sneered. "Kid, that's a nice story. But it's just your word against theirs. Got anyone to back you up?"
[AMBIENT: DEAD SILENCE]
The crowd fell dead silent. A faint, knowing smile touched Caleb's lips, and Perrin could barely contain his glee.
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC TENSION]
They knew the other kids from the Great Houses wouldn't dare cross Caleb. And as for the thousand-plus students from regular houses? Anyone with half a brain knew better than to stick their neck out for some nobody.
Just as they expected, no one moved. Not a single person who had seen the whole thing, who had been simmering with anger just moments before, dared to step forward.
Anyone who spoke up would just become Caleb's next target. Who in their right mind would risk that? It was suicide to get on Caleb's bad side without the power to back it up.
