Ep 996 - Into the Storm
Word count: 1153
[AMBIENT: MYSTICAL LIBRARY]
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]
There was no denying that Zane’s Master knew far more than he could have ever imagined.
The book he’d been given, Myriad Manifestations of Nature, was a catalog of the universe’s most powerful and unique treasures. And right there in its pages was Sacred Mount Lodestone.
It didn’t just explain how to refine the mountain; it laid out secrets about it that completely blew Zane’s mind.
According to the text, collecting the mountain required three steps. First: compress it into an extremely small space. Second: use the power of the five elements to suppress it entirely. Third: subdue it. The divine runes Zane was preparing were the key to all three — and the materials they required were some of the rarest in the Heartlands.
[AMBIENT: CHAOTIC WINDS]
The mountain’s origins were a mystery, but it was a known fixture of the world of chaos. And as far as anyone could tell, it was completely unclaimed.
If the Soul Sea Realm masters from the Union of the Seven Divine Kingdoms could have budged it, the mountain would have been relocated to the Divine Kingdom Continent ages ago.
But even with the knowledge of how to claim Sacred Mount Lodestone, Zane faced a couple of massive hurdles.
[AMBIENT: VIOLENT STORM]
[SFX: WIND BLADES CUTTING]
For years, a violent storm had raged over the Chaotic Ocean of Stars, unleashing wind blades sharp enough to threaten even Spirit Supreme Realm warriors. Zane had already advanced two levels and was now at the third stage of the Life-and-Death Realm, but it was still a long shot to think he could withstand those blades with his current power.
The real key to claiming the mountain was severing it from its base—a base so tough that not even a team of dozens of Soul Sea Realm masters had been able to crack it. On his own, Zane didn’t stand a chance.
[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]
Then, a wild plan began to take shape in his mind.
[AMBIENT: BUSY MARKETPLACE]
[SFX: PARCHMENT RUSTLING]
Around that time, the World Merchants’ Guild put out a notice requesting all sorts of rare materials on his behalf. Most of them could only be found in The Heartlands, and they were notoriously difficult to acquire.
But there were always people willing to risk it all for a big enough payday. And with the prices the Guild was offering, who could really say no?
Even families that had held onto these materials for generations were tempted by the high prices, happily selling their heirlooms to the World Merchants’ Guild.
Slowly but surely, the materials were collected and funneled to Cloud Hall. This massive buying spree inevitably caught the attention of other organizations. It didn’t take much digging for them to figure out the World Merchants’ Guild was just a front for Cloud Hall—or more accurately, a front for Zane.
In the months that followed, Zane barely surfaced. Days were spent preparing divine runes and processing the materials as they arrived. Nights were spent cultivating. Three months passed like that, one long rhythm of work and training, before he finally had everything he needed.
[AMBIENT: HOWLING WIND]
[MUSIC: DRAMATIC ORCHESTRAL]
When the time came to leave, Tia and Cora both insisted on coming.
Zane didn’t even entertain it. The wind blades in the Chaotic Ocean of Stars carried up to fifty percent of the Law of Wind. His Relic-Class body could barely manage them — and even that was pushing it. Taking them into the storm would be sending them to their deaths. He told them no and didn’t discuss it further.
Tia didn’t like it. Cora liked it even less. Neither could argue with the logic.
Before he went, he spent some time with the World’s Will memories. Most of it was familiar territory. But two things stood out.
The first: somewhere north of the Divine Kingdom Continent, a vast wild landmass stretched on and on — chaotic, barely charted, populated by powerful beasts and primitive races that had no contact with the wider world. A civilization waiting to be found, or avoided.
The second was stranger. Five hundred thousand years ago, a great civilization had been erased. Not by war, not by plague — but by the collateral of a dispute between Upper Realm powers. The force that leaked out from their conflict was enough to sink an entire continent to the bottom of the sea. Whatever they had been arguing over, the world below had paid for it.
What he couldn’t find — and this bothered him — was anything about the storm itself. The World’s Will knew everything in the world. And yet the origin of the storm in the Chaotic Ocean of Stars was a complete blank. No record. No memory. Not even a rumor. Whatever had created it existed outside what the world could perceive.
That was not a comforting thought.
Zane had already used the World’s Will to pinpoint the mountain’s location west of the Chaotic Ocean of Stars. But instead of flying directly there, he flew south, plunging straight into the heart of the storm.
[SFX: THUNDER CRACK]
[AMBIENT: VIOLENT STORM]
He wasn’t diving into the storm’s center for fun; it was a calculated risk. If some unknown, powerful creature was lurking in the eye, he’d be toast.
Zane knew he couldn’t possibly sever the base of Sacred Mount Lodestone on his own. But maybe the storm could do it for him.
The wind blades were absurdly powerful. Zane was betting that if he could just channel their force against the mountain, it would be enough to do the trick.
A single blade wouldn’t be enough, but a hundred thousand? A million? He was convinced he could use the storm itself to shear Sacred Mount Lodestone clean off its foundation.
Now he just had to figure out how.
At the storm’s edge, the first wind blades hit him before he even registered them. Light-colored, shallow — one layer of Wind Law at most. He didn’t bother deflecting. They struck his Relic-Class body and dissolved without leaving a mark.
He pushed in deeper.
As Zane pushed deeper into the storm, the wind blades grew sharper and more frequent. Soon, he started seeing blades infused with the third and even fourth layers of Wind Law, their colors deepening from a pale aquamarine to a menacing teal.
Not enough, Zane thought, watching the blades tear through the air around him. These could cut me to ribbons, but they won’t leave a scratch on the mountain.
As a test, he redirected a few of the blades toward the mountain’s base. Just as he’d feared, they dissipated without leaving so much as a scratch.
I need to find the black ones! With his eyes locked on the churning chaos ahead, Zane pushed forward. After what felt like half a day, the first black wind blade finally screamed past him.
