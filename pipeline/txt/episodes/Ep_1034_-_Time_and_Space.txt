Ep 1034 - Time and Space

Word count: 1093

[AMBIENT: TENSE SILENCE]

[MUSIC: SUSPENSEFUL STRINGS]

Fenton hovered silently, his dark red jade sword in hand, looking completely calm and at peace.

“I could have just turned down the Saint Lord’s proposal,” he said suddenly, his voice low and mysterious as he idly toyed with the sword. “The truth is, this isn’t just about him. I’m also trying to prove a point to the thirteen palaces of the Alliance.”

“And what point would that be?” Zane asked, a smile playing on his lips.

“That they made the wrong choice,” Fenton answered.

[SFX: SWORD DRAWN]

[MUSIC: BATTLE THEME]

At that, Zane smirked. “Then bring it on. Prove it.” He raised his longsword, holding it at the ready.

“A Relic-Class weapon?” Fenton’s voice held a note of surprise.

“Yeah. What of it?” Zane asked.

“Nothing. It makes sense, actually,” Fenton said with a shrug. “You just ascended, so it’s not surprising you don’t have a Divine-Class weapon.” Then, he did something unexpected.

[SFX: SWORD SHEATHING]

[SFX: SWORD DRAWN]

He sheathed his dark red jade sword and pulled out an emerald green one. “I have two swords. The crimson one is a Low Grade-One Divine-Class weapon called Regis-Bane. I was going to use it on you, but that would tarnish my reputation. This Emerald one is a Low Grade-Three Divine-Class weapon, just one grade above yours. I know it’s still a little unfair, but I don’t own any Relic-Class weapons. So… sorry about that.” Fenton really came off like a guy who believed in fair play.

“A Low Grade-Three Divine-Class weapon? And a Low Grade-One?” Zane was completely lost.

[AMBIENT: CROWD LAUGHTER]

[SFX: MAGICAL SCRYING]

Seeing his confused expression, the warriors from the Myriad Spirit House burst out laughing. They were watching the whole fight from The Crucible on a Scrying Slate, which transmitted both image and sound. They weren’t missing a thing.

[AMBIENT: TRAINING GROUND CHATTER]

“See? I told you guys. Anyone from the Lower World is a total hick. Ha!”

“This dude’s probably never even seen a Divine-Class weapon before.”

“Probably. Which would be hilarious. It’s damn near impossible for a weapon refiner to forge a Divine-Class weapon in the Lower World.”

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS REVELATION]

But they were all wrong. Zane had seen more Divine-Class weapons in his life than any of them could even imagine.

The problem was, he’d never bothered to learn their grading system. He’d always just assumed Divine-Class weapons were categorized like Relic-Class ones: high, mid, and low. But from what Fenton just said, that obviously wasn’t how it worked.

Zane said nothing about this out loud. He had a Supreme Divine Weapon. But he couldn’t show it — not here, not yet. His current strength wasn’t enough to protect it. Revealing it would draw attention from figures he wasn’t ready to face.

“Now that we’ve covered the basics,” Fenton said with a faint smile, “let’s begin.”

[SFX: WIND SURGE]

[MUSIC: BATTLE THEME RISES]

Wind gathered around him — not a breeze, not a gust, but a roiling mass of elemental force that pulled at the air in the entire arena.

“Wind Law,” Zane said, watching it build. “Level Four.”

Fenton’s figure vanished into the wind.

Zane went still. He released his perception outward, tracking every movement in the churning air around him. Dozens of gusts bore down from different angles simultaneously — and then, from within them, the Emerald sword pierced through.

[SFX: SWORD CLASH]

Their blades met. Fenton reappeared at the edge of the wind, expression unchanged. “Good reaction.”

Zane lowered his sword slightly. He didn’t respond to the compliment.

“Everyone has a different affinity with the Laws,” Zane said. “Can’t you just cultivate the ones you’re strongest in?”

“No.” Fenton’s voice came from somewhere in the wind. “In the Upper World, Spatial Law and Time Law are the foundation. Everything else is optional. Those two are not.”

[SFX: WIND BUILDING]

“I’ve reached the first level of Time Law,” Fenton continued. “Second level of Spatial Law. That’s what I’m working on. It’s not enough.” He said it without embarrassment — just a problem to be solved. “The next strike won’t be as easy.”

The wind surged again — fiercer this time, more chaotic. Fenton’s figure fractured through it in multiple directions at once, impossible to pin down.

[SFX: MULTIPLE WIND STRIKES]

Zane’s sword moved at an angle — not straight, not obvious — and caught the blade anyway.

“Is Spatial Law specifically that essential?” Zane asked, as if he hadn’t just parried a strike that would have finished most Soul Sea Realm warriors.

He already knew the answer, but he wanted to hear Fenton say it.

Time Law — if an opponent could alter the flow of time at will, moving a hundred times faster than you, no body technique or Wind Law speed mattered. You couldn’t outrun time. The only counter was to cultivate it yourself.

Spatial Law worked the same way. A warrior at a high level could cross tens of thousands of miles in a single step — appearing behind you before you’d finished blinking. There was no way to fight someone at that level without your own spatial foundation.

These weren’t optional. They were the bedrock. And Zane had already mastered both.

[AMBIENT: ARENA CROWD MURMUR]

[MUSIC: TENSION BUILDING]

As Zane and Fenton faced off, the warriors back in The Crucible were buzzing.

“I can’t believe the new guy blocked two of Fenton’s attacks.”

“Relax. Fenton’s not even trying yet. You know his style—he always finishes his opponents in three strikes.”

“But the other guy isn’t going all out, either. Look how relaxed he is, still asking questions.”

“Hmph. He’s just faking it. I bet he’s scared to death and just putting on a brave face.”

“Quiet! Fenton’s about to make his third move!” someone shouted.

Fenton had a thing about it: he never beat an opponent with a single attack. His first strike was a greeting, a formality. The second was to apply pressure. Only on the third strike would he unleash his true power and claim victory.

In all his years and countless fights, Fenton had never once broken this habit, and many warriors from the Myriad Spirit House had felt the crushing power of that third strike.

In fact, some of them even wore it like a badge of honor, bragging to others, “I survived Fenton’s first two strikes.”

[SFX: EXPLOSIVE WIND PRESSURE]

[MUSIC: DRAMATIC CLIMAX]

Back in the Illusionary Arena, a crushing pressure suddenly exploded from the raging wind. The smile vanished from Zane’s face, his expression turning deadly serious.
