Ep 1309 - The Saint Killing Spear

Word count: 1081

[MUSIC: TENSE SUSPENSEFUL]
[AMBIENT: ILLUSION BATTLEFIELD ARENA]
When Zane finished speaking, Slade just looked baffled. His sharp, narrow eyes clouded with confusion. After all, whatever Zane had just said had come completely out of left field.
But the moment the meaning sank in, a tremor ran through Slade’s entire body. His eyes burned with a sudden, murderous rage.
“You’re Zane Lowell!”
Slade was the only son of Supreme Lord Gunter. The Demon Night were born warriors, but their people struggled to have children—it was the one thing holding them back. Despite his many wives, Supreme Lord Gunter had only ever fathered a single child: Slade.
He’d never gotten over his father’s death.
Now, he was finally face-to-face with his sworn enemy. 
He had never seen Zane before, but he’d pictured this exact moment countless times, swearing he’d be the one to kill Zane with his own two hands. He just never dreamed it would happen here, of all places.
[AMBIENT: CROWD MURMURING AND GASPS]
On the city walls, the reveal sent a ripple through the crowd of watching warriors.
“No way. *That’s* Zane?”
“The one who killed the four Supreme Lords?”
“I have to admit, those stories he was telling with the Roaring Token weren’t half bad.”
“This is going to be good! Zane’s from the Soul Sea Realm, so it makes sense he’d show up in the Illusion Battlefield. We’re lucky to be here for this.”
“I want to see just how strong this guy really is, if he can actually take down Supreme Lords.”
[MUSIC: OMINOUS TENSION BUILDING]
Moments ago, most would have bet on Slade. But learning his opponent was the infamous Zane changed everything.
Zane had become a living legend. Of the one hundred and twenty-eight Supreme Lords, most warriors couldn’t name half - but everyone knew Zane. He’d been broadcasting with that Roaring Token for six straight years.
“Yeah, I’m Zane Lowell,” Zane confirmed with a nod.
[SFX: DARK ENERGY SURGE]
A wave of pure malice rolled off Slade, so thick it seemed to condense in the air and take on a demonic shape.
“I really didn’t expect to run into you in the Illusion Battlefield,” Slade said, his gaze so intense it felt like he was trying to burn a hole right through him. “Too bad this is just an illusion. I can’t kill you for real.”
Beneath the rage, Slade remained cautious. He dismissed the rumor that Zane was merely an ordinary ascender riding on a divine mansion’s coattails - anything capable of killing Supreme Lords wouldn’t offer trivial inheritances. More telling: Zane had recognized him in the city yet waited until they were outside to reveal himself. That wasn’t accidental. Such confidence suggested Zane believed he could win.
The killing intent bleeding off him gradually settled, cooling into something closer to calculation. He couldn’t kill Zane here, not really—but this was still a rehearsal for the day he would.
“You’re awfully confident,” Slade said.
“Confidence isn’t optional on this path,” Zane replied evenly. “Not if you want to get anywhere on it.”
Slade’s mouth curled. “Blind confidence is its own kind of stupidity.”
They were only in the first stage of the Illusion Battlefield; the fights were only going to get more intense from here. 
[SFX: SPEAR SUMMONED FROM RING]
With a flick of his fingers, twin spear tips dropped free of his Storage Ring—two shafts, one black and one white, spiraling around each other before splitting apart into a pair of wickedly curved points.
Zane’s eyes narrowed. He knew this weapon. Lavender had mentioned it to him once, in passing.
“Zane!” Janet called down from the wall. “That’s the Saint-Killing Spear of the Demon Night!”
[SFX: SPEAR WHOOSH]
As he spoke, Slade gave his long spear a casual wave, pointing it at Zane. “You should feel honored to be facing such a precious weapon.”
Hearing this, Zane grinned. “Oh, it’s a huge honor, really. But that spear isn’t yours, is it?”
“It’s in my hands, so of course it’s mine,” Slade shot back, his chin jutting out proudly. As a member of the Demon Night, he knew exactly how sacred the Saint-Killing Spear was.
Zane’s eyes lingered on the spear. *What a shame,* he thought. *If I could get my hands on that spear and use the hall’s reward to make a copy for the sword spirit… that would solve everything.*
The Saint-Killing Spear had held a permanent spot in the Cosmic Spirit Index’s top ten since the Demon Night’s earliest days—a ranking that had never once slipped. It didn’t belong to Slade, or to anyone. By right, only Lavender herself could wield it. 
Among the Demon Night watching from the wall, faces had gone grim the moment they saw it drawn. Weapons like Jeffrey’s borrowed Blade of Hallowed Censure were one thing—a mere projection, lent freely since nothing real was at risk.
 But the Saint-Killing Spear was no ordinary treasure. Alongside the Murderous Meteor Hammer, it was a symbol of who ruled the Demon Night itself: the hammer stood for punishment, the spear for slaughter.
“There are no rules left in the Demon Night,” one of them murmured bitterly. “Not since Gunter’s people took over.”
Slade gripped the spear tighter. “I was saving this for real Ace Talents,” he said, almost to himself. “Didn’t think I’d need it yet.”
[SFX: SWORD DRAWN FROM RING]
Zane slowly drew a longsword from his Storage Ring—Thundergust. He gave it a little twirl, a smirk playing on his lips. “If you’re shameless enough to claim the Demon Night’s Saint-Killing Spear as your own, then I guess there’s nothing left to say.”
“If you have nothing to say, then go to hell!”
[MUSIC: INTENSE BATTLE DRUMS]
Just like that, Slade attacked. He was arrogant and stubborn, sure, but he was no fool. Even with a clear advantage, he always went all out—especially when he had no idea what his opponent was truly capable of.
He lunged without warning.
[SFX: SPEAR SPINNING]
[SFX: POWERFUL THRUST WITH ENERGY BURST]
The Saint-Killing Spear spun in his hand, its twin tips flashing like cold stars as he thrust it forward. A massive shadow of the spear erupted into being, whipping wildly through the air before shooting straight for Zane, radiating a terrifying power that felt like it could tear through reality itself.
“Be careful!” Janet yelled from the top of the city wall.
She was the only one who knew exactly what kind of trouble her friend Zane was in.
