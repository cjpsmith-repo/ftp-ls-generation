[AMBIENT: TENSE ATMOSPHERE]

Yolande hadn't answered Zane's question. She just kept her eyes forward, as if the abyss ahead was more interesting than anything he had to say. He'd learned enough about her by now to know that pressing her rarely worked.

Yolande rolled her eyes, her gaze darting back and forth as she weighed her words. The look in her stormy gray eyes was restless, almost agitated.

Finally, she spoke. “Of course, I know you have a Crystal Butterfly.”

“So you know why I’m here?” Zane pressed.

She nodded. “Yep. I know exactly why. But you’re an outsider. You don’t have the full story.”

“An outsider?” Zane frowned.

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]

“Vesper got lucky,” Yolande said, her voice turning serious. “I never thought he’d be willing to rot in that celestial tomb for so many years, giving up his one shot at a new life. He was fortunate that someone like you—someone destined to lead the world of chaos—finally showed up. But he didn’t tell you everything.”

“What’s the full story?” It was true. Zane knew next to nothing about any of this. He didn’t even really know what this so-called inheritance he was chasing was.

“You have your own path,” Yolande advised. “I wouldn’t recommend getting tangled up in this game.”

“What game?” Zane asked. [MUSIC: OMINOUS TENSION]

The way she said it sent a chill down his spine. It sounded like he’d stumbled into some massive conflict the moment he’d left The Heartlands. Lavender’s guess must have been right—this was a fight between human factions, maybe even one spilling over from the upper world. And from the sounds of it, Vesper and Yolande were on opposite sides, leaving Zane caught in the middle with no idea what was going on.

Yolande just sighed. “It’s a dead end.”

Zane’s patience was wearing thin. “Can you just explain it to me?”

“It’s complicated.”

She shook her head, then took two steps closer to him.

After a deep breath, a rare smile touched her lips. “But since you’ve made it this far, you have a part to play now. I just hope that after you get the inheritance, you’ll make the right call. Be clear about the path you want to walk.”

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]

“I already know which path I’m taking,” Zane said, his voice firm.

Whatever this game was, whatever conflict they were in, he was sticking to his plan. He wouldn’t let anyone sway him. No matter how Vesper or Yolande tried to use him for their own ends, he’d make damn sure they didn’t get in his way. He didn’t care who was on the “right” side; it had nothing to do with him.

Yolande nodded, a knowing look in her eyes. “Right. Someone destined to lead a world of chaos always carves their own road. Changing your path would be like trying to redirect the stars. Maybe coming to the Dark Nether Cave was just another step on your journey, and I’ve accidentally become your guide.”

Her words were so cryptic that they left not only Zane but everyone else listening completely baffled.

[AMBIENT: OMINOUS WIND]

[MUSIC: DARK ORCHESTRAL]

The abyss never seemed to get closer — but it did. Four hours of flying low to the ground, the Mad Scorpion growing larger and larger against the horizon until finally they reached the edge.

[AMBIENT: VAST ABYSS]

Standing at the rim, everyone felt instantly small. This wasn't just an abyss. It was a wound in the earth wide enough to swallow a kingdom. The Mad Scorpion lay on the far side, its body covered in sharp spikes, its length disappearing into the endless dark below. Zane studied it. Whatever Yolande called it, it looked less like a watchdog and more like a dragon. Or a snake.

Malcolm stared into the dark. "Could this be it?" he said quietly. "The abyss that leads to Hell?"

[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS CHOIR]

Yolande shook her head. "Everyone thinks Hell is a place where ghosts and monsters live. It's not. Hell is just a door."

"A door?" Malcolm said. "Then this abyss—"

"If you want to know where it leads," Yolande said flatly, "you're welcome to jump in."

Malcolm said nothing.

"What do we do now?" Zane asked.

"Take out the Crystal Butterfly," Yolande said.

[SFX: MAGICAL FLAMES ERUPTING]

Zane reached into his ring and drew it out. The moment it cleared his fingers it erupted — flame blooming from the jade in every direction. Everyone around him flinched back instinctively. The butterfly burned in his palm like a living thing, sparks scattering off it into the dark air.

"Throw it in," Yolande said.

Zane hesitated. Vesper had guarded this thing for years in that tomb and handed it to him as the key to everything. He was supposed to just drop it into an abyss?

Yolande watched his face. "Do you know why Vesper never told you any of this?"

Zane looked at her. "Why?"

She turned to Malcolm. "Your people once had a peak War Lord called Huey?"

Malcolm and Timothy both nodded.

[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]

[AMBIENT: DISTANT BATTLE ECHOES]

Huey wasn't merely famous—he was mythic, a name whispered with equal parts reverence and dread throughout both the Flaming Place and the Dread Place. For over a century, this top-level War Lord had ruled the Tower of Sin with an iron grip, his dominance so absolute that it had become the foundation of ogre supremacy itself.

Normally, the top-level War Lords in the Tower did everything they could to suppress their power. It was a calculated risk. Once they’d collected enough Lucky Light, they would leave the Tower to finally level up—a well-known trick for warriors to shatter any plateaus in their training. Because of this, War Lords who left the Tower could often break through and become War Sages with ease.

But holding back your power was a dangerous game. Do it for too long, and you might level up automatically. So, when most top-level War Lords felt they were on the verge of a breakthrough, they’d get the hell out of the Tower and the Cursed Land. Leveling up to a War Sage inside the Tower was a death sentence.

But among the ogres, there was one exception: Huey.

For over a century, he had managed to remain a top-level War Lord. For that entire time, he was the undisputed strongest seat holder in the Tower of Sin. The ogres’ long-standing control of the Tower was almost entirely thanks to him.

How Huey had managed to suppress his power for a hundred years without breaking through was a complete mystery to everyone.
