Ep 634 - The Cave

[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS]
Even though Dorian had seen this coming, hearing the words straight from Zane’s mouth still landed like a punch to the gut. He couldn't hide his disappointment.
He truly believed that if Zane stayed, he could reach heights that even Amber of the Heavenly Feather Sanctuary could only dream of. Amber was a legend, a savior.
Two hundred years ago, the Heavenly Feather Sanctuary was a nobody. The entire continent was lorded over by the Flaming Place and the Dread Place, and no one else dared to breathe in their direction. There was another race back then, the Shadow Group, big enough to have their own sacred ground and ranked fourth overall. But after a single run-in with the Ogres, they were completely wiped from existence. That’s how terrifying the powers of that era were.
[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT HORNS]
Then Amber shot out of nowhere, a dark horse who single-handedly led the Demon Night race to the absolute peak, rewriting their entire destiny. Now, the Heavenly Feather Sanctuary stood as an equal to the Flaming Place and the Dread Place.
Amber’s name was on the second row of the Talent Tablet. Zane’s name was ranked even higher than the first row, earning him the singular honor of the celestial rank. And on top of all that, he could control the Tower of Sin. Dorian was certain the kid’s potential was limitless. It was just a damn shame a talent like that wasn’t from the Violet Heart Sanctuary. Still, the world was at Zane’s feet; Dorian knew he couldn’t force him to stay.
“The Dark Nether Cave?” Dorian finally asked. “You’re planning to go in this year?”
Zane nodded. “Yeah.”
Dorian’s hands started twisting together. “Isn’t that rushing things a little? Every race gets a few spots, but most of them wait until their warriors hit the War Lord level. Some send in War Generals to get some experience, sure, but those guys can only poke around the outer circle and that’s it. The deeper you go into that cave,” he warned, his voice dropping, “the less likely it is you’re coming out.”
Zane's expression didn't waver. "There's no time."
He'd been away from The Heartlands too long. He had no idea what was happening there, and it was something he thought about constantly.
Dorian studied him for a moment, then sighed. "Do you know the origin of the Dark Nether Cave?"
Zane shook his head. Vesper had given him a Crystal Butterfly and said almost nothing else. Whatever limited knowledge Zane had, he'd pieced together himself.
"We don't know why it was created," Dorian said slowly, "and we don't know the true origin of the Tower of Sin. But after years of exploration, we've come to believe the Dark Nether Cave is an ancient battlefield."
"A battlefield?" Zane's brow furrowed.
"Not an ordinary one. Our best guess is that it was a battlefield where powerful beings from the Upper Realm once fought."
Zane's eyes sharpened. The theory wasn't hard to believe. The power of the Cursed Land far exceeded anything on the main continent — it killed War Sages and Soul Sea Realm masters without hesitation. Whatever had created it wasn't an ordinary force. And Vesper himself, even weakened and near the end of his lifespan, had told Zane plainly that he couldn't enter the Tower. He was afraid of the Cursed Land's power.
If someone like Vesper gave it that much respect, the source had to be something far beyond the main continent's scale.
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS STRINGS]
Every talent who trained in the Tower of Sin eventually faced the Dark Nether Cave. For Dorian, going into that cave had changed his life. Despite being a saint-level genius, his name was only on the seventh row of the Talent Tablet—nothing special. But he’d risked it all, gone into the cave, and came back with the Limitless Sword Array, a prize that completely flipped his fortunes. Even now, as a master in the Soul Sea Realm, that sword array was still his ultimate weapon.
"The cave has three layers," Dorian continued. "The outer layer, the inner layer, and the core. Most War Lords who enter stay in the outer layer — and even there, one wrong step can kill you. The inner layer is a true land of death. Most who enter don't come back. I was lucky." He said it without pride. "The core is something else entirely."
Zane seemed to mull this over for a second. “And the ones who went deeper?” he asked. “What happened to them?”
A grim, humorless laugh escaped Dorian. “They never came back. At this point, you just have to assume they’re dead.”
The core of the Dark Nether Cave was shrouded in a dense, roiling fog. If you stood right at the edge, you could just make out a bloody, flickering light pulsing deep within the mist. No one had a clue what was actually in there.
“The core, huh?” Zane asked, a wry smile touching his lips.
[MUSIC: TENSE CONTEMPLATION]
Vesper really should have given him a better briefing. Zane couldn’t figure out why the man had been so cryptic, just handing over the Crystal Butterfly without a single word about the inheritance. Was he supposed to search for it in the outer circle? The inner circle? Or was he expected to risk everything and march straight into the core, a place he might never return from?
Dorian saw that smile, and his expression hardened. “You’re thinking about the core, aren’t you?” he asked, his tone turning dead serious. “If you are, take my advice and drop that idea right now.”
Zane shook his head. “I’m definitely going into the cave. As for the core… I haven’t decided if I need to go that deep yet.”
Seeing the resolve in Zane’s eyes, Dorian leaned in, his voice low and urgent. “I remember one year, all the races in the Tower of Sin formed an alliance. They gathered seventy-six of their top-level War Lords to explore the core together. Not a single one of them came back.”
“Seventy-six top-level War Lords?” Zane repeated, the number clearly staggering him.
Dorian nodded grimly. “The best of the best from every race. I know you’ve got a great destiny, kid, but the core isn’t a place you can conquer with good luck. Hell, even if I went in there right now, I wouldn’t stand a chance of coming back alive, let alone you.”
The weight of Dorian’s words settled in Zane’s gut, heavy as stone.
