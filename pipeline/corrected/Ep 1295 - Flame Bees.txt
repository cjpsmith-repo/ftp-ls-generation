Ep 1295 - Flame Bees

[MUSIC: EPIC ORCHESTRAL]
[AMBIENT: VOLCANIC ERUPTION]
[SFX: MASSIVE COLLISION]
[SFX: SHOCKWAVE BLAST]
The moment Janet launched herself from the Lava Giant’s head, the two behemoths collided behind her in a deafening crash. She flew through the air, the soundwave at her back, and stuck a perfect landing on Sacred Mount Lodestone.
Zane let out a breath he didn’t realize he’d been holding. She’d made it. Janet had been in an impossible position, but she’d still gambled everything to free that flame kitten first. He and the others had watched, hearts in their throats. A split second slower, and she would have been crushed.
Janet had barely caught her breath when Zane’s voice cut through the chaos from the shore. “Get to the top of the mountain! Now!”
His warning came just in time. The impact had sent the two giants stumbling. 
[SFX: GIANTS FALLING]
[SFX: MASSIVE LAVA SPLASH]
They lost their balance and toppled backward into the molten lake, their colossal bodies displacing the lava and sending a tidal wave of fire surging out in every direction.
“Move! Move!”
“Hurry!”
Coleman was the first to react, scrambling up the mountain with Hailey and Janet right behind him.
But they were already in deep trouble. If the lava completely swamped the mountain, they’d have nowhere to run.
[SFX: ROCK CLIMBING SCRAMBLE]
Coleman, always at his best in a crisis, practically flew up the rock face, using bursts of internal energy to find impossible handholds. Hailey followed close behind him, with the others not far off. The moment he hit the peak of Sacred Mount Lodestone, he didn’t hesitate, launching himself across the three-thousand-foot gap to the far shore. 
[SFX: BODY IMPACT ON GROUND]
[SFX: LAVA WAVE CRASH]
He hit the ground with a bone-jarring thud and took off running, the lava wave already crashing against the shore behind him. Zane, who’d been shouting warnings from the edge, was forced to scramble back to avoid the surging molten rock.
Unfortunately, Janet and the others had missed their window. With the shoreline now completely flooded, any jump would be a dive straight into lava. 
[MUSIC: TENSE STRINGS WITH DEEP BASS]
They could only watch in horror as the fiery tide crept up the mountainside, their hearts hammering against their ribs. They were already at the summit; there was nowhere left to run. If that wave reached them, it was over.
One hundred fifty feet.
One hundred feet.
Fifty feet.
Huddled together on the tiny peak, they watched the impossible happen. 
[SFX: CRACKLING HEAT WAVES]
The heat was becoming unbearable, the air thick with the smell of sulfur.
It climbed to within ten feet of the summit… and stopped. For a heart-stopping second, it just hung there, a wall of liquid fire. 
[SFX: LAVA RECEDING]
Then, like a receding tide, it began to fall.
“We actually made it,” Janet sighed, the tension draining out of her in a rush.
The others sagged with relief. 
Once the lava had fully retreated from the shore, the group made their way down and crossed over at last.
[SFX: MOUNTAIN SHRINKING]
Zane raised his hand toward Sacred Mount Lodestone. Slowly, it began to turn, shrinking with every rotation—from a towering peak down to a small, fist-sized stone—before settling back into his open palm.
“Does this count as us passing the fourth Hall of Causality?” Janet asked, a smile creeping across her face. She wasn’t the type to settle for anything less than a clean win.
It was a fair question to be proud of. In a previous run through the hall, two powerful prodigies had helped guide a much larger group through—but the two of them hadn’t gotten along, and their constant rivalry left everyone else to die along the way. Only the prodigies themselves made it out. This group, by contrast, had lost just four people the entire way through. For the fourth Hall of Causality, that was nothing short of remarkable.
Just as they turned to press on, a flicker of flame stirred at the edge of the lava lake. A small shape climbed up onto the shore.
[SFX: KITTEN MEOW]
Zane studied it for a moment, then let out a low laugh. “Hey—isn’t that the same flame kitten from before?”
Janet’s eyes narrowed as she looked closer. The kitten’s fur was dimmer than the others they’d seen, and she recognized it at once—this was the one they’d spent so long wrangling.
“What are you doing here?” Janet asked, watching it creep toward her without a trace of hostility. If anything, it looked like it wanted to be close to her.
“You want to come with me?” she asked.
The kitten meowed again, more insistent this time.
Janet reached down and picked it up. Where she’d braced for a burn, she felt only warmth.
“This little guy’s serious about following you,” Coleman said, clearly surprised.
“The kittens down in this lake are basically food for the Lava Giants,” Zane said. “Whatever’s further down must be even worse. Can’t blame it for wanting out.”
Janet cradled the kitten against her, a small smile tugging at her mouth. “Then I suppose I’ll look after it for a while.”
[MUSIC: MYSTERIOUS SHIMMERING]
[AMBIENT: BUZZING SWARM]
Just as they caught their breath, a swarm of Flame Bees descended from above. But instead of attacking, the glittering cloud just hovered there. It didn’t take long to figure out what was happening—the bees were the reward. They were ridiculously easy to kill, and each one was worth a staggering ten thousand Illusion Points. Taking down just a thousand would net a warrior over ten million.
This was the deal with the Hall of Causality: the trials were brutal, but the payoff was huge.
[SFX: RAPID STRIKES]
Everyone got to work, taking down as many of the docile bees as they could. Zane ended up destroying around eight hundred, racking up over eight million Illusion Points. It was less than he’d scored in the Second Hall, but that wasn’t a fair comparison. Back then, most warriors were thrilled to walk away with one or two million points, while the strongest might have gotten three or four. Zane’s ten million had put him in a league of his own.
The others made out well, too. Janet took down more than seven hundred Flame Bees, with Coleman and the rest averaging about six hundred each.
With the last of the bees dispatched and their points tallied, they left the hall behind and continued their climb. Janet had guessed there would be five trials in total, but that was all it was—a guess. No one knew what was waiting for them at the very top. Still, a current of excitement ran through the group. 
[MUSIC: TRIUMPHANT ASCENDING]
[AMBIENT: MOUNTAIN WIND]
They pushed forward, climbing as fast as they could, all of them thinking the same thing: this had to be the final test.
